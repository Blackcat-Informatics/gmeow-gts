# Part P9: Conversion from the first generation

## 0. Scope, dependencies and notation

This part defines what an implementation does with a file of the first generation and how a first-generation file becomes a file
of this generation. It defines: the reader obligation and the value of the two reader claims (section 1); the conversion function
`convert`, which is total and has no option, mode or tunable (section 2); the SOURCE FOLD, the object that the mapping takes as
input (section 3); the conversion vocabulary, the RDF terms under which first-generation metadata becomes statements (section 4);
the mapping `M` from the source fold to a fold of this generation, for every first-generation construct (section 5); the
conversion procedure (section 6); the conversion proof and what it proves (section 7);
what cannot be converted and the loud failure with its `conversion_reason` (section 8). Section 9 holds the worked examples.

It depends on, by part and section title:

- `P1` (Model and wire): the typed model and its one serialization, the generation of a file (the procedure for the first item of
  a file), the header, the frame envelope, the context table and `H_ctx`;
- `P2` (Registry): every enumeration, the finding `conversion_stopped`, the `conversion_reason` values (section "The conversion
  reasons"), the context `source_bytes`, the enumerations `readers_claim` and `file_result`;
- `P3` (RDF content): terms, lines, the fold, the labelled set and its `record` lines, the content digest, the `bindings` and
  `suppress` frames, the hiding rule and the visible dataset;
- `P4` (Blobs): the identity of a unit and a blob (the name `H_ctx(unit, bytes)`), and the blob set;
- `P7` (Canonical form and reading): the canonical file of a fold and compaction, the result of reading, and the reader's
  capacity;
- the first-generation specification, called `S1`: "GTS (Graph Transport Substrate) Specification", document version `1.0-rc1`,
  dated 2026-06-18, wire-format major version `1`. `S1` is a released document. This part cites it by section number and
  restates a rule of it only in 3.3, where it names a departure from it. Where this part says "the first-generation reader" it
  means a reader of `S1`. `convert` reads through it by THE CONVERSION READING of 3.3, which is `S1` and the nine cases of
  3.3, and the item view of 3.1 is presented under that reading;
- the labelling specification `L1`, only through `P3`.

Notation. `E(x)` is the deterministic CBOR encoding of the value `x` (`P1`). A record is written `[field, ...]` in the declared
order, a variant `[tag, fields...]`, `option(T)` is `[1]` or `[2, v]` (`P1`). Source values are shown in CBOR diagnostic
notation (RFC 8949, section 8) with text keys between double quotes, and are marked `S1`; values of this generation are typed
values. A term of this generation is written `iri("...")`, `literal(lexical, datatype)`, `lang_literal(lexical, language,
direction)`, `blank(label)` or
`triple(s, p, o)` as in `P3`. `<D>` followed by a name stands for 32 bytes of a digest that the example does not state: no example
states
a digest it cannot show by hand. Enumeration values are written by label; the codes are those of `P2`. A requirement has an
identifier `P9-n`,
states one thing. Registry version `1` is in force.

## 1. The reader obligation

A reader's claim is a typed value of its own documentation and of the `readers` field of its result, never of a file (`P7`):

```text
readers_claim = 1 second_only / 2 first_and_second          ; P2
```

THE VALUE OF THE CLAIM. An implementation can read this generation only, and says so: it claims
`second_only` and reports every first-generation file it meets as `unsupported_generation`. It owes nothing to a released format
that this generation does not extend, and no rule of this generation depends on the first generation. An implementation
that reads both generations claims `first_and_second`.

What a reader does with a file, by the generation of its first item (`P1`, "The generation", the outcomes of the procedure for
the first item of a file):

| first item | `second_only` | `first_and_second` |
|---|---|---|
| record grammar, generation `2` | reads the file as this generation | the same |
| first-generation grammar, `"v"` `1` (a map, bare or under the tag `55799`) | finding `unsupported_generation`, no segment, stops | offers the whole input to the first-generation reader and returns its result unchanged as `[1, first_generation_result]`, a `bytes` value in that reader's own form, which this generation does not read |
| first-generation grammar, `"v"` any other value | `unsupported_generation`, stops | `unsupported_generation`, stops |
| record grammar, generation `3` or more | `unsupported_generation`, stops | `unsupported_generation`, stops |

**P9-1.** A reader MUST state its `readers_claim` in the `readers` field of every result of reading, and a reader that does not
implement the first-generation reader MUST claim `second_only`.

**P9-2.** A `second_only` reader MUST report the finding `unsupported_generation` for a first item of the first-generation
grammar whose `"v"` is `1`, MUST read nothing after that item and MUST NOT translate, guess or partly read the file.

**P9-3.** A `first_and_second` reader MUST give a file whose first item is of the first-generation grammar with `"v"` `1` to the
first-generation reader, MUST return that reader's result unchanged in the variant `first_generation` of `file_result`, and MUST
NOT apply any rule of this generation to it.

**P9-4.** The first-generation reader that P9-3 offers a file to MUST apply `S1` and no rule of this generation: its
identification rule (a map with the
key `"gts"` and without the key `"t"`), its tag `55799`, its codecs, its signature identifiers and its diagnostics are those of
`S1`. It MUST implement every baseline codec of `S1` 8.4 with every parameter `S1` 8.5 gives it, the in-band dictionary `dct` of
`zstd` included.

**P9-5.** A reader of this generation MUST NOT read a file that holds a segment of each generation as one file: a file of this
generation holds no first-generation segment, and a first-generation header after the first item of a file of this generation is
an opaque item (`P1`). The bytes of a first-generation file followed by the bytes of a file of this generation are a
first-generation file with trailing items that `S1` judges.

**P9-6.** A reader that claims `first_and_second` MUST be able to produce the item view of 3.1 for every file it gives to the
first-generation reader, so that `convert` (section 2) reads the source only through it.

## 2. The conversion function

```text
convert(source: bytes) -> (result: bytes, proof: conversion_proof)  /  conversion_stopped(reason, offset)
```

`source` is the whole content of a first-generation file as bytes. `convert` is a TOTAL function: for every byte string it either
returns both values, or stops with the finding `conversion_stopped` (`P2`) carrying one `conversion_reason` and the offset in
`source` that the reason names (section 8). It has no option, no mode and no tunable.

THE RESULT OF A CONVERSION IS THE CANONICAL FILE of `M(SF)`, the fold that the mapping of section 5 gives, as `P7`
("The canonical form", "Compaction") defines it, with `profile` `absent`. This part states no rule
about how the result's frames are written: no order, reuse, cut, codec, label or placement is chosen here. The consequences, each
stated once:

1. the result is one segment, and it is a function of the content of the source and of nothing else;
2. the converter needs the fold, `M(SF)` and its labelled set, within its capacity (the part on canonical form and reading,
   "Capacity"), and stops with `source_exceeds_capacity` when it does not fit (8.3);
3. each case in which compaction stops is a conversion stop (2.1);
4. the content digest of the proof is the content digest of the canonical file (7.1);
5. the result is the canonical file in the HEAD CLAIM (every item other than the parity frames, the `offsets` item and the
   locator: its kind and order, and every frame's `frame_type`, `critical`, `transparent`, `content`, `decoded_length`,
   `payload_digest` and `identifier`; `P7`, "Definition and the two claims"), which every converter produces, so two converters
   produce the same head; its stored bytes (the `storage` and `payload` of every frame, the `parity` frames, the `offsets` item
   and the locator) equal the canonical bytes when the converter holds the reference encoder (`P7`, "The reference encoder and
   the bytes claim"); a converter that does not hold it writes its own storage and makes the head claim only. Its result is a
   conforming canonical file: a reader that holds the encoder reports the storage level `differs` and a reader that does not
   reports it `unchecked` with `encoder_not_held`, each with NO finding (the storage level only reports; the header's
   `canonical` field is the head claim and nothing else).

### 2.1 The stops of the conversion that come from the canonical file

Compaction (`P7`, "Compaction") stops in four cases, and the canonical file can hold a frame above the reader's capacity. Each
is a conversion stop with the `conversion_reason` of the second column and the `offset` of 8.1. The last column says, for each,
whether a correct converter can reach it from a source that the first-generation reader accepts.

| the case | `conversion_reason` | can a correct converter reach it? |
|---|---|---|
| a `terms` frame of the source was refused by the total formed length | `content_mismatch` | NO: the fold of `convert` is `M(SF)`, built from the first-generation source and not read from a file of this generation, so no `terms` frame is refused; a converter that meets this stop has a defect |
| the fold holds a held row, binding or entry | `content_mismatch` | NO: 8.2 item 9 stops the conversion on any source text that is not valid `UTF-8` and would become a term, a language tag or a value of the vocabulary, and 8.2 items 9 and 10 check every term against the position rules; every term of `M(SF)` is therefore readable, defined before it is used, and no row, binding or entry is held; a converter that meets this stop has a defect |
| the labelling of the labelled set is refused (`refusal_width`, `refusal_size`, `refusal_weight`, `refusal_term_node` or `refusal_limit`) | `source_exceeds_capacity` | YES: a fold can exceed what the converter's labelling allows (8.3 item 2); `refusal_term_node` only by a collision of `H_ctx` |
| a present blob does not rebuild to its declared name and length | `content_mismatch` | NO: the converter names each blob and states its length from the bytes it holds (5.7), so the rebuild equals both; a converter that meets it has a defect |
| a frame of the canonical result has a PAYLOAD above the converter's `stored_bytes_per_frame` (its stored length) or `decoded_bytes_per_frame` (its `decoded_length`), or the result has more items than `items_per_read`, a total decoded length above `decoded_bytes_per_read`, a total rebuilt length above `rebuilt_bytes_per_read`, a total formed length above `formed_bytes_per_read` or a total labelled weight above `labelled_bytes_per_read` | `source_exceeds_capacity` | YES: the frames that can exceed the minimum capacity are those listed by `P7`, "The canonical frames that can exceed the minimum capacity", so a source whose every item is small can still give one, for instance through the `index` frame of a fold with very many named graphs; and the recipes of a result whose blobs share units rebuild more bytes than the result stores (8.3 item 2) |

The `content_mismatch` of the first, second and fourth rows is stated so that the function is total and the stop has one reason;
it means a
defect of the converter. The other two rows are conditions of the source and of the converter's capacity, and
`source_exceeds_capacity` is their reason.

**P9-7.** `convert` MUST be a total function of the bytes of `source`: it MUST return a result and a proof, or stop with
`conversion_stopped` and one `conversion_reason`, and it MUST NOT return a result with a failed proof, a partial result or a
result with a stop recorded elsewhere.

**P9-8.** `convert` MUST take no option, mode, parameter, plan, key or environment value that changes its result or its proof.

**P9-9.** `convert` MUST read `source` only through the first-generation reader under THE CONVERSION READING (3.3), MUST apply the
rules of this part to what that reader presents, and MUST NOT apply any rule of `S1` itself.

**P9-10.** The result of `convert` MUST be the canonical file of `M(SF)` with `profile` `absent` in the head claim (`P7`,
"Definition and the two claims"), with the stored bytes of the canonical file when the converter holds the reference encoder (a
converter without it makes the head claim only, and the storage level of its result is reported `differs` or `unchecked` with no
finding), and the proof MUST be of that file.

**P9-11.** `convert` MUST NOT modify, truncate or remove the source, and its caller MUST keep the source at least until the proof
has been checked (section 7).

**P9-12.** `convert` MUST stop with the reason of 2.1 in each case of that table, and MUST write no result and no proof then.

## 3. The source fold

### 3.1 The item view of the first-generation reader

The first-generation reader reads a first-generation file by `S1` (sections 3, 5, 6 and 9.1) under THE CONVERSION READING (3.3).
`convert` needs from it, for each item of the file: the byte offset and length of the item, which with `source` give the exact
bytes of the item; for a header, its map; for a frame, its map without the interpretation
of its payload, its decoded payload (`S1` 6.1) or the reason that it cannot be decoded, the presence of `"sig"`; and the
diagnostics of `S1` (2.4) with the offset of the item that raised each. This is the ITEM VIEW. It is a view of the reader and
not a second reader: every statement of `S1` about reading stays the reader's. The length of an item is the distance from its
offset to the offset of the next item, to the torn offset or to the end. A diagnostic that names no item (a layout claim that
the segment breaks) has the offset of the header of its segment. The view presents what `S1` admits even where a reader's
library refuses it: a text string that is not valid `UTF-8` is presented as its bytes (8.2 item 9 judges it), a value under
the tag `2` or `3` is presented under its tag (4.1), and a frame under `zstd` with an in-band dictionary is decoded (P9-4).

### 3.2 What the fold holds

The SOURCE FOLD `SF` of a first-generation file is the file fold of `S1` (7.5) in this form. It is a tuple of the following
components, and `M` (section 5) is defined over it and over nothing else.

- SEGMENTS: the list of the segments in file order, each with its zero-based ORDINAL. For each segment: the header fields
  `"prof"`, `"layout"` (when present), `"meta"` (when present) and every header key that `S1` does not define (the header
  extension keys, `S1` 22.2), as CBOR values; and the segment's OWN `meta`, the shallow union in frame order of the maps of its
  `meta` frames and of the `"meta"` map of its `snapshot` frames (`S1` 7.5; a later key replaces an earlier one).
- TERMS of each segment: the sequence of its terms by id (`S1` 7.2), the terms of a `snapshot` taking ids shifted by the number of
  ids the segment holds before the snapshot (the conversion reading, 3.3).
- ROWS of each segment, with ids resolved to terms of the segment: `quads` rows `(s, p, o, g?)`; `reifies` rows `(r, s, p, o, g?)`
  as the statements `r rdf:reifies <<( s p o )>>`; `annot` rows `(r, p, v, g?)`. The rows of a `snapshot` are the rows of the
  frames it stands for.
- FRAME ENVELOPES: for each frame, its zero-based position among the frames of its segment (the header not counted), its type
  text `"t"`, and every CONTENT key of the frame that no other component holds (5.10): the `"pub"` of a frame that is not a `blob`
  frame, `"to"`, the extension keys of the frame map and of its payload map, and the keys that the fold ignores in a term map.
- BLOB DECLARATIONS in file order: for each `blob` frame (and each entry of the `"blobs"` map of a `snapshot`, which `S1` 10
  folds as the equivalent `blob` frames) its digest, its bytes (inline) or its digest alone (external), and its `"pub"` map.
- SUPPRESSION DIRECTIVES in file order: for each `suppress` frame its targets in order, its `"reason"` and its `"by"`.
- DIAGNOSTICS with their offsets, in ascending offset of the item that they name (3.3 case 7), and within one item in ascending
  bytewise order of the `S1` class name (`S1` 2.4). The order in which a reader raises them is not part of `SF` and selects
  nothing.

`SF` holds every CONTENT key of the header and of every frame, and holds no STRUCTURE key. The two lists are the tables of 5.10,
and every key of `S1` is in exactly one of them. The proof covers content and not structure (7.3).

### 3.3 The conversion reading

THE CONVERSION READING is the reading of `S1` from which `convert` takes the source fold `SF`. It is `S1` plus the nine cases
below. Where `S1` states a rule the conversion reading is that rule. Where `S1` is silent, or the conversion reading departs
from it, a case below states the reading. The cases are the whole of the difference: nothing else of the conversion reading is
left to an implementation.

`convert`, `SF`, the item view, the proof and every stop of section 8 are defined by the conversion reading and by nothing else;
the behaviour of whichever reader an implementation uses is not normative. The conversion reading defines nothing of the result
that P9-3 returns: that result is the first-generation reader's, returned unchanged in that reader's own form, and this part
says nothing of what it holds.

A map key is ABSENT when the map has no such key and PRESENT otherwise. A present key whose value is `null` (CBOR simple value 22)
is present and its value is not text.

1. TEXT FIELDS: ABSENT, NULL, EMPTY. For each field the result is the entry of its row. A value that is present and of another
   type than the row requires, `null` included, is the stop of the row, with the stop of 8.2 named in it.

   | field | absent | present, not of the type stated (`null` too) | empty text |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | `"k"` of a term | `unrepresentable_term` | `unrepresentable_term` | not text: `unrepresentable_term` |
   | `"v"` of `k` `0` (IRI) | `unrepresentable_term` | `unrepresentable_term` | an `iri` with empty text |
   | `"v"` of `k` `1` (literal) | `unrepresentable_term` | `unrepresentable_term` | a lexical form of empty text |
   | `"v"` of `k` `2` (blank node) | an anonymous node (`S1` 7.1) | `unrepresentable_term` | an anonymous node (`S1` 7.1) |
   | `"l"` | no language | `unrepresentable_term` | a `lang_literal` whose language is the empty text |
   | `"dir"` | no direction | `unrepresentable_term` | `unrepresentable_term` (not `"ltr"` or `"rtl"`) |
   | `"dt"` | the datatype of case 2 | `unrepresentable_term` | `unrepresentable_term` (not an id) |
   | `"tt"` of `k` `3` | `unrepresentable_term` | `unrepresentable_term` | not an array: `unrepresentable_term` |
   | header `"prof"` | `"generic"` (case 6) | `unrepresentable_frame` | the empty text, `gtsc:profile ""` |
   | header `"layout"` | no statement | `unrepresentable_frame` | the empty text, `gtsc:layout ""` |
   | `"mt"`, `"rep"`, `"reason"` | none | `unrepresentable_frame` | the empty text, carried as it is |

   A text is carried as it is: no empty text is replaced, trimmed or defaulted, except the label of a blank node, whose empty
   text and absence are both the anonymous node. A key that `S1` ignores on a term (`"tt"` on a term that is not `k` `3`, `"rf"`,
   `"l"` on an IRI) is not read: it is carried by 5.6a.
2. THE DATATYPE OF A LITERAL. When `"dt"` is absent the datatype is `rdf:dirLangString` when `"l"` and `"dir"` are both present,
   `rdf:langString` when `"l"` is present, and `xsd:string` otherwise (`S1` 7.1). `S1` is silent on a `"dt"` beside an `"l"`:
   the conversion reading accepts it only when it names an `iri` whose text, compared whole, is the datatype just stated (5.2
   item 2); any other is `unrepresentable_term`.
3. SNAPSHOT SCOPE AND IDS. The terms of a `snapshot` take the ids `n`, `n + 1`, ... where `n` is the number of terms the segment
   holds before the snapshot, and the `"dt"`, `"rf"` and `"tt"` ids of those terms and the ids of the snapshot's `quads`,
   `reifies` and `annot` rows are shifted by `n`. The terms of the snapshot belong to the dictionary and to the blank-node scope
   of
   its segment: one label in a snapshot and in a frame of the segment is one node. A frame after the snapshot counts the
   snapshot's terms among the ids of its segment. (`S1` 7.1 names the snapshot dictionary as a scope of its own; the
   conversion reading departs from `S1` here.)
4. METADATA MERGING. The segment's own `meta` is the shallow union, in item order, of the maps of its `meta` frames and of the
   `"meta"` maps of its `snapshot` frames, each snapshot map taken at the item of the snapshot. Keys are compared by the
   deterministic encoding of the key, a float key being encoded as `fb` and the eight bytes of its 64-bit form widened by bits
   (case 5), so that two spellings of one float are one key; a later entry replaces an earlier entry of the same key whole (no map
   is merged into a
   map); a top-level key of such a map that is not text is `unrepresentable_frame` (8.2, rule 14). The merged `"pub"` of a blob
   digest is the shallow union, in file order, of the `"pub"` maps of every `blob` frame and snapshot blob entry of that
   digest, its keys compared and its entries replaced in the same way (5.7); a key of a `"pub"` map can be of any type and
   is carried by `V`, and it stops nothing. The header
   `"meta"` is not merged: it is carried as it is. The file-level view of `S1` 7.5 is not computed (5.6).
5. FLOATS. A floating-point item of half, single or double width is read as its 64-bit form WIDENED BY BITS: the sign bit is
   kept; a finite value, either zero and a subnormal included, and an infinity take the 64-bit form of the same value, which
   is exact; a NaN takes the exponent of all ones and its significand bits followed by zero bits to the width of 52, so that
   it keeps its sign and its payload and no bit is set or cleared. (a) `S1` 4 fixes the form of integers, lengths and
   keys in the content that an `"id"` hashes and not the form of a float, and the first-generation writers differ in it. The
   conversion reading knows TWO FORMS of that content, each applied to every float of the item. In both, positive infinity is
   `f9 7c 00`, negative infinity `f9 fc 00` and every NaN, whatever its sign and payload, `f9 7e 00`. In THE WIDE FORM a finite
   value, either zero included, is written in 64 bits (`fb` and eight bytes). In THE SHORTEST FORM a finite value is written in
   the narrowest of the half, single and double widths that holds the value exactly (`1.5` is `f9 3e 00`, `100000.0` is
   `fa 47 c3 50 00`, `0.1` is `fb 3f b9 99 99 99 99 99 9a`). The `"id"` of an item HOLDS when it equals the hash of the
   content written in the wide form, or, failing that, of the content written in the shortest form; an item that holds no
   float has one form. Which form held is not carried and changes nothing in `SF`. (b) As a value (`V`, 4.1) a float is
   the 16 upper-case hexadecimal digits of its 64-bit form widened by bits: negative zero is `8000000000000000`, the
   infinities `7FF0000000000000` and `FFF0000000000000`, the half-width NaN `f9 7e 00` is `7FF8000000000000` and the
   half-width NaN `f9 fe 01` is `FFF8040000000000`. Two sources that differ only in the sign or the payload of a NaN have two
   images.
6. A HEADER WITHOUT `"prof"`. The CDDL of `S1` 5 lists the key without `?` and its comment gives the default `"generic"`; the
   conversion reading takes the default: the header holds `"prof"` `"generic"`, and `M` states `S gtsc:profile "generic"` for it.
7. WHICH DIAGNOSTIC SELECTS THE STOP. A stop is selected by position and by rule number, and by nothing else. The items are judged
   in source order (6, step 1); a diagnostic that names no item (a layout claim that its segment breaks) belongs to the header
   item of its segment and has its offset; at one item the stop is the rule of the lowest number in 8.2 that holds anywhere in
   the item. The order in which a first-generation reader raises its diagnostics, and the order in which it reports them, selects
   no stop and no reason.
8. THE BYTES OF AN ITEM. The conversion reading changes no byte of the source and verifies no signature: the exact bytes of an
   item are `source` from its offset for its length, and a `"sig"` is counted (7.1), never read. A key that `S1` does not define
   is carried by 5.6a and 5.10, as the value `V` gives, and is not interpreted.
9. NESTING. `S1` states no greatest depth of an item, and the conversion reading has none: an array, a map or a tag of the
   source is read at any depth, an item is judged at depth `d` as the same bytes are judged at depth `1`, and `V` (4.1) gives a
   value at depth `d` the image that its recursive rule gives. No stop of section 8 and no diagnostic names a depth. The depth
   of an item of the source is bounded by its bytes alone.

**P9-13.** The first-generation reader that `convert` uses MUST present the item view of 3.1 under the conversion reading, MUST
fold by `S1` with the nine cases of 3.3, and `convert` MUST take `SF` from that fold.

**P9-14.** `convert` MUST NOT interpret an item of the source in any way that `S1` or the nine cases of 3.3 do not state. Nothing
is left to the reader that `convert` uses: for a field that is absent, `null` or empty, for the scope of a snapshot, for the
merging
of metadata, for a float, for a header without `"prof"`, for the diagnostic that selects a stop, for the datatype of a literal,
for the bytes of an item and for the depth of an item, the reading is the case of 3.3 that names it. A reading that neither `S1`
nor 3.3 states is not made; the item is classified by 8.2, and an item that no rule of
8.2 names is converted.

**P9-43.** `convert` MUST take `SF` from the conversion reading even where a first-generation reader reads the source
otherwise, and MUST NOT report such a difference as a stop or a finding.

**P9-44.** `convert` MUST read a field that is absent, present and `null`, or present and empty as the table of 3.3 case 1
says, and MUST NOT read a `null` as an absent field or as an empty text.

## 4. The conversion vocabulary

First-generation metadata has no place in the structure of this generation: there is no `meta` frame, no header metadata and no
public envelope (`P3`, "Metadata is RDF"). The converter therefore states it as RDF. The statements use one vocabulary, whose
namespace is `https://w3id.org/gts/conversion#`, written below with the prefix `gtsc:` for the exact IRI formed by the namespace
followed by the local name. The prefix is a notation of this part and is never stored.

THE VOCABULARY IS USED ONLY BY CONVERSION. No other part reads it, no rule of any other part gives an IRI of it a meaning, and a
reader never treats a statement differently because it uses it (`P3`, "Metadata is RDF": no rule reads a vocabulary). It is a
specification of what `M(SF)` holds so that a consumer can find the source's metadata; a consumer can read it.

### 4.1 Every first-generation CBOR value as a term

The function `V` takes a CBOR data item, in the generic data model of RFC 8949 section 2 with tags kept as tags (a bignum under
the tag `2` or `3` is a `gtsc:Tagged` node whose content is the byte string, and the tag `55799` inside a value is a tag like
any other), to a term of `M(SF)` and the statements that define it, so that the mapping is total and injective. Statements are
in the graph `D`.

| the value | its term | the statements |
|---|---|---|
| text | `literal(the text, xsd:string)` | none |
| unsigned or negative integer | `literal(decimal digits, xsd:integer)`, the literal of the typed value `[3, n]` of kind `int` (`P3`, "Typed values"): a minus sign before the digits of a negative value, no plus sign, no leading zero | none |
| `false`, `true` | `literal("false" or "true", xsd:boolean)`, the literal of the typed value `[1, v]` of kind `bool` | none |
| byte string | `literal(two upper-case hexadecimal digits for each byte, xsd:hexBinary)`, the literal of the typed value `[5, b]` of kind `octets`; the empty string for no bytes | none |
| floating-point number of any width | `literal(16 upper-case hexadecimal digits of its binary64 form, gtsc:Float64)`, as text; a half-precision or single-precision value is widened first, by bits (3.3 case 5), a NaN keeping its sign and payload | none |
| `null` | `iri(gtsc:Null)` | none |
| `undefined` | `iri(gtsc:Undefined)` | none |
| any other simple value `n` | `literal(decimal digits of n, gtsc:Simple)`, as text | none |
| array of `n` values | `rdf:nil` for `n` equal to `0`; otherwise a fresh blank node `c1` | for the values `v1` to `vn`: `c_i rdf:first V(v_i)`, and `c_i rdf:rest c_(i+1)` with `c_(n+1)` the term `rdf:nil` |
| map | a fresh blank node `m` | `m rdf:type gtsc:Map`; for each entry, in ascending bytewise order of the deterministic encodings of the keys, a fresh blank node `e`, `m gtsc:entry e`, `e gtsc:key V(key)`, `e gtsc:value V(value)` |
| value `v` under the tag `t` | a fresh blank node `g` | `g rdf:type gtsc:Tagged`, `g gtsc:tag t` (an integer literal), `g gtsc:content V(v)` |

A text literal and a literal of another datatype never collide: the datatype is a field of the term. A byte string holds the
tag-free value, so the tag `2` or `3` of a bignum is a `gtsc:Tagged` node whose content is the byte string. The self-describe tag
`55799` of a header or a file is not a value of any map and is not carried; the same tag inside a value is a tag like another.

CLAIM V1 (`V` is total and injective on the CBOR data model). Every value of the data model has one image. Two values have equal
images, up to a renaming of the fresh blank nodes, only when they are equal. Proof. The cases of the table are exhaustive over the
major types and the simple values of RFC 8949. The term of a value fixes its type: a literal's datatype names text, integer,
boolean, byte string, float or simple value; `gtsc:Null` and `gtsc:Undefined` name themselves; `rdf:nil` names the empty array; a
node with `rdf:first` is an array and its items are the chain of its `rdf:rest`; a node of class `gtsc:Map` is a map, and its
entries are the entries it holds, each with one key and one value; a node of class `gtsc:Tagged` has one tag and one content. A
literal's lexical form fixes its value: an integer, a truth value and a byte string are typed values whose texts are generated by
rules that give different texts for different values (`P3`, "Typed values"), and the 16 digits of a float fix its 64 bits. Each
recursive call gives the same, so two values never give one image, and no step reads a value back from a text. End of
proof.

**P9-15.** `convert` MUST state each CBOR value that it carries by `V` of 4.1 and by no other form, and MUST give a map's entries
in ascending bytewise order of the deterministic encodings of their keys (the bytes compared as unsigned integers from the first
byte, a proper prefix before the longer string). This order fixes the sequence in which the fresh blank nodes of the entries are
created; the result does not record it, because its blank nodes are labelled by position.

**P9-16.** `convert` MUST state every statement of the vocabulary in the named graph `D` as `quad` lines, MUST NOT state one in
the default graph or in a graph that a source row names, and MUST NOT use a term of the vocabulary for any other purpose.

**P9-17.** No reader or writer of this generation other than `convert` MAY give a meaning to an IRI listed in the table of this
section, and no rule of any part reads it.

### 5.2 Terms

A source term maps to a term of this generation, a typed value of `P3`, by the rows above and these rules. Terms are values: no
id of the result is chosen by this part.

1. IRI. `"v"` is the text of the IRI. A term of `k` `0` with no text `"v"` is `unrepresentable_term`.
2. LITERAL. `"v"` is the lexical form; a literal with no text `"v"` is `unrepresentable_term`. The presence of `"l"`, `"dt"` and
   `"dir"` decides the variant, and no text is parsed. The literal is copied as text: `M` gives it no pair of VALUES (5.11).
   - With `"l"` absent the result is a `literal`. Its datatype is the IRI term that `"dt"` names, which has to be an IRI term with
     a smaller id in the source, and is `xsd:string` when `"dt"` is absent (3.3). A `"dir"` beside an absent `"l"` is
     `unrepresentable_term`.
   - With `"l"` present the result is a `lang_literal`. Its language is the text `"l"`, unchanged. Its direction is `absent` when
     `"dir"` is absent and otherwise `"dir"`, which has to be `"ltr"` or `"rtl"`. A `"dt"` beside `"l"` has to be absent, or name
     an
     IRI term whose text, compared whole and bytewise, equals the datatype that `P3`, 1.1, generates for the direction state of
     the literal (`http://www.w3.org/1999/02/22-rdf-syntax-ns#dirLangString` when `"dir"` is present,
     `http://www.w3.org/1999/02/22-rdf-syntax-ns#langString` when it is absent); a `"dt"` that names any other IRI is
     `unrepresentable_term`, because a language literal of this generation has no datatype to keep.
3. TRIPLE TERM. The three ids of `"tt"` has to be smaller than the term's own id, the first has to name an IRI or blank term, the
   second an IRI term and the third any term; a triple term in the first place is `unrepresentable_term` (it would be a triple
   term as a subject), a literal in the first place and a non-IRI in the second likewise.
4. LANGUAGE TEXTS ARE COPIED. `S1` compares language tags by exact string (7), and this generation compares language texts by
   bytes (`P3`, 1.1): the language of the result is the text `"l"` with no byte changed and no case converted. Two source terms
   that differ in the case of a letter of their tags are two terms in the result, and `M` gives no two source terms one image
   through their languages.

**P9-39.** `convert` MUST map a source literal with `"l"` to a `lang_literal` and a source literal without `"l"` to a `literal`
by 5.2 item 2, and MUST stop with `unrepresentable_term` for a `"dt"` beside an `"l"` that does not name the generated datatype of
the literal.

**P9-40.** `convert` MUST copy the text `"l"` into the language of the result byte for byte, and MUST NOT convert its case or any
other character.

### 5.3 Blank nodes

A blank node of the first generation is identified by its segment and its label (3.3, `S1` 7.1); an anonymous blank node (`"v"`
absent or empty) is one node for each term entry. The nodes of `M(SF)` are exactly these: two term entries of one segment with
one non-empty label are one node, two entries of different segments with one label are two nodes, and each anonymous entry is
its own node. `M(SF)` is defined up to a renaming of its blank nodes, and the content digest is invariant under it (`P3`, "What
the digest is a function of", claim D2). How a node is labelled in the result is the canonical form's matter and not this
part's. The label text of a source node is not carried: `S1` 7.1 gives it no meaning beyond the identity of the node. A blank
node of the conversion vocabulary (`D`, a segment, a list cell, an entry, a map, a declaration) is a node of `M(SF)` that is not
a node of the source.

### 5.4 Rows

1. A `quads` row `(s, p, o, g?)` is the `quad` line `(s', p', o', g'?)` of the mapped terms, `g` absent being the default graph.
   `s` has to be an IRI or blank term, `p` an IRI, `g` an IRI or blank term; any other is `unrepresentable_term` (a subject that
   is a triple term, a literal subject, a literal graph name, a predicate that is not an IRI: `S1` 7.4 diagnoses these and drops
   the row; `convert` stops).
2. A `reifies` row `(r, s, p, o, g?)` is the `reifier_binding` line `(r', t, g'?)` where `t` is the `triple` term `(s', p', o')`
   (5.5); `r` an IRI or blank term, `s`, `p`, `o` under the rule of 5.2 item 3. Every row is kept, each with its own graph:
   `rdf:reifies` is not functional (`S1` 7.3).
3. An `annot` row `(r, p, v, g?)` is the `annotation` line `(count, r', p', v', g'?)` with `r` an IRI or blank term and `p` an
   IRI.
4. The default graph and each named graph of the source are exactly the default graph and the named graphs of `M(SF)`; `M(SF)`
   holds no `empty_graph` line: `S1` has no empty named graph.
5. The source rows of a `snapshot` are the rows of the frames it stands for, with the ids shifted (3.2).
6. A row whose id names no term defined by an earlier frame of its segment is `source_damaged` (`S1` 7.2: a forward reference);
   a row of a length other than the frame type allows, or an id that is not an integer, is `unrepresentable_frame`.

### 5.5 Counts and the triple terms of `reifies` rows

ANNOTATION COUNTS. `S1` keeps equal annotation rows as an ordered multiset (7.4, 7.8). The fold of this generation adds the
counts of equal annotation lines (`P3`, "The fold"). So the count of an annotation line of `M(SF)` is the number of source rows
equal to it after the ids are resolved to values, over every frame and every segment of the file. A suppression entry of the kind
`annotation` hides the line whatever its count (`P3`), as the first generation's value-wise target hides every row.

TRIPLE TERMS OF `reifies` ROWS. The source row names the triple by three ids, and the `reifier_binding` line of this generation
names it by one `triple` term. The line's triple is the `triple` term of the values of the row's `s`, `p` and `o`; equal values
are one term (`P3`, "The term model").

### 5.6 The statements about a segment

For each source segment, `M(SF)` holds a node `S` with `D gtsc:segment S`, `S rdf:type gtsc:Segment`, `S gtsc:ordinal` the
ordinal, `S gtsc:profile` the text of `"prof"`, and when they exist `S gtsc:layout`, `S gtsc:headerMeta`, `S
gtsc:headerExtension`, `S gtsc:catalogExtension` and `S gtsc:segmentMeta`, each as the tables of section 4 and 5.1 say. The node
`D` and the statement `D rdf:type gtsc:Dataset` are in every `M(SF)`. The file-level `meta` view of `S1` (7.5), the shallow
merge of the segments' maps in file order, is a function of the segment maps and states nothing further: it is not carried, and
the proof covers the per-segment maps from which it is computed.

The segment's own `meta` is the shallow union, in frame order, of the maps of its `meta` frames and of the `"meta"` maps of its
`snapshot` frames (3.2): a later key replaces an earlier one. A `meta` payload that is not a map, or a map one of whose own
top-level keys is not text, is `unrepresentable_frame` (`S1` 7.8: a key is compared as a string). The map under the keys of any
depth is carried by `V`.

**P9-18.** `M(SF)` MUST hold, for each source segment, the node and the statements of 5.6 with the values the source holds, and
`D rdf:type gtsc:Dataset` once for the file.

### 5.6a The content of a frame outside its image

A source frame holds, besides its image under 5.1, content that no row, term or entry states: the public envelope `"pub"` of any
CBOR value (`S1` 6, 9.4), the recipients `"to"` (a list of recipient maps; a frame that has an `encrypt` stage and cannot be
opened is never converted, 8.2 item 7), extension keys of the frame map, extension keys of its payload map, and the keys of a
term map that the fold of `S1` ignores (the key lists of 5.10). `M(SF)` holds, for each source frame that holds any of them, a
blank node `F` and, in the graph `D`: `F rdf:type gtsc:Frame`; `F gtsc:inSegment S`; `F gtsc:frameOrdinal n` with `n` the
frame's zero-based position among the frames of its segment (a frame with no image counted); `F gtsc:frameType` the text of
`"t"`; and then, for each that the frame holds:

- `F gtsc:pub V(value)`, for a frame that is not a `blob` frame (the `"pub"` of a `blob` frame is part of the merged `"pub"` of
  its digest, 5.7; one that is not a map is `unrepresentable_frame`);
- `F gtsc:to V(value)` of the `"to"` value;
- `F gtsc:extension V(map)` of the map of the extension keys of the frame map, in ascending bytewise order of the deterministic
  encodings of their keys (P9-15);
- `F gtsc:payloadExtension V(map)` of the map of the extension keys of the payload map of a `snapshot` or `index` frame;
- for each term map of a `terms` frame or of a snapshot's `"terms"` that holds such keys, a node `N` with `F gtsc:termNote N`,
  `N rdf:type gtsc:TermNote`, `N gtsc:termIndex i`, `N gtsc:termValue` the term it was converted to, and `N gtsc:termKeys V(map)`.

`V` is total on the CBOR data model, so no value that the first-generation reader decodes is without an image. A frame that holds
none of them has no node. The node names no frame of the result and no identifier. For a `suppress` frame the extension keys of
the payload map and of each target map are stated on the nodes `Y` and `W` of 5.8, which then exist whenever such a key does.

**P9-19.** `M(SF)` MUST hold, for every source frame that holds a `"pub"` that is not a `blob` frame's, a `"to"`, an extension
key of the frame map or of its payload map, or a term map with keys that the fold ignores, the node and the statements of 5.6a,
each value stated by `V`, and MUST NOT drop one.

### 5.6b The profile declaration

A first-generation header names a profile by the text `"prof"` (`S1` 5, 13): `generic`, `dist`, `evidence`, `opaque`, `bundle`,
`files`, `stream`, `image`, `ai-package`, `music-package`, a domain profile, or any other text. The result's header has `profile`
`absent` whatever the source declares, and the text is CONTENT: `S gtsc:profile` states it for the segment (5.6). The decision and
its reasons:

1. A header `profile` of this generation is a claim that the profile document's checks hold of the segment (the profile table of
   the registry part and the profile documents `PF-dist`, `PF-evidence`, `PF-stream`, `PF-bundle` and `PF-files`, cited by their
   titles). The counterparts of the source names `dist`, `evidence`, `stream`, `bundle` and `files` each require what a result of
   conversion does not have: the profile of the first title bounds every frame by ceilings that a result of conversion need not
   meet; the
   second requires signed commits at a cadence (the source's signatures are never carried, 5.9); the third requires commits and
   parity at a cadence; the fourth and the fifth require bindings and a vocabulary that conversion does not write for source
   content (below). A claim that a check of the profile refutes would be false, and `convert` has no option to set it.
2. `container` has no counterpart in the first generation; `generic` is no profile; `opaque`, `image`, `ai-package`,
   `music-package` and every domain or unknown text have none in the profile table.
3. THE PROFILE-SPECIFIC CONTENT stays as the source states it. A source `files` or `stream` archive states its entries in rows
   under the IRIs of its own vocabulary (`https://w3id.org/gts/files#`, `https://w3id.org/gts/stream#`); these are ordinary IRIs
   and map one for one like any term. Lifting them to the vocabulary of a profile document of this generation is not conversion:
   it would split path strings on a separator, decode the bits of a decimal mode, read digest literals to find blobs and give
   meaning to a spelling, each of which this generation forbids (P9-22 and the principle that no reader parses a spelling to
   learn a meaning), and it would need the bytes of every blob for the binding.
4. A writer who wants a profile of this generation for converted content authors it from the converted file, in a further
   step that is outside `convert`, and the proof of equal content does not apply to it.

**P9-20.** `convert` MUST write the result header with `profile` `absent`, and MUST state the source's `"prof"` text by
`gtsc:profile` for each segment.

### 5.7 Blobs

A first-generation blob is named by the plain BLAKE3-256 digest of its bytes (`S1` 12). A blob of this generation is named by
`H_ctx(unit, bytes)` in a `qualified_name` (`P4`). The two names are different values of different hashes, so a name of this
generation needs the bytes of the blob, which the converter holds.

DIGESTS IN THE SOURCE. The source writes a blob digest, and the stored identifier of a frame, as a byte string of 32 bytes or as a
text. A DIGEST POSITION is `"pub".digest`, the key of a snapshot's `"blobs"` entry, and the `"digest"` or `"id"` of a `suppress`
target. The converter never reads a text to learn a digest. It HOLDS a digest when it has the 32 bytes: the plain digest of the
bytes of a held blob, a byte string of 32 bytes written in a digest position, and the stored `"id"` of a frame. The DIGEST TEXT of
a held digest is generated, never read: the seven characters `blake3:` followed by 64 characters that write the 32 bytes, two
lower-case hexadecimal digits for each byte, in order. A source text NAMES a held digest when it equals the digest text of that
digest whole and bytewise. The digests held are those of the whole source, taken before any text is judged and whatever the
order of the frames; a `blob` frame whose digest is a text that names a held digest is a frame of that digest, and its `"pub"` is
merged into the merged `"pub"` of that digest in file order. A text that equals the digest text of no held digest names nothing,
and is identified by itself, compared whole.

The source fold holds, for each distinct plain digest `d`, the bytes when any frame holds them (an inline `blob` frame, or an
entry of the `"blobs"` map of a `snapshot`) and a MERGED `"pub"`: the shallow union, in file order, of the `"pub"` maps of every
`blob` frame of that digest, a later key replacing an earlier one (`S1` 7.5, 7.8). `M` states each distinct digest once, by the
merged `"pub"`.

1. A HELD BLOB, one whose bytes `b` some frame holds. Its name is `N = [blake3_256, H_ctx(unit, b)]`. `M(SF)` holds the blob
   `N`: its blob-set entry (`P4`, "The blob set") has `presence` `present`, `name` `N`, `length` the number of bytes of `b` (a
   plain `uint`) and `media_types` the list of the one merged `"mt"` when it is present and the empty list otherwise. Several
   frames that hold the same bytes are one blob (`S1` 12).
2. THE DIGEST CHECK. A `"pub"` entry `"digest"` of a frame that holds bytes has to be the plain digest of those bytes as a byte
   string, or the digest text of that plain digest as a text, compared whole; any other value is `source_damaged`. The key of a
   snapshot's `"blobs"` entry is checked the same way.
3. THE DECLARATION NODE `X` of a held blob is a blank node of `M(SF)` that exists when the merged `"pub"` holds `"rep"` or any
   key other than `"mt"` and `"digest"`. `X rdf:type gtsc:BlobDeclaration`; `X gtsc:digest` the 32 bytes as an `xsd:hexBinary`
   literal; `X gtsc:representation` the text of `"rep"` when present (a `"rep"` or `"mt"` that is not text is
   `unrepresentable_frame`); `X gtsc:pub V(map)` of the map of the remaining keys when there are any. `M(SF)` holds the binding
   of the leaf `X` to the referent `blob N` (`P3`). The node is a term "bound to the blob"; no vocabulary of the binding is
   defined (`P3`). A held blob whose merged `"pub"` holds only `"mt"` and `"digest"` has no node and no statement: its media type
   is in its blob-set entry, and its plain digest is a function of its bytes, which the result holds.
4. AN EXTERNAL BLOB, a plain digest `d` of which no frame holds bytes. The frames name bytes held elsewhere by `"pub".digest`. A
   name of this generation needs the bytes, which the converter does not hold, so it cannot be computed. THE CHOICE, by the
   principles of no silent loss and no invented name: `M(SF)` holds no declaration and no binding for it and invents no name; the
   declaration is stated as RDF. `M(SF)` holds a blank node `X` with `X rdf:type gtsc:ExternalBlob`, `X gtsc:digest` the 32 bytes
   of `d` when the source wrote them as a byte string or wrote a text that names them, and otherwise `X gtsc:digestText` the text
   whole, `X gtsc:mediaType` the text of the merged `"mt"`
   when present, `X gtsc:representation` the text of the merged `"rep"` when present and `X gtsc:pub V(map)` of the remaining
   keys when there are any. The blob set of `M(SF)` holds no entry for it. A `blob` frame with neither bytes nor a digest, or a
   digest that is neither a byte string of 32 bytes nor a text, is `unrepresentable_frame`. The consequence: the external blob
   stays findable by its plain digest,
   which is the only name its source gave it, and a holder of the bytes can convert it later by declaring the blob and a binding
   for `X`; nothing of this generation reads `X`.
5. DIGEST LITERALS. A literal or an IRI of the source that spells a blob digest (`blake3:` and 64 hexadecimal digits, or any other
   spelling) is ordinary RDF content: it maps as every other term (5.2) and is never read. `M` adds no binding for it, for two
   reasons: recognising a spelling is parsing it to learn a meaning (the format reads no spelling), and the name it spells is a
   plain digest that is not a name of this generation. A consumer that wants the link to the bytes has the node `X` of the blob,
   which carries the digest and, for a held blob, is bound to it.

The consequence for suppression of a blob target is in 5.8.

**P9-21.** `M(SF)` MUST name a held blob `[blake3_256, H_ctx(unit, b)]` from the bytes it holds, MUST state each distinct digest
by its merged `"pub"` as items 3 and 4 say, and MUST hold no declaration and no binding for an external blob.

**P9-22.** `convert` MUST NOT write a binding for a literal or an IRI of the source, and MUST NOT read the spelling of a digest
anywhere. In a digest position (5.7) it MUST generate the digest text of each digest it holds and compare a source text with it
whole, and a source text that equals no digest text of a held digest MUST be kept whole, as 5.7 and 5.8 say.

### 5.8 Suppression

A source `suppress` frame is a list of targets in order, with a `"reason"` and a `"by"` that can each be absent. `S1` 11 defines
what each
target names and that suppression is additive and a display contract. `M(SF)` holds, for each target, the entries of this
generation that hide the same content (`P3`, "The hiding rule"), by this table. An entry names a term by its value: `M(SF)` is one
scope, so no entry names a node by a pair of another segment.

| target (`S1` 11) | entries |
|---|---|
| `{"kind": "term", "id": i}` | one `term` entry on the term of `i` |
| `{"kind": "quad", "q": [s, p, o]}` or `[s, p, o, g]` | one `quad` entry on the four terms; the graph `absent` when `q` has three ids |
| `{"kind": "reifier", "id": i}` | one `reifier` entry on the term of `i` |
| `{"kind": "frame", "id": d}` | the entries of the frame that has the stored identifier `d` (below) |
| `{"kind": "blob", "digest": d}` | the entries of the blob that has the plain digest `d` (below) |
| any other `"kind"`, or a target that is not a map, or one that lacks a member that the row of its kind lists or holds it with a type that the row does not list | `stop`: `unrepresentable_frame` |

The members and the types that a row lists are exactly these: for `term` and `reifier`, `"id"` an integer; for `quad`, `"q"` an
array of three or four integers; for `frame`, `"id"` a byte string of 32 bytes or a text; for `blob`, `"digest"` a byte string of
32 bytes or a text. A key of the target map other than the members of its row and the extension keys is an extension key.

**P9-38.** `convert` MUST judge a `suppress` target by the kind, members and types of the table and list of this section and by
nothing else, and MUST stop with `unrepresentable_frame` for a target that breaks them.

A `term`, `quad` or `reifier` target names term ids of the suppress frame's own segment, as `S1` 11 says; each id has to be
defined by an earlier frame of the segment (else `source_damaged`). A target is resolved to values and applies to the whole
file; the entry does the same, because `M(SF)` is one scope.

FRAME TARGETS. A `frame` target's `d` is a byte string of 32 bytes or a text (DIGESTS IN THE SOURCE, 5.7). It names every frame of
the source, in any segment, whose stored `"id"` equals `d`, or whose digest text equals `d` when `d` is a text, both compared
whole
(two byte-identical segments hold frames with equal stored identifiers). The entries of the target are the union, as a set, of the
entries of those frames, the entries of each frame
being those below, by its type, and a row that two of those frames hold being one entry (`P3`):

- `quads`: one `quad` entry for each distinct row, the rows distinct as values;
- `reifies`: one `reifier_binding` entry `[r, t, g?]` for each distinct row, with `t` the triple term of the row's `s`, `p`, `o`;
- `annot`: one `annotation` entry for each distinct row;
- `snapshot`: the entries of each section as above, and for each inline blob of its `"blobs"` map one `blob` entry and, when its
  digest has a node `X` (5.7), one `term` entry on `X`;
- `blob`, holding bytes: one `blob` entry with the name of the blob, and, when its digest has a node `X` (5.7), one `term` entry
  on `X`;
- `blob`, holding no bytes: one `term` entry on the node `X` of its digest;
- any other type (`terms`, `meta`, `suppress`, `index`, `opaque`): no entry; a `d` that names no frame of the file, or the
  header of a segment: no entry.

BLOB TARGETS. A `blob` target's `d` is a byte string of 32 bytes or a text. It names the blob of the source whose plain digest
equals `d`, or whose digest text equals `d` when `d` is a text, both compared whole; a text that equals the digest text of no held
digest is identified by itself and names the external blob whose digest was written as that same text, if there is one. The
entries are: one `blob` entry with the name `[blake3_256, H_ctx(unit, b)]` when the source holds the bytes `b` of that
blob; and one `term` entry on the node `X` of `d` when it has one (5.7). A `d` that names no blob of the source:
no entry.

Entries equal after resolution are one entry of the record (`P3`).

REASON AND AUTHOR. `S1` 11 gives a `suppress` frame a `"reason"` (text) and a `"by"` (a term id), each of which can be absent.
This
generation's entry has neither (`P3`, "Suppression hides"). A reference to a frame by its identifier would not survive
compaction, so `M` states the directive as RDF that names no frame. When a source `suppress` frame holds a `"reason"`, a `"by"`,
or an extension key of its payload map or of any target map, `M(SF)` holds a blank node `Y` with the statements `Y rdf:type
gtsc:SuppressDirective`, `Y gtsc:inSegment S` (the node of its segment), `Y gtsc:frameOrdinal n` (its zero-based position among
the frames of its source segment, the header not counted), `Y gtsc:reason` the text and `Y gtsc:by` the result term of the id,
as the frame holds them, and `Y gtsc:extension V(map)` of the extension keys of the payload map when there are any. For each
target `i` of the frame, in order, `M(SF)` holds a blank node `W` with `Y gtsc:target W`, `W rdf:type gtsc:SuppressTarget`, `W
gtsc:targetOrdinal i`, `W gtsc:targetKind` the text of `"kind"`, and: for a `term` or `reifier` target `W gtsc:targetTerm` the
result term of the id; for a `quad` target `W gtsc:targetTerm` the `triple` term of its subject, predicate and object, and `W
gtsc:targetGraph` its graph term when it has one; for a `frame` or `blob` target `W gtsc:targetDigest` the 32 bytes when `d` is a
byte string or a text that names a
held digest, and otherwise `W gtsc:digestText` the text whole;
and `W gtsc:extension V(map)` of the extension keys of the target map when there are any. A `"reason"` that is not text, or a
`"by"` that is not an id of a defined term, is `unrepresentable_frame`. The statements of the directive are RDF, so the entries
that hide the term they mention hide them too, as the hiding rule says; the conversion vocabulary has no exemption.

CONVENTIONS OF THE HIDING RULE. `S1` 11 states the effect of a target in one sentence each: a `term` target hides "a term +
quads it appears in", a `quad` target hides one quad, a `reifier` target hides "a reifier + its annotations", a `blob` target
hides the blob from default extraction, and a `frame` target names "a frame, by its id". This generation's `term` entry hides
every line of every kind in which the term occurs, at any depth of a triple term; its `reifier` entry hides the reifier-binding
and annotation lines of the reifier. `M` takes these as the meaning of the first generation's targets and states them as four
conventions: C1, a `term` target hides the lines of every kind that hold
the term (a reified or an annotated statement that mentions it included); the datatype IRI and the language of a literal are
not occurrences of a term, as in `P3`, "The hiding rule"; C2, a `reifier` target hides the reifier-binding and
the annotation lines of the reifier; C3, a `frame` target hides what the frame carried, as the entries above; C4, a `blob`
target hides the declarations of the blob (the bytes from extraction and the statements about them). 7.3 argues that the visible
dataset is the same under them and names what rests on them.

**P9-23.** `M(SF)` MUST hold for each `term`, `quad` and `reifier` target the entry of the table, and for a `frame` or `blob`
target the entries listed for it.

**P9-24.** `M(SF)` MUST hold the node `Y` of a source `suppress` frame that holds a `"reason"`, a `"by"` or an extension key, with
the statements of the paragraph above for it and for each of its targets, and MUST hold no binding that names a frame of the
result.

**P9-25.** `M(SF)` MUST NOT hold a `graph` or a `binding` suppression entry, which have no first-generation source, and MUST NOT
hold an entry for a target that names nothing.

### 5.9 Signatures

The signatures of the source (`S1` 9.2) are COSE structures over first-generation frame identifiers, which do not exist in the
result. They are COUNTED (the field `source_signatures` of the proof, 7.1) and never carried: no signature is written, no key,
and no statement about one. A holder who needs them keeps the source, which the proof names by its digest. Carrying a frame
signature as detached provenance is the first generation's rule for its own rewrites (`S1` 10.1); this generation has no signed
subject for a first-generation frame.

### 5.11 VALUES of the result

VALUES of `M(SF)` (`P3`, "The fold") holds one pair for each literal of the result that `M` states from a typed field, and no
other: the literal that `V` (4.1) gives for a CBOR integer, with the typed value of kind `int` of that integer; for `false` or
`true`, with the value of kind `bool`; for a byte string, with the value of kind `octets` of its bytes; and each literal that the
converter states from a number or a digest of its own, `gtsc:ordinal`, `gtsc:frameOrdinal`, `gtsc:targetOrdinal`,
`gtsc:termIndex` and `gtsc:tag` with values of kind `int`, and `gtsc:digest` and `gtsc:targetDigest` with values of kind
`octets`. A literal of a source `terms` frame is copied as text whatever its datatype (5.2), and a floating-point or simple value
is stated as text; neither has a pair. The text that each pair generates is the literal that 4.1 or section 4 states, since the
rule of `int`, `bool` and `octets` (`P3`, "Typed values") is the rule that 4.1 writes. A source literal of datatype
`xsd:hexBinary`
is text in the result, so the canonical file of the result holds it as text, by reference when it reaches the literal threshold
(`P7`, "The constants").

**P9-41.** `convert` MUST give VALUES of `M(SF)` exactly the pairs of 5.11, and MUST NOT give a pair to a literal that it copies
from a source text.

**P9-42.** `convert` MUST stop with `source_exceeds_capacity` and the `offset` `0` at step 4 when the read of the result gives
a declaration the rebuild state `refused`, before it tests any condition of (b) of that step.

## 6. The conversion procedure

Every step has a typed outcome: it continues, or it stops with `conversion_stopped` and the reason and offset of section 8. The
two content digests of the proof are written `Ds` (of `M(SF)`) and `Dr` (of the result).

1. READ THE SOURCE, twice. Read the source through the first-generation reader under the conversion reading (3.1, 3.3), from its
   first byte to its end, and again.
   Compute `source_bytes = H_ctx(source_bytes, all bytes of the source)` in each read, count the segments and count the frames
   that hold
   `"sig"`. Then judge, in this order, and stop at the first that gives a stop: (a) the two reads: if they differ (8.3 item 1),
   stop with `source_not_deterministic`; (b) the items in source order, and at each item first the capacity test of 8.3 item
   2(a) (an item above the converter's `stored_bytes_per_frame` or `decoded_bytes_per_frame`, the item after the
   `items_per_read`-th, and an item that brings the sum of decoded payloads above `decoded_bytes_per_read` stop with
   `source_exceeds_capacity`) and then the rules of 8.2 in their numbered order. The first item that gives a stop gives the reason
   and the offset; a stop by a rule at an earlier item is not replaced by a capacity stop at a later item.
2. BUILD `M(SF)`. Fold the source into `SF` (3.2) and map it by section 5 to the lines, bindings, suppression entries and blob
   declarations of `M(SF)`, with every STRUCTURE key left out and every CONTENT key mapped (5.10). Build its labelled set (`P3`,
   "The labelled set") and compute its content digest `Ds` with `L1`, from `M(SF)` AS TYPED VALUES: no frame of this
   generation is written or read to obtain `Ds`, so that `Ds` and `Dr` come by two paths and their equality tests the writer
   and the reader of the result. A refusal of the labelling stops with
   `source_exceeds_capacity`.
3. COMPACT. Compact the fold `M(SF)` (`P7`, "Compaction") with `profile` `absent`. The result is the canonical file. A stop of
   compaction is a conversion stop (2.1).
4. READ THE RESULT. Read the result with this generation's reader (`P7`, "The typed result of reading") with the converter's own
   capacity, and test in this order. (a) If any frame has the payload state `unchecked` with `exceeds_capacity`, or a `terms`
   frame is refused by the total formed length, or reading stopped at `items_per_read`, or a rebuild state of the result is
   `refused`, or the reader refuses the labelling (a refusal by weight included), stop with `source_exceeds_capacity`. (b) The
   `file` of the `read_result` has to be of the variant
   `second_generation` with one segment; the header's `canonical` has to be claimed and the canonical check's `structure` and
   `labelling` levels has to be `verified` (the `storage` level is only reported and every state is accepted, because it produces
   no finding: the converter's own reader gives `verified` when it holds the reference encoder and `unchecked` with
   `encoder_not_held` when it does not; `differs` is what another reader that holds the encoder reports of a result written
   without it, as 2 item 5 says); the file's `content_digest` has to be `present` and equal to the segment's `content_digest` (the
   segment's own fold is the segment read alone, which is the whole file, so the two folds are equal); `visible` has to be
   `complete`, `integrity` `intact`, and the findings hold none of the severity `error` or `fatal` (`P2`, "The finding table").
   `Dr`
   is that digest. If a condition of (b) fails, stop with `content_mismatch`.
5. COMPARE. If `Ds` and `Dr` differ, stop with `content_mismatch`. Otherwise, when no earlier step gave a stop, make the
   visibility check of 7.3 item 5 over every line, binding and blob declaration of `SF`, and stop as it says with
   `unrepresentable_term`. Otherwise return the result and the proof of section 7.

THE MEMORY OF THE PROCEDURE. Reading the source holds one item at a time. Building `M(SF)`, compacting and reading the result each
need the fold's lines (and its bindings, entries and blob declarations) within the converter's capacity (`P7`, "Capacity"): the
labelling of step 2 and the labelling of the reader in step 4 each label the whole fold, so a source whose fold exceeds the
capacity stops with `source_exceeds_capacity`. The read of step 4 also rebuilds every `recipe` and `value` of the result within
the converter's `rebuilt_bytes_per_read` (`P7`, "Capacity"); a source of the first generation holds no recipe, so steps 1 and 2
rebuild nothing. No other bound is stated, because no part states one. Every read of this part is a read with the access
`whole` (`P7`, "Access: the steps by access").

**P9-27.** `convert` MUST perform steps 1 to 5 in that order, MUST stop at the first stop that a step gives, and MUST return no
result when it stops.

**P9-28.** `convert` MUST hold the fold within its capacity and MUST stop with `source_exceeds_capacity` rather than return a
result whose proof it could not compute.

**P9-29.** `convert` MUST take every value of the result's frames from the canonical form and from `M(SF)` and from nothing else:
it MUST NOT take a frame budget, a codec choice, an order or an identifier from the source.

## 7. The conversion proof

### 7.1 The record

```text
conversion_proof = [ source_bytes:      digest,   ; H_ctx(source_bytes, the bytes of the whole source file)
                     content:           digest,   ; the content digest (P3) of M(SF), which is that of the canonical file
                     source_segments:   uint,     ; the segments of the source
                     source_signatures: uint ]    ; the frames of the source that hold "sig"; none is carried
```

The proof covers the content of the source and not its structure. The proof holds no head of the result: the result is the
canonical file of the mapped fold, so its head is a function of the content that the proof already names by its content digest,
and a holder recomputes it by compaction. The proof has no verdict field: it exists only when its two digests are equal. It
travels beside the result and never inside it. The result has one segment, so the proof states no count of result segments, and
the segment's own fold (the segment read alone) and the file's fold are the same fold: the segment's `content_digest` and the
file's `content_digest` are equal. The digest `source_bytes` is of the context that `P2` lists for it, so it differs from every
other digest of the format over the same bytes.

**P9-30.** `convert` MUST return the proof record of 7.1 with `content` the digest `Ds`, equal to `Dr`, which is the content
digest of the canonical file, and MUST NOT return a proof whose two digests differ or whose `content` is absent.

**P9-31.** The proof's `source_signatures` MUST be the number of frames of the source whose map holds the key `"sig"`, whatever
the state of the signature.

### 7.2 What the holder of a proof and both files can check

Given the source `A`, the result `B` and the proof `p`, and with no help from the converter:

1. Hash `A` with the context `source_bytes`; it equals `p.source_bytes`.
2. Read `A` with the first-generation reader; its segment count and its count of frames that hold `"sig"` equal
   `p.source_segments` and `p.source_signatures`.
3. Read `B` with a reader of this generation: the `file` of the `read_result` has to be of the variant `second_generation` with
   one segment, the header's `canonical` claimed with the `structure` and `labelling` levels `verified` and the `storage` level
   in any state (it produces no finding), `integrity` `intact`, `visible` `complete`, and both the file's `content_digest` and
   the segment's `content_digest` `present` and equal to `p.content` (the two folds are equal, because the file is one segment).
4. From the source fold compute `M(SF)` by section 5 and its content digest with `L1`; it equals `p.content`.

Step 3 uses nothing of the first generation, and steps 1, 2 and 4 use nothing of this generation's reader. Two different programs
produce `p.content`, and each of them is a reader that the holder can choose.

### 7.3 What the proof proves, and what it does not

CLAIM Q1 (the result is `M` of the source, up to renaming). When the proof exists, the fold of the result is isomorphic to
`M(SF)`: the same RDF lines with the same annotation counts, the same bindings, the same suppression record, the same blob set.
Proof. The content digest of a fold is the digest of the canonical line sequence of its labelled set (`P3`, "Definition", under
"The content digest"), and equal digests mean the labelled sets are isomorphic (`P3`, "What the digest is a function of", claim
D3, under the assumption of `L1`). The labelled set holds every line, binding, entry and blob declaration of the fold, hidden or
not, each with its terms lowered, and isomorphic labelled sets are those of isomorphic lines and records before lowering (`P3`,
"Nested terms, lowering and term nodes", claim L), so isomorphic labelled sets are isomorphic folds. `Ds` is computed from `M(SF)`
and `Dr` from the fold of the canonical
file. End of proof. No value of the first computation is taken from the writer: `M` names no frame of the result.

CLAIM Q2 (`M` is the right mapping: equal visible datasets up to the stated mapping, outside the conversion graph). The visible
dataset of the result, less the graph `D` and less the bindings and entries of the vocabulary, equals the image under `M` of
the visible dataset of the source, `M(vis(SF))`, where `vis(SF)` is `SF` less what the suppression directives of `S1` 11 hide
under the four conventions of 5.8. Inside the graph `D` the claim does not hold: an entry that hides a term hides the
vocabulary statements that mention it (5.8). This claim is a property of `M` and is argued
here; the proof does not prove it. The argument, by construct:

1. TERMS AND ROWS. A source term maps to one term of this generation (5.2) by an injective function (5.2); a source
   row maps to one line of the kind of its frame (5.4), annotation rows with their
   multiplicity as a count (5.5). Identity of values is by typed value in both (`S1` 7, `P3`, "The term model"), so the set of
   lines is the image of the set of rows.
2. BLANK NODES. The nodes of `M(SF)` are the source's nodes, scopes kept apart (5.3), so the nodes of the result correspond one
   to one to the nodes of the source, and lines that share a node in the source share the image node.
3. RDF-STAR AND REIFICATION. A `reifies` row is a `reifier_binding` line whose triple term has the row's components: one for
   each row, with the row's graph, as `S1` 7.3 projects every row to a statement; quotation does not imply assertion in either
   generation, so no `quad` is added for the quoted triple. A `k` `3` term with `"tt"` is a `triple` term with the mapped
   components and is used by value wherever a row names it. The annotation counts are the multiset of `S1` 7.4.
4. BLOBS. The set of blobs of the result is the set of inline blobs of the source by name (5.7); the statements about a blob are
   statements about a node bound to it; an external blob has statements only. A suppression of a blob hides the declaration, which
   in this generation is its entry of the blob set and the statements about it (C4).
5. SUPPRESSION. For `term`, `quad` and `reifier` targets the entry of 5.8 matches by value exactly the lines that the
   first-generation target names under C1 and C2, because the entry names the same value in the same (one) scope. A `frame`
   target is a set of entries of the kinds of 5.8 and a `blob` target a blob entry and node entries (C3, C4). Order does not
   matter to the effect in either generation: a target applies value-wise to the whole file (`S1` 11), and an entry has the same
   effect before or after what it names (`P3`, "Suppression hides"); the order in which the result lists its lines, bindings and
   entries is that of `P7`, "The typed result of reading" (THE ORDER OF THE CONTENT), in both files alike. `M` gives no two
   source terms one image through their languages (5.2 item 4), so an entry hides the image of exactly the terms that its source
   target names. The converter
   CHECKS this all the same: for every line, binding and blob declaration `x` of `SF`, `x` is hidden in `SF` by
   the conventions exactly when its image is hidden in `M(SF)` by the hiding rule of `P3`, and when two source lines have one
   image, both are hidden or neither is. A violation stops the conversion with `unrepresentable_term` at the start of the item
   that holds the first source term entry whose image is also the image of a different source term, the entries taken in item
   order and, within an item, by their index in its term list.
6. METADATA. The statements of section 4 are in a graph that no source row names (P9-16), so the source's graphs are unchanged,
   and the visible dataset of the source is the visible dataset of the result less the graph `D`, and its bindings and entries
   less those of the vocabulary.
7. VALUES. The pairs of VALUES of the result are those of 5.11, a function of `SF`; a pair hides nothing and is hidden by
   nothing (`P3`, "Suppression hides"), so it changes no visibility.

The claim rests on four conventions (C1 to C4 of 5.8, the readings of one-sentence rules of `S1` 11) and on the scope reading of
3.3.

WHAT THE PROOF DOES NOT PROVE. (a) That `M` is the right mapping: that is Q2, argued above. (b) That the first-generation reader
read the source correctly: the proof assumes its fold; a holder who distrusts it reads the source with another first-generation
reader. (c) Anything that `SF` does not hold (3.2): the STRUCTURE keys of 5.10 (the identifiers, links, transform chains,
catalogues and signatures of the source's own bytes), and the label text of blank nodes. Every CONTENT
key of 5.10 is in `M(SF)`, so the proof covers it. The source is kept, and `source_bytes` binds it. (d) That a signature of the
source verified: the signatures are counted. (e) The authenticity of the result: it is unsigned. (f) The order of the source's
frames: the order of frames enters the fold of this generation only through `P3`, "What the order of frames and the order of
segments
decide", and the result of `convert` is a canonical file, in which every value and unit stands before what names it (the order of
the lines the fold delivers is that of `P7`, THE ORDER OF THE CONTENT), and the first generation's `meta` and blob-metadata
overwrite rules are an
order of the source (a later key replaces an earlier one), which the statements state after the overwrite. (g) The bytes of an
external blob, which are not in the file.

**P9-32.** A consumer that relies on a converted file's visible dataset MUST rely on the claim Q2 and on the conventions of 5.8
and not on the proof, and a tool that reports a proof MUST say that it proves isomorphism of folds and not the correctness of
`M`.

**P9-33.** `convert` MUST perform the visibility check of 7.3 item 5 over every line, binding and blob declaration of `SF`.

### 7.4 What an outside holder can do with a proof alone

The proof is two digests and two counts, with no word. A holder who has the proof, the source and the result checks it as 7.2
says. A holder who has the proof and the result only learns the digest of the source it came from and the content it holds; the
proof claims nothing about a source the holder cannot read.

## 8. What cannot be converted, and the loud failure

`convert` stops with the finding `conversion_stopped` (`P2`, "The finding table", severity fatal). The finding carries the
`reason`, a `conversion_reason`, and the `offset`, the byte offset in the source of the item named below; no other field is
present. It never carries a segment unconverted, never drops one, and never returns a result that is partial or has a recorded
stop.

### 8.1 The reasons and their offsets

| `conversion_reason` | condition (8.2 lists them exactly) | `offset` |
|---|---|---|
| `source_damaged` | an item of the source fails a check of `S1` itself, or a rule of 5 on a value that `S1` requires | the start of the item |
| `source_not_decodable` | a frame of the source cannot be decoded by the first-generation reader, which implements every baseline codec of `S1` | the start of the item |
| `unrepresentable_term` | a term, or a use of a term, has no image in this generation, or a source text that would become a term, a language tag or a vocabulary value is not valid `UTF-8`, or `M` would change what a suppression hides | the start of the item that holds it |
| `unrepresentable_frame` | a frame, or a field of a frame, has no image | the start of the item |
| `source_not_deterministic` | the two reads of the source differ (8.3 item 1) | `0` |
| `source_exceeds_capacity` | a source item, the number of items, the total of decoded payloads, a canonical frame's payload, the total rebuilt length, the total formed length or the total labelled weight of the read of the result exceeds the converter's capacity, or the labelling of a fold is refused (by a capacity of the converter, by `refusal_width`, or by `refusal_term_node`, which is the refusal of a fold in which two different nested terms have one digest and which no capacity changes) (8.3 item 2) | the start of the item for an item; `0` for a labelling and for the read of the result |
| `content_mismatch` | the two content digests differ, or the read result fails step 4, or compaction stops for a refused `terms` frame, a held row, binding or entry or an unrebuilt blob (8.3 item 3) | `0` |

### 8.2 The classification of an item, exactly

The converter applies these rules to each item, in the order of the list, and the first that holds gives the stop. The reasons of
the whole list apply in source order: a stop at an earlier item wins. Every rule is judged over the whole item, and the rule of
the lowest number that holds anywhere in the item gives the stop. The capacity test of 8.3 item 2(a) comes before every rule
of this list for the same item: an item above the capacity is not decoded. The test of the two reads (8.3 item 1) comes before
the first item (section 6 step 1).

`source_damaged`:

1. the first item of the source, or any later header, is not a header of `S1` whose `"v"` is `1` (an input of zero bytes, a file
   of this generation, a header whose `"v"` is another value, a first item that is not a map; a later header with another `"v"`
   is a major version that `S1` 17 requires a reader to refuse); the offset is the start of that item;
2. bytes that no well-formed CBOR item contains, or a complete item that is neither a header nor a frame (`S1` 3.1, 17);
3. a header or frame whose `"id"` is not the BLAKE3-256 of its content (`S1` 9.1; for an item that holds a floating-point
   number, an `"id"` that does not hold under either form of 3.3 case 5(a)), a frame whose `"prev"` is not the previous
   item's `"id"` (`BrokenChain`), an `index` frame whose `"head"` or `"mmr"` does not match the frames it covers
   (`IndexMmrError`: also a `"count"` that is not an integer or exceeds the frames before the index, and a `"head"` or an
   `"mmr"` without a `"count"`), a segment that claims `"layout": "streamable"` and breaks the claim (`StreamableLayoutError`);
4. a torn item at the end of the source (`TornAppendError`): the offset is the start of the incomplete item;
5. a row or target that names an id that no earlier frame of its segment defines (`ForwardReference`), a `"dt"` or a `"tt"`
   component that is not smaller than the term's own id;
6. an inline blob whose `"pub".digest`, or the key of a snapshot's `"blobs"` entry, is neither the plain digest of its bytes as a
   byte string nor the digest text of that digest as a text (5.7 item 2).

`source_not_decodable`:
7. a frame whose transform chain names a codec outside the baseline of `S1` 8.4 that the first generation does not define
   (`UnknownCodec`), or an `encrypt` stage whose key the converter does not hold (`MissingKey`, `KeyWrapFailed`: it holds none);
   every baseline codec, in-band dictionaries included, is implemented by the first-generation reader and is not a stop;
8. a frame whose payload does not decode to the structure of its type after the transforms are reversed (a payload the handler
   "cannot safely fold", `S1` 7.8): a `terms`, `quads`, `reifies` or `annot` payload that is not an array, a `snapshot` or
   `index` payload that is not a map.

`unrepresentable_term`:
9. a term of `k` outside `0` to `3`, a `k` `3` term without `"tt"`, a term that is not a map, an IRI or a literal without a text
   `"v"`, a blank node whose `"v"` is present and not a text string; in a literal, a `"dir"` other than `"ltr"` or `"rtl"` or
   without
   `"l"`, an `"l"` that is not text, a `"dt"` that names a term that is not an IRI or that is not an integer; in a `k` `3`
   term, a `"tt"` that is not three ids or an element of it that is not an integer; a `"k"` that is not an integer (a key
   that the fold of `S1` ignores for the kind of its term is judged by none of these and is carried, 5.6a); in a literal, a
   `"dt"` beside an `"l"` that does not name an IRI term whose text, compared
   whole, is the datatype that 5.2 item 2 gives for the literal; any text string of
   the source that would become a term, a language tag or a value of the conversion vocabulary, never a text of an item that
   rule 12 stops, which has no image (a term's `"v"` and `"l"`, a map
   key or value that `V` carries, a `"prof"`, `"layout"`, `"rep"`, `"mt"` or `"reason"`) and is not valid `UTF-8` (RFC 3629):
   `S1` asks only for well-formed CBOR, in which a text string need not be valid `UTF-8`, and the terms of this generation have to
   be; the offset is the start of the item that holds the text. The label of a blank node is the one exception: a label is
   opaque bytes, is not carried as text, and a label that is a text string but not valid `UTF-8` does not stop the conversion;
10. a term used where this generation has none: a triple term or a literal as a subject, reifier or graph name, a non-IRI
    predicate, a triple term as the subject of a triple term (`S1` 7.3 and 7.4 diagnose these as `PositionConstraint` and drop
    the row; `convert` stops); the same position rules bind the components of a `quad` suppression target (its `"q"`: the
    subject an IRI or blank node, the predicate an IRI, the graph, when present, an IRI or blank node), whether or not its frame
    holds a `"reason"`, a `"by"` or an extension key, so that no directive node names an unreadable triple term;
11. no rule of an item: the visibility check of 7.3 item 5 is judged over the whole fold at step 5 of section 6, only when
    the earlier steps gave no stop, and stops with `unrepresentable_term` at the offset that 7.3 item 5 states.

`unrepresentable_frame`:
12. a frame whose `"t"` `S1` does not define, or a frame of type `opaque`;
13. a row of a length that its frame type does not allow, or an id that is not an integer;
14. a `blob` frame whose `"pub"` is not a map, a `meta` payload or a snapshot's `"meta"` that is not a map or has a top-level key
    that is not text, a header `"prof"` or `"layout"` that is not text, a
    `"rep"` or `"mt"` that is not text, an external blob frame whose digest is neither a byte string of 32 bytes nor a text, a
    `suppress` target of a kind
    `S1` does not define or one that breaks the list of members and types of 5.8, a `suppress` payload that is not a map with a
    `"targets"`
    array, a snapshot's `"blobs"` entry whose value is not a byte string, a `"reason"` that is not text, a `"by"` that is not
    the id of a defined term.

Everything else is converted. The diagnostic `IllTypedLiteral` of `S1` (7.1) is information: the literal is preserved verbatim
and converted. A sealed frame of the source is item 7, whatever recipients it names: the converter holds no key, so it decrypts
no frame and the result carries no encryption of the source.

### 8.3 The three reasons that no single item names

These three are produced by the label of the registry, each with the one condition below, and by no other condition. The finding
is `conversion_stopped` with the `reason` of the label and the `offset` stated.

1. `source_not_deterministic`. The converter reads `source` twice (section 6 step 1), each through the
   first-generation reader. The reason is produced exactly when the two reads give a different `source_bytes` digest, a
   different number of items, or a different pair `(offset, length)` for some item, in that order of test. `offset` is `0`.
2. `source_exceeds_capacity`. Produced exactly when (a) a source item has a stored length above `stored_bytes_per_frame` or a
   decoded payload above `decoded_bytes_per_frame` of the converter's capacity (`P7`, "Capacity"), or the items of the source
   are more than `items_per_read`, or the sum of the decoded payloads of the items, taken in source order, is above
   `decoded_bytes_per_read`, tested in source order, with `offset` the start of the first item that is such an item, that is the
   item after the `items_per_read`-th, or that brings the sum above the capacity; or (b) the labelling of the labelled set of
   `M(SF)` in step 2, of the compactor in
   step 3, or of the fold of the result read in step 4, is refused (`P3`, "Refusals and the reader's limit"): the refusal
   `width`, the refusal `size` of its `labelled_lines`, the refusal `weight` of its `labelled_bytes_per_read`, the refusal
   `term_node` of two nested terms with equal digests, or the refusal
   `limit` of a reader's own `labelling_units`, which is applied only to an input that is not
   `guaranteed`; or (c) a frame of the canonical result has a payload above those two limits, or the read of the result in step 4
   is stopped by
   `items_per_read`, has a frame refused by `decoded_bytes_per_read`, has a `terms` frame refused by `formed_bytes_per_read` or
   has a
   declaration refused by `rebuilt_bytes_per_read` (2.1), tested in step 4 before the
   other conditions of that step; in cases (b) and (c) `offset` is `0`. No other condition (a number of terms, blobs or
   segments) produces it. In (a) the items are counted from the first item of the source, the header of each segment counting as
   one, and THE DECODED PAYLOAD of a source item is the byte string that its transform chain gives from `"d"`: `"d"` itself when
   the frame names no transform, and no bytes when the item has no `"d"` or its chain fails. In (b) the size of `M(SF)` is the
   size of the fold of its canonical file, as `P3`, "Refusals and the reader's limit", THE SIZE OF THE LABELLED SET, defines it;
   a size equal to `labelled_lines` is labelled.
3. `content_mismatch`. Produced exactly when steps 2 to 4 have given no stop of an earlier reason, and (a) `Ds` differs from
   `Dr`, (b) a condition (b) of step 4 fails (the `file` of the `read_result` is not of the variant `second_generation` with one
   segment, `content_digest` is `absent` or differs between the file and the segment, `visible` is `incomplete`, `integrity` is
   not `intact`, the canonical claim or a level is not as step 4 says, or a finding has the severity `error` or `fatal`), or (c)
   compaction stops for a refused `terms` frame, a held row, binding or entry or for a present blob that does not rebuild (2.1).
   `offset` is `0`.

**P9-34.** `convert` MUST produce `source_not_deterministic` exactly under 8.3 item 1, `source_exceeds_capacity` exactly under 8.3
item 2 and `content_mismatch` exactly under 8.3 item 3, each with the `offset` of 8.3, and MUST produce none of them under another
condition.

**P9-35.** `convert` MUST judge, after the test of the two reads, the items in source order, at each item the capacity test of
8.3 item 2(a) and then the rules of 8.2 in their numbered order, MUST stop with the reason of the first that holds at the
earliest item, MUST report the offset of 8.1 for that reason, and MUST report nothing else.

**P9-36.** `convert` MUST apply items 1 to 14 as written and MUST NOT convert, omit, repair or carry an item that one of them
stops.

**P9-37.** `convert` MUST stop with the reasons of 8.3 at the steps of section 6 that 8.3 names: step 1 for the first;
steps 1, 2, 3 and 4 for the second; steps 3, 4 and 5 for the third.
