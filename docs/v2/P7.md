# Part P7: Canonical form and reading

## 0. Scope, dependencies and notation

This part ties the other parts together. It defines the typed result of reading (section 1), the minimum capacity of a reader
(section 2), the reader procedure for a file and for a segment in one total order (section 3), the canonical form with every
constant, its construction, its head claim and the storage level a reader reports, the reference encoder, compaction and the
canonical check (section 4), conformance (section 5) and the profile mechanism (section 6). It restates no rule of another part:
it orders their procedures, defines the records of the result and fixes every constant of the canonical form.

It depends on:

- part `P2` (Registry) for every enumeration, code, finding, reason, severity and the fields of each finding that are present;
- part `P1` (Model and wire) for the typed model, the file, segment and item structure, the generation, the header and its
  outcomes, the `offsets` item, the locator, the envelope, the effective identifier, the predecessor, the link, healing, stored
  and payload checks and framing ("The typed model and its one serialization", "File, segment, items, header, offsets and
  locator", "The frame envelope", "Identifiers, hashing and every preimage", "The checks of every frame", "Framing: torn, damaged
  and undelimitable input");
- part `P3` (RDF content) for terms, rows, the fold, the labelled set, the content digest, bindings, suppression, the visible
  dataset and the canonical dictionary, rows, bindings and suppression frames;
- part `P4` (Blobs) for the blob frames, the pack, the blob set, the rebuild and the canonical value and blob regions;
- part `P5` (Durability) for the range, the commit and the committed prefix, the index, parity and the recovery procedure;
- part `P6` (Signatures) for the signature record, verification, coverage and authentication;
- the labelling specification, called `L1`, "Canonical labelling of blank nodes: version 1", cited by its heading "Scope and
  result" and, under its heading "The whole input: order, result, record, identity, map, guarantee, outcomes, hash", by the label
  of the paragraph: THE CANONICAL LINE SEQUENCE AND ITS DIGEST, THE STATISTICS RECORD, THE GUARANTEE, THE PREDICATE `guaranteed`
  AND THE SEARCH-FREE RUN, OUTCOMES AND REFUSALS, MEMORY, ORDER AND DETERMINISM and VERIFICATION OF A CLAIM.

Notation. `E(x)` is the deterministic CBOR encoding of `x` (`P1`). A record is `[field, ...]` in declared order, a variant `[tag,
fields...]`, `option(T)` is `[1]` or `[2, v]`. Enumeration values are written by label; the codes are those of `P2`. A byte string
is written in hexadecimal, two digits a byte, a space between bytes; `<D>` followed by a name stands for the 32 bytes of a digest
that the vectors give. A requirement has an identifier `P7-n` and states one thing.

Terms used throughout, each defined once by the part named:

- `position`: the zero-based index of an item among all items of its segment, the header `0`, transparent frames, opaque items and
  a damaged region of the recovery procedure counted (`P1` "File, segment, items, header, offsets and locator"; `P2` "The fields
  of a finding that are present"; `P5` "The one recovery procedure"). It is the only meaning of the word in this part.
- the fields `anchored_frames`, `committed_frames` and `authenticated_frames` count ordinary frames; `ordinary_position` is the number of ordinary frames of the segment
  before a frame (`P5` "The `index` frame").
- a TRANSPARENT frame and an ORDINARY frame, the EFFECTIVE IDENTIFIER, a frame with no effective identifier, and the HEAD of a
  segment: each is defined by `P1`, "The predecessor and transparent frames", and this part states none of them.
- the OWN FOLD of a segment: 1.2. It is the one name of that fold in this part.

REQUIREMENT LANGUAGE. The key words "MUST", "MUST NOT", "REQUIRED", "SHALL", "SHALL NOT", "SHOULD", "SHOULD NOT",
"RECOMMENDED", "NOT RECOMMENDED", "MAY", and "OPTIONAL" in this document are to be interpreted as described in BCP 14
[RFC2119] [RFC8174] when, and only when, they appear in all capitals, as shown here. This document uses the key words MUST,
MUST NOT and MAY, and no other.

## 1. The typed result of reading

Reading delivers two things: the CONTENT, which is the fold of `P3` with its suppression record, blob set and VALUES, and
the REPORT,
which is the `read_result` of this section. The fold stands beside `read_result` and is not a field of it; the report carries its
content digest and its visibility state. The labelling's statistics record, a typed value that `L1` defines ("The whole input:
order, result, record, identity, map, guarantee, outcomes, hash", paragraph THE STATISTICS RECORD) and gives no byte form, is part
of what a reader computes and delivers with the content: one for the fold of the file (`R18`) and one for each segment's own fold
(`R15`), each absent when its labelling refused. It describes the lowered labelled set, term nodes and `nested_term` records
included (`P3`, "Nested terms, lowering and term nodes", WHAT A FOLD CAN HOLD), while the `statistics` of an index count neither.
It is not encoded in `read_result` and no index stores it.

THE ORDER OF THE CONTENT. The content a reader with the access `whole` delivers (the lines, bindings, suppression entries, blob set, VALUES, visible
dataset and named graphs of the fold of the file) is listed in the order in which the canonical file of the same fold holds it
(4.3 step 9): the `quad` lines, then the `reifier_binding` lines, the annotation rows, the `empty_graph` lines, the bindings and
the suppression entries, each kind in the order of its canonical frames (`P3`, "The canonical rows", "The canonical bindings
frames", "The canonical suppression frames"), with the terms in the order of the canonical dictionary (`P3`, "The canonical
dictionary"); the blob set in ascending bytewise order of `E(name)` (`P4`); VALUES in the order of its literals in the canonical
dictionary. The visible dataset is the same sequence of lines without the hidden items, and its named graphs are listed in
ascending order of the place of the graph term, the default graph first. This order is a function of the fold, so it is the same,
up to an automorphism of the fold, for the fold of a file and for the canonical file that compaction writes from it (THE PLACE OF
A TERM, below).

The canonical dictionary gives a blank node its place by its final position, which exists only when the labelling of the fold of
the file succeeded. When the labelling is refused (`P3`, "Refusals and the reader's limit"), the content is listed in the order
that `P3` states for that case ("The fold", THE ORDER OF DELIVERY): the same orders, with the blank nodes compared with one
another bytewise on their source identifiers `E([ordinal, label])` in the place of their positions, and every other comparison
unchanged. That order needs no position and runs no labelling. It is a function of the fold and of the labels of its blank
nodes, so it is not invariant under a renaming of labels: two files whose folds differ only in the labels of their blank nodes
can list their content in two orders.

THE PLACE OF A TERM is its id in the canonical dictionary (`P3`, "The canonical dictionary") when the labelling succeeded and,
when the labelling is refused, its id in the dictionary built with the comparison of blank nodes just stated. It is what a
profile uses to break a tie between two terms (PF-bundle, PF-files, PF-container). Two terms of the fold have two places.
The order of delivery and the place of a term are those of the final positions of THIS run of `L1`. For a canonical file whose
stored labels are those positions, the delivery is in the file's own order. For a canonical file whose labels name a labelling
that differs from this run's by an automorphism (4.6, LABELLING; possible only when a nested term of its fold holds a blank
node), the order of delivery and the places are those of this run, the same for every reader, and need not follow the stored
labels.

THE SIGN. A reader lists the content in the order after a refusal exactly when the findings of its `file_result` hold the
finding `labelling_refused`, which stands there at most once and with no `position` (`P3`, P3-39); otherwise it lists it in the
canonical order. No field records the order, and the content digest does not tell it: the digest is `absent` also for a fold
with a held row, binding or entry whose labelling succeeded. A read that stops at `items_per_read` or fails forms no fold of
the file and delivers no content. A reader with the access `forward` delivers the content as the arrival listing, in arrival
order (3.8), and a reader with the access `sparse` delivers an answer (3.9); neither runs the labelling, so neither states
the order of this paragraph.

**P7-39.** A reader with the access `whole` MUST deliver the lines, bindings, suppression entries, blob set, VALUES, visible
dataset and named graphs of a fold
in the order stated by THE ORDER OF THE CONTENT.

**P7-46.** A reader with the access `whole` MUST report `labelling_refused` in the findings of its `file_result` exactly when it delivers the content in
the order after a refusal of THE ORDER OF THE CONTENT, and MUST break a tie between two terms, where a profile asks for one, by
THE PLACE OF A TERM in the order it delivered in.

One record structure for a file, a segment, a frame and a signature, deterministically encoded, so that the corpus stores a result
and two engines compare results byte for byte. No result holds a printed word, a phrase or a mode.

### 1.1 The records

```text
read_result      = [ readers:          enum(readers_claim),
                     registry_version: uint,                   ; the registry version the reader implements in full
                     profiles:         list(profile_checks),   ; the profiles it implements, with the checks it runs (P2, 9a)
                     capacity:         capacity,
                     marks:            option(limits),         ; the reader's input (2, "Honouring `hostile`"); `absent` honours no mark
                     inputs:           inputs,
                     file:             file_result ]
profile_checks   = [ profile: uint, checks: uint ]             ; ascending by profile, no repeat; checks 1 to `checks` are run
capacity         = [ stored_bytes_per_frame:  uint,
                     decoded_bytes_per_frame: uint,
                     labelling_units:         uint,
                     labelled_lines:          uint,
                     decoded_bytes_per_read:  uint,
                     items_per_read:          uint,
                     rebuilt_bytes_per_read:  uint,
                     formed_bytes_per_read:   uint,
                     labelled_bytes_per_read: uint,
                     access:                  enum(access) ]   ; whole, forward or sparse (2, 3.7)
limits           = [ stored_bytes_per_frame:  uint,            ; eight `uint`: the fields of `capacity` but `items_per_read`
                     decoded_bytes_per_frame: uint,            ; and `access`, in the order of `capacity`
                     labelling_units:         uint,
                     labelled_lines:          uint,
                     decoded_bytes_per_read:  uint,
                     rebuilt_bytes_per_read:  uint,
                     formed_bytes_per_read:   uint,
                     labelled_bytes_per_read: uint ]
file_result      = [1, first_generation_result: bytes]         ; tag first_generation: that reader's result, in its own form
                 / [2, segments:       list(segment_result),   ; tag second_generation
                       findings:       list(finding),
                       content_digest: option(digest),         ; of the fold of the whole file
                       visible:        visibility,
                       end:            enum(file_end),         ; where the bytes of the file end (1.2)
                       left_out:       uint ]                  ; frames and items left out of the fold (1.2)
segment_result   = [ ordinal:              uint,
                     offset:               uint,
                     length:               uint,
                     opener:               opener,
                     header_identifier:    option(digest),
                     registry_version:     option(uint),
                     hash_algorithm:       option(uint),
                     profile:              option(uint),
                     integrity:            enum(integrity_state),
                     authenticity:         enum(authenticity_state),
                     anchored_frames:      uint,
                     committed_frames:     uint,
                     authenticated_frames: uint,
                     transparent_frames:   list(uint),
                     canonical:            canonical_result,
                     content_digest:       option(digest),     ; of the fold of this segment alone
                     frames:               list(frame_result),
                     findings:             list(finding) ]
frame_result     = [ position:       uint,
                     offset:         uint,
                     length:         uint,
                     frame_type:     option(uint),
                     link:           enum(link_state),
                     payload:        payload_state,
                     stored:         option(enum(stored_state)),
                     signatures:     list(signature_result),
                     fold:           fold_state,
                     rebuilds:       list(rebuild_result),
                     findings:       list(finding) ]
rebuild_result   = [ member: option(uint), state: rebuild_state ]   ; P4 "The total rebuilt length"
finding          = [ code: enum(finding_code), reason: option(enum(reason)), subject: option(subject_kind),
                     position: option(uint), offset: option(uint), length: option(uint), count: option(uint) ]  ; P2
canonical_result = [ claimed: bool, structure: check_state, labelling: check_state, storage: storage_check_state,
                     first_difference: option(difference) ]
difference       = [ check: enum(canonical_check), item_position: uint ]
opener           = [1]                                         ; the segment is opened by a header
                 / [2, reached:       enum(resync_reach),      ; the segment is opened by a resync item: walked or scanned
                       displaced:     bool,                    ; its `offset` differs from its place in the input
                       past:          enum(past_state),        ; absent, matches, differs or not_checked
                       marks_ignored: uint ]                   ; the marks that are not applicable to this reader (`P1`, "The resync item")
```

`inputs` is the record of `P6` "The typed inputs of a verifier" and `signature_result` the record of `P6` "Verification: the
procedure and its typed outcomes" (its records); this part embeds both by reference and restates no field of them.

`file_result.end`, a `file_end`, says where the bytes of a file end (1.2); the `end` of a stream point (3.8), a `bool`, says
whether the caller declared the end of the input. They are two fields of two records. `access` is a field of `capacity`, and
`limits` has neither `access` nor `items_per_read`, so that no input has two fields that have to agree and none has a field without
an effect. `segment_result.ordinal`, the position of a segment in the file read, and the `ordinal` of a `lineage` (`P1`, "The
header"), the number that a writer states in an opener, are two fields of two records.

**P7-38.** A reader MUST write the lists `trusted_keys` and `held_keys` of `inputs` in the result as the sets they are: each
list without repeats, its elements in ascending bytewise order of their deterministic encoding (the bytes compared as unsigned
integers from the first byte, a proper prefix before the longer string). Two callers whose lists hold the same elements get the
same `read_result`, whatever the order and the repeats of their lists.

`payload_state`, `fold_state`, `rebuild_state`, `check_state`, `storage_check_state`, `visibility` and `subject_kind` are the
variants of `P2` ("Enumerations of the typed result"). A field typed `uint` where an enumeration would stand (`frame_type`,
`hash_algorithm` and `profile` of a segment) holds the code as read, which can lie outside the reader's registry version, as the
`subject` of a finding does (`P2`); a reader never maps such a code to a label it does not hold.

**P7-1.** Except for a `sealing_input` that is not valid (P7-12), a reader MUST return, of the records of this part and of no
other form, exactly one `read_result` for every input read with the access `whole`, the stream points of 3.8 for an input read
with the access `forward`, and one `answer` for each query of a read with the access `sparse` (3.9), which takes no
`sealing_input`; and it MUST encode every record it returns (a `read_result`, an `answer`) deterministically (`P1`), the content
that it delivers beside them being typed values of the reading interface (section 1; 3.8, THE ARRIVAL LISTING; 3.9, THE ANSWER).

**P7-2.** A reader MUST give `readers` its own claim of section 5, `registry_version` the registry version it implements in full,
`profiles` the profiles it implements, each with the number of its checks that it runs, in ascending order of profile and without
repeats (`P2`, section 9a), `capacity` its capacity of section 2 and `inputs` the inputs of its caller (`P6` "The typed inputs of
a verifier"), with each of the two lists `trusted_keys` and `held_keys`, which are sets, written without repeats in ascending
bytewise order of the deterministic encoding of its elements (P7-38).

### 1.2 What fills each field

Every field is filled by exactly one rule, named here by part and section title. "The procedure" is section 3.

`file_result`:

- tag `first_generation`: the result of the first-generation reader, unchanged, when the reader claims `first_and_second` and the
  generation dispatch gives the first generation (`P1` "File, segment, items, header, offsets and locator", the generation);
- `segments`: one `segment_result` for each segment, in file order (`P1`, "File, segment, items, header, offsets and locator");
- `findings`: the findings of the file (1.4);
- `content_digest`: the content digest of the fold of the whole file (`P3` "The content digest"), `absent` exactly in the cases
  that section states, when a read failed (3.1), when the file has no segment (`R1`) and when `capacity.access` is `forward`
  (3.7). A segment whose header outcome is
  not `readable` contributes nothing to that fold and does not make the digest `absent`: a file whose every segment is such
  a segment has an empty fold, and its `content_digest` is the digest of the empty sequence that `L1` defines;
- `visible`: the visibility state of the fold of the whole file (`P3` "The visible dataset and visibility");
- `end`: the first value of this list that holds, computed from the results of the read alone:
  1. `not_read`: the read stopped before the end of the input (`read_failed`, or `exceeds_capacity` with the reason `item_count`);
  2. `undelimitable`: an `undelimitable_input` finding runs to the end of the input;
  3. `torn`: the input ends inside an item, that is, a `torn_tail` finding whose bytes run to the end of the input; a
     `torn_tail` whose bytes end at an accepted resync item does not make the end `torn`;
  4. `no_commit`: the last segment holds no valid commit (`P5`, "The checks of a commit"), or its opener is not `readable`;
  5. `after_commit`: an ordinary frame other than an `index` frame, an opaque item that is not a frame, or a damaged region
     stands after the last valid commit of the last segment;
  6. `at_authenticated_commit`: the last valid commit carries a signature that verifies under a key of `trusted_keys` (`P6`);
  7. `at_valid_commit`: otherwise.

  Transparent frames, `index` frames, and every item of kind `offsets` or `locator` (placed, misplaced or breaking its own
  grammar: its class is its first field) after the last valid commit do not move the end; an
  opaque item that is not a frame and a damaged region do, since no commit covers them and each can stand where a frame of
  content stood. The value says
  where the bytes of the file end and not whether a write is durable (`P5`, "What a commit does not show"). Under the access
  `forward` it is given at a stream point whose `end` is `true` (3.8). A file with no segment has no last segment, and its `end`
  is `no_commit` when none of the values before it holds;
- `left_out`: the number of frames and items left out of the fold of the file, the sum of:
  - each frame of a segment whose header outcome is `readable` whose fold state is `opaque`, whatever its reason (1.3, FOLD); a
    frame whose fold state is `not_content` or `folded` is never counted;
  - each opaque item that is not a frame (`P1`, "Classification of an item", steps 1, 3, 4 and 5) of a segment whose header
    outcome is `readable`, counted as one, but for every item whose kind is `offsets` or `locator`, which is never counted,
    whether it is placed, misplaced or opaque by its own grammar (step 6 of that classification), since it holds no content;
  - each damaged region of the file, wherever it stands, counted as one, and counted once when it stands in a segment whose
    opener is not readable;
  - each `torn_tail` finding of the file, wherever it stands, counted as one: the bytes before an accepted resync item that are a
    proper prefix of an item and the incomplete item at the end of the input alike (`P1`, "Finding a resync item where framing
    is lost"). A torn tail is not an item and is not counted among the items of a segment, and is counted here;
  - for a segment whose header outcome is `unreadable` or `links_unavailable`, each item after its opener, as `P1`, "The header",
    delimits and counts them, nothing else of them being judged; a closing item of a segment whose header outcome is `unreadable` or `links_unavailable` is counted with the rest, and
    in a readable segment the `offsets` item and the locator are classified and are not counted.

  A held row of a folded frame adds `0`: it is a row and not a frame, and it makes the content digest `absent` (`P3`, "The content
  digest"). The items that a stopped read did not read add `0`, and `end` reports the stop. A labelling that is refused adds `0`
  and makes the content digest of that fold `absent`. So `left_out` is `0` exactly when every frame of every readable segment is
  folded or not content, and the file holds no opaque item that is not a frame but a closing item, no damaged region, no
  `torn_tail` and no item of a segment whose opener is not readable. It differs from `integrity`, which is `intact` for a frame of
  a type that the reader does not know and that is not critical, for the frames after a stopped fold, and for a sealed frame that
  the reader cannot open (registry version `2`).

  FOR A CONSUMER: two content digests are comparable only when both are present and each covers the whole of the foldable content
  of its file, that is, when the `left_out` of each file is `0` and its `end` names no stopped read (`not_read`, `undelimitable`
  or `torn`).

  **P7-79.** A reader MUST give `left_out` as the sum above and MUST give `end` as the list above states.
Vector `end-frame-no-effective-identifier` (a frame that has no effective identifier after the last valid commit: `end` is
`after_commit`).
Vector `end-locator-own-grammar` (a `locator` item that breaks its own grammar after the last valid commit:
`malformed_value` with `wrong_length` at the item, `end` `at_valid_commit` and `left_out` `0`).
- `marks` of the `read_result`: the reader's input of 2, "Honouring `hostile`": `absent` when the reader honours no mark and
  otherwise the `limits` it applies. `limits` has no `access` and no `items_per_read`.

`segment_result`:

- `ordinal`: the zero-based index of the segment in the file read; `offset`: the input offset of the first byte of its opener;
  `length`: the number of bytes from the first byte of its opener to the first byte of the next opener, or to the end of the
  input, a torn item and a remainder that is not read excluded. When a resync item is accepted inside the complete item that
  precedes the item at which framing is lost (`P1`, "Finding a resync item where framing is lost"), `length` ends at the offset of
  the accepted item although the complete item ends after it, and the `offset` plus the `length` of that complete item passes the
  end of its segment. When the bytes before an accepted resync item are a `torn_tail`, `length` excludes them, as it excludes a
  torn item at the end of the input; when they are `undelimitable_input`, `length` includes the damaged region;
- `opener`: `[1]` for a header; for a resync item `[2, reached, displaced, past, marks_ignored]`, where `reached` is `walked` when
  the walk delimited the item and `scanned` when a scan found it (`P1`, "Finding a resync item where framing is lost"),
  `displaced` is `true` when its `offset` field differs from its place in the input, `past` is the check of the bytes before the
  item (below) and `marks_ignored` is the number of marks of the item that are not applicable to this reader (`P1`, "Reader
  advisory marks"; a mark that is applicable and is not applied because the item stands below its `offset` is not counted).
  For a resync item that is `unreadable`, `past` is `absent` and `marks_ignored` is `0`, whatever of the item was read:
  no past and no mark of an item that breaks its grammar is judged; `displaced` is `false` only when its `offset`
  field was read and equals its place, so that an item `unreadable` before that field has `opener`
  `[2, reached, true, absent, 0]`. The marks of a resync
  item are handed over as content of the item, which stands in the input at the `offset` of the
  segment, and no field of a result repeats them;
- `opener.past`: for a resync item at place `p` in the input, the first of these that holds:
  1. `absent`: the `past` field of the item is `absent`;
  2. `not_checked`: `p` is below the `offset` field (the file is cut, or it begins with the item); or, under the access `forward`,
     the `offset` field differs from `p`; or the check would bring the past count above the `decoded_bytes_per_read` of the reader,
     in which case that item and every later one are `not_checked`;
  3. `matches` when the unit digest (`P1`, "Every preimage, typed") of the bytes `[p - offset, p)` equals the `past` field, and
     `differs` when it does not.

  Under `forward` one running hash serves every item whose `offset` equals `p`, since the range then begins at byte `0`. THE PAST
  COUNT: the bytes hashed for these checks are kept in a count of their own and charge no total of the read, so that a check can
  never refuse a frame after the item; the charge of an item is the number of bytes of its range that the range of no earlier
  checked item with the same first byte holds. A mark does not change the value, a finding, a state or a count of any segment;
- `header_identifier`: `h0` of the opener (`P1`, "The header" and "The resync item"), `absent` whenever the header outcome is not
  `readable` (`unreadable` and `links_unavailable`);
- `registry_version`, `hash_algorithm`, `profile`: the opener's fields as read, `absent` when the header outcome is `unreadable`;
  `profile` is also `absent` when the header's `profile` is `absent`;
- `integrity`: 1.3; `authenticity`, `authenticated_frames`: `P6` "Coverage and authentication", over the counted frames (1.3);
- `anchored_frames`: the number of ordinary frames, counted from the first, before the first ordinary frame whose link state is
  neither `linked` nor `healed` (all of them when there is none);
- `committed_frames`: `P5` "The committed prefix";
- `transparent_frames`: the positions of the frames whose `transparent` field is `true`, ascending (`P1`, "The predecessor and
  transparent frames");
- `canonical`: section 4.6;
- `content_digest`: the content digest of the segment's OWN FOLD: the fold of that segment read alone as a file, where a pair
  `[frame, label]`, a value, a unit or a referent that lies in another segment is unresolved there (`P3` "The content
  digest", `P5` "The `index` frame"); `absent` in the cases `P3` states for that fold, when the header outcome is not
  `readable`, when a read failed (3.1) and when `capacity.access` is `forward` (3.7);
- `frames`: one `frame_result` for each frame of the segment (each item that passes the envelope test, `P1` "The frame envelope"),
  in item order; empty when the header outcome is not `readable`;
- `findings`: 1.4.

**P7-83.** A reader MUST give `opener.past` as `opener.past` of 1.2 states, MUST keep the bytes it hashes for it in the past count
alone, and MUST NOT let `past` or any mark change a finding, a state or a count of any segment.

`frame_result`:

- `position`, `offset`, `length`: the position of the frame, the input offset of its first byte and its encoded length;
- `frame_type`: `absent` when element 1 of the envelope is not a `uint` in its one form (`P1`, "The locator", A FRAME ITEM HAS
  A TYPE ONLY BY A VALUE), and `present(t)` with the `uint` `t` of element 1 otherwise;
- `link`: `P1` "The link check" and "Healing";
- `payload`: `P1` "The payload check";
- `stored`: `P1` "The stored check"; `absent` for `stored_plain` and for a `storage` outside its grammar;
- `signatures`: one `signature_result` for each element of the envelope's `signatures` list, in list order (`P6`), empty when `P6`
  gives none (an unreadable header, a hash not implemented, a transparent frame, a `signatures` field outside its grammar, a
  signature of an unknown algorithm in a segment whose version is at most the reader's, a frame with no effective identifier
  (`P6`, "The procedure"));
- `fold`: 1.3;

### 1.3 How each state value is decided

LINK, STORED, PAYLOAD. The value is the outcome of the check of `P1` named in 1.2. For a frame of a segment whose header outcome
is `readable`, every frame has a link state, every frame has a payload state, and a stored state exists exactly for a well-formed
`stored_compressed` storage.

FOLD. The fold state of a frame of type `parity` is ALWAYS `not_content`, damaged or not, but for a frame that the later-version
case handles (`P1`, "The record, in identity order", THE EXCEPTION OF PARITY FRAMES, clause (ii)), which is `opaque` by the
order below: a parity frame is durability and never content, so it is never `opaque` otherwise; its damage of any kind is the
one finding `parity_damaged` (`P5`, "Checking a parity frame").
The `fold_state` of every other frame is decided in this order:

1. `opaque` with the reason that the precedence of opaque reasons of `P2` "Enumerations of the typed result" selects among the
   reasons that apply, when at least one applies. The reasons and when they apply: `malformed` when the frame carries
   `malformed_value` (`P1`; or, for a frame whose link state is `linked` or `healed`, a value of its content record or the payload
   grammar, by the part of its type); `chain_break` when its link state is `broken` and it
   is ordinary; `stopped` when an earlier frame of the segment gave `unsupported_critical`; `damaged` when its payload state is
   `damaged`; `unsupported_critical` when the frame itself gave that finding; `unknown_codec` and `exceeds_capacity` when its
   payload state is `unchecked` with that reason, and `exceeds_capacity` also for a `terms` frame that the total formed length
   refuses (`P3`, "Front coding"); `unknown_frame_type` when its frame type is a value (`P1`, "The locator") that is not a code of the reader's version and its
   `critical` is `false`. Under registry version `2`, a sealed frame that the reader cannot open is `opaque` with the reason `P8`
   gives, and the fold continues (`P8`). A `damaged_frame` that `P4` reports for a unit or a rebuild (a unit whose bytes disagree
   with its digest, `blob_length`, `blob_digest`) does not change the fold state: the declaration stays in the blob set (`P4` "The
   blob set"); it makes the segment's integrity `damaged`. A refusal by the total rebuilt length (`P4`, "The total rebuilt
   length") does not change the fold state either; it makes the segment's integrity at best `intact_unchecked`.
2. Otherwise `not_content` for a frame of type `commit` or `index`.
3. Otherwise `folded`.

`missing_dependency` is a finding and never an opaque reason: a missing unit, value, blob, frame or state leaves the frame folding
what it can (`P3`, `P4`).

Only `folded` frames contribute to a fold (`P3` "The fold").

INTEGRITY, over the ordinary frames of the segment (transparent frames never enter; a frame of type `parity` whose `transparent`
field is `false` is an ordinary frame, and its link state and payload state enter here like those of any other ordinary frame,
although its findings are replaced by `parity_damaged`):

- `intact`: the header outcome is `readable`; every ordinary frame has the link state `linked` or `healed` and the payload state
  `verified`; no item other than a transparent frame carries `malformed_value`; no ordinary frame carries `damaged_frame` (a
  failed rebuild, `blob_length` or `blob_digest`, included); no rebuild state of the segment is `refused` or `not_run`; no `terms`
  frame of the segment is refused by the total formed length; and the segment has no damaged region (`P5` "The one recovery
  procedure");
- `intact_unchecked`: the same, except that at least one ordinary frame has the payload state `unchecked`, at least one rebuild
  state of the segment is `refused` or `not_run` or at least one `terms` frame of the segment is refused by the total formed length, and no
  ordinary frame has the payload state `damaged`;
- `not_checked`: no link of the segment could be checked: the header outcome is `links_unavailable` (its hash algorithm is not
  implemented);
- `damaged`: every other case, a header outcome `unreadable` included. `damaged` never stands for "not checked".

AUTHENTICITY. `P6` "Coverage and authentication" decides it, over the counted frames, the ordinary frames other than `index`
frames (none of which is a frame with no effective identifier): `authenticated` when there is at least one such frame and every
one is an authenticated frame, `partly_authenticated` when some are and not all, `unauthenticated` otherwise. An `index` frame is

carries an authenticating signature is `authenticated`.

VISIBILITY. `P3` "The visible dataset and visibility", over the items of the whole file: `complete` when every item of the file
that passes the envelope test with `frame_type` `suppress` is `folded` and no sealed frame is unopened, otherwise `incomplete`

CHECK. Section 4.6.

### 1.4 Where each finding stands, and in what order

Each finding stands in exactly one list:

1. a finding whose `position` names a frame of a segment stands in that frame's `findings`;
2. any other finding produced for a segment (a finding of its header, of an item that is not a frame, or a finding of the segment
   with no `position`, such as `unsupported_registry_version`, `lineage_begins`, `lineage_mismatch`, `unprotected_tail` and
   `labelling_refused` of the own fold) stands in the segment's `findings`;
3. a finding of the generation dispatch (`empty_input`, `not_this_format`, `unsupported_generation`), of framing (`torn_tail`,
   `undelimitable_input`, `read_failed`, `exceeds_capacity` with `item_count`) and of the fold of the whole file with no `position` (`labelling_refused`) stands in the
   file's `findings`.

ORDER. Every finding is produced by exactly one step of the procedure of section 3, which numbers its steps. Each list is sorted
ascending by the number of the step that produced the finding, then ascending by `position` (`absent` first, then the integers
in increasing order), then ascending bytewise on the finding's deterministic encoding (the bytes compared as unsigned integers
from the first byte, a proper prefix before the longer string). The order is therefore a function of the findings, not of the
order in which an implementation computes them, and two readers that produce the same findings produce the same lists.

EQUAL FINDINGS. A list can hold findings equal in every field, and none is dropped: a rule that gives one finding for each of
several things gives each. Two members of one pack that fail their digests are two `damaged_frame` findings at the pack, and
two declarations of one pack that conflict are two `conflicting_declaration` findings there. Equal findings stand together in
the order.

  fold of the file is not formed, the content digests are `absent`, and the finding is `exceeds_capacity` with the reason

  plus one, the number of items the read would have reached. A region of bytes that cannot be delimited counts one item and runs to

### 2.1 Honouring `hostile`

A reader whose input `marks` is present honours an applicable `hostile` mark (`P1`, "The resync item" gives applicability;
`P1`, "Reader advisory marks" the marks). The result of the read gives for what a mark refuses exactly what a capacity refusal
gives: a frame that a limit refuses is `unchecked` with `exceeds_capacity` and never `damaged` (`P1`, "The payload check"), its
fold state is `opaque` with `exceeds_capacity` (1.3), the rows that name the terms of a frame that is not folded are held (`P3`,
"Unreadable terms"), a refused labelling is `labelling_refused` and makes the content digest `absent` (`P3`, "Refusals and the
reader's limit"), and every later commit of the run of a frame that is not folded is unavailable (`P3`, "Graph states").

HONOURING. A reader honours `hostile` when its input `marks` is present and the mark is applicable. Nothing else admits or refuses
a mark: it does not depend on a signature, on the policy of a commit or on the access of the read, so the decision is a function
of the bytes and the inputs. Under the access `forward` a mark of the scope stands before its frames, so a forward reader holds it
when it judges them. For an honoured mark, with `limits` the eight fields of the input:

- TARGET `scope`: every frame of the scope is judged, after the capacity step of the payload check and THE TOTAL, against the
  smaller of each of `stored_bytes_per_frame` and `decoded_bytes_per_frame` of `limits` and of `capacity`, and against the totals
  of `limits` kept over the frames of the scope alone (`decoded_bytes_per_read`, `formed_bytes_per_read`,
  `rebuilt_bytes_per_read`); the own fold of the scope and the fold of the file, which holds the lines of the scope, are judged
  against the smaller of `labelled_lines`, `labelled_bytes_per_read` (for the weight of that fold) and `labelling_units` of
  `limits` and of `capacity`. `items_per_read` is not a field of `limits`: framing bounds the number of items before any mark is
  read.
- TARGET `bytes`: only the folds are judged so: the own folds of the segments that hold a frame of the range MOVED BY `d`, and the
  fold of the file. For an item at place `p`, `d` is `p` minus its `offset` field (`0` for an item that is not displaced): the
  mark names `[offset_t, offset_t + length)` of the file in which the item is written, which stands at `[offset_t + d, offset_t +
  d + length)` of the file read. When `p` is below the `offset` field the range cannot be placed and the mark is not applied; it
  is not counted in `marks_ignored`, which counts the marks that are not applicable. The frames of a range before the item are not
  judged again: their payload checks stand before the item in the order of judgement and cannot depend on it.

WHAT A BYTE TARGET JUDGES. A `hostile` mark with a byte target judges the folds of the segments it covers and the fold of the
file, and not their frames. An honouring reader gives `labelling_refused` and the content digests `absent` for the folds of the
segments the mark covers and for the fold of the file when its `limits` refuse their labelling; `strains` and `measures` play no
part; every frame of the covered segments keeps the fold state it
had, and `left_out` (1.2) does not move. A reader that ignores marks is not affected.

THE READ'S TOTAL IS NEVER MOVED BY A MARK. The total decoded length of the read (`P1`, "The payload check", THE TOTAL) is kept by
the `capacity` of the reader alone, in the order stored size, decoded size, total: a frame that these admit is added to it, and
refused by it when the sum is above `decoded_bytes_per_read`, whether or not `limits` then refuses the frame, as if the frame had
been checked. The limits of a mark refuse a frame after that, so a frame that they refuse is still counted in the total, and the
charges of the candidates of a scan are charged against the same total (`P1`, "Finding a resync item where framing is lost"). The
point at which the total is exhausted, the candidate that it refuses and the item that is accepted are the same with a mark
honoured and with it ignored. The totals of `limits` are separate counts over the frames of the scope and refuse only by
themselves.

THE REFUSAL. A frame or a fold so refused is left out of the fold with its finding, as a capacity refusal leaves it: it gives the
states, findings and reasons of the first paragraph of this section, and a refused frame keeps its item, position, link state,
stored check and signatures and is never absent from the result and never `damaged`. No state or reason exists for an honoured
refusal; the result tells it from a capacity refusal by `marks`, which records `limits`, and by the sizes of the frame, which lie
within `capacity` and above `limits`. A badly behaving scope makes an honouring reader refuse the labelling of the fold of the
file (its digest `absent`) while the own fold and digest of every other scope stand.

WHAT THE CONTENT DIGEST AND THE COUNT SHOW. A refused labelling (by a byte target, by the labelling fields of a mark of the scope,
or by capacity) leaves every frame folded and `left_out` unmoved, and makes the content digest of that fold `absent`. For a
refused frame that is a row frame (a `quads` frame, say) the content digest is the digest of the fold as it stands, which is
`present` and covers a smaller fold, since the digest is `absent` only in its two cases (a refused labelling; a held row, binding
or entry), exactly as a capacity refusal of such a frame gives it; the frame is shown by its own `exceeds_capacity` finding, its
fold state `opaque`, and `left_out`, which counts it. A consumer that compares two content digests applies FOR A CONSUMER of 1.2,
`left_out`.

**P7-82.** A reader whose input `marks` is present MUST judge the target of an applicable `hostile` mark against `limits` as this
section states, MUST keep the total of the read by its `capacity` alone, counting a frame that `limits` refuse as if it had been
checked, and MUST give for what it refuses exactly the states, findings and digests of a capacity refusal, `left_out` counting a
refused frame and not a refused labelling; a byte target judges the folds of the segments it covers and not their frames. Vectors
`marks-hostile-scope` (a frame within `capacity` and above `limits`: `unchecked` with `exceeds_capacity`, `exceeds_capacity` with
`decoded_size`, `opaque` with `exceeds_capacity`, link and signatures kept), `marks-hostile-labelling` (the fold of the file
refused with `refusal_limit`, the digest `absent`, the own digests of the other segments present), `marks-hostile-range-fold-only`
(a byte target: the folds judged and no frame), `marks-total-unmoved` (a `hostile` mark of the scope whose `limits` refuse a large
frame, and a later damaged region whose candidate the total of the read refuses: the same candidate refused and the same items
accepted with the input `marks` absent and present), `marks-refused-row-frame` (a `quads` frame refused by `limits`: the content
digest `present`, the frame `opaque`, `left_out` `1`), `marks-hostile-displaced` (the input `F` of `1000` bytes joined before `G`,
whose item at `400` carries a `hostile` mark on `[100, 150)`: a reader that honours marks refuses the fold of the first segment of
`G`, at `[1100, 1150)`, and not that of `F`, as `G` read alone does) and `marks-hostile-forged-earlier` (the honest file `H T Q
C`, then an appended resync item with a `hostile` mark on the byte target `[0, n)`, `n` the length of the honest file, `strains`
`stored_bytes_per_frame`, which is not the limit that refuses, and a scope `T' Q' C'`: read with `marks` absent, the honest result for the first segment, its own digest and
the digest of the file `present`, `left_out` `0`; read with `marks` present and `limits.labelling_units` below what the fold of
the first segment needs, `labelling_refused` for the own fold of the first segment and for the fold of the file, both digests
`absent`, `Q` still folded, `left_out` `0`, the own digest of the last segment `present`).

## 3. The reader procedure

The procedure is TOTAL: under the access `whole` every byte string, a read that fails included, yields exactly one `read_result`,
under the access `forward` one stream point at each input point for each value of `end` (3.7, 3.8), and under the access `sparse`
one `answer` for every byte string, length and query (3.9). It has twenty-four steps. Each step has a NAME, which other documents
cite and which does not change if steps are renumbered, and a number `R1` to `R24`, which is the sort key of 1.4. A step names the
rule it applies, and the outcome of each rule is the typed outcome that the named part states. Phases run in order: the file phase
(GENERATION); framing and the wire phase, segment by segment in file order (FRAMING to UNPROTECTED TAIL, with FRAMING and OPENER
interleaved: the framing of a segment's items follows the judgement of its header); the content phase of every segment in file
order (CONTENT to OWN FOLD); the fold of the whole file (FOLD to LABELLING); the closing phase of every segment in file order
(INDEX to STATES); and the visibility of the file (VISIBILITY). A phase that runs for every segment runs all its steps for one
segment, in step order, before it runs any step for the next segment in file order; the one check that runs once for the file,
after the closing phase of the last segment, is the check over the whole file in PROFILE CHECKS (`R21`). No step decodes a payload
before the link check of its frame (`P1`, "The checks of every frame").

THE STEPS AND THE FINDINGS EACH PRODUCES. Every finding of a reading has exactly one producing step, named here; where one code
has two producing steps, the condition named beside each tells them apart, so every finding has one. The order of every findings
list (1.4) is then determined.

### 3.1 The file phase and framing

`R1` GENERATION. Apply the procedure for the first item of `P1` "The generation" to the first item.

- `empty_input`, `not_this_format` or `unsupported_generation`: the result is `file_result` tag `second_generation` with no
  segment, that one finding, `content_digest` `absent` and `visible` `[1, 0]`; the procedure ends. When the first item is
  incomplete or cannot be delimited, this step delimits it as far as it can, FRAMING (`R2`) reports `torn_tail` alone or
  `undelimitable_input` at offset `0`, and that finding has the sort key of `R2`; the result is the same with that finding. When
  the first item is incomplete, cannot be delimited, or is complete and fails the framing test of `P1`, "Finding a resync item
  where framing is lost", that section scans it from offset `0` for a resync candidate before the read stops, and the generation
  of the file from an accepted item on is the generation of that item.

`R2` FRAMING. Read the items of the input forward from offset `0` by `P1` "Reading the items of a file: the outcomes together". An
opener, a `header` item or a `resync` item, begins a segment (`P1` "File, segment, items, header, offsets and locator"), and `R3`
judges it before the items after it are framed. Where framing is lost at an item (bytes that cannot be delimited, an incomplete
item, or a complete item that fails the framing test), run `P1`, "Finding a resync item where framing is lost": the run of the
item is scanned for a resync candidate, and when one is accepted the bytes before it are reported as that section states and
reading continues at it with a segment that it opens. Where no candidate is accepted and no next item can be delimited, apply the
step RESUME of `P5` "The one recovery procedure" with the `h0` of the current segment: a resumption point ends the DAMAGED REGION,
which counts as one item of the segment for positions, and reading continues at the resumption point. A segment whose header
outcome is `unreadable` or `links_unavailable` has no `h0` to test a parity frame against, so it has no resumption. Once a reader
resynchronises, the recovery procedure of `P5` governs: the `length` of an `undelimitable_input` finding runs to the resumption
point when there is one, else to the end of the input, and an incomplete last item is `torn_tail` only when no resumption and no
accepted resync candidate follows it. `torn_tail`, `undelimitable_input` and `read_failed` are reported here and stand in the
file's list, whether or not reading resumed (1.4). When a read fails, the items before the failed read are read and judged in full
(links, payloads, findings); the fold of the file is not formed, the content digests are `absent`, and the result names the range
by the `offset` and `length` of `read_failed`. Nothing is folded, digested, proven or repaired from bytes whose read failed (`P1`
"A failed read is not the end of the input"), and the levels of the canonical check of every claimed segment are `unchecked`
with `read_failed` (4.6, A FAILED READ).

THE ONE COUNT of section 2 bounds the items of the walk, and `R2` stops at it as that section states.

### 3.2 The wire phase of a segment

`R3` OPENER. Judge the opener, a header by `P1` "The header" or a resync item by `P1` "The resync item", and in both by `P1` "A
value against registry versions": its outcome (`readable`, `unreadable`, `links_unavailable`), `h0`, and
`unsupported_registry_version` once when `h` is above `r` (`P2` "A value against the registry versions"). Judge `lineage` against
the previous segment (its head from its LINKS step and the `ordinal` of its lineage when it has one, or that its opener is not
readable, in which case neither field is compared; `ordinal` is not compared when the previous segment has no lineage) by `P1`
"The header". When
the outcome is not `readable`, the items of the segment are delimited and counted and NOTHING ELSE is judged: no classification
finding, no field rule, no `offsets` or locator claim and no profile finding; only the framing findings (`torn_tail`,
`undelimitable_input`, `read_failed`) and the opener's own findings are reported: for an `unreadable` opener its
`malformed_value` alone, and for a `links_unavailable` opener `unsupported_registry_version` and, when its `lineage` is `absent`
in a segment after the first, `lineage_begins` (a `lineage` that is `present` is compared with nothing). Classification runs
only far enough to see an
opener, which ends the segment, and to count for VISIBILITY the items that pass the envelope test with `frame_type`
`suppress`: such an item is a `suppress` frame that is not folded, and the visibility state fails closed (1.3). The later steps give the segment nothing but its integrity (`damaged` for
`unreadable`, `not_checked` for `links_unavailable`), its authenticity
(`unauthenticated`) and an unchecked canonical result (4.6).

`R4` ITEMS. Classify every item after the opener by `P1` "Classification of an item" and apply the envelope test, the field rules
in their order (one `malformed_value` for one item, `P1` "The frame envelope"), the place rules of `offsets` and locator items
(`P1` "File, segment, items, header, offsets and locator") and the table of values the reader does not know (`P1` "Values the
reader does not know"; `P2` "A value against the registry versions"), for a value of the envelope or of an item; no value of a
content record is judged here (`R11`). Three rules of other
parts read fields of the envelope alone and are field rules judged here, after those of `P1`: the `decoded_length` of a `chunk`,
the `decoded_length` and then the `storage` of an `external` (`P4`, "The frames"), and the `decoded_length` of a `commit` (`P5`,
"The checks of a commit"). A frame has one type, so at most one of these applies to it. Check every
`offsets` item and every locator item where it stands (`P1` "The `offsets` item", "The locator"). Decide
`unsupported_critical` of a frame whose `frame_type` the reader does not hold and the frames after it (`stopped`).

`R5` LINKS. Decide the link state of every frame in item order by `P1` "The link check" and "Healing", with the predecessor rule
(`P1` "The predecessor and transparent frames"); this gives every effective identifier and the head (`P1`, "The predecessor and
transparent frames"). Report `chain_break` and `healed_link`, except for a frame of type `parity` (`R8`).

`R6` STORED CHECK. Run the stored check on every frame with a well-formed `stored_compressed` storage (`P1` "The stored check").

`R7` PAYLOAD CHECK. Run the payload check on every frame in item order (`P1` "The payload check", with the zstd constraint),
whether or not the fold of the segment has stopped; there is no reader that skips payload checks of the frames whose bytes it
reads.

`R8` PARITY. Judge every `parity` frame by `P5` "Checking a parity frame" (the frame, the placement, the shards), replacing for
such a frame every finding of `R4` to `R7` by `parity_damaged`; a non-empty `signatures` list on a parity frame is part of its
damage.

`R9` REPAIR. Run the steps ELIGIBILITY and REPAIR, THEN RE-CHECK of `P5` "The one recovery procedure": `repaired`,
`repair_failed`. The result reports the input as it is (`P5` "The one recovery procedure"); a repaired copy is a separate output.

`R10` UNPROTECTED TAIL. Report `unprotected_tail` by `P5` "The unprotected tail".

### 3.3 The content phase of a segment

`R11` CONTENT. For every frame of a content type whose link state is `linked` or `healed` and that `R4` to `R9` leave free of
`malformed_value`, `stopped`, `damaged`, `unknown_codec` and `exceeds_capacity`, in item order, judge the values of its content
record by the part of its type, in the order of the fields of the record (`P3`, "The order of the payload checks", step 1),
decode the payload by that part and apply the rest of that part's rules: `P3`, "The `terms` frame" (ids, front coding, the
declared order), "The row frames", "From ids to typed lines", "Typed references: the `bindings` frame" (the frame's own rules),
"Suppression hides" (the wire record); `P4`, "The frames" and "The pack"; `P5`, "The `index` frame" (its grammar). A value of
the content record that `P2-57` and `P2-63` make malformed (`0`, a value of no registry version, a value of a later version than
the segment's) is `malformed_value` with the reason they give, and the frame is
opaque with `malformed`; a value of a newer registry version than the reader's gives the frame the outcome of `P2-62`, and
`unsupported_critical` stops the fold of the segment at the frame, so that every later frame of the segment is `stopped` and is
not judged by this step; a later frame whose `frame_type` the reader does not hold and whose `critical` is true keeps its own
`unsupported_critical` (`R4`) beside the fold reason `stopped`. The content of a frame whose link state is `broken` is not
interpreted: this step gives it no finding. A frame of type `parity` is judged by `R8`: whether the later-version case of `P1`,
"The record, in identity order", holds is decided there from the envelope, the content record, the link and the segment's
registry version alone, never from the fold state or the payload state; this step gives such a frame `unknown_enumeration_value`
with `unknown_frame_type` when it is not `stopped` and its payload has verified, and no finding of its value otherwise. A
payload outside the grammar of its part is `malformed_value` with the reason that
part names, and the frame is opaque with `malformed`; a broken declared order is `declared_order_violated`. Each `terms`
frame is charged to the total formed length before its texts are formed (`P3`, "Front coding"); a frame that it refuses is
`exceeds_capacity` with `total_formed`, and FOLD STATES makes it opaque with `exceeds_capacity`. This step records
which terms are unreadable (`P3`, "Unreadable terms", THE DICTIONARY IN DOUBT included), which rows, bindings and entries are dropped or held, and leaves every `literal_ref` and
`literal_value_ref` term PENDING; the
findings of terms and rows are produced by FOLD.

`R12` FOLD STATES. Decide the `fold_state` of every frame by 1.3.

`R13` COMMITS. Judge every `commit` frame by `P5` "The checks of a commit"; give `committed_frames` and report `uncommitted_tail`
by `P5` "The committed prefix".

`R14` UNITS AND VALUES. Build the unit index of the segment, checking every unit against its digest; charge every folded
`recipe` and `value` declaration, frames and members, to the total rebuilt length of the read in the order of the charges, and
rebuild every declaration that is admitted, by `P4`, "Rebuilding a blob or a value"; give every charged declaration its rebuild
state; report `damaged_frame` for a unit whose bytes disagree with its digest and for a rebuild (`blob_length`, `blob_digest`),
`missing_dependency` with `chunk`, `exceeds_capacity` with `total_rebuilt` for each refused declaration, and
`unreferenced_unit`. Every value of the file is charged here, and rebuilt here when admitted, before any fold reads it.

`R15` OWN FOLD. Form the segment's own fold (1.2), with its `literal_ref` and `literal_value_ref` terms resolved against the
values rebuilt at UNITS AND
VALUES for this segment only, and its labelled set; run `L1` once on it and give the segment's `content_digest` (`P3`, "The
labelled set and what the format takes from the labelling", "The content digest"). For the own fold a reader reports exactly two
things: the `content_digest`, and `labelling_refused` in the segment's list when the labelling refuses. No other finding is
reported for it.

### 3.4 The fold of the whole file

`R16` FOLD. Resolve every pending `literal_ref` and `literal_value_ref` term against the values rebuilt at UNITS AND VALUES in
every segment (`P3`, "`literal_ref`: a lexical form held by a `value` frame"), by the value used for the term (`P4`, P4-54): the
first verified declaration in file order whose `value_digest` equals the term's digest and whose `total_length` equals the
term's length and stands before the `terms` frame that holds the term. When no `value` declaration has both and stands there,
the finding is `missing_dependency` with `value`, once for each pair of a digest and a length, at the first `terms` frame in file
order that holds a term of the pair with no such declaration; when some have both and stand there and none is verified,
the finding of each such declaration stands at its own position (a failed rebuild, or a refusal by the total rebuilt length,
reported at UNITS AND VALUES) and no `missing_dependency` is added. In both cases the term is unreadable and whatever names
it is held with `opaque_term`. Then report the findings of terms and rows that CONTENT recorded (`forward_reference`,
`position_constraint`,
`ill_formed_term`, `opaque_term`, each at most once for a frame, `P3`, "Unreadable terms", with THE DICTIONARY IN DOUBT, and
"What happens to a row"), and form
the fold of the file from the `folded` frames of every segment (`P3`, "The fold"): its lines, bindings, blob set (`P4`, "The blob
set", with `conflicting_declaration` at each declaration whose length is not the smallest), suppression record and VALUES,
resolving every
leaf against the whole file (`P3`, "Naming a blank node in a binding or an entry"). Every finding about content is reported for
this fold of the file, and none for an own fold.

`R17` RESOLUTION. Resolve every referent (`P3` "Typed references: the `bindings` frame": `missing_dependency` with `blob`,
`frame`, `state` or `state_unavailable`; `binding_unanchored`) and match every suppression entry (`P3` "The hiding rule":
`suppression_unmatched`). A `state` referent is resolved by `P3` "Graph states": the commits that name a state to the reader
are decided from the fold states of `R12`, the link states of `R5` and the rebuild outcomes of `R14`, and the fold of a state is
formed only for a commit that a referent names. When a read stops at `items_per_read` or fails, the fold of the file is not
formed, so no referent is resolved and no `missing_dependency` is reported.

`R18` LABELLING. Form the labelled set of the fold of the file, run `L1` once on it and give the file's `content_digest` (`P3`); a
refusal is `labelling_refused` in the file's list.

### 3.5 The closing phase of a segment, and visibility

`R19` INDEX. Check every checked `index` frame of the segment by `P5`, "Checking an index": its structural claims (`frame_count`,
`head`, `type_runs`, `graph_directory`, `range_root`, `pack_members`) wherever it stands. Its `content_digest` and `statistics`
are compared, against the own fold of OWN FOLD, only when the index is the last ordinary frame of its segment AND every ordinary
frame before it has the fold state `folded` or `not_content`; otherwise they are not compared and no finding is made (the frames'
own findings say why). No labelling of a prefix is run.

`R20` SIGNATURES. Verify every signature of every ordinary frame by `P6` "Verification: the procedure and its typed outcomes", and
decide coverage, authentication, `authenticated_frames` and `authenticity` by `P6` "Coverage and authentication" and 1.3. No
signature is consulted by any earlier step (`P6` "Verification: the procedure and its typed outcomes").

`R21` PROFILE CHECKS. When the header declares a profile: if the reader implements it, run its checks (section 6) and report each
failure as `profile_violation`; a check of one segment stands in the list that 1.4 gives for its `position`; a check over the
content of the whole file is run once, after the closing phase of every segment of the file, and reported as a finding of this
step in the segment's list of the LAST SEGMENT THAT DECLARES THE PROFILE, which is the last segment of the file whose header
outcome is `readable` and whose header `profile` is `present` with that profile; the finding has a `count`. Several such
findings stand in that list in the order of 1.4. If the reader does not implement it, report `profile_not_checked` once in the
segment's list.

`R22` CANONICAL CHECK. Run the canonical check of 4.6 when the header claims the canonical form: `canonical_violated` at the first
difference.

`R23` STATES. Decide `integrity`, `anchored_frames` and `transparent_frames` by 1.2 and 1.3.

`R24` VISIBILITY. After the closing phase of every segment, decide `visible` by 1.3.

**P7-11.** A reader MUST run the steps `R1` to `R24` in the order of section 3, each by the rule it names, and MUST produce each
finding at the step that names its rule. In a phase that runs for every segment, a reader MUST run all steps of the phase for one
segment, in step order, before it runs a step for the next segment in file order.

**P7-12.** A reader MUST return a `read_result` for every input, including an input of zero bytes, a prefix at any byte, bytes
that are not CBOR and an input whose read fails (`P1` "Every input has a result"), under the access `whole`, and under the access `forward` in the
stream points of 3.8 (P7-1).
Besides a read with the access `sparse`, which returns an `answer` (3.9), the one case in which a reader returns no
`read_result` is a `sealing_input` of a reader of registry version `2` that is not valid: it returns `sealing_input_refused` before it reads
any byte of the input (3.6; `P8`, "The reader's key input"), and that outcome is a function of the `sealing_input` alone.

**P7-13.** A reader MUST run every step for every segment, except as the table STEPS BY ACCESS (3.7) states for the `access` of its
capacity; no step is optional and no mode skips one (`P5` "The one recovery procedure", `P1` "The checks of every frame").

### 3.6 A reader of an older registry version

A reader of registry version `r` that meets a segment of version `h` above `r` runs the same procedure: `R3` reports
`unsupported_registry_version` once; when it holds the header's `hash_algorithm`, every link is checked (`R5`), every payload
whose codec it holds is checked (`R7`), frames of an unknown type and values it does not know are treated by `P2` "A value against
the registry versions", it folds what it can, and every frame it does not fold is `opaque` with its reason; when it does not hold
the hash algorithm, the header outcome is `links_unavailable`, the items are delimited and counted only, and `integrity` is
`not_checked`. A sealed frame of registry version `2` met by a reader of version `1` is a critical frame of an unknown type: the
fold of its segment stops there (`P2` "Registry versions"). A reader of version `2` that cannot open a sealed frame continues the
fold; the frame is opaque and `visible` is `incomplete` with the count of sealed frames not opened (`P8`).

A READER OF REGISTRY VERSION `2` runs the same twenty-four steps, and every finding that `P8` defines has its producing step
here, so that the order of 1.4 is defined for it:

- CONTENT (`R11`), in item order with the other frames: a `keys` frame is judged and applied (`P8`, "The key table: the `keys`
  frame"); a sealed frame is opened by steps 2 to 9 of `P8`, "Opening a sealed frame", whose step 1 is LINKS and PAYLOAD CHECK;
  and the inner frame of an opened sealed frame is then judged as a clear frame of its type at that position. This step produces
  every `malformed_value` that `P8` defines (its rule on the flags of the two types, `P8-6`, excepted: that is the rule of `P1`
  on the registry columns, judged at ITEMS and by no other step; `P8-19` leaves the flags to it and judges the `content` and the
  `storage` of a sealed frame), `segment_key_not_held`, `unwrap_failed`, `key_conflict`, `seal_failed`,
  `exceeds_capacity` with `failed_openings`, and the `exceeds_capacity` and `damaged_frame` of an inner payload.
- LAYOUT (`R11`, the same step): the layout of a `keys` frame (`P8-61`) is judged in the order of `P8-59`, after the rule on the
  `key_id` of its entries and before the rules of its recipient entries, from the envelope fields of the frames of the segment,
  which the wire phase has judged; the layout of a `sealed_members` frame (`P8-62`) is judged when its inner frame is judged, after
  the rules of `P8-43` and before `R19` INDEX compares its claims. Each gives the `malformed_value` of its frame with the reason
  `unordered_or_repeated`, one for the frame (the first rule that it breaks), and a `keys` frame so malformed adds no entry to the
  key table (`P8-13`).
- FOLD STATES (`R12`): a `keys` frame to which no opaque reason applies is `folded` by rule 3 of 1.3 (it gives the segment its
  key entries and the fold nothing); a sealed frame that is opened has the fold state of its inner frame; one that is not opened
  is `opaque` with the reason `P8` gives.
- UNITS AND VALUES (`R14`): the members of every opened sealed pack are in the unit index; its `recipe` and `value` members are
  charged to the total rebuilt length at the position of the sealed frame, in directory order, and their rebuild states stand in
  the `rebuilds` of the sealed frame's result; a sealed frame that is not opened charges nothing; this step produces
  `seal_scope_violated`.
- INDEX (`R19`) produces `index_claim_failed` with `sealed_members`, one for each failure that `P8` names, at the position
  of the `sealed_members` frame whose claim fails, and for a run of sealed packs (`P8-45`) with no `sealed_members` frame directly
  after its last pack, at the position of that last pack (`P8-62`; an absent claim is a failed claim).
- A `malformed_value` that `P8` gives for ONE RECIPIENT ENTRY is a finding of its `keys` frame, one for each such entry, and
  does not make the frame opaque: the rest of the frame is applied (`P8`, "Rules of recipient entries"). The segment's
  integrity is `damaged`, as for every `malformed_value`.
- The capacity step of PAYLOAD CHECK comes before every rule of `P8` on a sealed frame: a sealed frame above the reader's
  capacity is `unchecked` with `exceeds_capacity` and is not judged further. A sealed frame whose inner payload exceeds the
  capacity has the payload state `unchecked` with `exceeds_capacity`. The `decoded_length` of the sealed frame enters the total
  decoded length at the capacity step, and the `decoded_length` of its inner frame enters it when the inner payload is judged, at
  the place of the sealed frame in item order; an inner payload that would bring the total above `decoded_bytes_per_read` has the
  payload state `unchecked` with `exceeds_capacity` and the reason `total_decoded`.
- VISIBILITY: `unopened_sealed` counts every frame of type `sealed` that is not `folded` at the end of the read, at whatever
  step of `P8` its opening ended.
- The reader's `sealing_input` (`P8`, "The reader's key input") holds secrets and is no field of `read_result`. Two results
  of one file under two sealing inputs differ in the outcomes of its sealed frames and are not told apart by `inputs`.
- BEFORE `R1`: the reader judges its `sealing_input` valid or not (`P8`, "The reader's key input"). An input that is not valid
  gives `sealing_input_refused`, with its `reason` and `index`, in place of a `read_result`; no step runs, no finding is
  produced, and the outcome is the same for every file. The twenty-four steps run only on a valid input.

**P7-14.** A reader MUST read a segment of a registry version above its own by 3.6 and MUST NOT call a value malformed for being
unknown in it.

### 3.7 Access: the steps by access

The reader procedure is one procedure, and `access` (section 2) is the one field that selects how a reader obtains the bytes and
so which steps it runs. With the access `whole` every step of 3.1 to 3.6 runs as stated. With the access `forward` the steps run
as the table STEPS BY ACCESS states: a step that the table does not run is not run, and the result says so by the `access` of its
`capacity` and by the typed values of this section (`content_digest` `absent`; `rebuild_state` `not_run`; a canonical level
`unchecked` with the reason `access`; `profile_not_checked`). With the access `sparse` no step of 3.1 to 3.6 runs: the reader runs
the procedure of 3.9 and returns an `answer` in the place of a `read_result` (P7-1). There is no tier, no mode and no other
selector. A `read_result` is a function of the bytes, of the capacity, of the access it carries and of the inputs, and under
`forward` also of the field `end` of the stream point (3.8); an `answer` is a function of the bytes, of the reader's registry
version, of the capacity and of the query, all of which it records (3.9). Each is also a function of a read that failed, when
one did: its offset and the length asked for are inputs of the read, which a `read_result` records by its finding `read_failed` and an
`answer` does not record.

THE SCOPE OF A READER REQUIREMENT. A requirement of any part or of a profile document that a reader run a step, make a check,
report a finding or return a `read_result` applies to a read with the access `whole` as it is written. Under the access `forward`
it applies where the table STEPS BY ACCESS runs the step that applies it, and as that table runs it. Under the access `sparse` the
requirements of 3.9 apply, and another such requirement applies only where the procedure of 3.9 cites the rule that it states,
and as 3.9 applies it. Every requirement of a reader is scoped by this
paragraph, whether or not it cites it.

### 3.8 Reading as a stream

A STREAM READ is the reader procedure run with the access `forward` over the input points in increasing order. An INPUT POINT
`j` is a byte offset at which the reader has received `j` bytes and no more. An ITEM BOUNDARY is an input point at the end of a
complete item. Every prefix of a file that ends at an item boundary is a file (`P1`, "Structure"), and a reader returns a typed
result for a prefix at any byte (`P1`, "Every input has a result"). The END of the input is declared by the caller at an input point; before it a
trailing proper prefix of an item gives no finding, and a caller that sees no further item boundary declares the end to learn
what the bytes are. Bytes that cannot be delimited, the first byte after the item at which the count of `items_per_read` reaches
the limit (2, THE ONE COUNT) and a read that fails (`P1`, "A failed read is not the end of the input") end the read: each is
reported at the input point at which it is met, and no later byte changes the result but the `length` of `undelimitable_input` or
of that `exceeds_capacity` (THE STREAM POINT), and, for bytes that cannot be delimited, the acceptance of a resync candidate in
their run (`P1`, "Finding a resync item where framing is lost"): bytes that cannot be delimited end the read only when no
candidate
is accepted in their run. Two judgements that a read of a prefix as a file makes of a frame depend on the item after the frame,
and
a frame for which one of them stands is PENDING:
an ordinary frame whose link does not hold, that has a stored identifier of the length of the segment's digest and a computed
identifier, so that the item after it can heal it (`P1`, "Healing"), while the item after it is not complete. The item after it
is its only witness: a frame, a transparent frame included, whose link holds against the computed identifier heals it, and any
other
item (an opaque item that is not a frame, bytes that cannot be delimited, an incomplete item, an opener or the end of the
input) leaves it `broken`. The pending frame is decided when that item is complete, when the bytes after the frame are found to
be bytes that cannot be delimited, or when the read fails, and at the declared end the result states it as the prefix read as a
file does; a frame at which the count of `items_per_read` reaches the limit has no witness and is `broken`. And, under registry
version `2`, a `keys`
frame that `R11` CONTENT judges (one with no finding of `R4` ITEMS and not `stopped`), whose link state is `linked` or `healed`, whose payload state is `verified`, that holds exactly one key entry and to which no rule of `P8-59` before the
layout gives a `malformed_value`, when no item of its segment other than a transparent frame and no opener follows it, since
its layout depends
on that item (`P8-61`), which is decided when such an item or an opener arrives. The declared end decides no pending frame in the
listing: with `end` `true` the result is that of the bytes read as a file and the listing is that of `b`. Every
other frame is decided at its own end, but for a frame that fails the framing test (`P1`, "Finding a resync item where framing is
lost"), which is decided with the scan of its run (THE ARRIVAL LISTING).

A concatenation of files is read as `P1`, "Growing a file", states, and no step of this section depends on it.

THE STREAM POINT. The value of a stream read at an input point `j` is a STREAM POINT:

```text
stream_point = [ end: bool, result: read_result, listing: list(arrival_item) ]
```

`end` is `true` exactly when the caller has declared the end of the input at `j`. Let `b` be the last item boundary at or before
`j` that the reader establishes. With `end` `true`, `result` is the result that 3.7 gives for the access `forward` to the first
`j` bytes read as a file. With `end` `false`: when the bytes after `b` cannot be delimited whatever bytes follow them (`P1`,
"Undelimitable input"), when `b` ends the item `items_per_read` and a byte follows it, or when a read of the input fails at `j`
(`P1`, "A failed read is not the end of the input"), `result` is the result that 3.7 gives for the access `forward` to the first
`j` bytes read as a file, the failed read included; otherwise, when `b` is `0`, it is `file_result` tag `second_generation` with
no segment and no finding, `content_digest` `absent` and `visible` `[1, 0]`, and when `b` is above `0`, the result that 3.7 gives
for the access `forward` to the first `b` bytes read as a file. A read that fails ends the stream read: no later input point
exists, and no end is declared at the point where it failed. When a resync candidate is accepted in the run of bytes that cannot
be delimited, or of an item at which framing is lost, the result at the point at which the accepted item is complete and at every
later point is that of the longer prefix read as a file, which holds the accepted item and reports the bytes before it as `P1`,
"Finding a resync item where framing is lost", states. So `torn_tail` stands only in a result whose `end` is `true`
or, for the bytes before an accepted resync item, from the point at which that item is accepted, `undelimitable_input` and the
`exceeds_capacity` of `items_per_read` stand from the point at which they are met, the findings of `R1` stand from the first
item boundary, a pending
last ordinary frame is `broken`, and a pending `keys` frame has the `malformed_value` that `P8-61` gives a `keys` frame that no
item follows, each with the findings and the fold state of the prefix read as a file, in every result until it is decided. For an
input that the generation dispatch gives to the first generation, every stream point with `end` `false` at which no read has failed has the result given above
for `b` equal to `0` and an empty listing, the stream point at which a read fails has the result of the access `whole` for the
bytes received and that failed read, but for `capacity.access`, and an empty listing, and the stream point with `end` `true` has the result of the access `whole` but for
`capacity.access` and an empty listing. `listing` is the arrival listing at `b`, whatever the value of `end`. A reader hands the
stream point over incrementally: at each point it hands over what the point adds to the listing and the fields in which its result
differs from the result of the point before (RESULT FIELDS OF A STREAM READ). The stream point is the value that the caller holds after
it has applied the hand-overs up to the point: its form of delivery is that of the reading interface (section 1), it has no bytes of
this format, and the vectors state stream points. What
the caller keeps of earlier hand-overs is the caller's; the reader holds none of it beyond THE MEMORY OF A FORWARD READER.

CONTINUING A STREAM READ. The stream point at an input point is a function of the bytes before it, of the capacity, of the inputs,
of `end` and of a read that failed alone (P7-58). A reader that stops at an item boundary, keeps its own state and continues when more bytes arrive
gives the stream points of the stream read of the longer input; how it keeps its state is its own, and no record of this format
holds it. A stream point with `end` `true` states what the bytes received are as a file and is no event of the stream read: a
reader that continues after it gives at every later input point the stream point of the longer input, and what a point with `end`
`true` states holds for that point alone.

EACH STEP AS SOON AS ITS INPUTS EXIST. A step is computed when its inputs have arrived and every list of findings is sorted by the
key of 1.4 at each input point, which that key allows because it is a function of the findings (1.4, ORDER). The inputs of the
steps are: `R1` to `R4`, `R6`, `R7`: the item; `R5`: the frame and the one item after it; `R8`: the span's bytes, held while
the span holds fewer than `262144` bytes and then digested in shards of `4096` bytes (`P5`, "Shards, stripes and parity shards",
step 0), and the parity frame, at which the shard digests are compared; `R10`: the used parity frames; `R11`, `R12`: the frame,
the dictionary of its segment and the earlier frames, once the link state of the frame is `linked` or `healed`, and for a `keys` frame the next item of
its segment that is not a transparent frame or the next opener; `R13`: the range stack and the commit;
`R16`: the dictionary and the VALUE TABLE; `R19`: the counters and the range stack; `R20`: the frame and the keys; `R21`, `R23`,
`R24`: as 3.7 states.

THE ARRIVAL LISTING at an item boundary `b` is the sequence of the arrival items of the frames DECIDED at or before `b`, in ORDER
OF DELIVERY: the items of a frame are delivered at the item boundary at which it is decided, which is its own end unless it is
pending or fails the framing test; a frame that fails the framing test (`P1`, "Finding a resync item where framing is lost"), the
items after it and a frame it witnessed are delivered at the decision point of the run of the item at which framing is lost, and
when a candidate is accepted in that run they are no items of the result and deliver nothing, except that a frame it witnessed is
delivered `broken`; the frames decided at one item boundary deliver in ascending segment ordinal, then position; and the items of
one frame are its item of kind `frame`, then its other items in ascending `index` and, for one `index`, in ascending code of
`arrival_kind`.
The items of a pending frame are therefore delivered after the items of every frame decided before it is, and the listing is in
order of delivery, which this part does not promise to be the order of position: every item carries its segment and its position. ARRIVAL ORDER is this order of delivery. A frame still pending at the last boundary has no item in any listing; its state is in the result. Each item is an
ARRIVAL ITEM:

```text
arrival_item = [ kind: enum(arrival_kind), segment: uint, position: uint, index: uint, status: enum(arrival_status),
                 finality: finality ]
finality     = [1]                                      ; settled
             / [2, open: list(enum(open_kind))]         ; open: one or more kinds, ascending, without repeats
```

`arrival_kind`, `arrival_status`, `open_kind` and the variant `finality` are those of `P2`, "Enumerations of the typed result".
Each arrival item is delivered with its VALUE, which stands beside the record and is not a field of it, as the content of a whole
read stands beside the `read_result` (section 1): a typed value of the reading interface, named here by its type, to which this
part gives no bytes.

The items of a frame: one item of kind `frame`, `index` `0`, for every ordinary frame that has an effective identifier (`P1`, "The
predecessor and transparent frames"), whatever its link state and its fold state, its value that effective identifier; a
transparent frame and a frame with no effective identifier give none. A frame that was PENDING and is decided `broken` gives
that one item and nothing else of it is delivered, unless a resync item is accepted in its run, when the frame is no item of
the result and gives none; the item is delivered when the frame is (`P1`, "Finding a resync item where
framing is lost": at the decision point of the run that holds it, when it is the item at which framing is lost). A frame whose link state is `linked` or `healed` and whose fold state is `folded`
gives one item for each term, row, binding, suppression entry, declaration and verified unit of the frame, `index` its zero-based
index in its list of the frame (a member of a pack by its index in the directory; the items of the inner frame of an opened sealed
frame at the position of the sealed frame, with no item of kind `frame` for the inner frame), a `blob` frame or a `blob` member
giving one item of kind `declaration` and, when its unit is verified (`P4`, P4-5 and P4-24), one of kind `unit` with the same
`index`. An `index` frame that is checked (`P5`, "Checking an index") gives, whatever its fold state, one item of kind
`index_claim` for each claim that 3.7 does not compare under `forward`, `index` its code in the enumeration `index_claim`, status
`unverified`. A frame gives no other item. The values are: of a term, its id and the term (`P3`, "The term model"), and of a term of tag `5`
or `7`, the digest, the length and the form of its value (`P3`, "`literal_ref`: a lexical form held by a `value` frame"); of a row
that is folded or of status `by_reference`, its typed line (`P3`, "Typed lines"), and of a row that is held or dropped, its graph
id (`absent` for the default graph) and the values of its columns in column order (`P3`, "Columns and their fixed codings"); of a
binding or an entry, the binding or entry as `P3`, "Typed references: the `bindings` frame" and "Suppression hides", type it; of a
declaration, the frame type of its frame or the member kind of its member (`P4`, "The pack") with its `content` record or its
record in the directory; of a unit, its `unit_digest` and its bytes (`P4`); of a claim, its value (`P5`, "The `index` frame").

`status` is decided from the frame, the dictionary and the value table alone: `folded` (the rules of its frame make it an item of the fold, before what other items do to its effect);
`held` or `dropped` (a row by `P3`, "What happens to a row", a binding by `P3`, "Typed references: the `bindings`
frame", an entry by `P3`, "Suppression hides", "The wire record"; a dropped row is delivered so that a consumer can match the findings to it); `unreadable` (a term by `P3`, "Unreadable
terms", item 1 but for the octets of a value and items 2 to 4, or by the rule that a value serves only what stands after it);
`by_reference` (a term of tag `5` or `7` that those items of `P3`, "Unreadable terms", do not make unreadable and for which a
declaration standing before its `terms` frame provides its pair, a term of
kind `triple` that is not `unreadable` and that names such a term at any depth, and a row, binding or entry that names such a term,
directly or inside a `triple` at any depth, and would otherwise be `folded`: the value is named by its digest and length and its rebuild is not checked, so the item is
in the fold when the value rebuilds, and for a `literal_ref` term when its octets are valid `UTF-8`, and is held with
`opaque_term` otherwise; the consumer reads the value from the arrival items of kind `declaration` and `unit`, or reads the file
with the access `whole`); `unverified` (a claim of an index that the stream did not compare: a claim and nothing more). An item of
kind `frame`, `unit` or `declaration` has the status `folded`, which for an item of kind `frame` says only that it is delivered:
the link state and the fold state of its frame are those of the frame in the result, which its segment and position name.

THE LISTING IS APPEND-ONLY. For item boundaries `j <= k`, the listing at `j` is a prefix of the listing at `k`, and an item is never
changed. What a later item does to the effect of an earlier one (hiding, a count, the removal of an `empty_graph` line, the
smallest length of a blob, the naming of a leaf, the resolution of a referent) is applied by the consumer over the listing, by the
rules of `P3` and `P4` that the table below names, whatever the order of arrival.

THE FINALITY OF AN ARRIVAL ITEM is a total function of its kind, its status, the type of its frame or, for a member of a pack, the kind of the member, and, for a binding or an entry, whether it holds a leaf of tag `2`, in the table below. SETTLED: no later item changes
the effect of the item. OPEN: a later item can change its effect, in the stated direction only, and the item carries the kinds
that apply.

| item | finality | later items, and direction |
|---|---|---|
| a `frame` item, a verified unit, a `value` declaration, a claim of an index | settled | none |
| a term (ids by `P3`, "The content record and the ids"; `unreadable` or `by_reference` by the rule that a value serves only what stands after it, `P3`, "`literal_ref`: a lexical form held by a `value` frame") | settled | none; a whole read of the same bytes holds a term of status `by_reference` unreadable, and the items that name it with `opaque_term`, when its value does not rebuild or, for a `literal_ref` term, its octets are not valid `UTF-8` |
| a row of a `quads` or `reifier_bindings` frame | open `hiding` when its status is `folded` or `by_reference`, settled when it is `held` or `dropped` | a `suppress` entry in this or any other segment (`P3`, "The hiding rule": an entry has the same effect whether it stands before or after what it names); visible becomes hidden, never the reverse |
| an annotation row | open `count` and `hiding` when `folded` or `by_reference`, settled otherwise | an annotation row with the same reifier, predicate, object and graph adds to its count (`P3`, "Rows are sets, annotations are counted"); the count only grows |
| an `empty_graphs` row | open `empty_graph` and `hiding` when `folded`, settled otherwise | a line that carries the graph's name, before or after it, removes it (`P3`, "The fold") |
| a binding of status `folded` or `by_reference` | open `hiding` and `referent`, with `name` when it holds a leaf `[frame, label]` | a segment that holds the frame, a `terms` frame that defines the label in its holder, the healing of a last frame (`P3`, "The visible dataset and visibility", WHAT AN APPEND CAN STILL CHANGE), the frame, blob declaration or commit that the referent names; an unnamed leaf becomes named, a referent is resolved |

| a `blob`, `recipe` or `external` declaration | open `blob_entry` and `hiding` | a declaration of the same name (`P4`, "The blob set"): the length of the entry only shrinks, its presence goes from `external` to `present` and never back, and its media types only grow; a `suppress` entry of kind `blob` with that name (`P3`, "The hiding rule") |

The open kinds are the closed list of `P2`, "Enumerations of the typed result": `name`, `referent`, `hiding`, `count`,
`empty_graph`, `blob_entry`.

RESULT FIELDS OF A STREAM READ. The result at a later item boundary is the result of the longer prefix read as a file (THE
STREAM POINT), so any finding or state of the result at an earlier boundary that a longer prefix does not have is withdrawn by
the items that follow, and no rule of this section adds to that or takes from it. A stream read promises only this about what it
hands over: an item of THE ARRIVAL LISTING is never taken back or changed, and its `finality` says what a later item can add to
its effect. Two judgements of one frame are made against the item after it (the definition of PENDING above); a frame for
which one stands is pending, and its items are held back until it is decided, so that no item delivered rests on a judgement that
the next item withdraws.

NOT RUN, AND HOW THE RESULT SAYS SO. The steps that cannot be run in a pass that holds a bounded state are those that the table of
3.7 does not run under `forward`, and each is stated by the `access` of the capacity: the labelling and so the content digest,
the canonical order, the canonical check and the digest claims of an index (they need the whole labelled set); the comparison
of `type_runs`, `graph_directory`, `pack_members` and of the claims of a `sealed_members` frame (they need a store that grows with
the frames); the rebuild of declarations (units can stand anywhere before the declaration, so their bytes would be held), and with it the
check of `P3`, P3-89, for a literal one of whose two definitions is a term of status `by_reference`;
`unreferenced_unit` and `conflicting_declaration` (they need every declaration of the segment or the file); the step RESUME of
recovery and the writing of repairs (they need bytes that have passed); the comparison of the digests of a span that an item of
more than `stored_bytes_per_frame` bytes reaches (STEPS BY ACCESS, `R8`: the digests of every span that the item crosses would be
held); the matching
of suppression entries, the anchoring of bindings and the resolution of referents (set membership over the whole fold). A
consumer that needs one of them reads the file once with the access `whole`, or assembles the fold from the arrival listing and
runs `L1` itself. A stream read does not resume at a parity frame after an item in flight whose declared length is never reached
(a damaged length head in a file that keeps growing): a resync item that arrives is found and accepted (KEPT PREFIXES below), and
otherwise the read gives no further item and no finding until the end is declared, where a read with `whole` resumes at the next
parity frame (`P5`).

THE MEMORY OF A FORWARD READER. A forward reader holds at most:

- the ITEM IN PROGRESS: of a frame, its stored and decoded bytes, at most `stored_bytes_per_frame` plus `decoded_bytes_per_frame`,
  its `content` record and its `signatures` list, of the lengths that the item gives them, which no capacity bounds,
  and under registry version `2` for a sealed frame also its inner payload, at most a further `decoded_bytes_per_frame`;
  of an item that is not a frame, its heads as they are walked and no content, its walk holding one count of the elements still
  to be walked for its definite-length arrays and maps, which does not grow with their depth, and one such count for each
  indefinite-length item open in it (`P1`, "Primitive types", NESTING HAS NO LIMIT), at most the number of its bytes received (a header is judged head by head and hashed as it
  passes, `P1`, "The header"; the lengths of an `offsets` item are compared one by one with the LENGTH LIST as they pass; an opaque
  item is walked);
- the LINK STATE of the reader: `h0`, the effective identifier of the last ordinary frame, the stored and computed identifiers of a pending
  frame, the range stack of at most `floor(log2(n)) + 1` entries (`P5`, "Leaves, nodes and root"), the head and the `ordinal` of the
  lineage of the previous segment, or that its opener is not readable (for `lineage`, 3.2), the offset of the last `index`
  frame of the segment (for the check of its locator) and
  the counters;
- the SPAN STATE: the bytes of the open span while it holds fewer than `262144` bytes, at most `262143`; from then on the
  `16`-byte digests of its data shards, at most `1024`, and one shard in progress of `4096` bytes; the digests of each WAITING span,
  a span whose end the last non-transparent item has reached and whose parity frame has not arrived, at most
  `ceil(stored_bytes_per_frame / 4194304) + 1` spans; and one bit for each data shard of the open and the waiting spans, set when
  an item that does not pass overlaps it (`P5`, "Checking a parity frame"). The digests of a waiting span are discarded when a
  non-transparent item has passed after the item that reached its end, since a parity frame of that span that stands after that
  item fails the placement (`P5`, "Checking a parity frame", STAGE 2), and no digest is kept for a span that an item of more than
  `stored_bytes_per_frame` bytes reaches (STEPS BY ACCESS, `R8`);
- the PENDING FRAME: the stored and decoded bytes of the pending frame, at most `stored_bytes_per_frame` plus
  `decoded_bytes_per_frame`, its `content` record and its `signatures` list, and under registry version `2` for a sealed frame
  also its inner payload, at most a further `decoded_bytes_per_frame`, held until it is decided;
- the KEPT PREFIXES and the matcher state of the search for a resync item (`P1`, "Finding a resync item where framing is lost"):
  the state of the matcher of the scan pattern, one state for each pattern byte, and the KEPT PREFIX of each of three items, the
  item before the last complete item, the last complete item and the item in flight, which is the first `stored_bytes_per_frame +
  resync_max` bytes of its encoding as received, all of it when it is shorter (an item of at most that length is KEPT WHOLE); once
  framing is lost, the item before the lost item, the lost item and the bytes after it up to the decision point of its run, with
  the results of the items held after the lost item until the scan of its run is decided, and the running hash of the past
  (`opener.past`). TWO BOUNDS, never added: the bytes kept are at most `3 stored_bytes_per_frame + 3 resync_max` bytes, beyond the
  memory that this list states for a read without a resync item; and the number of items held undelivered, each with the result of
  one item, is at most `resync_max`, since each begins at its own offset within the `resync_max` bytes after the lost item, a
  count of results and not of bytes. A held item longer than its kept prefix is not kept whole: its payload is
  checked as it arrives, its result is one record, and its charge waits for its place in the order of charges (`P1`, "Finding a
  resync item where framing is lost", THE ORDER); this work is bounded for each held item by `decoded_bytes_per_frame`. A
  candidate is tested under `forward` only when the reader keeps every byte from the candidate to the end of the lost item and the
  `resync_max` bytes after it, namely when the item in which the candidate begins and every item from it to the lost item are kept
  whole; a candidate in a run that holds a longer item is not tested (FIELDS BY ACCESS, 3.7);
- the count of folded `suppress` frames that no signature covers yet, for `unauthenticated_suppress`;
- the DICTIONARY of the current segment: for each term id its typed term, and whether the dictionary is in doubt (`P3`,
  "Unreadable terms", THE DICTIONARY IN DOUBT), which the item that puts it in doubt decides at its arrival, so that nothing
  delivered before that item changes; emptied at the next opener, since no step of a forward
  read uses a term of another segment; its size is bounded by `decoded_bytes_per_read` plus `formed_bytes_per_read`;
- the LAST ROWS: for each of the types `quads`, `reifier_bindings` and `annotations`, the last row of the nearest earlier frame of
  that type in the current segment and whether that frame declares `canonical` and is decoded (`P3`, "The declared order of
  rows"), emptied at the next opener;
- the VALUE TABLE: the pair of digest and length of every `value` declaration of a folded frame that has been decided, and the pairs for which
  `missing_dependency` with `value` has been reported (`P3`, "`literal_ref`: a lexical form held by a `value` frame": once for
  each pair), a reported pair staying reported; its size is bounded by `items_per_read` pairs for `value` frames and by
  `decoded_bytes_per_read` for `value` members of packs and for the reported pairs;
- the LENGTH LIST: the encoded length of each item of the current segment, in item order, for the check of an `offsets` item
  (`P1`, "The `offsets` item"), emptied at the next opener; it holds at most `items_per_read` lengths;
- the SPAN LIST: the `first` of each frame of type `parity` judged in the current segment, for condition 6 of `P5`, "Checking a
  parity frame", STAGE 1, emptied at the next opener; it holds at most `items_per_read` values;
- under registry version `2`, the KEY TABLE of the current segment (`P8`, "The key table of a segment"), with, under each of its entries, the count of failed
  openings (`max_failed_openings`), the position of the first sealed frame and the number of sealed frames of the segment (`P8`, "Resolving the segment key of a key entry", NONE), and the KEY INDEX SET, the `key_index` of every sealed frame of the
  segment that is at or above the number of entries of the table (`P8-61`), both emptied at the next opener; the table holds at
  most `items_per_read` entries and the set at most `items_per_read` values;
- `O(1)` counters.

The dictionary, the value table, the length list, the span list, the key table and the key index set are KEYED STORES: ids are fixed at definition by
position (`P3`, "The content record and the ids"), pairs, values, key identifiers and key indexes are compared whole and lengths are taken by
their index, so a lookup is by key and where a store is
kept changes no result; a reader MAY keep any of them outside memory. The reader holds no row but the last rows above, and no binding, entry, line, fold, labelled
set, suppression record, blob or unit bytes, graph state, parity payload or `frame_result` other than the item in progress and the
state above; what it has handed over is the caller's. The state that grows with the input is the keyed stores, the range stack by the
logarithm of the number of frames, and the counts of the indefinite-length items open in the item in progress. This memory does not bound a read of an input that the generation dispatch gives to the first
generation: that reader's memory is its own.

**P7-57.** A reader with the access `forward` MUST read each byte of the input once and in order, and MUST NOT seek or read a byte

### 3.9 Reading by range

A READ BY RANGE is a read with the access `sparse` of a file that can be read by byte ranges. It reaches the content of the
frames that the index that a locator names and the `offsets` item before that locator place in one segment, without reading the
rest of the file, and returns an ANSWER. It judges what it reads by the rules of a whole read, cited below by part and section title, and
states no rule of its own for a term, a row, an entry or a unit: what differs is which frames it reads, which steps it does not
run and what its answer says. The fact on which it rests is that the identifier of a frame commits to `payload_digest` and not to
the payload (`P1`, "What the identifier commits to"), so that a frame read alone is verified against its own identifier. The
reads of one answer are reads of one byte string: an answer is a function of that byte string, of the reader's registry version,
of the capacity, of the query and of a read that failed (its offset and the length asked for), and a holder that lets the file change between the reads of one answer has no answer that this
part defines. A read by range opens no sealed frame and takes no `sealing_input`. It serves a segment whose covered frames the rule
of step PLACE places: every canonical segment; a segment that holds no item among its covered frames but those frames; and a
segment that holds, among its covered frames, exactly the parity frames of its full spans, each at its place. A holder reads any other segment with the access `whole`; the procedure gives the reason
`placement` where the count of step PLACE, or the type of a head read at a place that it computed, shows that the items of a
segment are not laid out by that rule.

TERMS OF THE PROCEDURE. The HEAD of a frame is its bytes from its first byte to the last byte of the head of its `payload`
(elements `0` to `7` and the head of element `8`, `P1`, "The record, in identity order"); it holds the `content` record. The TAIL
of a frame is its bytes after the last byte of its `payload`, by the declared length of the `payload`: the `identifier` and the
`signatures` list. Where the procedure or its walk needs the declared length of a `payload` and element `8` of the item is not a `bytes`
item, the outcome is `unknown` with the reason `payload`, at that read, and the charges of that read are those of the bytes read up to
it. A head is read by walking its CBOR heads (`P1`, "Framing: torn, damaged and undelimitable input"); a walk skips the content of a
byte or text string by its declared length unless the step says that it reads it (CHARGES). A tail is
read by walking its heads at step INDEX, and after step OFFSETS as one range, which ends at the end of its item that the `offsets`
item gives. An
item's OFFSET in the procedure is the offset that the `offsets` item gives it (step OFFSETS); it is a claim of that item. A run of
bytes that the procedure has read is kept, and is not read and not charged again. NO READ PASSES THE END OF ITS ITEM: every read
of a covered frame, whole or of its head alone, is made inside the range that the `offsets` item gives that item, and a head or a
`payload` that declares more bytes than remain in that range is not followed: the outcome is `unknown` with the reason `payload`,
at that read, and nothing beyond the range of the item is read or charged. The item before a frame (READING A FRAME, item 1) and
the index (step INDEX) are bounded in the same way, with the reasons stated there.

READING A FRAME. A covered frame at an offset `x`, of length `l`, which is item `i` of the segment (step OFFSETS), is read whole in
this order.

1. THE ITEM BEFORE IT, item `i - 1` at an offset `p`, when it has not been read. Item `0` is the opener of the segment, read at step HEADER. Of
   any other item the reader reads its first CBOR head; when that head is not the head of an array of `11` elements in its shortest form, check 1 fails
   with nothing more read. Otherwise the reader reads the head of that item by walking its CBOR heads from `p`, reading the content of no byte
   or text string but the `anchor` of a frame of type `parity`; when that walk reaches `x` before the head ends or meets bytes
   that cannot be delimited (`P1`, "Undelimitable input"), or the end of its `payload` by the declared length is above `x`, check 1 fails with nothing
   more read; otherwise the reader reads the tail of that item, the bytes from the end of its `payload` to `x`, and makes check 1.
2. THE HEAD OF THE FRAME, when it has not been read, and the CHECK OF THE FRAME TYPE (`P1`, "The locator", A FRAME ITEM HAS A TYPE
   ONLY BY A VALUE): the reader walks the head from `x`. When the item is not an array of `11` elements, its head in its shortest form, whose element `0` is the
   `uint` `2` in its one form, or its element `1` is not a `uint` in its one form, no frame stands at `x` and the reason is `placement`, with
   nothing more read. Otherwise, when its element `1` is a value that is not a frame type of the reader's registry version, the
   reason is `frame_type`; otherwise, when the frame is not of the type that `type_runs` names for it, the reason is `placement`.
   Then check 2 is made on elements `0` to `7`, before the payload is charged.
3. Its payload and its tail; then check 2 on elements `8` to `10` and on the length `l`, then checks 3 and 4, in that order.

A step that reads the head of a frame alone makes 2 above and nothing else.

LOCAL VERIFICATION OF A FRAME. A frame read whole is VERIFIED LOCALLY when every check below holds:

1. THE ITEM BEFORE IT. The item at `p` is a complete item that ends at `x` and is: the opener of the segment, a header or a resync item, whose predecessor
   value is its `h0`; a frame of type `parity` whose `transparent` is `true`, whose predecessor value is the `anchor` of its
   `content` record (`P5`, "The `parity` frame"); or an ordinary frame (`P1`, "The predecessor and transparent frames") whose
   stored `identifier` has the length of the segment's digest, whose predecessor value is that stored `identifier`. Any other item
   fails the check with the reason `identifier`.
2. THE ENVELOPE. The item at `x` is a complete item of `l` bytes to which a whole read gives no finding at `R4` ITEMS (3.2) as a
   frame: the envelope test, the field rules in their order and the rules of its type that read the fields of the envelope
   alone, judged as READING A FRAME orders them. A failure has the reason `payload`.
3. THE IDENTIFIER. The identifier that `P1`, "Every preimage, typed", computes from elements `1` to `6` of the frame and the
   predecessor value of check 1 equals its stored `identifier`; otherwise the reason is `identifier`. No frame is healed: healing
   needs the frame after it (`P1`, "Healing").
4. THE PAYLOAD. The frame is judged by `R6` STORED CHECK and `R7` PAYLOAD CHECK (3.2: the stored check, the decode, the
   `decoded_length` and `H_ctx(payload, decoded)` against `payload_digest`) and the rules that `R11` CONTENT (3.3) applies to its
   content record and its payload and to no other frame, in the order of `P1`, "The record, in identity order", ORDER OF THE
   FIELD CHECKS; a rule that
   reads another frame (the ids of a `terms` frame against the frames before it, the dictionary, the rules of `P3`, "The row frames", that compare ids,
   the resolution of a row) is
   applied at THE DICTIONARY, FETCH or SUPPRESSION. A frame is not verified, with the reason `payload`, exactly when a read of the
   whole file would give it a payload state other than `verified` or a fold state `opaque`, by a rule that needs no other frame. A
   finding that leaves the frame folded (an unreadable term, a row, binding or entry that is dropped or held,
   `declared_order_violated`) does not fail the check: the dictionary and the rows are then what `P3` gives. A stored state
   `mismatch` beside a payload state `verified` does not fail it either: the decoded bytes are the writer's.

Its signatures are not judged.

THE QUERY. A read by range is asked a QUERY, an input of the read that the answer records:

```text
query = [ segment_end: uint, target: query_target, suppression: bool ]
```

`segment_end` is the offset of the end of the segment asked: the length of the file for its last segment, the offset of a
segment's header for the segment before it (an answer states the offset of the header of its segment, so a holder asks for the
segments of a file one after another from the last). `target` is a value of the variant `query_target` of `P2`, "Enumerations of
the typed result": the rows of one graph (`[1, absent]` for the default graph, `[1, present(t)]` for the graph named by the IRI
whose text is `t`, the term `iri(t)` of `P3`, "The term model"), or the unit of one digest (`[2, d]`). A graph named by a blank node (`P3`, "Position rules") is not reached by a read by
range. `suppression` is `true` when the suppression entries of the segment are to be applied to the rows of the answer (step
SUPPRESSION).

THE PROCEDURE. The input is a file that the reader reads by byte ranges, its length `L` and a query. The steps run in order, and
each states the ranges it reads in their order. The procedure STOPS at the first check that fails, in the order in which the steps
make their checks, and the answer is then `unknown` with the reason of that check (`P2`, `range_reason`). Every range is charged
before it is read (CHARGES). A read of a range that fails (`P1`, "A failed read is not the end of the input") has the reason
`read`, at that read.

1. LOCATE. When `segment_end` is below `11` or above `L`, the reason is `locator`, and nothing is read. The reader reads the `11` bytes before `segment_end` and
   applies steps `1` to `3` of `P1`, "The locator", RECOGNITION, with `segment_end` in the place of the length of the input; a
   failure has the reason `locator`, and otherwise `q = p - d` is the offset of the index that the locator names.
2. INDEX. The reader reads the item at `q` whole, its head first: it has to be a complete envelope of the frame type `index` (`P1`,
   "The locator", A FRAME ITEM HAS A TYPE ONLY BY A VALUE) that ends at or before `segment_end - 11`. It then walks the items from
   the end of the index to `segment_end - 11`, reading of each its heads and, for a frame, its head and its tail: the walk has to end
   exactly at `segment_end - 11`, and its last item has to be an array whose element `0` is the `uint` `4` in its one form, which the reader reads whole. A failure
   has the reason `index_item`, but for an element `8` that is not a `bytes` item, which has the reason `payload` (TERMS OF THE
   PROCEDURE).
3. OFFSETS. In this order: the `offsets` item is in its grammar (`P1`, "The `offsets` item"); every element of its
   `item_lengths`, `l_0` to `l_(n - 1)`, is at least `1`; `n + 1` is judged against `items_per_read` (CHARGES); and the
   `item_lengths` give the offset of the header `h`, the offset of the `offsets` item less their sum, which is at least `0`; the offset of item `i` is `h`
   plus the sum of `l_0` to `l_(i - 1)`. The offset `q` is the offset of an item `i_q`, and the lengths of the items from `i_q` to
   `n - 1` are those that step INDEX walked. A failure has the reason `offsets`, and a refusal by `items_per_read` the reason
   `capacity`.
4. HEADER. The reader reads the item at `h` of `l_0` bytes: the opener of the segment, a header or a resync item of
   generation `2` whose `registry_version` is at most the reader's, whose `hash_algorithm` the reader holds, and whose outcome
   is `readable` (`P1`, "The header" and "The resync item"); it computes `h0` by the rule of its kind. The `offset`, the
   `past` and the marks of a resync item are not judged, and no mark is honoured. A failure has the reason `header`. Vector
   `idx-resync-opener` (a closed segment that a resync item opens, after a header segment: a query of a graph of the
   second segment is `answered` with the rows that the whole read gives, `scope` names the offset of the resync item).
5. QUERY. A target digest has the length of the digest of the header's `hash_algorithm`; otherwise the reason is `query`.
6. THE INDEX VERIFIED. The reader reads the item `i_q - 1` before the index as step 1 of READING A FRAME states, makes check 1
   for the index and then checks 2 to 4 on the index
   (its head was read and its frame type judged at step INDEX), so that the index is verified locally and its payload is in the
   grammar of `P5`, "The `index` frame". The checks that follow are made in this order. Its `type_runs` meet the three properties that table states (each `run` at least
   `1`, no two consecutive entries of one type, the runs adding up to `frame_count`), and its `graph_directory` and `pack_members`
   have the properties that table states of each list in itself: the entries of `graph_directory` in the order of their graphs
   with no graph twice, and in each entry ranges whose `frame_type` is `quads`, `reifier_bindings`, `annotations` or
   `empty_graphs` and whose `row_count` is at least `1`, in strictly ascending order of `first_frame` and then `first_row`; the
   entries of `pack_members` in their order with no entry twice. Otherwise the reason is `index_claims`. When
   `type_runs` names a frame of type `sealed` or `keys` (`P8`), the reason is `sealed`, which only a reader of registry version `2` reaches (for a reader of version `1` those types
   are values outside its registry, and the index fails check 4 with `payload`): sealed content is read with the access
   `whole` or `forward`.
7. PLACE. The COVERED FRAMES are the `frame_count` frames that `type_runs` names, of ordinary positions `0` to `frame_count - 1`,
   each of the type that its run names. They are placed among the items `1` to `i_q - 1`: when `i_q - 1` equals `frame_count`, the
   frame of ordinary position `k` is item `k + 1`; otherwise the reader takes the items `1` to `i_q - 1` in order with an S-position
   `s` that starts at `l_0` and a span end `e` that starts at `4194304`, and takes item `j` as the parity frame of the span that ends
   at `e` when `s` is at least `e`, `e` then growing by `4194304`, and otherwise as the next covered frame, `s` then growing by `l_j`:
   the placement of `P5`, "The stream and the spans". This rule and no other places the covered frames. When the items taken as covered
   frames are not `frame_count` in number, the reason is `placement`. No byte is read by this step. The item before the first covered frame is the header read at step HEADER, so
   that frame is verified against the `h0` of that header.
8. THE DICTIONARY. For the target `graph`, the reader reads every covered `terms` frame whole, in item order, by READING A FRAME,
   verifies each locally, and forms the DICTIONARY of the segment over them as a whole read forms it, by `P3`, "The `terms` frame"
   (the expected bases and the ids, front coding, duplicate definitions and unreadable terms), with no value rebuilt: item 5 of
   `P3`, "Unreadable terms", and the octets of a value in its item 1 are not judged, and a term of tag `5` or `7` (`literal_ref`,
   `literal_value_ref`) that no other item of that list makes unreadable is BY REFERENCE: it is named by its digest, its length and
   its form (`P3`, "`literal_ref`: a lexical form held by a `value` frame"), and its value is not read. A covered `terms` frame that the rules of `P3`, "The `terms` frame", applied over the covered `terms` frames before it, make
   opaque (an `id_base` other than the expected base) has the reason `payload`, given when that frame has been verified. For the
   target `unit` no `terms` frame is read.
9. THE FRAMES OF THE TARGET.
   - For the target `graph`, the reader keeps the entry of the default graph of `graph_directory` for the default graph, and for
     a named graph every entry whose graph id names in the dictionary the term `iri(t)` of the target's text `t` (`P3`, "The term
     model" and "Duplicate definitions"). The FRAMES OF THE TARGET are, for each range of the entries kept, the covered frames that the range
     names (`P5`, "The `index` frame": a range continues into the next covered frame of its type), in item order.
   - For the target `unit`, the reader reads the head of every covered `chunk` and `blob` frame, in item order, by READING A FRAME, with the
     content of the strings of its `content` record,
     and the FRAMES OF THE TARGET are those whose `unit_digest` is the digest of the target and the `pack` frames that the entries
     of `pack_members` with that digest name, by `ordinary_position`.
   An entry kept or used that names an ordinary position at or above `frame_count`, a range of `graph_directory` whose frame
   `type_runs` gives another type than the range's `frame_type`, or an entry of `pack_members` whose frame `type_runs` does not
   give the type `pack`, has the reason `index_claims`, given before any head of this step is read.
10. FETCH. The reader reads each frame of the target whole, in ascending item order, by READING A FRAME, and verifies it locally;
    whether a covered frame belongs to a range is decided by the frames of that range before it, which stand earlier in item order
    and are read first.
    - For the target `graph`, the rows of a fetched frame are judged over the dictionary by `P3`, "The row frames" and "From ids to
      typed lines", whole, the ids defined at the frame being those that the covered `terms` frames before it reserve; a frame that
      those rules make malformed has the reason `payload`, given when that frame has been verified. The rows of the frame
      whose graph is the target (by its own `graph_runs`, `P3`, "Columns and their fixed codings", or for an `empty_graphs` frame by
      its column; for a named graph, an id that names that term in the dictionary, whether or not it is defined at the frame) are exactly the rows that the ranges kept assign to that frame.
      When the rows of a range run past the last covered frame of its type, when the `first_row` of a range is at or above the
      number of rows of its frame, or when two ranges kept assign one row, the reason is `contradiction`, given when that frame has
      been verified. A frame that those rules make malformed and whose rows contradict a range has the reason `payload`.
    - For the target `unit`, the unit is verified by `P4`: the `unit_digest` of a fetched `chunk` or `blob` frame equals
      `H_ctx(unit, payload)` over its decoded payload (`P4`, "Identity", P4-5), and in a fetched `pack` frame the member that the
      entry names is of kind `chunk` or `blob` with the digest of the target, and `H_ctx(unit, body)` equals that digest (`P4`, "The
      pack", P4-24). A unit whose bytes do not hash to its digest has the reason `payload`.
    A frame that does not hold what the claim that named it states has the reason `contradiction`.
11. SUPPRESSION. When `suppression` is `true` and the target is `graph`, the reader reads every covered `suppress` frame whole, in
    item order, by READING A FRAME, verifies each locally, and judges its entries over the dictionary by `P3`, "Suppression hides"
    (P3-47), the ids defined at the frame being those that the covered `terms` frames before it reserve. When those frames hold no
    entry that `P3` neither drops nor holds, every row of the answer is `visible`. Otherwise, when a row of the answer or such an
    entry names a term by reference, directly or inside a `triple` at any depth, the hiding rule cannot be applied without a
    rebuild, and the reason is `value`; and otherwise each row of the answer is `hidden` when an entry of those frames hides
    it by `P3`, "The hiding rule", the entries being resolved as in the segment's own fold (`P3`, "Naming a blank node in a binding
    or an entry"), and `visible` otherwise. When `suppression` is `false`, or the target is `unit`, no `suppress` frame is read and
    the items carry no visibility.
12. ANSWER. The answer is `answered`, with the scope and the items of THE ANSWER.

CHARGES. Every range of bytes that the procedure reads is charged its length in bytes to the total decoded length, judged
against `decoded_bytes_per_read`, before it is read and once: a range that has been read is kept and is not read or charged
again. A payload whose `storage` is `stored_compressed` is charged its `decoded_length` besides, before check 4
decodes it; a payload stored `stored_plain` is charged the bytes of its range and nothing more. Nothing else is charged. The
ranges are those that the steps state: the `11` bytes of the locator, the `l_0` bytes of the header, each CBOR head of an item
that a step walks (its first byte, and then the bytes of its argument as a second range when it has any, each charged before it is read), the content of a byte or text string that a step reads, a tail read as one range, and the head, the payload
and the tail of a frame read whole. In every walk of the procedure (the items at step INDEX, the item before a frame, a head read alone) the content of a
byte or text string is not read and not charged unless the step says that it reads it, and the charge of a walk is the bytes of
the heads that it reads; the head of a frame read whole is read by the same walk of its heads, and the content of each byte or text string of it
is read as one range more.
So the total is a function of the file and the query. A frame read whole is judged against `stored_bytes_per_frame` and
`decoded_bytes_per_frame` by its head before its payload is read. At step OFFSETS, after the check that every element of the `item_lengths` is at least `1`
and before the offsets are computed, `n + 1`, the items that the `offsets` item lists and that item, is judged once against
`items_per_read`; the items of the walk of step INDEX are not counted on their own. Each `terms` frame read is charged to the
total formed length (`P3`, "Front coding") before its texts are formed. A charge that a capacity refuses has the reason
`capacity`, and the range is not read. No rebuild and no labelling runs, so `rebuilt_bytes_per_read` and
`labelled_bytes_per_read` are not applied. Every byte a read by range holds is a byte it read, a payload decoded from bytes it
read or a value computed from them, so its memory is bounded by its charges to `decoded_bytes_per_read` and to
`formed_bytes_per_read`.

THE ANSWER.

```text
answer       = [ registry_version: uint, capacity: capacity, query: query, outcome: enum(answer_outcome),
                 reason: option(enum(range_reason)), scope: option(answer_scope), items: list(answer_item) ]
answer_scope = [ header_offset: uint, index_offset: uint, frame_count: option(uint) ]
answer_item  = [ position: uint, member: option(uint), index: uint, by_reference: bool,
                 visibility: option(enum(item_visibility)) ]
```

`answer_outcome`, `range_reason` and `item_visibility` are those of `P2`, "Enumerations of the typed result". `registry_version`
and `capacity` are filled as P7-2 fills them in a `read_result`. An answer whose
`outcome` is `unknown` has `reason` `present` and no item: nothing of the segment is returned. An answer whose `outcome` is
`answered` has `reason` `absent`. `scope` is `present` exactly when the procedure has passed step HEADER: the offset `h` of the
header of the segment, the offset `q` of the index that the locator names and, when checks 1 to 4 hold for that index, whatever step
THE INDEX VERIFIED then finds in its claims, its `frame_count`, `absent` otherwise; so an answer `unknown` for a later reason still names the header from
which a holder asks for the segment before it. The `items` of an `answered` answer are, for the target `graph`, the rows of the
target's graph in the fetched frames that `P3`, "What happens to a row", neither drops nor holds (in a file whose `offsets` item is
true such a row is no line of a whole read either; it is not returned), and for the target `unit`, the units of the digest in the fetched frames: `position` the
item number `i` that the `offsets` item gives the frame (step OFFSETS), `member` the index of the member in the directory of a `pack`
frame and `absent` otherwise, `index` the zero-based index of the row in its frame and `0` for a unit, `by_reference` `true`
exactly when the row names a term by reference, directly or inside a `triple` at any depth, and `visibility` as step SUPPRESSION
gives it, `absent` when no suppression is applied. Each item is delivered with its VALUE, beside the record as the value of an
arrival item is (3.8, THE ARRIVAL LISTING): of a row, its typed line (`P3`, "Typed lines"), each blank node of the segment as in
the segment's own fold (`P3`, "The fold", WHICH FOLD: the ordinal `0`) and each term by reference as the digest, the length and the
form of its value; of a unit, its bytes. `items` are in ascending order of `position`, then `member` (`absent` first), then `index`.

WHAT AN ANSWER ESTABLISHES, AND WHAT IT DOES NOT. An `answered` answer establishes exactly this: the frames of steps THE DICTIONARY,
FETCH and, when it runs, SUPPRESSION, that the index and the heads read name, were read and each was verified locally; its rows
are the rows that the rules of `P3` cited above neither drop nor hold over the dictionary of the covered `terms` frames that the `offsets` item places, and its units have the
digest asked for. It does NOT establish, and each of these is a claim of the index, of the locator, of the `offsets` item or of
the heads read:

- that a frame read stands in the segment's chain from the header: its predecessor value is the stored identifier, or the
  `anchor`, of the item before it as read, which nothing verifies, and a false `offsets` item can place the procedure on the
  frames of a file held in a payload;
- that the frames fetched stand in one segment: a frame is bound to the item before it and to nothing else, so a false `offsets`
  item can put the covered `terms` frames on one chain and the frames of the target on another (another segment, or a file held in a
  payload), and the rows of one chain are then resolved over the dictionary of the other; an answer over a false `offsets` item can
  hold rows that no whole read gives;
- that the index is the last index of the segment, so that no covered frame stands after it;
- that a row returned is a line of the fold: an `empty_graphs` row is no line when a line of the file carries its graph, equal rows
  are one line, and equal annotation rows are one line whose count is their sum (`P3`, "The fold");
- that no other frame of the segment holds content for the query: rows of the graph in a frame that `graph_directory` omits, a unit
  in a frame whose head or whose pack entry does not name it, a suppression entry in a frame that `type_runs` does not name;
- anything of the frames after the index, of another segment (the suppression entries of another segment included, which can hide
  a row of the answer), of the commits, of the signatures and the authentication of any frame, of the head, of the link states, or
  of a frame not read (a frame that stops the fold of a whole read, `P1`, "Values the reader does not know", included);
- that a value named by reference serves its term and rebuilds: a row that names a term by reference is a line of a whole read
  only when a declaration of its pair stands before the `terms` frame that holds the term (a stream read decides that, a read by
  range does not), the value rebuilds and, for a `literal_ref` term, its octets are valid `UTF-8` (`P3`, "`literal_ref`: a lexical
  form held by a `value` frame", A VALUE SERVES ONLY WHAT STANDS AFTER IT).

A holder that needs any of them established reads the file with the access `whole`.

## 4. The canonical form

### 4.1 Definition and the two claims

The CANONICAL FILE of a fold `F` under `canonical_form_v1` is one segment, standing alone, that the construction of 4.3 gives from
`F` and the constants of 4.2 and from nothing else, except the `signatures` list of its `commit` frame, which is an input that no
check of the canonical check judges for its content, and which is written in the order of P7-37. The header's `profile` value changes the header and what is computed from it (`h0`, the identifier of every frame,
the head, the commit's `root`, the index's `head` and `range_root`) and nothing else: the frames, their order, every `content`
other than those two records and every payload other than the index's are the same whatever profile is declared (section 6). The
fold of a segment for this purpose is its own fold (1.2).

The header's `canonical` field is the HEAD CLAIM and nothing else. It covers the kind and order of every item other than the
parity frames, the `offsets` item and the locator, and of every ordinary frame its `frame_type`, `critical`, `transparent`,
`content`, `decoded_length`, `payload_digest` and `identifier`; hence the head (`P1`, "The predecessor and transparent frames").
It can be falsified by the fold, the constants and the order, and it is what the structure and labelling levels of the canonical
check judge (4.6). No storage rewrite can falsify it, because no identifier depends on a stored byte (`P1`, "What the identifier
commits to").

The STORED BYTES of the segment (the `storage` and `payload` of every frame, the `parity` frames, the `offsets` item and the
locator) are not claimed. A segment whose head claim holds and whose stored bytes are another encoder's is a conforming canonical
file. Whether the stored bytes equal the CANONICAL BYTES, those that steps 13 to 15 of 4.3 give with the reference encoder, is a
property a reader REPORTS (the storage level, 4.4 and 4.6), never a claim of the file, and it produces no finding.

A CANONICAL FILE HOLDS NO RESYNC ITEM. Compaction writes header segments only (4.5), and a resync item, whose marks describe bytes
that the compacted file does not hold, is not carried. A segment opened by a resync item has no `canonical` field and so makes
no canonical claim.

**P7-15.** A writer MUST NOT write `canonical` `present` in a header unless the head claim holds: the segment's items, other than
its stored bytes, are those that 4.3 gives for its own fold under the form it names.

### 4.2 The constants

Every constant of the canonical form, with its value. No writer chooses any of them; a different value is a different
canonical form.

- terms frame budget `Bt`; value: `4194304` bytes of term text. A term counts its text length plus `8`: an `iri` its IRI bytes, a
  `literal` its lexical form bytes (a language text counts nothing), a `literal_value` the length of the deterministic encoding of
  its typed value, a `blank` its label bytes, a `triple`, a `literal_ref` and a `literal_value_ref` `0`. A frame takes terms in
  dictionary order until the next would bring the sum above `Bt`; a term alone above `Bt` forms a frame by itself
- row frame size `Nr`; value: `1048576` rows a frame, and `1048576` entries a `bindings` or `suppress` frame; the last frame of a
  type holds the rest
- restart group `Ng`; value: `64` terms
- literal threshold; value: a literal, typed or language, that VALUES does not hold and whose lexical form has at least `1048576`
  bytes is a `literal_ref` term; every other such literal is a `literal` term
- value threshold; value: a typed literal that VALUES holds with a value of kind `octets` of at least `524288` octets is a
  `literal_value_ref` term; every other typed literal that VALUES holds is a `literal_value` term
- literal order; value: the keys of `P3` "The canonical dictionary": `(lexical form, form, datatype or language)` for a `literal`
  and `(value digest, length, form, datatype or language)` for a `literal_ref`
- front coding; value: on, with the longest common prefix (`P3` "Front coding")
- column codings; value: fixed by `P3` "Columns and their fixed codings" for every file
- blob order; value: ascending blob name, bytewise on `E(name)` (`P4` "The canonical value region and blob region")
- chunker; value: `P4` "The chunker": `min` `16384`, `b` `16`, `max` `262144`, `n` `2`
- pack size `P`; value: `16777216` bytes of body length
- codec; value: `zstd` (`P1` "The zstd constraint"), level `12`, window `2^26`, one zstd frame a frame that begins with the
  generated header `H(D)`, produced by the reference encoder of 4.4
- storage choice; value: a frame is `stored_compressed` exactly when its decoded length is at least `64` and the reference
  encoder's output plus `16` is strictly less than the decoded length; otherwise `stored_plain`. A `parity` frame is always
  `stored_plain` (`P5`)
- stored check; value: `16` bytes on every `stored_compressed` frame (`P1`)
- parity; value: present, `geometry_1`, placed by `P5` "The stream and the spans"
- commit; value: exactly one `commit` frame, after the last frame that contributes to the fold
- index; value: exactly one `index` frame after the commit, with `content_digest` `present`; then the `parity` frame of the final
  span, then the `offsets` item, then the locator
- header; value: `generation` `2`, `registry_version` `1` (the smallest version holding every value used, `P2` "Registry
  versions"), `hash_algorithm` `blake3_256`, `profile` the value compaction is given (4.5), `canonical`
  `present(canonical_form_v1)`, `lineage` `absent`

THE WINDOW. A pack is at most `P` plus one unit of at most `262144` bytes, a terms frame about `Bt` plus one term below the
threshold, a row, bindings or suppression frame about `Nr` entries, so the window covers each whole frame except those of 4.7.

**P7-16.** A writer of a canonical file MUST use the constants of 4.2 and no other value for any of them.

**P7-43.** A writer of a canonical file MUST store a typed literal as a `literal_value` or `literal_value_ref` term exactly when
VALUES of the fold holds it, and MUST decide the storage form of every literal by VALUES and the two thresholds of 4.2 and by no
test of a text.

### 4.3 The construction, from a fold to the item sequence

Input: a fold `F` (`P3`, "The fold"), the bytes of every present blob of `F`, the header's `profile` value, and the `signatures`
list of the commit (empty unless the caller supplies one). Output: the items of the canonical file, in order. Each step is a
function of its inputs; no step reads a file. A WRITER names each blank node of `F` by `E(position)` of its final position in
the run of step 1. The construction that the canonical check forms (4.6, STRUCTURE) names each blank node by its stored label in
place of `E(position)`, a name compared whole as bytes, and every other step is the same.

1. LABELLED SET. Form the labelled set of `F` (`P3` "The labelled set") and run `L1` once on it: the final position of every blank
   node of `F`, the digest of the canonical line sequence (the content digest) and the counts `lines`, `records` and `blank_nodes` for the
   index, as `P5`, "The `index` frame", defines them (no `nested_term` record and no term node counted).
2. DICTIONARY. Form the dictionary of `P3` "The canonical dictionary": its members, the storage form of each literal (`P3`, THE
   STORAGE FORM OF A LITERAL: `literal_value` or `literal_value_ref` for a typed literal that VALUES holds, by the value threshold
   of 4.2; `literal_ref` or `literal` for every other literal, by the literal threshold of 4.2), the blank labels `E(position)`,
   the kind order and the order within each kind; the ids are `0, 1, 2, ...` in that order. A fold holds no `value`
   declaration: in the fold a literal is a literal, whatever form stored it. THE VALUES OF THE CANONICAL FILE of `F` are defined
   here, once: the octets of each `literal_ref` term, which are the UTF-8 bytes of its lexical form, and of each
   `literal_value_ref` term, which are its typed value. The value digest of such a term is `H_ctx(unit, octets)` and its length
   the number of octets (`P4`, "Identity"). The values are ONE value for each distinct octet string among those terms, shared by
   every term that names it; two such literals with equal octets are still two terms when they differ in kind, form, datatype or
   language. No other value exists in the canonical file.
3. VALUE REGION. Chunk every value of the canonical file (`P4`, "The chunker") and emit the members of the value region in their
   order (`P4` "The canonical value region and blob region"); cut them into packs of at most `P` bytes of body length by `P4`'s
   packing rule.
4. BLOB REGION. Chunk every present blob of the blob set and emit the members of the blob region; cut them into packs; then form
   the `external` frames of `P4`, sorted by `E(name)` then `E(media_type)`.
5. TERMS FRAMES. Cut the dictionary into `terms` frames by `Bt` (4.2); in each frame consecutive terms of one kind form one run;
   every frame has `order` `canonical`, `restart_group` `Ng` and `id_base` the number of terms before it (`P3` "The `terms`
   frame").
6. ROW FRAMES. Sort the lines of each kind under their keys and cut them into frames of `Nr` rows (`P3` "The canonical rows"): the
   `quads`, `reifier_bindings`, `annotations` and `empty_graphs` frames, each with one graph run for each graph present in it.
7. BINDINGS AND SUPPRESSION. The `bindings` frames: the bindings of `F` in canonical order (`P3`, "The canonical bindings
   frames"), cut into frames of `Nr` entries, the last holding the rest; none when `F` has no binding. The `suppress` frames: the
   entries of the suppression record in canonical order (`P3`, "The canonical suppression frames"), cut into frames of `Nr`
   entries, the last holding the rest; none when the record is empty.
8. HEADER. The header of 4.2; `h0 = H_ctx(header, E(header))` (`P1`).
9. ORDER. The frames of steps 3 to 7 stand in this order: the value packs, the blob packs, the `external` frames, the `terms`
   frames, the `quads`, then `reifier_bindings`, then `annotations`, then `empty_graphs` frames, the `bindings` frames, the
   `suppress` frames. A kind with nothing to hold has no frame. So every value pack stands before the `terms` frame that names its
   value, and, within a region, every unit before the `recipe` or `value` member that names it (`P3`, "`literal_ref`: a lexical form
   held by a `value` frame"; `P4`, "The unit index and the unit used"), as the rule that a value or a unit serves only what stands
   after it requires.
10. ENVELOPES. For each frame in order, its `content` record (`P1`, "The content record of every frame type of registry version
    1"), its decoded payload, `decoded_length`, `payload_digest`, predecessor and `identifier` (`P1` "Every preimage, typed"),
    from `h0` along the chain.
11. COMMIT. One `commit` frame with `count` the number of ordinary frames before it and `root` the range root over `h0` and their
    identifiers (`P5` "The `commit` frame and the committed prefix"), its envelope by step 10, and the given `signatures` list
    written in ascending bytewise order of `E(signature)`, an entry given twice written once (P7-37).
12. INDEX. One `index` frame over the ordinary frames before it (`P5` "The `index` frame"): `frame_count`, `head`, `type_runs`,
    `graph_directory`, `statistics` `[lines, records, blank_nodes, per_predicate]` of `F` as `P5` defines them (step 1), `range_root`,
    `content_digest` `present` with the digest of step 1, and `pack_members`; its envelope by step 10.
13. STORAGE. For every frame other than `parity`, the storage choice of 4.2 with the reference encoder of 4.4 and, for a
    `stored_compressed` frame, its stored check (`P1`). This fixes the encoded length of every item.
14. PARITY. Over the stream of the header and the ordinary frames of steps 9 to 13, the `parity` frames of `P5` "Parity: one
    geometry", each placed by `P5` "The stream and the spans"; the parity frame of the final span stands immediately after the
    `index` frame.
15. ACCELERATORS. The `offsets` item over every item before it, the header first and the parity frames included (`P1` "The
    `offsets` item"), then the locator whose distance leads back to the `index` frame (`P1` "The locator").

NOTHING IS LEFT TO A WRITER. Every choice that a writer of the file could make is fixed by the steps above and the parts they
name, so any producer of a canonical file, a converter included, needs no rule of its own: the ORDER OF TERMS and their ids (step
2, `P3` "The canonical dictionary"); the REUSE OF A DEFINED TERM (each term of the fold is defined exactly once, step 2, `P3` "The
canonical dictionary"); the ORDER AND CUT OF ROWS (step 6, `P3` "The canonical rows", `Nr`); ANNOTATION ROWS (one row for each
distinct reifier, predicate, object and graph, its count the sum of the fold, `P3` "The fold" and "The canonical rows"); the ORDER
AND DEDUPLICATION OF SUPPRESSION ENTRIES (one entry for each resolution, sorted bytewise on the encoding, no repeat, step 7, cut
by `Nr`, `P3` "The canonical suppression frames"); and UNITS SHARED BETWEEN BLOBS (one emission for each distinct digest in each
region, steps 3 and 4, `P4` "The canonical value region and blob region"). The same holds for bindings (step 7), cuts of terms
(`Bt`), packs (`P`), storage (4.2, 4.4) and parity (`P5`).

A frame of step 9 is a function of `F` alone; storage, parity, the `offsets` item and the locator depend also on the encoder and
on the bytes of the commit's `signatures` list. A signature added to the commit later is a storage rewrite (`P1`): it is inserted at its place in the order of
P7-37, it changes no identifier, and the writer writes the parity frames, the `offsets` item and the locator again over the new
bytes (`P5` "The stream and the spans").

**P7-17.** A writer of a canonical file MUST produce the items of steps 1 to 12 exactly, in that order, and the stored bytes of
steps 13 to 15 by their rules with the encoder it uses.

**P7-37.** A writer of any frame, canonical or not, MUST write its `signatures` list in ascending bytewise order of
`E(signature)` (the bytes compared as unsigned integers from the first byte, a proper prefix before the longer string), with no
signature written twice; a writer that adds a signature to a frame MUST insert it at its place in that order and rewrite the
stored bytes that follow (4.3). The bytes of the canonical file of a fold and of one set of signatures are therefore the same
for every writer, whatever the order in which the signers signed.

**P7-40.** A reader MUST report a frame whose `signatures` list is not in the order of P7-37, that is, one in which an element
equals or precedes the element before it under that order, as `malformed_value` with the reason `unordered_or_repeated`, at
ITEMS (`R4`), as a violation of the grammar of the `signatures` field (`P6`, section 1) that stands at the first such element
in the order of the bytes; the frame is opaque with `malformed` and its signatures are not verified.

**P7-18.** A canonical file MUST hold no item and no frame that 4.3 does not give.

### 4.4 The reference encoder and the bytes claim

THE REFERENCE ENCODER is `libzstd` version `1.5.7`, called through its one-shot interface `ZSTD_compress2` with exactly two
parameters set, the compression level `12` and the window log `26`, every other parameter left to the library and no worker
thread, run on the `x86-64` architecture. Its output for a payload of `D` bytes is one zstd frame that begins with the
header `H(D)` of `P1` "The zstd constraint".

A reader HOLDS the reference encoder when it runs the reference encoder and, for every input of the storage vectors of section 8,
its output equals the bytes the vector holds. The determinism of the encoder at window log `26` on that ONE architecture is
measured by `OW-2` (`A1`): eleven builds and bindings of the library gave identical bytes on 29 inputs. Other architectures
are not measured. The storage level below is stated so that it is true whatever a further measurement shows.

THE STORAGE LEVEL reports whether every frame's `storage` and `payload`, every `parity` frame, the `offsets` item and the locator
equal the canonical bytes, recomputed by steps 13 to 15 of 4.3 over the segment's own frames and its commit's `signatures` list. A
reader that holds the reference encoder reports `verified` or `differs`; a reader that does not hold it, on any architecture or
build, reports `unchecked` with `encoder_not_held`, which is a conforming result; the one reason of an unchecked level follows the
order of 4.6. `differs` is a report, not a violation: it produces no finding and is never a first difference. Assumption `A-ENC`:
two runs of the reference encoder that pass the storage vectors give equal output for equal input. Under `A-ENC` two readers that
hold the encoder report one storage level; where a run finds an input on which `A-ENC` fails, the storage vectors gain that
input, and a run that then fails them does not hold the encoder.

**P7-19.** On a segment whose `claimed` is `true`, a reader MUST report the storage level `verified` or `differs` only when its
access is `whole`, it holds the reference encoder of 4.4, and neither the rule of a failed read nor the capacity rule of 4.6
applies; `unchecked` with the first reason that applies in the order of 4.6 (`access`, `read_failed`, `exceeds_capacity`) when one
of them applies; and otherwise `unchecked` with `encoder_not_held`. It MUST produce no finding from the storage level.

**P7-20.** A reader that holds the reference encoder MUST compare every `storage`, `payload`, `parity` frame, `offsets` item and
locator of a segment that claims the canonical form with the canonical bytes of steps 13 to 15 of 4.3 over the segment's own
frames and its commit's `signatures` list.

### 4.5 Compaction

COMPACTION is the total function `compact(F, profile) -> compaction_result` that writes the canonical file of a fold, the fold of
a read with the access `whole` (a read with the access `forward` or `sparse` forms no fold of the file, 3.7). Its only
input besides the fold is the header's `profile` value, which it writes unchanged (the `profile` of the segment it compacts, or
`absent`); it takes no parameter, and the commit it writes has an empty `signatures` list. A signature is added to the commit
afterwards, as a storage rewrite that keeps the order of P7-37 (4.3). The `profile` value changes the header and what is computed from it, and nothing else
(4.1).

```text
compaction_result = [1, file: bytes]                                          ; written: the items of 4.3, concatenated
                  / [2, code: enum(finding_code), reason: option(enum(reason))]   ; stopped: no file is written
```

The tag enumeration is `compaction_outcome` of `P2`, with exactly two values:

- `written` (code `1`): no case below applies; the result holds the bytes of the canonical file of 4.3;
- `stopped` (code `2`): one of the cases below applies; the result holds its finding code and reason, and no byte is written.

Compaction STOPS, and writes no byte, in exactly these cases, checked in this order:

1. a `terms` frame of the source was refused by the total formed length of the read that gave the fold (`P3`, "Front
   coding"), so the fold lacks what that frame holds: `[2, exceeds_capacity, present(total_formed)]`;
2. the fold holds a held row, binding or entry (`P3` "Held"), so its content digest is `absent`: `[2, opaque_term, absent]`;
3. the labelling is refused: `[2, labelling_refused, present(refusal_width)]`, `present(refusal_size)`,
   `present(refusal_weight)`, `present(refusal_term_node)` or `present(refusal_limit)`;
4. a present blob does not rebuild to its name and the `length` of its blob-set entry from the units of ANY of its declarations (a
   blob declared more than once has to rebuild from at least one declaration's units). The blob first in ascending `E(name)` among
   those decides the value: `[2, exceeds_capacity, present(total_rebuilt)]` when some declaration of it was refused by the total
   rebuilt length of the read that gave the fold (`P4`, "The total rebuilt length"); otherwise
   `[2, missing_dependency, present(chunk)]` when no declaration of it has every unit; otherwise `[2,
   damaged_frame, present(blob_length)]` when some declaration rebuilds to another length, else `[2, damaged_frame,
   present(blob_digest)]`. A blob of one unit whose only declarations are `blob` frames or members that fail their unit digest
   is `[2, damaged_frame, present(blob_digest)]`: its bytes do not have its name.

These are the only cases that can occur. A refused `value` declaration leaves its terms unreadable, which is case 2. A literal of
the fold is a literal, so the values of the canonical file come from the
fold's literals (4.3) and no value has to be rebuilt; a `literal_ref` or `literal_value_ref` term that could not be read in the source holds its rows,
bindings and entries, which is case 2.

The details (which declaration, at which position) are those of the `read_result` of the source. Compaction never writes a
canonical file of another fold and never turns a present blob into an external one (`P4` "The canonical value region and blob
region"). It keeps hidden content, which is content (`P3`). A consequence, not a stop: a graph state is a property of the append
history, and compaction writes new commits, so a binding whose referent is a `state` of the source names no commit of the
canonical file; a reader of that file reports `missing_dependency` with `state` for it (`P3` "Graph states"). Otherwise it returns
`[1, file]` with the items of 4.3 and an empty `signatures` list on the commit.

A canonical file holds no resync item: compaction writes header segments, and the marks of a resync item, which describe bytes
that the compacted file does not hold, are not carried (4.1).

**P7-21.** Compaction MUST return `[2, code, reason]` in the cases of 4.5, the first that applies, and MUST then write no byte.

**P7-22.** Compaction MUST return the canonical file of 4.3 in every other case, as a function of the fold and of the `profile`
value alone.

**P7-81.** A writer that compacts MUST write header segments only and MUST NOT carry a resync item or its marks.
Vectors `canonical-no-resync` and `canonical-resync-claim` (8.2).

### 4.6 The canonical check

`claimed` is `true` exactly when the header's `canonical` is `present` with a value the reader holds. With `claimed` `false` the
result is `[false, [2, not_claimed], [2, not_claimed], [2, not_claimed], [1]]`. A segment whose header outcome is not `readable`
has `claimed` `false`, and so has a segment opened by a resync item, which has no `canonical` field. A reader with the access
`forward` runs no level (3.7): the structure level compares the items
with the frames that 4.3 forms from the own fold, the labelling level runs `L1`, and the storage level recompresses each frame,
and a stream does not form that fold; its result for a claimed segment is `[true, [2, access], [2, access], [2,
access], [1]]`. With `claimed` `true` and the access `whole` three levels run, each on its own, over the segment and its own fold. Two of them,
STRUCTURE and LABELLING, judge the head claim and can VIOLATE it; the third, STORAGE, only REPORTS.

STRUCTURE. The structure level never judges a label against a position. Where it compares with 4.3, it compares with the
frames that the construction of 4.3 forms from the own fold WITH EACH BLANK NODE NAMED BY ITS STORED LABEL: the label is a name
there, compared whole as bytes, and the blank block of the dictionary stands in the bytewise order of the stored labels (`P3`,
"The canonical dictionary"). Whether those names are the positions of a labelling is judged by the labelling level alone, so no
label is judged twice: a file that 4.3 forms with two positions exchanged throughout passes the structure level and fails the
labelling level. The result of the level is the comparison of WHERE A DIFFERENCE STANDS; the rest of this paragraph states what
a reader holds
to make it. The level compares the items in order. It compares each item with the rules of 4.2 and 4.3: the header fields; the
kind
and order of every item (header, value packs, blob packs, `external` frames, `terms`, the four row kinds, `bindings`, `suppress`,
`commit`, `index`, then the `offsets` item and the locator) and nothing else in the segment, parity frames standing where `P5`,
"The stream and the spans", places them over the segment's OWN stream; the `terms` frames (membership, order, cuts by `Bt`, runs,
`restart_group`, `id_base`, front coding, the storage form of each literal by VALUES and the two thresholds); the row frames
(keys, cuts by `Nr`, graph runs); the value and blob regions (member order, one emission for each digest, the units of each value
and blob, packing by `P`, `external` frames); the `bindings` frames and the `suppress` frames (sorted across their frames, no
repeat, cut by `Nr`, term ids, one entry for each resolution, pairs only where `P3` keeps them); the `commit` (`count`, `root`,
one); the `index` (every field equal to what `P5` gives, `content_digest` present).

ONE PASS. These checks are decided in one pass over the items with this state: one frame; one previous term; one previous row for
each row kind; one previous binding and one previous entry; counters; for each kind of the sequence of kinds above, the position
of the last item of that kind and the room that its cut leaves (the bytes left under `Bt` or `P`, the rows left under `Nr`),
judged when the next item of the kind arrives; for each term id, one bit of use, and the ids that the term names (the datatype of
a `literal` or `literal_ref` term of the form `typed`, the three components of a `triple` term), and for each `triple` term its
depth; one digest for each distinct unit and recipe and one bit for each value digest (named by a term); and the stack of `P5`,
"Decomposition and aggregation", over the ordinary frames before the item, which the reader holds for `P5`, "The checks of a
commit". Membership is decided at the end of the pass: a term is used when it is a `literal_value` or `literal_value_ref` term
(the literal of a pair of VALUES), when a row, a binding or an entry names it, or when a used term names it, and the first
difference of membership stands at the `terms` frame of the smallest unused id.

NO BOUNDED STATE. Four checks are decided by no state bounded by one frame and one entry for each term id, unit and recipe, and
the
reader makes them by comparing with the frames that 4.3 forms from the fold it holds: (1) whether a row frame of a kind that
follows a frame of that kind shorter than its cut repeats rows that the earlier frame holds, which decides whether the difference
is the later frame (`frame_sequence`) or the earlier (`rows`), and needs the rows of the kind before it (the case arises only
for a frame that carries no counted finding, a row frame that declares `unordered` or follows one, or an `empty_graphs` frame:
a repeated row in a frame that declares `canonical` after a frame that declares `canonical` gives `declared_order_violated`,
and the frame is decided as a frame with a counted finding); (2) whether the units of
a value or blob are the cuts of the chunker (`P4`) over its bytes, which needs the bytes of each value and blob, those the reader
rebuilds; (3) whether the `bindings` and `suppress` frames hold one entry for each resolution of the fold's bindings and entries,
none twice, which needs the entries of the kind before it (a pair that names no node of the segment's own fold stays as
written and is no difference, `P3`, "Naming a blank node in a binding or an entry"); and (4) the `graph_directory`, `statistics` and `pack_members` of
the `index`, which are lists that grow with the graphs, the rows and the units, and which the reader judges by the findings of
`P5`, "Checking an index". The results of these four are those of the comparison, whatever the state of a pass.

Every frame of the segment other than a frame of type `parity` has to be `folded` or `not_content` with no finding
of `P1`, `P3`, `P4` or `P5`; a frame that is not is a difference at its position, except as the capacity rule below says. These
findings do not count for that sentence: `stored_check_mismatch`, `index_claim_failed` with `index_offsets` or `index_locator`
(stored bytes, outside the head claim); `uncommitted_tail` and `unprotected_tail` (consequences of another difference); the
findings of RESOLUTION, `missing_dependency` with `blob`, `frame`, `state` or `state_unavailable`, `binding_unanchored` and
`suppression_unmatched`
(a canonical file can carry them, 4.5 and `P3`); and `index_claim_failed` with `index_digest`, which is the labelling level's.
A finding that the fold of the file gives and the own fold of the segment does not (a `conflicting_declaration` against a
declaration of another segment) does not count either: the levels judge the segment and its own fold.
When some frame is not folded or has a counted finding, the structure level does not form the frames that 4.3 gives, so named,
for the fold that remains: the difference is the header when it differs, or that frame, or an earlier item of the wrong kind or order. A parity
frame's STATE (damaged, stale, mismatched) is outside the head claim and has its own finding (`P5`, "Checking a parity frame"): it
is never a structure difference and never `canonical_violated`. Only a parity frame that is MISSING or MISPLACED (not where `P5`,
"The stream and the spans", places it over the segment's own stream) is a difference, with the check `frame_sequence`.

LABELLING. Run `L1` on the labelled set of the own fold (`P3`, records included) and compare: the stored blank labels of the
dictionary NAME A LABELLING, and the `index`'s `content_digest` equals the digest of the canonical line sequence. A segment that
holds no `index` frame has no digest to compare: its labelling level is judged by the labels alone, and the missing `index` is a
difference of the structure level only. Let `tau` send
each blank node `b` of the fold to the blank node of the fold whose stored label is `E(position of b)`, the position being that of
this run. The stored labels NAME A LABELLING when `tau` is defined for every blank node of the fold (exactly one blank node
carries that label) and `tau` maps the fold onto itself: every line of the fold, and every binding, suppression entry and other
item that gives a record (`P3`, "Record lines"), with each blank node replaced by its image wherever it stands, inside a triple
term at any depth included, is again a line or an item of the fold, with the same annotation count. (1) When every stored label
equals `E(position)` of its node, `tau` is the identity and the condition holds with nothing more to compute; a reader tests
that first. (2) By `L1`'s Lemma I and item (2) of `P3`, "The canonical suppression frames", THE POSITIONS OF THE CANONICAL FILE, this is the
case for every canonical file whose fold has no nested term that holds a blank node. (3) The test is made on the terms of the fold by
their ids, forming no expanded term, and its work is linear in the lines and the distinct terms of the fold. (4) The
`content_digest` comparison is that of the first sentence of this paragraph. A canonical file whose labels come from either of two labellings that differ by an
automorphism of the labelled set has the same bytes, since the automorphism maps its lines and records onto themselves. EXACTLY THE CANONICAL FILE. The stored
labels name a labelling exactly when the dictionary and the content frames that 4.3 forms from the fold with the positions of
this run are, byte for byte, those that it forms with each blank node named by its stored label in place of `E(position)`: if `tau` maps the fold onto
itself the two constructions write one set of lines and items under one set of names, and if the two constructions are equal
the renaming between them maps every line and item onto a line or item. So the labelling level accepts no file whose labels a
labelling of its fold could not have given, and, with the structure level, a file is accepted exactly when its head-claimed
items are those of the canonical file of its fold; its stored bytes are judged by the storage level. This equivalence is a
property of the check and not a procedure: no step reads a label back to a position (`P3`, P3-37), and a stored label that is
not the encoding of any position names its node all the same. THE WORK of the comparison is that of the run of `L1`, which the reader makes in any case,
plus work linear in the lines, the items and the distinct terms of the fold. By `L1`, "The whole input: order, result, record, identity, map, guarantee, outcomes, hash", paragraph VERIFICATION OF A
CLAIM, this is a re-run of the labelling; the search-free run on every top component precedes any search (P7-9).

STORAGE. 4.4: `verified`, `unchecked` or `differs`, and no finding.

CAPACITY. A level that cannot be computed within the reader's capacity is `unchecked` with `exceeds_capacity`, never a difference
and never `canonical_violated`: structure when some frame's payload is `unchecked` for capacity, when some rebuild state of the
segment is `refused` (the value and blob regions of 4.3 are formed from the bytes of every present blob and value), or when some
`terms` frame of the segment is refused by the total formed length (its terms are not formed); labelling
when some content frame is not folded for capacity, when some `value` declaration of the segment has the rebuild state
`refused`, or when the payload of the `index` whose `content_digest` it compares is `unchecked` for capacity; storage when some
frame is `unchecked` for capacity (the reasons `stored_size`, `decoded_size` and `total_decoded`). A finding `exceeds_capacity` is never a counted finding of the structure
level. When the capacity rule applies to a level, the level reports no difference, whatever else it found.

A FAILED READ. When a read of the input failed, every level of every segment of the result that claims the canonical form is
`unchecked` with `read_failed`, never a difference and never `canonical_violated`. A failed read is an event of the source and
no fact of the file: no fold is formed and every content digest is `absent` (3.1), an item that was not delivered is no missing
item, and nothing is compared with bytes whose read failed. A read that stops at `items_per_read` (section 2) is no failed
read: for it, every level of every claimed segment is `unchecked` with `exceeds_capacity`, for the same reasons. When either
rule applies to a level, the level reports no difference, whatever else it found.

ONE REASON FOR AN UNCHECKED LEVEL. A level that is `unchecked` carries exactly one reason, the first that applies in this order,
for all three levels: `not_claimed` (the segment does not claim the canonical form); then `access` (the reader's access is
`forward`); then `read_failed` (a read of the input failed); then `exceeds_capacity` (the capacity rule applies); then the level's own reason, `labelling_refused` for labelling (the labelling is refused, `P3` "Refusals and the reader's limit") and `encoder_not_held` for storage (4.4).
Structure has no reason of its own.

THE LEVELS' VALUES:

- structure (`check_state`); `verified`: every item agrees; `failed`: some item differs; `unchecked`: `not_claimed`, `access`,
  `read_failed` or `exceeds_capacity`
- labelling (`check_state`); `verified`: the labels name a labelling and the digest agrees; `failed`: the labels name no
  labelling or the digest differs; `unchecked`:
  `not_claimed`, `access`, `read_failed`, `exceeds_capacity` or `labelling_refused`
- storage (`storage_check_state`); `verified`: every stored form equals the canonical bytes; `differs`: some stored form does not;
  `unchecked`: `not_claimed`, `access`, `read_failed`, `exceeds_capacity` or `encoder_not_held`

`unknown_codec` is not a reason of a check (`P2`, "Enumerations of the typed result"); a frame of a codec the reader does not hold
cannot stand in a claimed canonical segment of the reader's version and is a structure difference at its position.

WHERE A DIFFERENCE STANDS (P7-41). The items of the segment are compared, position by position from the header at position `0`,
with the items that 4.3 forms for the own fold with each blank node named by its stored label (STRUCTURE), the parity frames
standing where `P5` places them; the checks of the structure level (`header`, `frame_sequence`, `terms`, `rows` and the others)
are the differences of that comparison; the `offsets` item, the
locator and the parity frames are compared for kind and place only, since their bytes are not claimed. A difference stands at
a position when the segment's item there is not the item that 4.3, so named, gives there, for whatever cause: an item of another kind; an item
of the same kind with another `content` or decoded payload (a frame that holds fewer terms or rows than its cut allows, a
member emitted a second time, a row out of order); an item that 4.3 does not give, which stands at its own position; a missing
item, which stands at the position where it would stand: the position of the item that stands there instead, or, when the
segment ends first, the position after its last item. For an item whose kind precedes the kind of an earlier item, this gives the
position after the last item of a kind at or before its own, or the position of that last item when its cut leaves room for the
first content of the late item. The check of a difference is `frame_sequence` when the two items are of different kinds
or one is absent, and otherwise the check of the kind of the item (`header`, `terms`, `rows`, `value_region`, `blob_region`,
`bindings`, `suppression`, `commit`, `index`). One rule stands before this one (`P4`, P4-50): a `value` frame outside a pack is a
difference of check `value_region`, and a `chunk`, `blob` or `recipe` frame outside a pack is one of check `blob_region`,
whatever the kind of the item that 4.3 gives at its position. When the stored blank labels do not name a labelling, the first blank term, in the
order of the dictionary, whose node `tau` moves or for which `tau` is not defined is a difference of check
`labelling` at the position of the `terms` frame that holds the term, and a `content_digest` of the `index` that differs from
the digest of the canonical line sequence is a difference of check `content_digest` at the position of the `index`; both stand
beside the differences of the structure level at those positions. When some frame is not folded or has a counted finding, the
frames of 4.3 are not formed, and the difference stands at the smallest of three positions: position `0`, with the check `header`,
when the header differs from the header that 4.2 requires (the header is compared whatever the frames); the first frame of the
segment that is
not folded or has a counted finding, with the check of the kind of its type (`frame_sequence` for a frame of no frame type, `P1`, "The locator", A FRAME ITEM HAS A
TYPE ONLY BY A VALUE, and for a type that has no check of its own; `value_region` and `blob_region` for the frames that P4-50
names), and the first item before it whose kind is not allowed at that point of the sequence of kinds of 4.6 (a
kind that precedes the kind of an earlier item in that sequence, a second `commit` or `index`, a frame outside the sequence),
with the check `frame_sequence`, or, for a `value`, `chunk`, `blob` or `recipe` frame outside a pack, the check of its region
(P4-50).

THE FIRST DIFFERENCE. Each of structure and labelling that is `failed` names the smallest `item_position` at which a difference
stands (above) and the `canonical_check` of what differs there: `header` (the header), `frame_sequence` (an item of the wrong kind, a
missing or an extra item, an item out of order, other than a frame outside a pack that P4-50 names), `terms`, `rows`, `value_region`, `blob_region`, `bindings`, `suppression`,
`commit`, `index` (an item of that kind whose content differs from the construction so named, and, for the two regions, a
`value`, `chunk`, `blob` or `recipe` frame outside a pack), `labelling` (a blank label of
labels that name no labelling, judged by the labelling level alone), `content_digest` (the `index`'s
`content_digest`). `first_difference` is the one of these with the smallest `item_position`, and among those at one position the
one of the smallest `canonical_check` code; it is `absent` when neither level is `failed`. A storage difference is never a first
difference. The finding is ONE `canonical_violated` with that check's reason (`P2`, "The finding table") and `position` the
`item_position`. The segment still folds; the check changes no other result.

WHAT EACH LEVEL HOLDS. Structure holds the state of ONE PASS, the stored label of each blank term being its name in the
construction, and, for the four checks of NO BOUNDED STATE, the fold, the bytes of the rebuilt values and blobs, and the
resolution
of `P3`, which a reader with the access `whole` holds for the fold and the rebuild in any case, and one frame of 4.3 at a time.
Labelling holds what `L1`, "The whole input: order, result, record, identity, map, guarantee, outcomes, hash",
paragraph MEMORY, ORDER AND DETERMINISM, states for the labelled set, and for the `tau` test one image for each blank node of
the fold, one image id for each term of the dictionary that holds a blank node at any depth, computed in id order, and the set of
the lines and items of the fold keyed by their term ids, so that the test of one image is one lookup in that set. Storage holds one frame and its recompressed form, and one
span for parity.

**P7-23.** A reader with the access `whole` MUST run the three levels of 4.6 on every segment whose header claims the canonical form with a value it
holds, MUST give each level the value of 4.6, and MUST give a level that cannot be computed within its capacity `unchecked` with
`exceeds_capacity` and no difference, unless a reason that 4.6 places before it applies.

**P7-24.** A reader MUST report at most one `canonical_violated` for a segment, at the first difference of the structure and
labelling levels, MUST report none for the storage level, and MUST NOT let the canonical check change the fold, the states or any
other finding.

**P7-50.** A reader MUST compare, at the structure level, the items of a claimed segment with the frames that 4.3 forms from
its own fold with each blank node named by its stored label, and MUST NOT judge a stored label against a final position at that
level.

**P7-41.** A reader MUST place each difference of the structure and labelling levels by WHERE A DIFFERENCE STANDS, and MUST
name as the first difference the one at the smallest position, the one of the smallest `canonical_check` code among those at
that position, whatever the level that found it.

**P7-25.** A reader MUST give a segment that does not claim the canonical form the canonical result `[false, [2, not_claimed], [2,
not_claimed], [2, not_claimed], [1]]`.

### 4.7 The canonical frames that can exceed the minimum capacity

This is the list, stated once. Capacity is measured on a frame's stored and decoded PAYLOAD (section 2), and the list holds only
frames whose payload can exceed `2^26` bytes. Every payload of a canonical file is bounded by a constant (4.2, the window reason)
except where one entry of it carries a quantity that no constant bounds; the list names each such case, and it is complete:

- an `index` frame of a very large fold (its directory, statistics and member list grow with the fold);
- a pack that holds the `recipe` member of a very large blob, or the `value` member of a very large value (its entry list is one
  member, `P4`, "The pack");
- a `terms` frame that holds one term whose text alone exceeds `Bt`, or whose literals carry very long language tags (a language
  tag is not counted in the budget);
- a `bindings` or `suppress` frame whose entries carry very long labels or terms (a pair's label, or a term written in a pair).

A reader whose capacity such a frame exceeds reports its payload `unchecked` with `exceeds_capacity`, never `damaged`.

## 5. Conformance

There are no tiers and no modes. An implementation claims, as a typed record of its own documentation and never of a file:

```text
implementation_claim = [ readers:                 enum(readers_claim),   ; second_only or first_and_second (P9)
                         registry_version:        uint,                  ; implemented in full (P2)
                         profiles:                list(profile_checks),  ; the set it implements with its checks, ascending, possibly empty
                         capacity:                capacity,              ; section 2
                         writes_canonical:        bool,                  ; it writes the canonical form by 4.3
                         holds_reference_encoder: bool ]                 ; 4.4
```

The value of the reader claim: an implementation outside this repository can read this generation only, and says so in its result;
the reference engines of the repository claim `first_and_second`, as the repository's rule on format generations requires (`P9`).
Vectors are per part and per registry version; the corpus is generated by the reference engine and regenerated byte-identically.

**P7-26.** An implementation that claims registry version `n` MUST implement every value, frame type, algorithm, codec, check and
finding of versions `1` to `n` (`P2` "Registry versions"), MUST offer the three accesses `whole`, `forward` and `sparse`, and
MUST run the reader procedure of section 3 in full for the access `whole` and as 3.7 states for the other two.

**P7-27.** An implementation MUST NOT claim a tier, a mode, a subset of a registry version or a reader that skips a check. It
reads with the `access` that its caller selects in `capacity`; under the access `forward` it runs section 3 as the table STEPS BY
ACCESS (3.7) states and no step differently, and under `sparse` the procedure of 3.9; a tool that does less than section 3 for the access it states is
outside this specification.

**P7-28.** An implementation that claims `writes_canonical` MUST write canonical files by 4.3 and compaction by 4.5, and its
result for each written file MUST be the canonical result with structure and labelling `verified`.

**P7-29.** An implementation MUST meet every vector of every part for the registry version and the profiles it claims, at its
stated capacity.

## 6. The profile mechanism

The core defines the mechanism and no profile.

1. A profile is ONE VALUE of the profile table of `P2` (the enumeration `profile`), named by the header field `profile:
   option(enum(profile))`. A profile is never a registry version: it never raises a header's `registry_version`, and a reader of a
   registry version is never asked to implement a profile. The table grows by appending and an entry never changes.
2. A reader states the profiles it implements, each with the number of checks it runs (`implementation_claim`, section 5; the
   field `profiles` of its result, 1.1). For a segment whose declared profile it implements, it runs the checks `1` to that number
   of the profile at PROFILE CHECKS.
3. For a segment whose header outcome is `readable` and whose header declares a profile outside that set (a profile the reader
   does not implement, or a value that is not in its profile table), whatever the registry versions, the reader reads the
   segment in full by section 3, runs no profile check and reports the ONE finding `profile_not_checked` (severity `information`)
   with the subject `[4, profile, absent]` (on the wire `[4, p, [1]]`, every other optional field `absent`). It is never
   `unsupported_registry_version` and never an error.
4. Each profile numbers its own checks from `1`, densely, in its own document. A failed check is the finding `profile_violation`
   with the `subject` `[4, profile, present(check)]` (on the wire `[4, p, [2, c]]`: the profile and the number of the check in
   that profile's own enumeration) and the `reason` `absent`; its location fields are those the profile states for the check
   (`P2`). A check over the content of the whole file is run once, after the closing phase of every segment, and reported as a
   finding of the step PROFILE CHECKS in the last segment of the file that declares the profile (a readable header whose
   `profile` is `present` with it), with a `count`; the findings of a list stand in the order of 1.4. No profile adds a value to `reason` or to any enumeration of the core. Each check reports its own finding;
   overlapping checks of one defect each report, and the profile lists the overlaps in its vectors.
5. There are NO PROFILE FRAMES. A profile consists of checks, an RDF vocabulary, constants, and requirements on core frames
   (parity, signatures, commits). It never changes the header or frame grammar, framing, identifiers, signature or hash preimages,
   codec rules, the fold, the labelled set, the content digest or the meaning of `authenticated` (`P6` "Coverage and
   authentication"). It has no placement rule: the canonical file of a fold has the same frames in the same order whatever profile
   the header declares; the `profile` value changes the header and what is computed from it, and nothing else (4.1).

**P7-30.** A reader that implements the declared profile of a segment MUST run the checks `1` to the number it states for that
profile in `profiles`, and no check above it (`P2`, P2-94), at PROFILE CHECKS and report each failure as `profile_violation` with the subject `[4, profile, present(check)]` and the `reason` `absent`, a check over
the whole file once, after the closing phase of every segment, as a finding of the step PROFILE CHECKS in the last segment that
declares the profile (R21), with a `count`.

**P7-31.** A reader that does not implement the declared profile, or meets a value not in its profile table, MUST read the segment
by section 3, MUST run no profile check and MUST report exactly one `profile_not_checked` with the subject `[4, profile, absent]`
for the segment.

**P7-32.** A profile specification MUST NOT add a registry version, a frame type, a value of a core enumeration or a reason, and
MUST NOT change anything that item 5 of section 6 names.

**P7-42.** Every check of a profile MUST be one of: a rule of presence, a rule of type, a rule of count (a length is a count), or a
rule of equality of whole values. No check of a profile reads the inside of a `text` value or a `bytes` value.

### 7.5 The canonical construction of a fold of three lines, one blob and one binding

The fold `F`, with IRIs written in full: `a` is `http://e/a`, `b` `http://e/b`, `g` `http://e/g`, `p` `http://e/p`, `q`
`http://e/q`, `s` `http://www.w3.org/2001/XMLSchema#string`.

- lines: `quad(a, p, _:x)`, `quad(_:x, p, "v"^^s)` and `quad(a, q, b, g)`;
- one blob: the three bytes `61 62 63`, name `[1, <Dabc>]` with `<Dabc> = H_ctx(unit, 61 62 63)`, declared with the media type
  `text/plain`; its blob-set entry is `[1, [1, <Dabc>], 3, ["text/plain"]]` (`P4` "The blob set"; its `length` is a plain `uint`,
  always present, as is the `length` of every `external` frame);
- one binding: the term `a` bound to the referent `[1, [1, <Dabc>]]`;
- no suppression entry.

The steps of 4.3:

1. Labelled set: the three lines, `record(E([1, [[1], [1, [1, <Dabc>]]]]), [a])` for the binding and `record(E([3, [1, [1,
   <Dabc>], 3, ["text/plain"]]]), [])` for the declaration (`P3` "Record lines"). `L1` gives the one blank node `_:x` the position
   `0`, the digest `<CD>` and the statistics record that the reader delivers beside its result (section 1).
2. Dictionary: `a`, `b`, `g`, `p`, `q`, `s` bytewise (`http://e/` sorts before `http://w`), ids `0` to `5`; the literal `"v"^^s`,
   id `6` (its lexical form is below the threshold); the blank node with label `E(0)`, the one byte `00`, id `7`.
3. Value region: no value, no pack.
4. Blob region: one one-unit blob (3 bytes, below `16384`), so one `blob` member `[2, [<Dabc>, [2, "text/plain"]], 3]` with the

## 8. Vectors

Every vector is an input with the reader's registry version, capacity and inputs, and its expected value is a `read_result` (or a
`compaction_result`, or the bytes of a canonical file), encoded deterministically and produced by the reference engine. Names are
opaque labels, not paths: no step reads a part of a name, and a vector that expects a finding holds its `code` and `reason` as
typed data.
