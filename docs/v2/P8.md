# Part P8: Sealing

## 0. Scope and notation

This part defines registry version `2` of the format: authenticated encryption of frames with key commitment. It defines the
`keys` and `sealed` frame types, the sealed inner record and its inner types, the sealing cipher `aes256_gcm_siv` with its key
schedule, synthetic nonce and commitment, the one padding policy, the per-key limits, the one key-wrap suite, sealed packs and the
sealed member index, the registry values of version `2`, and the security properties of the construction.

The container format into which this part fits is defined by the other parts; section 1 states, as three numbered assumptions,
everything this part takes from it. Section 2 defines every primitive with its standard and the security notion assumed of it;
sections 4 to 10 are the construction as exact procedures; section 11 states the security properties that the construction is
designed to have, with their assumptions, and section 12 what is not claimed.

Notation.

- `E(x)` is the deterministic encoding of the value `x` in CBOR (RFC 8949, "Concise Binary Object Representation (CBOR)",
  December 2020, section 4.2.1, "Core Deterministic Encoding Requirements"): every head in its shortest form, every length
  definite.
- The typed notation: `uint` (a CBOR unsigned integer), `bool`, `bytes`, `text`, `digest` (32 bytes in registry versions `1` and
  `2`), `list(T)` (a CBOR array of values of `T`). `enum(E)` is a value of the enumeration `E`, encoded as the `uint` of its code.
  `option(T)` is the variant `[1]` for absent and `[2, x]` for a present value `x` of `T`. A RECORD is a CBOR array of its fields
  in declared order; a VARIANT is a CBOR array whose first element is a tag of its tag enumeration. Every field is always present.
- `bytes32` is written for a `bytes` field whose length MUST be exactly 32; any other length is `malformed_value` with reason
  `wrong_length`.
- `a || b` is the concatenation of byte strings; `len(x)` the length of `x` in bytes; `x[i..j]` the bytes of `x` from index `i`
  included to `j` excluded, from `0`; `0^n` the string of `n` zero bytes.
- `H_ctx(context, message)` is the container's one hash construction (assumption `A-1`): the key-derivation mode of BLAKE3 (J.
  O'Connor, J.-P. Aumasson, S. Neves and Z. Wilcox-O'Hearn, "BLAKE3: one function, fast everywhere", 2020) with the spelling of a
  row of the hash context table as context string and `message` as key material, 32 bytes of output. The rows this document uses
  are `header` (spelling `gts2 header`), `frame` (`gts2 frame`), `payload` (`gts2 payload`), all three of the container (`A-1`),
  and `recipient_key_id` (`gts2 recipient key id`, 3.3); a spelling is hashed as its ASCII bytes, whole, with no length prefix or
  terminator. Every identifier, payload digest and header identifier of section 13 is computed with these spellings.
- `HMAC(K, M)` is HMAC with SHA-256; `HKDF-Extract` and `HKDF-Expand` are those of RFC 5869 with SHA-256 (section 2).
- A frame is TRANSPARENT exactly when its envelope field `transparent` is `true`; every other frame is ORDINARY. A `keys` frame
  and a sealed frame are not transparent (`P8-6`). The ORDINAL of a frame is the number of ordinary frames of its segment before
  it that have an effective identifier (`A-1`); a frame with no effective identifier has no ordinal and is not counted.
- Outcomes of reading. The container reports what a reader finds as FINDINGS, each with a code from the finding table, a fixed
  severity and, for some codes, a reason; a frame whose content the reader does not fold is OPAQUE, with one opaque reason; a
  frame's PAYLOAD STATE says whether its stored bytes matched its payload digest. Section 3 lists the codes this part adds. These
  outcomes matter to sections 3 to 9.
- Requirements are numbered `P8-1`, `P8-2` and on, densely. MUST, MUST NOT and MAY have their standard meaning.

REQUIREMENT LANGUAGE. The key words "MUST", "MUST NOT", "REQUIRED", "SHALL", "SHALL NOT", "SHOULD", "SHOULD NOT",
"RECOMMENDED", "NOT RECOMMENDED", "MAY", and "OPTIONAL" in this document are to be interpreted as described in BCP 14
[RFC2119] [RFC8174] when, and only when, they appear in all capitals, as shown here. This document uses the key words MUST,
MUST NOT and MAY, and no other.

## 1. What this part needs from the container

The three assumptions below are guarantees of the container format, restated so that this part can be read without that
format. The property of 11.5 uses `A-1` and `A-3`; section 10 uses `A-3`; the properties of 11.2 to 11.4 use none of them.

**Assumption `A-1` (what an identifier covers).** A file is a sequence of segments; a segment is an opener, a header or a
resync item (`P1`, "The resync item"), followed by items. A
header is the record `[item_kind, generation, registry_version, hash_algorithm, profile, canonical, lineage]`; `lineage` is absent
in the first segment of a file and otherwise names the HEAD of the previous segment and an `ordinal` (`P1`, "The
header"). A header holds NO
per-file unique or random value: the first segments of two files written with the same settings have equal headers. The segment's
HEADER IDENTIFIER `h0` is the identifier of its opener: `H_ctx(header, E(header))` for a header, and `H_ctx(header,
resync_body)` for a resync item
(`P1`, "The resync item"). A frame is a record of eleven fields: `item_kind`, `frame_type`, `critical`,
`transparent`, `content`, `decoded_length`, `payload_digest`, `storage`, `payload`, `identifier`, `signatures`. Its identifier is
`H_ctx(frame, E(identity))`, where `identity = [predecessor, frame_type, critical, transparent, content, decoded_length,
payload_digest]` and `predecessor` is the effective identifier of the nearest preceding ordinary frame that has one, or `h0` when
there is none. A frame is TRANSPARENT exactly when its envelope field `transparent` is `true`; every other frame is ORDINARY. The
EFFECTIVE IDENTIFIER of an ordinary frame is its stored identifier, or the identifier computed for it when its link state is
`healed`, that is, when the stored identifier is damaged and the item directly after it, a frame whose link explains the
damage, witnesses it (`P1`,
"Healing"). A frame whose `identifier`
field is not a `bytes` item of the digest length has NO stored identifier: if elements 1 to 6 are well typed its effective
identifier is the COMPUTED one, and if they are not it has none. A frame with no effective identifier is an item of its segment
with a position; it is not an ordinary frame for any count: it has no ordinal, is not a leaf of the range, is not counted by a
commit or an index, is not a predecessor and is never the head. The HEAD of a segment is the effective identifier of its last
ordinary frame that has one, or the header identifier when there is none. So the identifier is a function of the frame's type,
flags, content record, decoded length and payload digest, and of every earlier ordinary frame of the segment that has an effective
identifier and of the header; it is a function of nothing else. `payload_digest` is `H_ctx(payload, d)` for the frame's decoded
payload `d`, and `decoded_length` is `len(d)`; this part defines both for a sealed frame over its ciphertext (section 6). The
spellings of the three container rows used here are `gts2 header` (for `h0`), `gts2 frame` (for an identifier) and `gts2 payload`
(for a payload digest). What this part needs of `A-1`: that these preimages are exactly as stated, so that a party without keys
computes every identifier and digest (11.5 and the worked examples of section 13), and, only to read "equal `h0`" as "equal
header", that `H_ctx` has no known collision.

**Assumption `A-2` (what lies outside the identifier).** The fields `storage`, `payload` and `signatures` of a frame are not
inputs of any identifier. A party that rewrites them changes no identifier; the stored bytes are bound to the identifier only
through the check `payload_digest = H_ctx(payload, decoded payload)`, which any party can compute. A signature is a separate
record over an identifier; it is never an input of the seal. Neither the seal nor the identifier binds storage or signatures.

**Assumption `A-3` (what a party without keys sees and can do, and what a reader computes).** Every field of every item is
readable by every party. A party that holds no key can delimit every item, compute and check every identifier and every payload
digest, and count frames; it can also recompute every identifier after changing any field, since no identifier involves a secret.
So an adversary can replace, insert, delete, reorder or rewrite any item and re-link the chain, and the chain alone never
authenticates (an authenticating signature, outside this document, does). A reader's view of a segment (its items in order, the
fields of each, the ordinal of each frame and the segment's `h0`) is a deterministic function of the bytes of the file alone.

What follows from the three. The seal has to bind, in its own associated data, every clear value that a key holder relies on (the
header through `h0`, the key and the frame's place, section 6.2); it can leave to the chain the values that are functions of the
ciphertext (`decoded_length`, `payload_digest`, the identifier), because a change of those without a change of the ciphertext
changes nothing a key holder decrypts. 11.5 states the property; section 10 states what a keyless party sees.

1. `P8-1` A segment that holds a frame of type `keys` or `sealed`, or a value that this part adds to any enumeration, MUST declare
   `registry_version` `2`.
2. `P8-2` This part MUST NOT be read as adding a field to, or changing a rule of, any record or procedure of registry version `1`;
   every rule of this part applies to the frame types, inner types and values it adds.

## 2. Primitives

Every primitive the construction uses, with the exact standard, the parameters fixed here, where it is used and the security
notion assumed of it. Each assumption is numbered in 11.1.

- SHA-256. standard and parameters: FIPS 180-4, "Secure Hash Standard", August 2015; 32-byte output, 64-byte block; used for:
  inside HMAC; notion assumed: collision resistance (assumption `S-0`); through HMAC, the pseudo-random
  properties of its compression function that assumptions `S-1` and `S-2` state.
- HMAC-SHA-256, written `HMAC`. standard and parameters: RFC 2104, "HMAC: Keyed-Hashing for Message Authentication", February
  1997, and FIPS 198-1, "The Keyed-Hash Message Authentication Code (HMAC)", July 2008 (in force; NIST has proposed its withdrawal
  in favour of SP 800-224, still a draft); every HMAC key of sections 5 and 6 is exactly 32 bytes, so never longer than the block;
  HPKE's internal HMAC keys (9.2) are as RFC 9180 fixes them, one of them empty, and lie outside the property of 11.2; used for: the
  synthetic value `sv` (6.3); inside HKDF; notion assumed: pseudo-random function under a uniform 32-byte key (assumption `S-2`);
  collision resistance over (key, message) pairs with keys of one length follows from that of SHA-256 (11.2).
- HKDF-SHA-256. standard and parameters: RFC 5869, "HMAC-based Extract-and-Expand Key Derivation Function (HKDF)", May 2010,
  sections 2.2 and 2.3; salt the 32 bytes `0^32` (the default of section 2.2 for an absent salt), output length 32; used for: the
  key schedule (5); notion assumed: the two derived keys are pseudo-random and independent from a uniform 32-byte input key
  (assumption `S-1`).
- AES-256-GCM-SIV. standard and parameters: RFC 8452, "AES-GCM-SIV: Nonce Misuse-Resistant Authenticated Encryption", April 2019,
  `AEAD_AES_256_GCM_SIV`: key 32 bytes, nonce 12 bytes, tag 16 bytes, plaintext and associated data each at most `2^36` bytes (its
  section 6); used for: the sealing cipher (6.4); notion assumed: multi-user misuse-resistant authenticated encryption, with
  AES-256 modelled as an ideal cipher (assumption `S-3`). It is TIDY, a fact of the specification and not an assumption:
  decryption that accepts recomputes the tag from the plaintext it returns (RFC 8452, section 5), so encrypting that plaintext
  under the same key, nonce and associated data gives back the ciphertext.
- BLAKE3 key-derivation mode, written `H_ctx`. standard and parameters: the container's construction (`A-1`); 32-byte output; used
  for: identifiers and payload digests (container); the recipient key identifier (9.2); notion assumed: none in the properties of 11.2 to 11.4; collision resistance where the container claims it of identifiers and digests, and for the binding of a
  recipient key identifier to a public key (12, items 13 and 16).
- X-Wing. standard and parameters: `draft-connolly-cfrg-xwing-kem-11`, "X-Wing: general-purpose hybrid post-quantum KEM", 23
  September 2026: ML-KEM-768 of FIPS 203 combined with X25519 of RFC 7748; encapsulation key 1,216 bytes, ciphertext 1,120 bytes,
  shared secret 32 bytes, decapsulation key a 32-byte seed; used for: the KEM of the key wrap (9); notion assumed: IND-CCA key
  encapsulation in the random-oracle model for SHA3-256, SHA3-512 and SHAKE-256, as that draft states it (assumption `S-4`).
- HPKE base mode. standard and parameters: RFC 9180, "Hybrid Public Key Encryption", February 2022, sections 4, 5.1, 5.1.1, 5.2
  and 6.1: mode `0x00`, KEM `0x647A` (X-Wing), KDF `0x0001` (HKDF-SHA256), AEAD `0x0002` (AES-256-GCM, key 32, nonce 12, tag 16
  bytes); single-shot interface; used for: the key wrap (9); notion: labelled IND-CCA public-key encryption with `info` as the
  label, which rests on `S-0`, `S-2`, `S-4` and `S-5` (11.4).
- AES-256-GCM. standard and parameters: NIST SP 800-38D, "Recommendation for Block Cipher Modes of Operation: Galois/Counter Mode
  (GCM) and GMAC", November 2007, inside HPKE only, as RFC 9180 fixes it; used for: the key wrap; notion assumed: one-time
  authenticated encryption under many independent keys (assumption `S-5`); never a sealing cipher.

Two hashes stand in this part, and each has one role. BLAKE3, through `H_ctx`, is the container's hash of public input. SHA-256 is
the hash of every KEYED function of the seal: the key schedule and the synthetic value. The reason is that the security properties rest on
properties that published results state for HMAC and HKDF: collision resistance of HMAC over pairs follows from collision
resistance of its hash, and the pseudo-randomness of HMAC under a uniform key is the subject of published theorems (assumption
`S-2`). The keyed and key-derivation modes of BLAKE3 have no published statement of either property with the key as an input
(`R-1`). With SHA-256 in every keyed role, the commitment of 11.2 rests on collision resistance of SHA-256 alone and on nothing
of BLAKE3.

1. `P8-3` A reader of registry version `2` MUST implement every primitive of this section, with exactly the parameters it states.
2. `P8-4` Every HMAC key of sections 5 and 6 MUST be exactly 32 bytes long, and a later registry version that keeps this
   construction MUST NOT admit an HMAC key of another length in them; the commitment of 11.2 fails for keys of two
   lengths or longer than the block.

## 4. The key table: the `keys` frame

### 4.1 Segment keys

A SEGMENT KEY `SK` is 32 bytes. It is generated outside the format, by a cryptographic random generator or by a key-derivation
function with its own inputs, and it reaches a reader either from the reader's own key input (4.4) or by a recipient entry (9). It
is never used directly: the key schedule derives the two keys the seal uses (5). A key is named in a file by its KEY IDENTIFIER, a
32-byte value chosen by whoever generates the key and independent of `SK` (for example 32 random bytes, or a digest of a name in
the owner's key management).

1. `P8-7` A segment key MUST be 32 bytes drawn uniformly at random or produced by a key-derivation function from a secret of at
   least 256 bits of entropy. A segment key derived from a password or another low-entropy secret is outside every claim of this
   part (11.3).
2. `P8-8` A key identifier MUST NOT be computed from `SK` or from any value derived from `SK`.

### 4.2 The records

```text
keys.content    = [ entry_count: uint, recipient_count: uint ]
keys_payload    = [ entries: list(key_entry), recipients: list(recipient_entry) ]
key_entry       = [ key_id: bytes32, cipher: enum(sealing_cipher), context: bytes ]
recipient_entry = [ suite: enum(key_wrap_suite), recipient_key_id: digest, target_key_id: bytes32,
                    encapsulation: bytes, wrapped: bytes ]
```

The decoded payload of a `keys` frame is `E(keys_payload)`. `entry_count` and `recipient_count` are the lengths of the two lists,
so that a reader bounds its allocation from the envelope. The `context` of a key entry is the KEY CONTEXT: bytes chosen by the
writer, an input and never randomness, which separates two files sealed under one key (6.2, `P8-29`). `recipient_entry` is defined
in section 9.

1. `P8-9` `entry_count` MUST equal the length of `entries` and `recipient_count` the length of `recipients`; a disagreement is
   `malformed_value` with reason `inconsistent_counts`.
2. `P8-10` `entry_count + recipient_count` MUST be at least `1`; a `keys` frame with no entry is `malformed_value` with reason
   `out_of_range`.
3. `P8-11` `context` MUST be at most `64` bytes long (the constant `max_key_context`, 7.1); a longer one is `malformed_value` with
   reason `wrong_length`.
4. `P8-12` A `keys` frame MUST be stored `stored_plain`; another storage is `malformed_value` with reason
    `forbidden_combination`.
5. `P8-57` The list `recipients` MUST be in strictly ascending bytewise order of the pair `E(recipient_key_id)`, then
    `E(target_key_id)` (the bytes compared as unsigned integers from the first byte, a proper prefix before the longer string);
    a recipient entry whose pair is equal to or below the pair of the entry before it in the list is `malformed_value` with
    reason `unordered_or_repeated` for the entry (9.3). The pair is a total order on the entries a frame admits, since two
    entries with one pair cannot both exist (`P8-50`).
6. `P8-59` A reader MUST judge a `keys` frame whose link state is `linked` or `healed` in this order, and the first violation is
    the one `malformed_value` of the frame:
    the storage (`P8-12`); the decoded payload against `keys_payload`, the first violation in the order of its bytes with the
    reason `P1` gives; `entry_count` against the length of `entries` and then `recipient_count` against the length of
    `recipients` (`P8-9`); the sum at least `1` (`P8-10`); the `context` of each entry in list order (`P8-11`); the `key_id` of
    each entry in list order (`P8-14`); the layout of the frame, in the order of `P8-61` (the number of entries, then the next
    item, then an earlier use of the key index). The layout is judged last of the rules of the frame because it needs the table
    that the earlier rules leave and the envelope fields of the items that follow, and before the rules of one recipient entry. The
    rules of one recipient entry (9.3) are judged only on a frame that has no violation of these.

### 4.3 The key table of a segment

The KEY TABLE of a segment is the list of the key entries of its `keys` frames, in file order: the entries of the first applied
`keys` frame, then those of the next, each in list order. The KEY INDEX of an entry is its zero-based position in the table. A
segment's table is its own: nothing of it is inherited from another segment.

1. `P8-13` A `keys` frame MUST be applied whole or not at all. A `keys` frame whose link is broken, whose payload is damaged or
    unchecked, or which breaks any rule of 4.2 or of this subsection (judged in the order of `P8-59`, one finding for the frame; the
    rules of one recipient entry, `P8-48`, `P8-49` and `P8-57`, cost that entry only), adds no entry to the table, and none of its recipient entries is tried.
2. `P8-14` The `key_id` of a key entry MUST differ from the `key_id` of every entry already in the table and of every other entry
    of the same frame; a repeated `key_id` is `malformed_value` with reason `unordered_or_repeated`, and the frame is not applied.
3. `P8-15` A key entry takes the next key index when its frame is applied; an entry is never replaced, and rotation is a new
    entry.

   A sealed frame is UNDER a key entry when its `key_index` names the entry, judged on the envelope alone as `P8-49` judges it,
   whatever the frame's link, payload or outcome. THE FIRST SEALED FRAME UNDER AN ENTRY is the one with the lowest position in the
   segment. The FIRST USE of a key entry is the position of that frame; an entry has no first use when no sealed frame is under it.
   A sealed frame names one key index, so no two entries have one first use.

4. `P8-58` A writer MUST write the key entries of a segment in ascending order of first use, and MUST NOT write a key entry
    that no sealed frame of the segment is under. A writer MUST write the `keys` frame that holds an entry immediately before
    the first sealed frame under it, with no other ordinary frame between them (a transparent frame, which `P5` places by its own
    rule, can stand between). That frame holds exactly that one entry, and holds as its
    recipient entries all the recipient entries whose target is that entry, in the order of `P8-57`; the recipient entries of a
    key are therefore all in the frame that holds the key. The key index of an entry is then the number of distinct key
    entries whose first use is below its own.
5. `P8-61` A reader MUST judge the layout of `P8-58` on every `keys` frame whose link state is `linked` or `healed`, and MUST
    report a `keys` frame that breaks it as `malformed_value` with the reason `unordered_or_repeated`, the reason this part gives
    to an item that is out of its order or not in its place (`P8-14`, `P8-49`, `P8-57`). Let `n` be the key index that the entry
    of the frame takes if the frame is applied. A `keys` frame BREAKS THE LAYOUT when it holds other than exactly one key entry;
    or the next item of the segment
    after it that is not a transparent frame is not a frame of type `sealed` whose `key_index` is `n`; or a frame of type `sealed`
    that stands earlier in the segment has the `key_index` `n`. The judgement is made on the envelope fields alone, as `P8-49`
    judges a first use, whatever the link, payload or outcome of the frames. An entry that no sealed frame is under, an entry
    out of the order of first use and a recipient entry that stands in another frame than the frame of its target are thereby
    refused: the first has no sealed frame next after its frame, and the second names a key index other than `n` in the frame
    that follows; the third is refused by `P8-49`. A `keys` frame that breaks the layout is treated as every `keys` frame with a
    `malformed_value` is (`P8-13`): it adds no entry, and none of its recipient entries is tried.

### 4.4 The reader's key input

A reader's key input for sealing is a value of its own, given to it beside the file:

```text
sealing_input = [ segment_keys: list([ key_id: bytes32, segment_key: bytes32 ]),
                  recipient_keys: list([ suite: enum(key_wrap_suite), recipient_key_id: digest, seed: bytes32 ]) ]
```

`seed` is the recipient's decapsulation key in the seed form of its suite (9.1). A reader HOLDS A KEY for a key entry when
`segment_keys` lists its `key_id`, and HOLDS A RECIPIENT KEY for a recipient entry when `recipient_keys` lists its `suite` and
`recipient_key_id`.

A `sealing_input` is VALID exactly when all of these hold:

- no two entries of `segment_keys` have one `key_id`;
- no two entries of `recipient_keys` have one `suite` and one `recipient_key_id`;
- for each entry of `recipient_keys`, the `suite` is a value of `key_wrap_suite` of this registry version and `recipient_key_id`
  equals `H_ctx(recipient_key_id, E([suite, public_key]))` of the public key that the suite derives from the entry's `seed` (9.1).

Two byte-equal entries are two entries with one `key_id`, or with one `suite` and one `recipient_key_id`: a duplicate is invalid
whether or not its entries are equal, so no reader chooses among duplicates, and no policy of first or last applies.

AN INVALID INPUT IS REFUSED. A reader that is given a `sealing_input` that is not valid returns the outcome `sealing_input_refused`
in place of a result of reading. It is an outcome of the reader's interface, not a finding of a file: it has no finding code, no
severity and no place in the typed result of reading (`P7`), and it does not depend on the file. Its fields are `reason`, a value
of the enumeration `sealing_refusal_reason` of `P2` (`duplicate_segment_key`, `duplicate_recipient_key`,
`recipient_key_mismatch`), and `index: uint`, the zero-based position in its list
of the first entry that makes the input invalid, found by judging the entries of `segment_keys` in order and then those of
`recipient_keys` in order, an entry being invalid when it repeats an earlier entry of its list or fails the third rule above (a
`suite` that this registry does not define is `recipient_key_mismatch`). At one entry the rules are judged in the order in which
this section lists them (the first rule for `segment_keys`; the second, then the third, for `recipient_keys`), and the first that
the entry breaks gives the `reason`: an entry of `recipient_keys` that repeats an earlier entry and also fails the third rule is
`duplicate_recipient_key`. The reader makes this judgment before it reads the file:
it opens no frame, unwraps no recipient entry and reports no finding.

1. `P8-16` Two readers of registry version `2` with equal `sealing_input` and equal capacity MUST give the same result for the
    same file; the result MUST NOT depend on any other key material. `sealing_input` holds secrets and is no field of the
    result of reading.
2. `P8-54` A reader MUST judge the validity of its `sealing_input` by the three rules of 4.4, in their order, before it reads the
    file, and MUST return `sealing_input_refused` with the reason and the index of 4.4 for an input that is not valid, whatever the file holds. It
    MUST NOT choose among entries that share a `key_id`, or a `suite` with a `recipient_key_id`, and MUST NOT ignore, replace or
    combine such entries.
3. `P8-55` A reader MUST NOT report `sealing_input_refused` as a finding, MUST NOT put it in the typed result of reading, and MUST
    NOT let it depend on the bytes of the file.
4. `P8-56` A reader MUST check each `recipient_keys` entry by deriving the public key from its `seed` by the key generation of
    the suite (9.1), computing its `recipient_key_id` by `H_ctx(recipient_key_id, E([suite, public_key]))`, and comparing it with
    the entry's `recipient_key_id`.

## 5. The key schedule

The key schedule derives, from one segment key `SK` and the cipher of its key entry, the two keys of the seal: `K_aead`, the key
of AES-256-GCM-SIV, and `K_commit`, the key of the synthetic value. It is HKDF with SHA-256 (RFC 5869):

```text
PRK      = HKDF-Extract(salt = 0^32, IKM = SK)                       = HMAC(0^32, SK)
info(p)  = E([ cipher: enum(sealing_cipher), purpose: enum(seal_key_purpose) ])   with purpose p
K_aead   = HKDF-Expand(PRK, info(aead),   32)                        = HMAC(PRK, info(aead)   || 0x01)
K_commit = HKDF-Expand(PRK, info(commit), 32)                        = HMAC(PRK, info(commit) || 0x01)
```

The right-hand forms are RFC 5869 written out: the extract step is HMAC keyed by the salt over `IKM`, and an output of 32 bytes is
the first block `T(1) = HMAC(PRK, info || 0x01)` of the expansion (its section 2.3). With the one cipher of version `2`,
`info(aead)` is the three bytes `82 01 01` and `info(commit)` the three bytes `82 01 02`, so the two HMAC messages are
`82 01 01 01` and `82 01 02 01`. Three HMAC calls derive both keys; each has a 32-byte key (`0^32` and `PRK`), as `P8-4` requires.

The schedule has no input but `SK` and the cipher. The header, the file, the key context and the frame's place are not in it; they
are in the associated data of each frame (6.2). So one segment key gives the same two keys in every file, and what separates files
under one key is the associated data. The cipher is in `info` so that a later registry version that adds a sealing cipher derives
keys independent of these from the same `SK`.

1. `P8-17` A writer and a reader MUST derive `K_aead` and `K_commit` exactly as above, with the `cipher` of the key entry.
2. `P8-18` `SK` MUST NOT be used except as the input key material of this schedule and as the plaintext of a recipient entry (9);
   `PRK`, `K_aead` and `K_commit` MUST NOT be used except in this schedule, the AEAD and the synthetic value of this part. In
   particular `SK` MUST NOT be an AES key, an HMAC key, a KEM key or a signing key.

## 6. The sealed frame

### 6.1 The clear envelope

A sealed frame is a frame of the container (`A-1`) with these field values:

| field | value |
|---|---|
| `frame_type` | `sealed` (18) |
| `critical`, `transparent` | `true`, `false` |
| `content` | `sealed_content = [ key_index: uint, ordinal: uint, sv: bytes32 ]` |
| `decoded_length` | `len(C)`, the length of the ciphertext |
| `payload_digest` | `H_ctx(payload, C)` |
| `storage` | `stored_plain` |
| `payload` | `C`, the ciphertext with its 16-byte tag |

`key_index` names the key entry of the segment's table that seals the frame; `ordinal` is the frame's own ordinal; `sv` is the
synthetic value (6.3). This is the one place where `decoded_length` and `payload_digest` are defined over bytes that are not a
decoded plaintext: for a sealed frame the "decoded payload" of the container is the ciphertext, so a keyless party checks the
stored bytes against the identifier (`A-2`, `A-3`) without a key and without a codec.

1. `P8-19` A sealed frame MUST have the field values of the table. The frame type and the columns `critical` and `transparent`
    are judged by `P8-6` and by the rule of `P1` on the registry columns, at the step ITEMS of reading, and not here;
    `decoded_length`, `payload_digest` and `payload` are judged by step 1 of 7.2, which takes the stored bytes as `C`, with no
    codec, and by the container's stored check, which runs on it as on any frame that carries one. This rule judges, in this
    order, the `content` against `sealed_content` (the first violation in the order of its bytes, with the reason `P1` gives) and
    then the `storage`: a sealed frame whose `storage` is not `stored_plain` is `malformed_value` with reason
    `forbidden_combination`.
2. `P8-20` `key_index` MUST be smaller than the number of entries the segment's key table holds when the sealed frame is met; a
    larger one is `malformed_value` with reason `out_of_range`.
3. `P8-21` `ordinal` MUST equal the frame's ordinal; a frame whose `ordinal` differs is `malformed_value` with reason
    `sealed_ordinal`. The check needs no key.
4. `P8-22` `len(C)` MUST be at least `17` and at most `2^27 + 16`; another length is `malformed_value` with reason
    `out_of_range`. The check needs no key, so every reader whose capacity admits the frame refuses the same frames; a frame
    above a reader's capacity is `exceeds_capacity` at step 1 of 7.2 and is not judged further.

### 6.2 The associated data

```text
seal_ad = [ header_identifier: digest, key_id: bytes32, context: bytes, key_index: uint, ordinal: uint ]
AD      = E(seal_ad)
```

`header_identifier` is the `h0` of the frame's segment; `key_id` and `context` are those of the key entry that `key_index` names.
`AD` is the associated data given to the cipher, whole: it is at most 153 bytes (`1 + 34 + 34 + 66 + 9 + 9`), ten blocks of 16
bytes, and AES-256-GCM-SIV accepts it as it is.

What `AD` binds, and why each field is there: `h0` binds the frame to its segment and through it to every field of the opener, the
lineage included, so a frame lifted to another segment does not open; `key_id` binds the key entry; `context` separates two files
whose first segments have equal headers under one key; `key_index` and `ordinal` bind the frame to its place, so a frame moved
within its segment, or to another key index, does not open. Not in `AD`: `sv` (an output of the seal), `decoded_length` and
`payload_digest` (functions of the ciphertext, which would make the ciphertext depend on itself), the frame type and flags
(constants of the type, fixed by `P8-6`), the predecessor (a damaged identifier of one frame must not make the next frame
unopenable), and `storage`, `payload` and `signatures` (outside the identifier, `A-2`). A change to any of these that leaves the
ciphertext unchanged changes nothing that a key holder decrypts.

`E(seal_ad)` is a definite-length CBOR array, a self-delimiting item: no encoding of a `seal_ad` is a proper prefix of another
string that begins with an encoding of a `seal_ad`. So `AD || P` determines `AD` and `P` (used in 6.3 and 11.2), and `AD`
determines `seal_ad`.

1. `P8-23` A writer and a reader MUST build `AD` from the segment's `h0`, the key entry named by `key_index`, and the `key_index`
    and `ordinal` of the sealed content, and from nothing else.

### 6.3 The synthetic value and the nonce

```text
sv    = HMAC(K_commit, AD || P)          ; 32 bytes, stored whole in sealed_content
nonce = sv[0..12]
```

`P` is the padded plaintext (6.5). The one value `sv` has two roles: its first 12 bytes are the nonce of the cipher, and the whole
32 bytes are the commitment to `(SK, AD, P)` that the reader recomputes after decryption. The commitment of 11.2 is the
claim for the second role and uses nothing of the cipher; the property of 11.3 is the claim for the first role and for the whole
construction.

1. `P8-24` `sv` MUST be stored and compared whole: a writer and a reader MUST NOT truncate it, and a reader MUST compare all 32
    bytes, in time that does not depend on where the first difference stands.

### 6.4 Sealing a frame

Input: the segment's `h0`; a key entry `[key_id, cipher, context]` at key index `k` with its segment key `SK`; the ordinal `o` of
the frame to be written; the inner frame (6.6).

1. Build the sealed inner record `inner` of the frame (6.6) and `P = pad(E(inner))` (6.5). If `len(P)` exceeds `2^27`, refuse:
   write nothing.
2. If `2^32` frames have been sealed under `SK` by all its holders together (counting every frame sealed under it in any file,
   cut tails and repeated renderings included), refuse: write nothing.
3. Derive `K_aead` and `K_commit` (5).
4. `AD = E([h0, key_id, context, k, o])`.
5. `sv = HMAC(K_commit, AD || P)`; `nonce = sv[0..12]`.
6. `C = AES-256-GCM-SIV.Encrypt(K_aead, nonce, AD, P)`, the ciphertext followed by the 16-byte tag (RFC 8452, section 4), so
   `len(C) = len(P) + 16`.
7. Write the frame of 6.1 with `sealed_content = [k, o, sv]`, `decoded_length = len(C)`, `payload_digest = H_ctx(payload, C)`,
   `payload = C`, and its identifier (`A-1`).

Nothing is random: the same key, segment, key entry, ordinal and inner frame give the same bytes. A writer's two refusals are
errors of the call that asked for the frame; they are not outcomes of reading.

1. `P8-25` A writer MUST seal by exactly these steps, and MUST NOT write a sealed frame that step 1 or step 2 refuses.
2. `P8-26` The count of step 2 MUST be the count of every frame sealed under the segment key by ANY of its holders, and a
    holder that cannot establish that count MUST take a new segment key. A recipient that did not generate a segment key MUST NOT
    seal under it unless the holders keep one count and give their files distinct key contexts.
3. `P8-27` A writer MUST write the `keys` frame that holds a key entry before the first sealed frame under that entry.
4. `P8-28` A segment that holds a frame of type `keys` or `sealed` MUST have `canonical` `absent` and MUST NOT hold an `index`
    frame; a violation is `malformed_value` with reason `forbidden_combination`, reported once for the header, at position `0`, when
    `canonical` is present (the header staying readable), and once at each `index` frame of the segment; a segment with both
    gets all of these findings. Its `offsets` item stays allowed; with no `index` frame the segment has no locator.
5. `P8-29` A writer MUST NOT seal under one segment key two segments that have equal `h0` and equal key contexts. A writer that
    seals several files under one key gives each file's key entry a different `context`; it takes it from a name or counter it
    holds, and MUST NOT take it from a digest of content.

### 6.5 Padding: the one policy

Every sealed plaintext is padded by the function `padme` (the Padmé function of Nikitin and others, "Reducing Metadata Leakage
from Encrypted Files and Communication with PURBs", Proceedings on Privacy Enhancing Technologies 2019, Algorithm 1), stated here
in full so that nothing depends on the reference. For a length `n`:

```text
n < 2:   padme(n) = n
n >= 2:  e = floor(log2(n)),  s = floor(log2(e)) + 1,  z = e - s,  padme(n) = ceil(n / 2^z) * 2^z
pad(x) = x || 0^(padme(len(x)) - len(x))
```

`padme(n)` is the smallest multiple of `2^z` that is not below `n`: the length keeps its `s` leading bits and the rest are rounded
up. Examples: `padme(82) = 88`, `padme(100) = 104`, `padme(129) = 144`, `padme(1000) = 1024`, `padme(2^27) = 2^27`. The overhead
is at most `15/129` of the length (about 11.6 per cent, at `n = 129`), at most 6.25 per cent from 257 bytes and at most 3.125 per
cent from 65,537 bytes. For lengths from `1` to `2^27` there are 524 padded lengths, so a padded length tells at most about nine
bits of the length it hides. Every `n` up to `2^27` pads to at most `2^27`, because `2^27` is a multiple of every `2^z` that
arises below it.

One policy, applied to every sealed frame, with no parameter: a pad policy per key would be a writer choice that leaks which
policy was chosen and gives two encodings of one plaintext.

1. `P8-30` The plaintext of every sealed frame MUST be `pad(E(inner))`.
2. `P8-31` A reader MUST decode from `P` one complete deterministic item as `inner` (6.6), and then MUST require that the
    remaining bytes number exactly `padme(n) - n`, where `n` is the length of that item, and are all zero. A violation of the
    second requirement is `malformed_value` with reason `padding`. A first item that does not decode as `inner` is judged by 6.6
    first.

### 6.6 The sealed inner record

```text
sealed_inner   = [ inner_type: enum(sealed_inner_type), content: inner_content, decoded_length: uint,
                   payload_digest: digest, storage: sealed_storage, payload: bytes ]
sealed_storage = [1]                         ; stored_plain: payload is the decoded payload
               / [2, codec: enum(codec)]     ; stored_compressed: payload is the codec output over the decoded payload
```

`sealed_inner` is a variant on `sealed_inner_type`: its tag decides the record `inner_content` and the decoded payload, which are
those that the table of `sealed_inner_type` (3.2) names: the content record and decoded payload of the frame type of the same
label in registry version `1`, and for `sealed_members` those of 8.3. `decoded_length` and `payload_digest` are the inner frame's
own: the length of its decoded payload `d` and `H_ctx(payload, d)`, exactly as for a clear frame of that type. `sealed_storage`
takes its tags from `storage_kind`; it has no stored check, because the cipher authenticates every byte. A compressed inner
payload obeys every rule that the container sets for the compressed payload of a clear frame.

Compression happens before sealing and is chosen per frame inside the seal. Compressing in one frame a secret together with data
an adversary influences lets an observer of lengths learn about the secret (10.2); padding narrows that channel and does not close
it.

1. `P8-32` `inner` MUST be a value of `sealed_inner` under the typed rules of section 0; a value outside it (a wrong length, an
   unregistered tag, a field of the wrong type, a non-deterministic encoding) is `malformed_value` with the reason of the rule
   broken.
2. `P8-33` `inner_content` MUST be the content record of the type its tag names and MUST satisfy every rule that registry
    version `1` sets for that type; a violation is reported with the outcome a clear frame of that type gets. In a segment
    whose `registry_version` is above the reader's, an enumeration value of `inner_content` that the reader does not hold is
    no violation: it is judged after step 9 of 7.2 has passed, as the values of a clear frame are judged after its payload, and
    the inner frame gets the outcome of `P2-62`; every inner type is critical, so the sealed frame gives `unsupported_critical`
    with the subject that a clear frame of the inner type gives, stops the fold of the segment, every later frame is `stopped`,
    the sealed frame is opaque with `unsupported_critical` and it counts in `unopened_sealed`.
3. `P8-34` A writer MUST NOT store in the clear, in the same segment, a frame that it also seals: a frame is either clear or
    sealed.

### 6.7 The inner frame

When a sealed frame opens and its inner record passes every check (7.2), the INNER FRAME is a frame of the type that `inner_type`
names, with the content record, decoded length, payload digest and decoded payload of `inner`, standing at the sealed frame's
position, with the sealed frame's identifier and predecessor. It is folded, and checked, by the rules of its type exactly as a
clear frame of that type would be at that position; its `critical` and `transparent` columns are those of the type in the
registry. An inner `suppress` frame is a folded `suppress` frame like any other, and is authenticated exactly when the sealed
frame that carries it is an authenticated frame.

## 7. Opening a sealed frame

### 7.1 Constants

- `max_sealed_plaintext`. value: `2^27` bytes of padded plaintext `P`; what fixes it: the bound of 11.3 at the per-key
  limit: with this length and `max_seals_per_key`, one key contributes at most `2^-42.33`.
- `max_seals_per_key`. value: `2^32` frames sealed under one segment key by all its holders, over all files; what fixes it:
  the bound of 11.3 at the per-key limit: the triple-nonce term is then `2^-98.59` and the AEAD term `2^-42.33` per key.
- `max_failed_openings`. value: `2^10` openings per segment key, within one read; what fixes it: the bound of 11.3:
  each failed opening of full length adds at most `2^-74.33` to the AEAD term, so `2^10` of them add at most `2^-64.33`.
- `max_key_context`. value: `64` bytes; what fixes it: bounds `AD` at 153 bytes, ten blocks, the figure the AEAD term uses (11.3).

### 7.2 The procedure

Input: a frame of type `sealed`, the segment's `h0` and key table, and the reader's `sealing_input`. Every step runs in this
order; the first that ends the procedure gives the outcome, and later steps do not run.

1. CONTAINER CHECKS. The link and the payload check of the container run first and need no key (`A-3`): the payload check compares
   `decoded_length` with `len(payload)` and `payload_digest` with `H_ctx(payload, the stored payload bytes)`. A payload that fails
   is `damaged_frame` with its reason; the procedure ends there, so the frame has no finding of steps 2 to 10, it is not opened
   and no opening is counted. A frame whose link state is `broken` is
   not judged by steps 2 to 10: its content is not interpreted, it is not opened, and no opening is counted.
2. CLEAR CONTENT. `P8-19` to `P8-22` are judged in the order of their numbers, and the first that the frame breaks gives the one
   `malformed_value` of the frame, with its reason; the frame is not opened.
3. KEY. The key entry is the one at `key_index`. Its segment key is resolved by 9.4. If no segment key results, the frame is not
   opened: opaque with reason `missing_key`, and the outcome is `segment_key_not_held` or `key_conflict` as 9.4 gives it.
4. COUNT. The sealed frames are opened in ascending position within a segment and the segments in file order, and the count runs
   in that order. If the reader has counted `max_failed_openings` failed openings under this segment key in this read, the frame is not
   opened: opaque with reason `exceeds_capacity`, finding `exceeds_capacity` with reason `failed_openings`.
5. DERIVE. `K_aead`, `K_commit` (5); `AD` (6.2); `nonce = sv[0..12]`.
6. DECRYPT. `P = AES-256-GCM-SIV.Decrypt(K_aead, nonce, AD, C)`. If the cipher refuses: `seal_failed` with reason `aead`; the
   frame is opaque with reason `damaged`, its payload state `damaged`, and one failed opening is counted.
7. COMMITMENT. `sv' = HMAC(K_commit, AD || P)`; if `sv'` differs from `sv` (`P8-24`): `seal_failed` with reason `commitment`; `P`
   is discarded unread; the frame is opaque with reason `damaged`, its payload state `damaged`, and one failed opening is counted.
8. INNER. Judge, in this order, `P8-32` (the first item of `P` against `sealed_inner`, the first violation in the order of its
   bytes), then `P8-31` (the padding after that item), then `P8-33` (the rules of the inner type). The first that fails gives
   the one `malformed_value`, with its reason (a `P` that holds no complete item: `wrong_length`); the frame is opaque with reason
   `malformed`.
9. INNER PAYLOAD. Check the inner payload by steps 1 to 7 of the payload check of `P1` ("The payload check", 5.4), in their
   order, applied to the inner `storage`, `payload`, `decoded_length` and `payload_digest`; the first step that fails decides.
   Beyond the reader's capacity the outcome is `exceeds_capacity` with the reason that step 1 of that check gives
   (`decoded_size`, or `total_decoded` when the inner `decoded_length` would bring the total decoded length of the read
   above the reader's limit), the frame opaque with reason `exceeds_capacity` and its payload state `unchecked`. Otherwise the payload is decoded by `storage` and the inner
   `decoded_length` and `payload_digest` are checked. A failure is `damaged_frame` with reason `decode_failed`,
   `decoded_length` or `payload_digest`, the reason of the first failing step; the frame is opaque with reason `damaged` and its
   payload state `damaged`.
10. FOLD. The inner frame (6.7) is folded at the frame's position.

A sealed frame that is not opened at step 3 or 4 keeps the payload state of step 1 and is counted in `unopened_sealed`, the count
of sealed frames not opened that the result of reading reports; so is a frame not opened at step 1 or 2, and a frame whose
opening ends at steps 6 to 9: the count is of every sealed frame that is not folded.

1. `P8-35` A reader of registry version `2` MUST open every sealed frame by the steps of 7.2, in that order.
2. `P8-36` A reader MUST NOT expose, fold or keep any byte of `P` before step 7 has passed, and MUST NOT fold any part of the
    inner frame before step 9 has passed.
3. `P8-37` A reader MUST count, per segment key and within one read, the sealed frames that end at step 6 or step 7, and MUST
    stop opening frames under that key when the count reaches `max_failed_openings` (step 4), the frames being opened in
    ascending position within a segment and the segments in file order. A frame that ends at an earlier step
    is not counted, so damage in storage, which step 1 finds, never uses up the count.
4. `P8-38` A reader without a usable key MUST still run step 1 on every sealed frame and step 2 on every sealed frame whose link
    state is `linked` or `healed`, and MUST continue with the next item;
    a sealed frame that is not opened MUST NOT stop the fold of the segment.

### 7.3 What each outcome says

`seal_failed` with `aead` says that `(nonce, AD, C)` is not a ciphertext of the cipher under `K_aead`; with `commitment`, that the
cipher accepted it and the recomputed synthetic value differs. A keyless party that submits frames and observes which of the two a
reader reports learns whether a triple it chose is accepted by the bare cipher: a verification oracle for AES-256-GCM-SIV. The
property of 11.3 allows the adversary exactly that oracle, and its bound pays for it, so the two reasons stay distinct. A
failure after step 7 (steps 8 and 9) is reported only for a frame that the cipher and the commitment accepted, so it is no oracle
for a party without the key. AES-256-GCM-SIV does not commit to its key: anyone who knows a set of candidate keys can build one
`(nonce, AD, C)` that the bare cipher accepts under each of them, and a reader's report of `commitment` rather than `aead` then
tells the submitter that the reader's key is in the set. With uniform keys this is of no use; with guessable keys the two reasons
are a partitioning oracle, and such keys are outside every claim (`P8-7`).

## 8. Sealed packs and the sealed member index

### 8.1 The unit of sealing for blobs is the pack

Blob bytes are sealed only as SEALED PACKS: sealed frames whose inner type is `pack`. The inner pack is a pack of registry version
`1`, with its directory and bodies, so every member's kind, content record, digest and length is inside the ciphertext.
Chunking, deduplication and compression happen on the plaintext before sealing, so a key holder deduplicates units within a key as
in a clear file, and nothing about the units is visible but the padded length of each pack. No keyed chunker and no keyed locator
exist: a unit's boundaries and digest are those of the clear format, and they are never in the clear for a sealed unit.

1. `P8-39` A writer MUST NOT write a `chunk`, `blob`, `recipe` or `value` frame of a unit or blob that it seals: the unit is a
    member of a sealed pack, and the pack is the sealed frame.
2. `P8-40` A key holder MUST include every member of every sealed pack it opened in the unit index used to rebuild blobs and
    values (the container's rebuilding of blobs and values), at the sealed frame's ordinal, exactly as a member of a clear pack.
3. `P8-63` A key holder MUST charge the `recipe` and `value` members of a sealed pack it opened to the total rebuilt length of
    the read (the container's "The total rebuilt length") at the position of the sealed frame in item order, in directory order,
    before it rebuilds any of them, and a reader MUST charge nothing for a sealed pack it did not open. `P8-42` is judged on the
    entries of a refused declaration as on those of an admitted one: it reads the digests the entries name and no byte.

### 8.2 Seal scope

A clear frame that names, by its digest, a unit held only inside sealed packs discloses that digest to every party, who can then
test a guess of the sealed content.

1. `P8-41` A writer MUST NOT write a clear frame whose recipe, value or member entry names a unit digest that only sealed packs
    of the segment hold.
2. `P8-42` A key holder that resolves an entry of a clear `recipe` or `value` frame, or of a clear pack member, only to a unit
    held inside a sealed pack MUST report `seal_scope_violated` at the clear frame, once for that frame; the fold is unchanged.

### 8.3 The sealed member index

Opening a sealed frame authenticates all of it (7.2), so finding one unit in a segment of sealed packs without an index costs the
opening of every pack. The SEALED MEMBER INDEX is a sealed frame of inner type `sealed_members`, under one key index, that lists
the members of that key's sealed packs. A segment that holds sealed frames has no clear `index` frame (`P8-28`), because the clear
index's statistics, graph directory, content digest and pack members are functions of plaintext.

```text
sealed_members.content = [ first: uint, entry_count: uint ]
sealed_members payload = list(member_entry)          ; the decoded payload is E of this list
member_entry           = [ unit_digest: digest, ordinal: uint, member_index: uint ]
```

The COVERAGE of a `sealed_members` frame at ordinal `o` is the ordinals from `first` to `o - 1`. Its entries list every `chunk`
and `blob` member of every sealed pack under the SAME key index whose ordinal lies in the coverage: the member's unit digest, the
pack's ordinal and the member's zero-based index in the pack's directory, sorted ascending bytewise on `E(unit_digest)` (the
bytes compared as unsigned integers from the first byte, a proper prefix before the longer string), then ascending on `ordinal`,
then ascending on `member_index`, the integers compared as numbers. When `first` is above `0`, the frame at ordinal `first` is the PREVIOUS member index: a sealed frame under
the same key index whose inner type is `sealed_members`, whose coverage ends at `first - 1`. The index of a key is so a chain from
the last `sealed_members` frame back to one whose `first` is `0`; the ordinal `0` is never a sealed frame, because a `keys` frame
stands before every sealed frame (`P8-27`).

A member entry is a CLAIM, as the clear index is: a unit is accepted only when its bytes match its digest, so a false entry
can hide a unit or name a wrong frame and never yields wrong content; and a digest the index does not list is not therefore
absent.

1. `P8-43` `entry_count` MUST equal the length of the payload list, `first` MUST be smaller than the frame's own ordinal, and
    the entries MUST be in the stated order with no repetition; a violation is `malformed_value` with reason
    `inconsistent_counts`, `out_of_range` (a `first` that names the frame itself or a later frame) or `unordered_or_repeated`.
2. `P8-44` A reader that opens every sealed frame of a key index MUST compare that key's chain of member indexes with the packs
    it opened: one `index_claim_failed` with reason `sealed_members`, at the position of the member index whose claim fails, for each entry that its pack does not bear
    out, for each member of a pack in a coverage that no entry lists, and for each `first` above `0` that does not name a valid
    previous member index. A reader that follows a chain of member indexes MUST require strictly decreasing ordinals. It MUST NOT
    report a unit as missing on the index alone.
3. `P8-45` A writer that seals at least one pack under a key index MUST write the `sealed_members` frames of that key index as
    follows, and no other `sealed_members` frame under it. The sealed packs under the key index, in ascending ordinal, are cut into
    consecutive RUNS: each run is the longest run of consecutive packs, beginning at the first pack that no earlier run holds,
    whose `chunk` and `blob` members together number at most `Nr` (`1048576`, the count constant of the entry frames of `P7`,
    "The constants"); a pack that alone holds more members than that forms a run by itself. One `sealed_members` frame covers each
    run. It stands immediately after the last sealed pack of its run, so that its coverage ends at the ordinal just before its own,
    and its `first` is the ordinal of the previous `sealed_members` frame under the same key index, or `0` when there is none. A key
    index has one `sealed_members` frame when the members of all its packs number at most `Nr`. The value it brings: a reader that looks up one unit opens the chain of member indexes
    and the frames no coverage includes, instead of every sealed pack of the key.
4. `P8-62` A reader that opens a `sealed_members` frame MUST judge its place and cut, after the rules of `P8-43` and before it
    compares the claims of the frame (`P8-44`), and MUST report a frame that breaks them as `malformed_value` with the reason
    `unordered_or_repeated`. A `sealed_members` frame BREAKS THE LAYOUT when its coverage holds no sealed pack under its key index;
    or the ordinary frame before it is not a sealed pack under its key index (judged on the envelope for the key index and on the
    inner type of a frame that was opened; a frame that was not opened to an inner frame counts as a pack); or its coverage holds
    more than `Nr` members of more than one pack; or the next sealed pack under its key index after the frame, with no member
    index of that key index between them, has members that together with those of the coverage number at most `Nr`. The frame is then opaque with `malformed`, lists no entry and is no
    member index for `P8-44`. A reader that opens every sealed frame of a key index MUST also form the runs of `P8-45` from the
    packs it opened, and MUST report, for each run whose last pack is not directly followed by an ordinary frame that is a
    `sealed_members` frame under the same key index, one `index_claim_failed` with the reason `sealed_members`, at the position of
    that last pack: an absent claim is a failed claim.

A reader that looks up one unit under a key opens the sealed frames of that key from the end of the segment backwards until one is
a `sealed_members` frame, follows `first` from there opening member indexes only, and then also opens, in ascending ordinal, every sealed
frame of the key that no coverage of that chain includes and that the backward pass has not already opened. A frame that the
chain names by a `first` is opened as a part of the chain even when it then proves to be no member index (`P8-62`); the chain
ends there, and that frame is not opened again. The frames from the end of the segment backwards are opened in
descending ordinal. A lookup is a use of the format, not a different reading: the reading of 7.2 opens every
frame.

## 9. The key wrap

### 9.1 The suite

The one key-wrap suite, `hpke_base_xwing`, transports a segment key to a recipient by HPKE (RFC 9180) in base mode with the
single-shot interface, with every choice fixed:

- mode: `mode_base`, `0x00`; `psk` and `psk_id` empty (RFC 9180, sections 5 and 5.1.1)
- KEM: X-Wing, `kem_id` `0x647A`, exactly as `draft-connolly-cfrg-xwing-kem-11` defines it; `Nsecret` 32, `Nenc` 1,120, `Npk`
  1,216, `Nsk` 32
- KDF: HKDF-SHA256, `kdf_id` `0x0001`, `Nh` 32
- AEAD: AES-256-GCM, `aead_id` `0x0002`, `Nk` 32, `Nn` 12, `Nt` 16
- `suite_id`: the ten bytes `48 50 4b 45 64 7a 00 01 00 02` (`HPKE` and the three identifiers, two bytes each, big-endian)
- `aad`: the empty string
- `pt`: `SK`, 32 bytes, so `wrapped` is 48 bytes

The two bytes `64 7a` are a constant of `suite_id`; the KEM is the construction of that draft revision, whatever a registry cites
later. A recipient's public key is the 1,216-byte X-Wing encapsulation key; its decapsulation key is held as the 32-byte X-Wing
seed, from which the encapsulation key is derived by that draft's key generation. The HPKE function `DeriveKeyPair` is not used.
The X25519 output inside X-Wing is used as that draft computes it, with no further check.

### 9.2 The recipient entry

```text
recipient_entry = [ suite: enum(key_wrap_suite), recipient_key_id: digest, target_key_id: bytes32,
                    encapsulation: bytes, wrapped: bytes ]
wrap_info       = [ header_identifier: digest, suite: enum(key_wrap_suite), recipient_key_id: digest, target_key_id: bytes32 ]
```

`recipient_key_id` is `H_ctx(recipient_key_id, E([suite, public_key]))` of the recipient's public key; `target_key_id` names the
key entry whose segment key is wrapped; `encapsulation` is HPKE's `enc` and `wrapped` its ciphertext. The HPKE `info` is
`E(wrap_info)`, 104 bytes, with `header_identifier` the segment's `h0`. It binds the segment, the suite, the recipient's key
identifier and the target key; `suite_id` binds the KEM, KDF and AEAD.

WRAP, by the writer: `(encapsulation, wrapped) = SealBase(pkR, info = E(wrap_info), aad = "", pt = SK)`, with `pkR` the
recipient's public key and the encapsulation randomness drawn from a cryptographic random generator.

UNWRAP, by a reader that holds the recipient key: `SK' = OpenBase(encapsulation, skR, info = E(wrap_info), aad = "", wrapped)`,
with `skR` derived from the held seed. A decapsulation error or a refusal of the AEAD is a failure of the entry.

The key schedule of HPKE base mode, written out from RFC 9180 sections 4 and 5.1 so that it can be built from HKDF:
`LabeledExtract(salt, label, ikm) = HKDF-Extract(salt, "HPKE-v1" || suite_id || label || ikm)`;
`LabeledExpand(prk, label, x, L) = HKDF-Expand(prk, I2OSP(L, 2) || "HPKE-v1" || suite_id || label || x, L)`; with
`(shared_secret, enc) = Encap(pkR)`: `psk_id_hash = LabeledExtract("", "psk_id_hash", "")`,
`info_hash = LabeledExtract("", "info_hash", info)`, `context = 0x00 || psk_id_hash || info_hash`,
`secret = LabeledExtract(shared_secret, "secret", "")`, `key = LabeledExpand(secret, "key", context, 32)`,
`base_nonce = LabeledExpand(secret, "base_nonce", context, 12)`, and `wrapped = AES-256-GCM.Seal(key, base_nonce, "", SK)`. The
labels are those of RFC 9180 and are not values of this format. The empty salt of `LabeledExtract` is an HMAC key of length `0`:
HPKE's internal keys are as RFC 9180 fixes them and lie outside `P8-4` and the property of 11.2.

1. `P8-46` A writer MUST wrap and a reader MUST unwrap exactly as above; a reader MUST NOT apply any other key schedule, KEM, KDF
    or AEAD to a recipient entry.
2. `P8-47` A writer MUST draw the encapsulation randomness of every wrap from a cryptographic random generator, MUST NOT derive
    it from any secret or from the wrapped key, and MUST NOT reuse it.
3. `P8-60` A recipient public key that a writer is given MUST be `bytes` of exactly 1,216 bytes; a key of another length is an
    error of the wrap call. Any check of the key that the primitive makes is the primitive's own, internal to `Encap` and to
    `Decap`: its failure is an error of the wrap call or a failure of the entry (9.2), and no finding depends on it. The format
    gives no meaning to any part of `encapsulation`, `wrapped` or a public key; the primitive of the one document of 9.1 splits them.

### 9.3 Rules of recipient entries

The rules of this subsection cost the one entry that breaks them: the entry is not tried, and the rest of its `keys` frame is
applied. The finding is one for each such entry, at the `keys` frame, and it does not make the frame opaque. The rules are judged
on an entry in this order: `P8-48`; the first clause of `P8-49` (the target); the second clause of `P8-49` (the first use);
`P8-57` (the order within the list, which also refuses a repeated pair). The first that the entry breaks gives its one finding
and its reason. The entries that have one target stand in one frame, the frame of that key (`P8-58`, `P8-49`), so they stand in
this ORDER OF ENTRIES: by their index in the list of that frame. An entry is ADMITTED when its frame is applied (`P8-13`) and it
breaks none of the rules of this subsection; an entry that breaks any rule is not tried (9.4).

1. `P8-48` `encapsulation` MUST be exactly 1,120 bytes and `wrapped` exactly 48 bytes; another length is `malformed_value` with
    reason `wrong_length` for the entry.
2. `P8-49` `target_key_id` MUST be the `key_id` of an entry of the key table after the entries of the same frame are added;
    another value is `malformed_value` with reason `out_of_range` for the entry. A recipient entry whose target key entry already
    has a sealed frame under it earlier in the segment is `malformed_value` with reason `unordered_or_repeated` for the entry: the
    recipient entries of a key are final before its first use. First use is judged on the envelope: any frame of type `sealed`
    earlier in the segment whose `key_index` names the target, whatever its link, payload or outcome; the comparison is of
    key indexes, so a frame that named the index before the entry existed counts.
3. `P8-50` Two recipient entries of one segment with equal `recipient_key_id` and equal `target_key_id` MUST NOT exist. A repeat
    within one frame is refused by `P8-57`, and a repeat in another frame by `P8-49`, since its target has a sealed frame under it;
    no further rule judges it.

### 9.4 Resolving the segment key of a key entry

The resolution runs on a valid `sealing_input` (4.4): an input that is not valid was refused before the file was read, so that in
`segment_keys` at most one entry lists a `key_id`, and in `recipient_keys` at most one entry lists a `suite` with a
`recipient_key_id`, whose seed derives that identifier. Before it opens the first sealed frame under a key entry, a reader resolves
that entry's segment key, once for the read:

1. CANDIDATES. If `segment_keys` lists the entry's `key_id`, that key is a candidate. For every recipient entry of the segment
   whose target is this entry, that `9.3` admits, and for which the reader holds a recipient key, taken in the ORDER OF ENTRIES
   (9.3), the reader unwraps it with the seed of that one recipient key; each success is a candidate, and each failure is one `unwrap_failed`, reported at the first
   sealed frame under the entry.
2. CONFLICT. If two candidates differ, the entry has no segment key: `key_conflict`, reported at the first sealed frame under the
   entry; every sealed frame under it is opaque with reason `missing_key`.
3. NONE. If there is no candidate, the entry has no segment key: one `segment_key_not_held`, at the first sealed frame under the
   entry, with `count` the number of sealed frames under it in the segment; every such frame is opaque with reason `missing_key`.
4. Otherwise the one candidate is the entry's segment key.

The recipient entries of a key are final before its first use (`P8-49`), so the resolution never changes after a frame under the
key was opened, and a streaming reader never waits.

1. `P8-51` A reader MUST resolve the segment key of a key entry by 9.4 before it opens any sealed frame under it, and MUST try
    every admitted recipient entry for which it holds a recipient key.
2. `P8-52` A recipient MUST hold its decapsulation key in the 32-byte seed form of 9.1. (The X-Wing draft states, in its section
    5.5.1, that the binding properties it names `MAL-BIND-K-PK` and `MAL-BIND-K-CT` do not hold when the regular ML-KEM
    decapsulation key is transmitted between implementations. Neither property is part of assumption `S-4`.)

A recipient entry gives confidentiality of `SK` toward everyone but the recipient. It authenticates no sender: anyone who holds
the recipient's public key can write one (12, item 4). It does not commit to the key: two recipients can be given entries that
unwrap to different keys, and what protects a frame then is the conflict rule for one reader and the commitment for two readers (11.2).

## 10. What a party without keys can do, and what leaks

### 10.1 What it can do

A party that holds no key (a relay, a mirror, a verifier of structure) can, with no key and no codec: delimit every item; compute
and check every identifier, the link of every frame, every commit and every signature; check every sealed frame's ciphertext
against its `decoded_length` and `payload_digest` (7.2 step 1), so that it finds accidental damage; check the clear content of
every sealed frame (7.2 step 2), the ordinal included, so that it finds a sealed frame moved within its segment; read the key
table and the recipient entries; count sealed frames per key index; and carry any segment verbatim. Nothing in this part requires
a key to carry a segment or to verify its chain.

It cannot: open a sealed frame, check `sv` or the tag, learn an inner type, content, length before padding or digest, fold the
segment, compute its content digest, or resolve a binding, a suppression entry or a recipe entry that names sealed content.

### 10.2 What leaks, by design

Every clear value of a sealed frame and of a `keys` frame is in one of these classes, and nothing else is in the clear:

- that a frame is sealed, its position and the order of all frames. from: the envelope; how much: exactly.
- how many sealed frames each key index seals, and which key index seals each. from: `key_index`; how much: exactly.
- the padded length of each sealed plaintext. from: `decoded_length`, `len(C) - 16`; how much: `padme` keeps the leading `s` bits
  of the length; at most about nine bits below `2^27` (6.5).
- equality of two sealings of one `(SK, AD, P)`. from: equal `sv` and equal `C`; how much: only between frames with equal `AD`, so
  at one place of one segment of one key context: two renderings of one file under one key and one context show which frames are
  unchanged.
- the key identifiers, key contexts and recipient key identifiers, and how many recipients each key has. from: the `keys` frame;
  how much: exactly; a recipient key identifier names a public key.
- that two files share a segment key. from: equal `key_id`; how much: exactly, when the writer reuses a key identifier.

The compression channel. A sealed plaintext is compressed before padding; an observer who controls part of a plaintext and sees
padded lengths learns about the rest of it. Padding narrows the channel to the leading bits of the compressed length and does not
close it. The writer's defence is `sealed_storage` `stored_plain` for a frame that mixes a secret with data an adversary
influences.

Equality. Deterministic encryption shows equality of identical inputs, and the property of 11.3 is stated with it: it is part
of the claim, not a gap in it. Because `AD` holds `h0`, the key context, the key index and the ordinal, equal ciphertexts arise
only for the same frame at the same place under the same key, the same segment header and the same context.

1. `P8-53` A reader MUST NOT derive any value of a sealed frame's inner frame from its clear fields; the only clear values of a
    sealed frame are those of 6.1.

## 11. Security considerations

This section states the properties that the construction of sections 4 to 10 is designed to have, the assumptions each rests
on, and their scope. It states no requirement. Section 12 lists what is not claimed.

### 11.1 Assumptions about the primitives

The properties below rest on these assumptions and, where a file is concerned, on the assumptions `A-1` to `A-3` about the
container (section 1).

- **`S-0`. SHA-256 is collision resistant.** FIPS 180-4 defines the function. The generic classical cost of a collision is
  `2^128` evaluations. No figure is assumed against a quantum adversary.
- **`S-1`. The key schedule.** From a uniform 32-byte `SK`, the pair `(K_aead, K_commit)` of section 5 is indistinguishable from
  two uniform independent keys. The extract step, `PRK = HMAC(0^32, SK)`, relies on the SHA-256 compression function being a
  pseudo-random function when it is keyed through its message input; the expand step is `S-2` for one key and two inputs.
- **`S-2`. HMAC-SHA-256 is a pseudo-random function** under a uniform 32-byte key. For `u` keys the advantage assumed is at most
  `u` times that for one key.
- **`S-3`. AES-256-GCM-SIV is a multi-user misuse-resistant authenticated encryption scheme**, with AES-256 modelled as an
  ideal cipher, as Theorem 6 of Bose, Hoang and Tessaro ("Revisiting AES-GCM-SIV: Multi-user Security, Faster Key Derivation,
  and Better Bounds", EUROCRYPT 2018, full version) states it. That AES-256-GCM-SIV is tidy is a fact of its specification
  (section 2) and not an assumption.
- **`S-4`. X-Wing is an IND-CCA key encapsulation mechanism** in the random-oracle model for SHA3-256, SHA3-512 and SHAKE-256,
  as `draft-connolly-cfrg-xwing-kem-11` states it. The binding properties that the draft names `MAL-BIND-K-PK` and
  `MAL-BIND-K-CT` are not part of this assumption and no property of this section uses them; the draft states that they do not
  hold when the regular ML-KEM decapsulation key is transmitted between implementations, which is why a recipient key has the
  seed form of `P8-52`.
- **`S-5`. AES-256-GCM is a one-time authenticated encryption scheme under many independent keys.** NIST SP 800-38D defines the
  mode. Each key of the wrap encrypts exactly one 32-byte message under one nonce.

`R-1`. The keyed mode and the key-derivation mode of BLAKE3 are used in no keyed role of this part. Collision resistance of
`H_ctx` is an assumption of the container; this part relies on it only to read equal header identifiers as equal headers, and in
items 13 and 16 of section 12.

### 11.2 Commitment

A sealed frame opens, up to a collision of SHA-256, under at most one `(SK, AD)` and to at most one `P`. So two readers that hold
different segment keys for one key entry, or two recipients whose entries unwrap to different keys, can never both accept one
sealed frame: one of them reports `seal_failed`. A reader that gets two keys for one entry opens nothing under it (9.4). What `P`
decodes to is fixed by `P` itself and the inner record's own checks; the commitment is to the sealed bytes `P`, padding included.

The property rests on `S-0` alone: it uses nothing of AES-256-GCM-SIV, of the secrecy or randomness of any key, or of any
idealised primitive. It needs every HMAC key to have exactly 32 bytes (`P8-4`): HMAC-SHA-256 has trivial collisions between a key
`K` and `K || 00`, and between a key longer than the block of 64 bytes and its digest. It needs `AD || P` to determine `AD` and
`P` (6.2). A cipher that a later registry version adds is named in `info` of the key schedule (section 5), and the property holds
for it when it keeps this schedule and this synthetic value.

### 11.3 Confidentiality and integrity under a segment key

For segment keys that are uniform and independent (`P8-7`) and that no adversary holds, and with AES-256 modelled as an ideal
cipher, the sealing of sections 5 to 7 is a deterministic authenticated encryption under many keys:

- the sealed frames show a party without the key nothing about their plaintexts except the padded length and which sealings
  have equal `(SK, AD, P)` (10.2);
- a party without the key produces no sealed frame that a reader opens, other than a frame that a holder of the key sealed for
  the same associated data.

The property rests on `S-1`, `S-2` and `S-3`. Its bound is the bound of `S-3` with these further terms: for the key schedule and
HMAC, the advantages of `S-1` and `S-2`, for which no figure is stated; `q_e^3 / (6 * 2^192)` for three frames of one key with one
synthetic nonce; `q_d / 2^256`; and `q_e * q_d / 2^136`. Here `q_e` is the number of frames sealed under a key and `q_d` the
number of openings under it of frames that an adversary submits.

WHAT THE PER-KEY LIMITS KEEP. With `max_sealed_plaintext` and `max_seals_per_key` (7.1), the bound of `S-3` for one key is at most
`2^-42.33` and the term of three frames with one nonce at most `2^-98.59`; a key seals at most `2^59` bytes. One failed opening of
full length adds at most `2^-74.33`, so `max_failed_openings` keeps the failed openings of one read at `2^-64.33` for each key.
`max_key_context` keeps the associated data at ten blocks of the cipher, the figure these numbers use. For `u` keys each at its
limit the first figure is `u * 2^-42.33`; it is proportional to the longest plaintext and to the total volume, so a deployment
that seals under many keys keeps its own count of the total volume.

HOW THE LIMITS COUNT. The limits are for one key and count its TOTAL use: `q_e` counts the frames sealed under the key by all of
its holders together (`P8-26`), and `q_d` counts the failed openings under it by every reader to which an adversary can submit
frames. No one writer can enforce them alone. Each reader counts failed openings within one read (`P8-37`), and no reader can
count across reads or files: a service that opens frames submitted by a party that does not hold the key bounds its failed
openings over its whole life.

NOT COVERED. A segment key that is not uniform: a key derived from a password, or from any secret an adversary can guess, is
outside the property, and any one sealed frame lets a holder of the file test a guess offline by the tag alone. An adversary with
a quantum computer: no figure is stated against it, and no claim is made against an adversary with superposition access to a
keyed primitive.

### 11.4 Keys wrapped to recipients

The property of 11.3 extends to a segment key that a file also wraps to honest recipients (section 9): the recipient entries
show a party that holds no recipient decapsulation key nothing about the segment key, even when that party obtains the
unwrapping, under any label, of other entries of its choice. This rests on `S-0`, `S-2`, `S-4` and `S-5`, with SHA3-256, SHA3-512 and SHAKE-256 modelled as random oracles that the key wrap shares with nothing else.

The property says nothing about who wrote a recipient entry or a key entry (section 12, items 4 and 16).

### 11.5 What a reader of a file gets

The setting. An honest writer holds segment keys that are uniform and independent (`P8-7`), named by key entries whose key
identifiers are independent of the keys (`P8-8`), and is the ONLY holder of those keys that seals. An adversary holds no segment
key of the writer and no recipient decapsulation key, obtains sealed frames from the writer for plaintexts and places of its
choice, and submits files to readers. A reader holds the writer's segment key directly, and holds no recipient key for an entry
whose target is a key entry of the writer.

- INTEGRITY. A sealed frame that such a reader opens, at ordinal `o` and key index `k` of a segment whose header identifier is
  `h0`, under a key entry `[key_id, cipher, ctx]`, holds a plaintext that the writer sealed under that key with that `h0`,
  `key_id`, `ctx`, `k` and `o`, except with the probability of 11.3, or of 11.4 when the writer also wraps its keys. So a sealed
  frame moved to another ordinal, another key index, another key entry or another key context, or into a segment with another
  header identifier, is not opened; and a reader folds, under a key it holds, only inner frames that the writer sealed for that
  exact place.
- CONFIDENTIALITY. An adversary that learns from each read only the outcomes of steps 1 to 7 of 7.2 for each sealed frame, and
  not the findings of steps 8 to 10, which are functions of the plaintext, learns of the plaintexts only their padded lengths
  and the pattern of equal `(SK, AD, P)`, except with twice that probability.

These use `A-1` (what an identifier covers) and `A-3` (what a reader computes is a function of the file). No property is stated for a reader whose segment key comes from a recipient entry (section 12, items 4 and 20), for a
holder other than the writer that seals (item 7), or for what a party learns from the findings of steps 8 to 10 (item 19).

## 12. What is not claimed

Each item states what is not claimed, its consequence, and the remedy, where the container offers one. "An authenticating
signature" is a signature of the container's signature mechanism (outside this document) over the identifier of a frame or of a
commit frame, by a key the reader trusts; the result of reading says which frames such signatures cover.

1. TRAFFIC ANALYSIS. The existence, position, order, key index and padded length of every sealed frame, the number of sealed
   frames per key, and the key table are in the clear (10.2). Nothing hides them.
2. EQUALITY. Identical `(SK, AD, P)` give identical bytes; this is what deterministic authenticated encryption means (11.3). Two renderings of one file under one key and one key context show which frames are
   unchanged.
3. FRESHNESS AND SPLICING. A whole sealed frame can be replaced by another valid sealed frame of the same segment header, key
   entry, key index and ordinal (a frame of an earlier rendering): the cipher accepts it and the chain re-links (`A-3`). Two
   holders that share a segment key and give their files equal headers and equal key contexts make their frames interchangeable in
   the same way. Remedy: an authenticating signature over a later commit, or a trusted head; distinct key contexts (`P8-29`).
4. ORIGIN (no origin integrity from sealing). Anyone who knows a recipient's public key can append, at the end of a segment, a
   `keys` frame with a new key entry and a recipient entry that wraps a key of its own to that recipient, and then sealed frames
   under it; appending at the end moves no ordinal. A reader whose segment key comes from that recipient entry opens and folds
   those frames as it folds the writer's: for such a reader sealing gives confidentiality and no integrity of origin. Remedy: an
   authenticating signature covering those frames; the result of reading says which frames are authenticated.
5. TRUNCATION. Deleting the frames at the end of a segment is not detected by sealing: the ordinal binds a frame's place, not the
   number of frames. Remedy: an authenticating signature over a commit frame, which fixes the frames before it; frames after the
   last signed commit have no such protection.
6. CLEAR FRAMES BESIDE SEALED ONES. A clear frame in a segment that also holds sealed frames is not authenticated by sealing, and
   a key holder folds it beside the inner frames it opened, with nothing in the fold to tell them apart. Remedy: an authenticating
   signature, and the authenticity that the result of reading reports for each frame.
7. EVERY HOLDER CAN SEAL. Every holder of a segment key, every recipient of a wrapped key included, can seal frames that every
   other holder accepts. Opening proves only that some holder of the key sealed the frame for that place. Remedy: an
   authenticating signature by the writer's own key.
8. TOTAL USE OF A KEY. The per-key limits of 7.1 are limits on the TOTAL use of a key by all its holders. One writer cannot
   enforce them alone, so the figures of 11.3 hold only if the holders together stay within them (`P8-26`). Remedy: a
   segment key for each writer.
9. AGREEMENT BETWEEN RECIPIENTS. The commitment of 11.2 is to each frame; nothing makes two recipients see one content. A writer can give
   different recipients different key entries, and so different plaintext views of one file; each sees `missing_key` for the
   frames of the others. A commit names a state (`P3`, "Graph states") only to a reader that opened every sealed frame before it
   in its run, so a recipient who lacks the key of one of them finds the state unavailable, and two recipients that each find the
   state find equal states (`P3`, claim ST3, by 11.2).
10. RECIPIENT AND SIGNER ANONYMITY. A recipient key identifier is a deterministic function of a public key and names it in every
    file, so it links a recipient across files. Signatures are in the clear and name their key; sealing hides no signer.
11. AVAILABILITY. A party that can rewrite the file can deny service: delete frames, give a key entry a conflicting recipient
    entry (so that `key_conflict` stops every frame under it), or put bogus sealed frames before honest ones to use up
    `max_failed_openings`. None of these gives more than deleting frames gives.
12. FORWARD SECRECY AND POST-COMPROMISE SECURITY. Whoever obtains a segment key, or a recipient's decapsulation key, opens every
    frame ever sealed under that segment key, past and future. There is no ratchet and no erasure.
13. WHAT THE PLAINTEXT DECODES TO. The properties of section 11 are about the padded plaintext `P`. That the inner payload decodes to its declared
    length and digest is the inner record's own check (7.2 step 9), resting on collision resistance of `H_ctx` as the container
    assumes it for every payload digest.
14. PROTECTION OF THE CLEAR ENVELOPE. The header, the `keys` frame, the clear content of every sealed frame, every clear frame,
    the signatures and the storage fields are readable by every party. The seal binds `h0` and the frame's key and place (6.2); it
    binds nothing else of the clear file, and it never binds `storage`, `payload` beyond the ciphertext, or `signatures`.
15. LOW-ENTROPY KEYS. Nothing is claimed for a segment key derived from a password or another guessable secret.
16. THE WRAP: COMMITMENT AND ORIGIN OF KEYS. Neither HPKE nor the recipient entry commits to the wrapped key; what protects frames
    when recipients are given different keys is the commitment and the conflict rule (11.2, 9.4). The property of 11.4 covers the
    confidentiality and integrity of frames toward the holders of a segment key, not who wrote a key entry or a recipient entry.
    That a recipient key identifier names one public key rests on collision resistance of `H_ctx`.
17. A NUMERIC SECURITY LEVEL IN THE STANDARD MODEL. The figures of 11.3 hold with AES-256 modelled as an ideal
    cipher, and no figure is stated for the key schedule and for HMAC (`S-1` to `S-3`).
18. A QUANTUM ADVERSARY (11.3). The level of the commitment is the classical birthday bound of SHA-256; no quantum figure is
    stated.
19. A READER'S RESULT. The confidentiality of 11.5 holds only toward a party that sees, of a reader's result, nothing but the
    outcomes of steps 1 to 7 of 7.2 for each sealed frame and the findings of clear frames that consult no opened content.
    Everything else a reader reports and delivers after it opens a frame can be a function of the plaintext and reveal it to
    whoever sees it. Examples, and the list is not complete: the outcome of step 8 (a malformed inner record or padding); of step
    9 (`damaged_frame`, or `exceeds_capacity` with `decoded_size` or `total_decoded`, which depend on the inner decoded length);
    of step 10 (every
    finding of the fold about the inner frame); the findings at other frames whose rules consult opened content, such as
    `seal_scope_violated`, `index_claim_failed` with `sealed_members`, and every finding of a clear frame that resolves against
    an inner frame or a sealed unit (terms, bindings and suppressions defined in an inner frame, recipes and values resolved
    against units of sealed packs); and the fold itself. Remedy: show a reader's result and content only to parties entitled to
    the plaintext.
20. A READER WHOSE KEY COMES FROM A RECIPIENT ENTRY. The properties of 11.5 are stated only for a reader that holds the segment
    key directly and holds no recipient key for an entry whose target is a key entry of the writer. For a reader that obtains it
    by unwrapping a recipient entry, or that holds such a recipient key beside it, none of them is claimed, and item 4 shows that such a reader can be given an adversary's key. Remedy: an authenticating signature over the
    `keys` frame and the sealed frames.

- byte `0` of `sv` changed: the nonce changes: `seal_failed` with `aead`; one failed opening counted

- `ordinal` written `2`, identifiers recomputed: `malformed_value` with `sealed_ordinal`, for every reader, with no key
- the frame moved to ordinal `2` with `ordinal` rewritten to `2`, identifiers recomputed: `seal_failed` with `aead`
- one byte of `C` changed, payload digest not recomputed: `damaged_frame` with `payload_digest` at step 1; not opened, nothing
  counted
- one byte of `C` changed, payload digest and identifiers recomputed: `seal_failed` with `aead`

- the frame copied into a segment whose header has a lineage: `seal_failed` with `aead`, since `h0` differs
