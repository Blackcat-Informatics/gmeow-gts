# Part P3: RDF content

## 0. Scope, dependencies and notation

This part defines how RDF content is carried and read: the `terms` frame and its typed columns (section 2); the four row frames
`quads`, `reifier_bindings`, `annotations` and `empty_graphs` (section 3); how ids resolve to typed lines (section 4); the fold
(section 5); what the format takes from the labelling specification and the labelled set (section 6); the `bindings` frame, its
referents and graph states (section 7); the content digest (section 8); the `suppress` frame, its entries, the hiding rule and the
visible dataset (section 9); metadata as RDF (section 10); the projection of a fold to RDF (section 10a); and the order of the
canonical dictionary, the canonical rows and the
canonical bindings and suppression frames (section 11).

It depends on:

- part `P2` (Registry) for every enumeration, code, finding and reason, and for which fields each finding carries;
- part `P1` (Model and wire) for the typed model, the frame envelope, the content records of frame types, identifiers, `H_ctx`
  and the payload check (sections "The typed model and its one serialization", "The frame envelope", "Identifiers, hashing and
  every preimage" and "The checks of every frame");
- the labelling specification, called `L1`: "Canonical labelling of blank nodes: version 1". `L1` is an external document. This
  part cites it by headings word for word: its section "Scope and result" (with its numbered items, among them "ROUTE
  INDEPENDENCE"), its section "Typed lines, masking and encoding" and its section "The whole input: order, result, record,
  identity, map, guarantee, outcomes, hash". Inside a section `L1` labels its paragraphs in capitals, and a labelled
  paragraph is cited as that paragraph under its heading, with its label as written; the labels used here are, under "Typed
  lines, masking and encoding", "TERMS", "LINES" (with "WHAT THE KINDS DENOTE"), "WELL-FORMED INPUT", "ENCODING" and "THE
  RECORD KIND", and, under "The whole input: order, result, record, identity, map, guarantee, outcomes, hash", "THE CANONICAL
  LINE SEQUENCE AND ITS DIGEST", "STABLE IDENTITY", "THE CANONICAL MAP" with its lemma "LEMMA I (IDEMPOTENCE: THE MAP OF A
  RELABELLED DATASET)", "THE GUARANTEE, THE PREDICATE `guaranteed` AND THE SEARCH-FREE RUN", "OUTCOMES AND REFUSALS", "LABEL
  SPELLING (the one place)" and "THE HASH, THE IDENTIFIER AND THE SECURITY ASSUMPTION"; and its sections "Final positions" and
  "The walk and the outcome of a limit". Where this
  part says "the encoding of a term" or "the canonical line sequence" it means the object that `L1` defines under that label;
- part `P4` (Blobs) for the blob set and the declarations that create a blob; part `P5` (Durability) for the commit frame and the
  committed prefix; part `P7` (Canonical form and reading) for every constant of the canonical form, the reading procedure, the
  typed result and the canonical check. This part states the order and the content of the canonical dictionary, rows and
  suppression frames, and `P7` states the constants and the checks.

THE `record` LINE KIND OF `L1`. `L1` defines a fifth line kind of its version `1`, `record(payload: bytes, terms: list(term))`
(its section "Typed lines, masking and encoding", paragraph "THE RECORD KIND"; its encoding has the tag `0x44`, and the line
tags run from `0x40` to `0x44`). This part relies on three properties of it and on nothing else. (1) The kind is part of version
`1` of the labelling and every engine implements it. It has no graph slot: a graph that matters is a member of `terms`. `terms`
can be empty. The payload is one constant, compared whole and never read, with no reserved value or prefix. (2) A `record` line
denotes nothing in RDF and takes no part in any rule about what a dataset denotes: a graph name that occurs only in a record does
not carry that graph, and the empty-graph rule ignores records. It takes full part in components, incidence, refinement, twin
tests, certificates, the canonical line sequence and its digest. Its `terms` list is position-sensitive, triple terms nested in
it included. Two identical `record` lines are ill formed, as for the other kinds, and a blank node that occurs only in records is
a node like any other. (3) Labels and positions are those of the whole input: the same RDF lines with and without records have
different positions and a different digest, and positions of one run are never mixed into another.

Notation. `E(x)` is the deterministic CBOR encoding of the value `x` (`P1`). A record is written `[field, ...]` in the declared
order; a variant `[tag, fields...]`; `option(T)` is `[1]` or `[2, v]` (`P1`). A byte string is written in hexadecimal, two digits
per byte, with a space between bytes. `<D>` or `<H>` followed by a name stands for 32 bytes of a digest that the vectors give, and `<P>`, `<T>` or `<G>` followed by
a name for a position, a map or a value of `guaranteed` that the vectors give: no example
states a digest it cannot show by hand. Enumeration values are written by label; the codes are those of `P2`. A requirement has an identifier
`P3-n` and states one thing. A requirement of a reader in this part applies under the access `forward` or `sparse` as `P7`, "Access: the steps by
access", THE SCOPE OF A READER REQUIREMENT, states. A finding is named by its code label and, when it has one, its reason label (`P2`). A `position` in
a finding is the zero-based index of the item among all items of its segment (`P2`).

## 1. Terms, lines and identity

### 1.1 The term model

The term model is the format's own. `L1` (section "Typed lines, masking and encoding", paragraph "TERMS") takes terms of its own
four variants, and 6.1 maps each term of this part to one of them. The format does not claim that its terms are terms of RDF 1.2
or of any other data model. A TERM is one of five variants.

```text
term = iri(text)
     / literal(lexical: text, datatype: iri)
     / lang_literal(lexical: text, language: text, direction: option(enum(direction)))
     / blank(segment: uint, label: bytes)
     / triple(subject: term, predicate: term, object: term)
```

A `literal` is a TYPED literal and a `lang_literal` a LANGUAGE literal. A typed literal has a datatype, and no language and no
direction. A language literal has a language, which is always present, a direction that is `absent` or `present`, and no
datatype. A direction without a language, and a language beside a datatype, are states of no variant and cannot be written.

The format adds no other term variant. The TERM KINDS of the wire (2.1) are storage forms and not variants: a term of kind
`literal` holds a typed or a language literal inline; a term of kind `literal_ref` holds one whose lexical form is the octets of a
`value` frame (`P4`); a term of kind `literal_value` holds a typed literal as a typed value (1.5), from which its lexical form
and datatype are generated; and a term of kind `literal_value_ref` holds a typed literal as a typed value of the kind `octets`
whose octets are in a `value` frame. The writer of a file chooses the form. A reader resolves every form to the term of the
variant and fields that it gives before the fold (2.6), and that term is equal to the term stored in any other form. Which
form a canonical file uses for each literal is defined by 11.1 (THE STORAGE FORM OF A LITERAL) with the thresholds of `P7`,
in its section "The constants", and the values of the canonical file by `P7`, "The construction, from a fold to the item
sequence".

- IDENTITY. Two terms are equal exactly when they are the same typed value: equal variants, equal fields, and at every depth
  equal segment ordinal and equal label at the blank leaves. No encoding is compared. A text is compared whole, bytewise: an
  `iri`, a lexical form and a language alike.
- A blank node is identified by the pair of the ORDINAL of its segment in the file read (its zero-based position among the
  segments of the file, the `ordinal` of the segment result of `P7`, and not the `lineage` `ordinal` of `P1`, which a writer
  states) and its label. A scope is one segment as it occurs in the file: two segments are two scopes even when their openers, or
  all their bytes, are equal, and blank nodes of different segments are different nodes. Two blank terms of one segment with equal
  labels are one node. A label is opaque `bytes`, compared whole, and no rule reads it. The identifier of an opener names no segment.
- THE DATATYPE OF A LANGUAGE LITERAL. A language literal holds no datatype. Where a datatype of it is needed, which is in the
  mapping to `L1` (6.1) and nowhere else, it is GENERATED as the `iri` text
  `http://www.w3.org/1999/02/22-rdf-syntax-ns#dirLangString` when its direction is `present` and the `iri` text
  `http://www.w3.org/1999/02/22-rdf-syntax-ns#langString` when its direction is `absent`. The two texts are constants of this
  part. Each is generated, never parsed, and never compared by a reader with a text that a file of this generation holds, except
  as one whole term with another. A typed literal whose datatype text equals one of them is a different term from every language
  literal, since its variant differs and it has no language; the format does not inspect a datatype text to notice that the text
  equals a constant, and it makes no finding of it.
- WHAT THE FORMAT DOES NOT JUDGE. An `iri` is a `text`, a lexical form is a `text`, a language is a `text`, and each is compared
  whole and bytewise. No rule of any part reads inside one. The format therefore does not judge whether the text of an `iri` is an
  IRI of any standard, whether a language text is a language tag of any standard, whether a lexical form suits its datatype,
  whether a direction suits a language, or whether any text holds a character that a standard excludes; the only property of a
  text that the format asks for is that its bytes are valid `UTF-8` (`P1`, 1.4). Two language texts that differ in the case of a
  letter are two languages. A consumer that needs one of these judgements makes it on the typed values that a read returns.

**P3-82.** A reader MUST treat a typed literal and a language literal as two different terms whatever their fields, and MUST NOT
inspect the text of a datatype to decide that a typed literal is equal to a language literal or is one.

**P3-83.** A reader MUST give a language literal, in the mapping to `L1` (6.1), the datatype generated by 1.1 for its
direction, and MUST NOT give it a datatype from anywhere else.

**P3-84.** A reader MUST NOT report a finding, hold or drop a row, make a term unreadable or change a result because of the
content of the text of an `iri`, a lexical form or a language, other than that a reconstructed text is not valid `UTF-8` (2.8).

**P3-1.** A reader MUST identify terms by the typed value of this section, and MUST identify a blank node by the pair of the
ordinal of its segment in the file read and its label and by nothing else.

**P3-2.** A reader MUST treat two segments of one file as two scopes for blank nodes whatever their openers or bytes, and MUST
give the labelling the source identifier `E([ordinal, label])` for each blank node (6.1).

### 1.2 Position rules

The position rules are the format's own.

- a predicate is an `iri`;
- a subject, a reifier and a graph name are an `iri` or a `blank`;
- an object is any term;
- inside a `triple` the subject is an `iri` or a `blank`, the predicate an `iri` and the object any term;
- the `triple` of a `reifier_bindings` row is a `triple` term.

A generalized term, an unbound term and a triple term as subject do not exist. A typed literal and a language literal, in every
storage form (`literal`, `literal_ref`, `literal_value`, `literal_value_ref`), stand where an object stands and nowhere else.

### 1.3 Typed lines

A LINE is one of four kinds, the `line_kind` values of `P2`, and is the typed value of `L1` section "Typed lines, masking and
encoding" (3.2) with the format's terms as its slots.

| `line_kind` | `L1` kind | slots, in order |
|---|---|---|
| `quad` | `quad` | `subject`, `predicate`, `object`, `graph: option(term)` |
| `reifier_binding` | `reifier-binding` | `reifier`, `triple`, `graph: option(term)` |
| `annotation` | `annotation` | `count: uint`, `reifier`, `predicate`, `object`, `graph: option(term)` |
| `empty_graph` | `empty-graph` | `graph: term` |

`graph` `absent` is the default graph. A line CARRIES a name when its kind has a graph slot and the slot is `present` and holds
the name (`L1`, section "Typed lines, masking and encoding", paragraph "LINES"); a name in any other place is not carried. The
frame type is the line kind, and no tag or marker is stored. A line is hashed and compared as a typed value. The tags `0x40` to
`0x44` of `L1` occur only inside `L1`'s own encodings and never in a frame.

**P3-3.** A reader MUST give every row of a row frame the line kind of its frame type: `quads` the kind `quad`,
`reifier_bindings` the kind `reifier_binding`, `annotations` the kind `annotation` and `empty_graphs` the kind `empty_graph`.

The consequence of P3-2: a file made of two byte-identical canonical files holds two scopes, and the nodes of equal labels of the
two are different nodes. The ordinal of a segment is a fact of the file read: the same segment read in a longer file has another
ordinal, and the content digest does not depend on it (8.2).

### 1.4 The order of the payload checks

A frame of a type of this part (`terms`, `quads`, `reifier_bindings`, `annotations`, `empty_graphs`, `bindings`, `suppress`) is
checked in the three steps below, after the field rules of `P1` and after its payload has verified, and only for a frame whose
link state is `linked` or `healed`. The first violation is the one `malformed_value` of the frame (`P1`, `P1-9`); no later rule is
judged. The steps say where each rule of this part stands. THE CONTENT OF A FRAME WHOSE LINK IS `broken` IS NOT INTERPRETED: none
of the three steps is run for it, it has its `chain_break` and what the field rules of `P1` and the payload check give, and no
finding of a value of its content record: no `malformed_value`, and no `unsupported_critical` and no
`unknown_enumeration_value` from a value of a newer registry version. A frame whose link is `broken` still reserves the ids that
2.1 gives it; the reading of `runs` for the expected base makes no finding.

1. THE CONTENT RECORD, in the order of its fields. `terms`: `id_base` (P3-6), `runs` (P3-5, its first violation in the order of
   its bytes, and last the ids it would reserve, P3-113), `order` (P3-7), `restart_group` (P3-6). `quads`, `reifier_bindings`, `annotations`: `order` (P3-26), `row_count`
   (P3-23). `empty_graphs`: `row_count` (P3-23). `bindings` and `suppress`: no rule before step 3. The order of the fields is the same
   in a segment of every registry version: an enumeration value that `P2` reads as a value of a newer version ("A value
   against the registry versions") is judged at the place of its field in this order, with the outcome `P2` gives it, and a
   violation of an earlier field comes first. The membership of an enumeration (the value `0`, and a value that the registry
   version of the segment does not hold, whether the reader holds it or not) is a rule on a value of the record and stands at the
   place of its field.
2. THE PAYLOAD AGAINST ITS TYPE, the first violation in the order of its bytes, with the reason that `P1` gives ("What a decoder
   rejects"); then the bytes that remain after the one item (`wrong_length`). The rules of this part that are rules of type are
   judged here, where they stand in the bytes. `terms`: a block tag outside the registry (P3-9), a digest of a wrong length, a
   `forms` element or a `typed_value` tag outside the registry, and a `time` value outside its range (P3-10).
   Row frames: a payload that is not the record of 3.1 (P3-23), a graph run
   with `rows` `0`, a run of a `run_column` with `count` `0`, a decoded value that is negative or not below `2^64` (P3-23),
   adjacent runs of one graph or of one value (P3-25), an annotation count of `0` (P3-26). `bindings` and `suppress`: a referent,
   leaf or entry tag outside the registry, a digest of a wrong length (7.1, 9.1, P3-46).
3. THE COUNTS AND THE RULES BETWEEN FIELDS, in this order, each over the elements in index order, the first element that breaks it
   deciding. `terms`: P3-8 (the number of blocks, then the lists of a block in field order, the blocks in run order); P3-9 (a
   block tag that is not the kind of its run); then the charge of the total formed length (2.3), a judgement of capacity and
   not a rule of the frame: a frame that it refuses is `exceeds_capacity` and not `malformed_value`, and no later rule is judged
   for it; P3-12 (the blocks in run order; in a block the sum of `suffix_lengths` first, then
   the terms in index order, each for `out_of_range`, `forbidden_combination` and `not_deterministic` in that order); P3-14 (a
   `language_indexes` element not below the table length, then the table). Row frames: P3-24 (the `rows` of the graph runs against
   `row_count`, then the columns in the order of their listing in 3.1, the `count` fields of a `run_column` last), then P3-27.
   `bindings` and `suppress`: `entry_count` against the number of entries (7.1, P3-46), then a leaf of tag `3` that holds no leaf
   of tag `2` (`forbidden_combination`), over the entries in index order.

**P3-74.** A reader MUST check a frame of this part whose link state is `linked` or `healed` in the three steps of 1.4, in the
order written there, and MUST give it the one `malformed_value` of the first violation. A reader MUST NOT judge a value of the
content record of a frame whose link state is `broken`, and MUST NOT give such a frame an `unsupported_critical` or an
`unknown_enumeration_value` for a value of its content record. Vectors `broken-link-content-value-not-judged` (the file `H T Q`
with `H` a header of `registry_version` `1`, `T` a `terms` frame that defines three terms and `Q` a `quads` frame of one row, each
linked as its writer wrote it; and the same file with the `order` of `Q` changed by one bit from `2` to `18`, a value of no
registry version, so that the link of `Q` fails: `Q` `broken` with `chain_break` alone, and no `malformed_value`, no
`unregistered_value`; its payload `verified`; its fold state `opaque` with `chain_break`; no finding of the file other than those
that the lack of a commit gives) and `broken-link-newer-value-not-interpreted` (a segment of `registry_version` `2`, reader
version `1`, `H T B`, with `T` a critical `terms` frame, the first of its segment, whose `order` is `9`, a value that the
reader does not hold, `B` a `blob` frame linked as its writer wrote it, and the `id_base` of `T` changed by one bit from `0` to
`16` so that the link of `T` fails: `unsupported_registry_version` once; `T` `broken` with `chain_break` alone, and no
`inconsistent_counts`, no `unsupported_critical`, no `unknown_enumeration_value`; `T` opaque with `chain_break`; the fold of
the segment does not stop: `B` `linked` and `folded`).
Vector `frame-enum-unknown-term-kind-newer` (a segment of `registry_version` `2`, reader version `1`, whose first frame is a
critical `terms` frame whose `runs` hold a `term_kind` that the reader does not hold and whose link fails: `chain_break`
alone, no `unsupported_critical`, no `malformed_value`).

### 1.5 Typed values

A TYPED VALUE is a value of one of five kinds, the `value_kind` values of `P2`. It is a STORAGE FORM of a typed literal: a
`literal_value` or `literal_value_ref` term holds one (2.2, 2.6), and a reader GENERATES from it the lexical form and the datatype
of the literal, which no file stores. Nothing is read back: no step derives a typed value from a text.

```text
typed_value = [1, v: bool]                           ; value_kind bool
            / [2, v: uint]                           ; value_kind uint
            / [3, v: int]                            ; value_kind int
            / [4, seconds: int, nanoseconds: uint]   ; value_kind time
            / [5, octets: bytes]                     ; value_kind octets
```

THE DATATYPES. The kind fixes the datatype, one of five `iri` texts that are constants of this part:

- `bool`: `http://www.w3.org/2001/XMLSchema#boolean`
- `uint`: `http://www.w3.org/2001/XMLSchema#nonNegativeInteger`
- `int`: `http://www.w3.org/2001/XMLSchema#integer`
- `time`: `http://www.w3.org/2001/XMLSchema#dateTime`
- `octets`: `http://www.w3.org/2001/XMLSchema#hexBinary`

The five texts differ from one another. Each is generated, never parsed, and never compared by a reader with a text that a file
of this generation holds, except as one whole term with another.

THE LEXICAL FORM. Each kind has one rule, total over its values, that gives one text for each value:

- `bool`: `false` gives `false`, and `true` gives `true`.
- `uint` `n`: the decimal digits of `n`, with no sign and no leading zero; `0` for zero.
- `int` `n`: for `n` below zero, `-` followed by the decimal digits of `-n`; otherwise as for `uint`. The text `-0` is never
  generated.
- `time` `(s, ns)`: defined when `s` is from `-62135596800` to `253402300799` and `ns` is below `1000000000`, which are the
  instants from `0001-01-01T00:00:00Z` to `9999-12-31T23:59:59.999999999Z`. The text is the civil date and time of day of the
  instant `s` seconds after `1970-01-01T00:00:00Z`, in the proleptic Gregorian calendar with no leap second and at offset zero,
  written `YYYY-MM-DDThh:mm:ss` with a four-digit year and two digits for every other field; then, when `ns` is not zero, `.`
  and the nine digits of `ns` with the trailing `0` digits removed; then `Z`. The civil date is integer arithmetic on `s`.
- `octets` `b`: two characters of `0123456789ABCDEF` for each byte, in byte order; the empty text for no bytes.

Examples: `[2, 7]` (`82 02 07`) gives `7` with the datatype `http://www.w3.org/2001/XMLSchema#nonNegativeInteger`; `[3, -5]` (`82
03 24`) gives `-5` with `http://www.w3.org/2001/XMLSchema#integer`; `[1, true]` (`82 01 f5`) gives `true`; `[4, 1781121600, 0]`
(`83 04 1a 6a 29 c2 40 00`) gives `2026-06-10T20:00:00Z`, and `[4, 1781121600, 500000000]` gives `2026-06-10T20:00:00.5Z`;
`[4, -62135596800, 0]` gives `0001-01-01T00:00:00Z`; `[5, h'ff 41']` (`82 05 42 ff 41`) gives `FF41` with
`http://www.w3.org/2001/XMLSchema#hexBinary`.

IDENTITY. The literal of a typed value is the typed literal of the generated lexical form and the generated datatype. It is equal
to every typed literal of the same two texts, however that literal is stored (1.1): the typed literal `7` with the datatype
`http://www.w3.org/2001/XMLSchema#nonNegativeInteger` stored as text and the typed value `[2, 7]` are one term. A literal is the
literal of at most one typed value: its datatype fixes the kind, since the five datatypes differ, and within a kind the rule of
the lexical form gives different texts for different values (the widths of the fields of a `time` fix each field, and the digits
after the point fix `ns`). No step computes the value of a text; the fact is what makes VALUES of the fold a function (5.1).

**P3-85.** A reader MUST generate the lexical form and the datatype of a `literal_value` or `literal_value_ref` term from its typed
value by the rule of 1.5 for its kind, and MUST NOT take a typed value from any text.

**P3-86.** A writer MUST NOT write a typed value of kind `time` whose `seconds` is outside `-62135596800` to `253402300799` or
whose `nanoseconds` is not below `1000000000`, and a reader MUST report such a value `malformed_value` with `out_of_range` at
step 2 of 1.4.

## 2. The `terms` frame

The dictionary of a segment is the sequence of the terms of its `terms` frames in file order. Ids are segment-scoped: a row, a
binding or a suppression entry names a term of its own segment by its id.

### 2.1 The content record and the ids

```text
terms.content = [ id_base: uint,
                  runs: list([ term_kind: enum(term_kind), count: uint ]),
                  order: enum(term_order),
                  restart_group: uint ]
```

A RUN is a maximal sequence of consecutive terms of one kind in the frame. The runs are listed in id order, so the kind of every
id is known from `content` alone, without decoding the payload. The ids of a frame are fixed by position, as follows.

- The EXPECTED BASE of the first `terms` frame of a segment is `0`. The expected base of each later `terms` frame is the expected
  base of the frame before it plus the sum of the run counts of that frame, taken over every `terms` frame of the segment whose
  `runs` field is well formed (P3-5), whether or not the frame is folded. A frame whose `runs` field is not well formed adds `0`.
- AN ID IS A `uint`. A `terms` frame whose expected base plus the sum of its run counts is above `2^64`, so that its last id
  would be above `2^64 - 1`, reserves no id and adds `0`: its `runs` field is not well formed (P3-5). The sum is the sum of
  the numbers, whatever its size, and is never reduced.
- The term at index `k` of a frame has the id `expected base + k`. Run order and index order are the same order.
- `id_base` is a claim that lets a reader place the frame without reading the frames before it. It is judged against the expected
  base and decides no id.

**P3-4.** A writer MUST write `id_base` equal to the expected base of the frame.

**P3-5.** A reader MUST judge the `runs` field of a `terms` frame well formed exactly when it is a list of at least one
`[term_kind, count]`, every `term_kind` is a value of the registry, every `count` is at least `1`, no two adjacent runs have
one `term_kind`, and every id that the list would reserve is a `uint` (P3-113). A `term_kind` outside the registry is `malformed_value` with `unregistered_value`; an empty list or a `count`
of `0` is `malformed_value` with `out_of_range`; two adjacent runs of one kind are `malformed_value` with `not_deterministic`
(one run is the one encoding of such terms). The empty list is judged first. Then the runs are judged in list order, and in
one run its `term_kind` before its `count`: the kind against the registry, then the kind against the kind of the run before
it, then the count. So a run that repeats the kind of the run before it and has the `count` `0` is `not_deterministic`.

**P3-113.** A reader MUST judge a `runs` list that passes every check of P3-5 last for the ids it would reserve, and MUST judge
the `runs` field not well formed, with `malformed_value` and `out_of_range`, when the expected base of the frame plus the sum of
the counts is above `2^64`. This is the last check of the `runs` field and not of the frame: `id_base` stands before `runs` in
the content record and is judged at its place, so a frame whose `id_base` differs from its expected base is
`inconsistent_counts` (P3-6) whatever its runs would reserve.

**P3-6.** A reader MUST report `malformed_value` with `inconsistent_counts` for a `terms` frame whose `id_base` differs from its
expected base, and MUST report `malformed_value` with `out_of_range` for a `restart_group` of `0`, each at its place in step 1
of 1.4 and only for a frame whose link state is `linked` or `healed`. Each of these frames is opaque
with `malformed` and is not folded; a frame whose `runs` is well formed still reserves its ids, which are unreadable terms (2.8).
Vector `terms-id-base-ill-typed-reserves` (a `terms` frame whose `id_base` is not a `uint` and whose `runs` are well formed,
then a row that names its ids: the frame is `malformed_value` with `wrong_type` and opaque, it reserves its ids, the row is
held with `opaque_term` and no `forward_reference`, and the content digest is `absent`).

**P3-7.** A reader MUST report `malformed_value` with `unregistered_value` for an `order` that is not a `term_order` value of its
registry version, at its place in step 1 of 1.4 and only for a frame whose link state is `linked` or `healed`. An `order` of a
newer registry version than the reader's, in a segment of that version, is not malformed: it has the outcome that `P2-62`
gives, at the same place.

### 2.2 The payload and its blocks

```text
terms_payload = [ blocks: list(block), languages: list(text) ]   ; one block per run, in run order
block = [1, prefix_lengths: list(uint), suffix_lengths: list(uint), suffixes: bytes]                    ; iri
      / [2, prefix_lengths: list(uint), suffix_lengths: list(uint), suffixes: bytes,
         forms: list(enum(literal_form)), datatypes: list(uint), language_indexes: list(uint)]         ; literal
      / [3, labels: list(bytes)]                                                                        ; blank
      / [4, subjects: list(uint), predicates: list(uint), objects: list(uint)]                          ; triple
      / [5, value_digests: list(digest), lengths: list(uint),
         forms: list(enum(literal_form)), datatypes: list(uint), language_indexes: list(uint)]         ; literal_ref
      / [6, values: list(typed_value)]                                                                  ; literal_value
      / [7, value_digests: list(digest), lengths: list(uint)]                                           ; literal_value_ref
```

The block tag is the `term_kind` of the run. THE PER-TERM LISTS of a block are `prefix_lengths`, `suffix_lengths`, `forms`,
`labels`, `subjects`, `predicates`, `objects`, `value_digests`, `lengths` and `values`: each holds exactly the run's count of
elements, and element `i` belongs to the term of index `i` of the run, counted from `0` in term order. THE FORM LISTS of a block
of tag `2` or `5` are `datatypes` and `language_indexes`: `datatypes` holds one element for each term whose `forms` element is
`typed`, and `language_indexes` one element for each term whose `forms` element is `language`, `language_ltr` or
`language_rtl` (a LANGUAGE FORM); element `j` of each belongs to the `j`-th such term of the run, counted from `0` in term order.
The list `suffixes` is the one `bytes` value of the block and has no element `i` (2.3). So a typed literal of a block has a
datatype and no language, and a language literal has a language and no datatype; no list holds an option. The decoded payload of
a `terms` frame is the deterministic encoding of exactly one `terms_payload`.

**P3-80.** A reader MUST attribute element `i` of every per-term list of a block to the term of index `i` of the run of that
block, and element `j` of `datatypes` or of `language_indexes` to the `j`-th term of the run, in term order, whose `forms`
element is `typed` or a language form respectively.

**P3-8.** A reader MUST report `malformed_value` with `inconsistent_counts` for a `terms` payload whose `blocks` list does not
hold one block per run, for a block one of whose per-term lists does not hold the run's count of elements, for a `datatypes` list
that does not hold one element for each `typed` element of the block's `forms`, and for a `language_indexes` list that does not
hold one element for each language form of the block's `forms`. It judges the number of blocks first, then the blocks in run
order, and within a block the lists in the order of their fields, so that a form list is counted only against a `forms` list
that holds the run's count.

**P3-9.** A reader MUST report `malformed_value` with `forbidden_combination` for a block whose tag is a `term_kind` value of the
registry other than the kind of its run. A block tag outside the registry is `malformed_value` with `unregistered_value`.

**P3-10.** A reader MUST report `malformed_value` with `wrong_length` for a `digest` of a block of tag `5` or `7` whose length is
not the digest length of the segment's `hash_algorithm`, with `unregistered_value` for a `forms` element outside `literal_form`
and for a `typed_value` tag outside `value_kind`, and with `out_of_range` for a value of kind `time` outside the range of 1.5.

### 2.3 Front coding

IRIs and the lexical forms of literals are stored by front coding, in the blocks of tags `1` and `2`; the typed and the language
literals of a block of tag `2` are one sequence, in term order. Within a run of `n` such
terms numbered `0` to `n - 1`, with `restart_group` written `g`:

- the TEXT of term `i` is the first `prefix_lengths[i]` bytes of the text of term `i - 1` followed by the next
  `suffix_lengths[i]` bytes of `suffixes`; the suffix bytes of the terms follow one another in term order and
  `suffixes` is exactly their concatenation;
- a term whose index `i` satisfies `i` modulo `g` equal to `0` (so term `0` of a run, and every group of `g` terms) is a
  RESTART: its `prefix_lengths[i]` is `0` and its text is its suffix bytes;
- `prefix_lengths[i]` is the length of the longest common prefix, taken bytewise, of the text of term `i - 1` and the text of term
  `i`. A prefix can end inside a multi-byte character; only the reconstructed text has to be valid `UTF-8` (2.8);
- the term of index `0` has no previous term and is a restart.

A run starts a fresh front coding: the previous term of the first term of a run is none. Groups of `g` are counted
from the first term of the run, so a block is decodable by itself and a reader finds the nearest restart of
any index without reading earlier runs.

A front-coded text is a text formed by the rule above, from bytes. Its validity as UTF-8 is judged once, on the whole
reconstructed text, by item 1 of 2.8; no prefix, no suffix and no run is judged alone (`P1`, "Scope, dependencies and notation",
paragraph "WHAT IS NOT A PARSE").

**P3-11.** A writer MUST write `prefix_lengths[i]` as `0` at every restart and as the longest common bytewise
prefix of the two texts at every other index, and MUST write `suffixes` as the concatenation, in term order, of the
bytes of the texts after the prefixes.

**P3-12.** A reader MUST report `malformed_value` with `inconsistent_counts` when the sum of `suffix_lengths` is not the length of
`suffixes`, with `out_of_range` when a `prefix_lengths[i]` exceeds the length of the text of term `i - 1`, with
`forbidden_combination` when a restart has a prefix length other than `0`, and with `not_deterministic` when a
`prefix_lengths[i]` at a non-restart is not the longest common bytewise prefix of the two texts. The term of index `0` of a
run has no term before it and is a restart: a prefix length other than `0` there is `forbidden_combination`, and `out_of_range`
is not judged for it. P3-8 counts the lists of a block as the lists of the block's own tag against the count of its run, so a
block whose tag is not the kind of its run and one of whose lists has another length is `inconsistent_counts`, before P3-9.

The check of the longest common prefix of P3-12 compares one byte: the prefix length `p` of term `i` is the
longest exactly when `p` equals the shorter of the two lengths, or the byte of the previous text at `p` differs
from the byte of the term's text at `p`.

THE TOTAL FORMED LENGTH. The prefix of a term is a copy of bytes that the reader already holds, and one text can be copied into
every term of a run, so the bytes of the texts that front coding forms are not bounded by the bytes that the reader decodes. The
total formed length bounds them, and it is judged before the work it bounds.

THE CHARGE of a `terms` frame is the sum of the `prefix_lengths` elements of its blocks of tags `1` and `2`: the number of bytes
that its prefixes copy. It is a sum of typed fields of the decoded payload, known before any text is formed. The suffix bytes are
not charged: they are bytes of the decoded payload, which the total decoded length counts (`P1`, "The payload check"). The charge
is the same whether a reader copies a prefix or shares the bytes it copies; it depends on no text and on nothing that an
implementation keeps.

THE ORDER OF THE CHARGES. A reader charges the `terms` frames of a read at the step CONTENT of the reader procedure (Canonical
form and reading, "The reader procedure"), in the order in which it judges frames: the segments in file order and within a
segment the frames in item order, the inner frame of an opened sealed frame (`P8`) at the position of the sealed frame. A frame is
charged in step 3 of 1.4, when P3-8 and P3-9 hold for it and before the first rule of P3-12; a frame that an earlier rule makes
malformed is not charged.

THE TOTAL. The reader keeps the TOTAL FORMED LENGTH of the read: the sum of the charges of the frames that it has admitted. A frame
is ADMITTED when the total kept so far plus its charge is at most `formed_bytes_per_read` (Canonical form and reading,
"Capacity"); its charge is added and its texts are formed. Otherwise the frame is REFUSED: no text of it is formed and no later
rule of 1.4 is judged for it; the finding is `exceeds_capacity` with the reason `total_formed` at the frame's `position`, with its
`offset` and `length` and, as its `count`, the total kept so far plus its charge, or `2^64 - 1` when that sum is above
`2^64 - 1` (`P7`, "Capacity"); the frame is opaque with the reason
`exceeds_capacity` and is not folded; its `runs`, which are well formed, still reserve its ids, which are unreadable terms (2.8,
item 6); its charge is not added; and every later `terms` frame of the read that reaches its charge is refused in the same way,
whatever its charge.

A refused frame is opaque for capacity. What follows for the rows that name its terms, for integrity, for graph states, for the
canonical check, for compaction and for conversion is stated where each is defined (4.2; 7.2; Canonical form and reading, "How
each state value is decided", "Compaction" and "The canonical check"; `P9`).

Worked example (typed values). A frame of one run of `4` IRIs with `restart_group` `4`, `prefix_lengths` `[0, 9, 9, 10]` and
`suffix_lengths` `[10, 1, 2, 0]` has the charge `28`. A reader whose total kept so far is `100` and whose
`formed_bytes_per_read` is `127` refuses it: `exceeds_capacity` with `total_formed` and `count` `128`; the four ids are reserved
and unreadable. With `128` it admits it, the total becomes `128`, and the four texts are formed and judged.

**P3-101.** A reader MUST charge every `terms` frame for which P3-8 and P3-9 hold the sum of the `prefix_lengths` elements of
its blocks of tags `1` and `2`, in the order of 2.3, and MUST make the charge before it forms any text of the frame and before it
judges P3-12.

**P3-102.** A reader MUST admit a `terms` frame only when the total formed length kept so far plus its charge is at most its
`formed_bytes_per_read`, MUST refuse every other `terms` frame and every later `terms` frame of the read that reaches its charge,
MUST form no text of a refused frame, and MUST report for it one `exceeds_capacity` with the reason `total_formed`, its
`position`, `offset` and `length`, and the `count` of 2.3.

**P3-103.** A reader MUST make a refused `terms` frame opaque with the reason `exceeds_capacity`, MUST keep the ids that its
`runs` reserve as unreadable terms, and MUST NOT let a copy it avoids, a text it shares or a cache change a charge, an admission
or a refusal.

### 2.4 Blank and triple blocks

A `blank` block lists the label of each blank term, as `bytes`. Two blank terms of one segment with equal labels are one node
(1.1). A `triple` block lists, for each term, the ids of its subject, predicate and object. A term refers only to ids smaller than
its own; an id that is not smaller is a forward reference (2.8). The components obey the position rules of 1.2; a component that
breaks them makes the term unreadable (2.8).

### 2.5 Literals, the language table and directions

A `literal` block (tag `2`) holds, for each term, its lexical form (front coded, 2.3) and its FORM, a `literal_form` value:
`typed` for a typed literal, and `language`, `language_ltr` or `language_rtl` for a language literal whose direction is `absent`,
`ltr` or `rtl`. A term of the form `typed` has, in `datatypes`, the id of an `iri` term smaller than the literal's own id, which
is its datatype. A term of a language form has, in `language_indexes`, the zero-based index of an entry of the payload's
`languages` table, which is its language. The form decides which of the two a term has, and it has that one only (2.2). The
datatype of a language literal is generated (1.1), and no id names it.

The `languages` table of the payload lists each distinct language text that a term of a block of tag `2` or `5` of the payload
uses, once, in the order of the first use over the blocks in run order and the terms of each block in term order. A language is a
`text`, compared whole and bytewise: two texts that differ in one byte, in the case of a letter included, are two languages and
two entries of the table. A reader reads no byte of a language text and converts no case.

**P3-13.** A writer MUST write the `languages` table as exactly the distinct language texts used, in the order of first use, and
MUST write the language of a term as its language text, byte for byte.

**P3-14.** A reader MUST report `malformed_value` with `out_of_range` for a `language_indexes` element not below the table length,
and with `not_deterministic` for a table that holds a text twice, holds a text that no term uses, or lists the texts in another
order than that of first use.

**P3-87.** A reader MUST read the language of a term of a language form as the table entry that its `language_indexes` element
names, byte for byte, MUST read its direction from its form (`absent` for `language`, `ltr` for `language_ltr`, `rtl` for
`language_rtl`), and MUST NOT convert a case or any other character of the language.

### 2.6 `literal_ref`: a lexical form held by a `value` frame

A `literal_ref` block (tag `5`) holds, for each term, the digest of the value octets (`value_digest`), their length in octets
(`lengths`), and its form, its datatype or language as in 2.5. A `literal_value_ref` block (tag `7`) holds, for each term, a
digest and a length and nothing else: its term is a typed literal stored as a typed value of the kind `octets` (1.5), whose
octets are the value. The `value` declarations (`P4`: a `value` frame, or a `pack` member of kind `value`) whose `value_digest`
equals the term's digest and whose `total_length` equals the term's length and whose frame stands before the `terms` frame that
holds the term are the CANDIDATES of the term, taken in file order: the segments in file order, the items of a segment in item
order, the members of a pack in directory order at the position of the pack. The first candidate that rebuilds (`P4`, "Rebuilding a blob or a value") provides the octets, and nothing is parsed:

- for a `literal_ref` term the octets are the lexical form, as a `text`; octets that are not valid `UTF-8` make the term
  unreadable (2.8);
- for a `literal_value_ref` term the octets are the typed value `[5, octets]`, from which the lexical form and the datatype are
  generated (1.5).

The length of the lexical form is `lengths` for a `literal_ref` term and twice `lengths` for a `literal_value_ref` term, so a
reader knows it before it rebuilds the value.

WHERE THE VALUE STANDS, AND WHEN IT IS REBUILT. A VALUE SERVES ONLY WHAT STANDS AFTER IT. The `value` frame (or `pack` member of
kind `value`, `P4`) that provides a term's octets stands in any segment of the file that is at or before the segment of the
`terms` frame, and in that segment before the `terms` frame in item order: a value named by a `literal_ref` or
`literal_value_ref` term is a reference that can cross segments backwards, and a `value` declaration that stands at or after the
`terms` frame provides nothing for the terms of that frame. A pack stands at its own position, so a `value` member of a pack that
stands before the `terms` frame serves it and a `value` member of a pack that stands after it does not. A recipe
resolves the units of its entries in its own segment only, and only the units that stand in its frame or before it (`P4`, "The
unit index and the unit used", A UNIT SERVES ONLY WHAT STANDS AFTER IT). The rule of values has the form of the rule of ids (an
id is defined before a row names it, 4.1) and of the rule of units: what an item names stands before it, or for a unit in its
frame, so the status of a term
is decided where the term arrives. Every value is REBUILT by the rules of `P4`, in
its section "Rebuilding a blob or a value", BEFORE the fold reads it; the reading procedure of `P7` orders that rebuild before
the step that resolves `literal_ref` and `literal_value_ref` terms.

THE PAIR of a `literal_ref` or `literal_value_ref` term is its digest and its length. A declaration PROVIDES a value for a term
exactly when it is a candidate of the term: its `value_digest` equals the term's digest, its `total_length` equals the term's
length and its frame stands before the `terms` frame that holds the term. This is the one rule of a value reference, and `P4`
and `P7` apply it. A declaration whose digest equals the term's and whose length differs provides nothing for that term,
whether or not it is verified, and neither does a declaration that stands at or after the `terms` frame. A term defined in
two `terms` frames (2.7) is judged at each definition by the candidates that stand before that frame: the outcome of one
definition is not the outcome of the other.

When the term has no candidate, the finding is `missing_dependency` with `value`. A reader reports for a reference whose value
stands later exactly what it reports for a reference whose value does not stand in the file: the term is unreadable, whatever
names it is held with `opaque_term`, and the finding stands at the `terms` frame. When it has candidates and none rebuilds, each
candidate having the rebuild state `incomplete` or `refused` (`P4`, "Rebuilding a blob or a value", "The total rebuilt
length"), the finding is the blobs part's finding at each candidate, at that candidate's own position, once for the candidate
however many terms it is a candidate of, and no `missing_dependency` is added. In both cases the term is unreadable and whatever
names it is held with `opaque_term`. When one candidate rebuilds the term is readable, and a candidate that fails keeps the
blobs part's finding at its own position. The finding `missing_dependency` with `value` is reported once for each PAIR that no
declaration standing before the term provides, however many terms have that pair, at the first `terms` frame in file
order that holds a term of the pair with no candidate, among the terms that reach item 5 of 2.8: a term that an earlier item of 2.8 makes unreadable has that item's finding
alone and does not name its pair for this finding. Two lengths of one digest are two pairs, and neither suppresses the finding
of the other: when terms name the digest `D` with the lengths `3` and `4` and every declaration of `D` states the length `3`, the
terms of length `4` give one `missing_dependency` with `value`, at the `terms` frame that first names `(D, 4)`, and the terms of
length `3` read the value or carry the findings of its candidates. The content digest is `absent` (8.1). The frame is not opaque
and folds everything else it holds.

The findings about content are reported once, for the FILE's fold (5.1). In a segment's own fold a term is unreadable
when no candidate stands before its `terms` frame in that segment, and no finding is made for it there.

**P3-15.** A reader MUST read the datatype of a term of the form `typed` of a `literal` or `literal_ref` block as the `iri` named
by its `datatypes` element, and MUST NOT derive, default or replace it. A language literal has no datatype but the generated one
(1.1), and the datatype of a `literal_value` or `literal_value_ref` term is the one that its kind fixes (1.5).

**P3-16.** A reader MUST take the lexical form of a `literal_ref` term as the text of the octets of its value, MUST generate the
lexical form of a `literal_value_ref` term from its octets by the rule of 1.5, MUST NOT parse any text to do either, and MUST treat
the term so obtained as equal to the term of the same variant and fields in any other storage form.

**P3-81.** A reader MUST take as the octets of a `literal_ref` or `literal_value_ref` term those of the first candidate, in file
order, that rebuilds.

**P3-92.** A reader MUST take as the candidates of a `literal_ref` or `literal_value_ref` term only the `value` declarations
whose `value_digest` and `total_length` both equal the term's digest and length, MUST report `missing_dependency` with `value`
once for each pair of a digest and a length that a term reaching item 5 of 2.8 names and that no declaration standing before
the `terms` frame of the term provides, at the first `terms` frame in file order that holds such a term, and MUST NOT let a
pair that a declaration provides suppress the finding of another pair with the same digest.

**P3-114.** A reader MUST take as the candidates of a `literal_ref` or `literal_value_ref` term only the `value` declarations
that stand before the `terms` frame that holds the term, MUST NOT let a `value` declaration that stands at or after that frame
provide a value for it, MUST give a term with no candidate the outcome of a term whose pair no declaration of the file has
(unreadable, and whatever names it held with `opaque_term`), and MUST report `missing_dependency` with `value` for its pair by
P3-92.

**P3-17.** A reader MUST rebuild every value before the fold reads it, MUST resolve the value of a `literal_ref` or
`literal_value_ref` term in the file's fold against the declarations that stand before its `terms` frame, in its segment and in
the earlier segments of the file, MUST treat a term whose value is not provided or cannot be rebuilt as unreadable
and whatever names it as held with `opaque_term`, MUST NOT make a frame opaque for it, and MUST report for it the
one finding that 2.6 states and no other.

### 2.7 Duplicate definitions

A term MAY be defined twice in a segment, in frames that declare `term_order` `arrival`: a streaming writer need not hold a table
of every term it has already defined. Both ids denote one term when both definitions are readable (2.6), since identity is by value (1.1), and so do a typed
literal stored as text and the same literal stored as a typed value (1.5). A frame that declares
`canonical` defines each term once (2.9).

**P3-18.** A reader MUST resolve two ids that are defined by equal term values to one term, and a writer MUST define a term
twice only in frames that declare `term_order` `arrival`.

### 2.8 Unreadable terms

A term is UNREADABLE when any of these holds, and then the term keeps its id and is not folded as a term. The terms of a frame
are judged in id order, and for one term the conditions are judged in the order listed: the first that holds decides, and it
alone gives the term its finding.

1. its reconstructed `iri` text or lexical form is not valid `UTF-8` (RFC 3629), or the octets of the value of a `literal_ref`
   term are not; finding `ill_formed_term`;
2. a `datatype` id or a `triple` component id is not smaller than the term's own id; finding `forward_reference`;
3. a `datatype` id names a term that is not an `iri`, or a `triple` component breaks the position rules
   of 1.2; finding `position_constraint`;
4. a `datatype` or a component is itself unreadable (no second finding);
5. it is a `literal_ref` or `literal_value_ref` term whose value is not provided or cannot be rebuilt (2.6, which states the
   finding);
6. it belongs to a frame that is not folded (the frame's `runs` are well formed; the frame is opaque);
7. it is defined by a `terms` frame in doubt, or it is named by a row, a binding or a suppression entry of a frame in doubt,
   for that row, binding or entry (THE DICTIONARY IN DOUBT), or it is named by a row, a binding or a suppression entry of a
   frame after a broken link, for that row, binding or entry (THE DICTIONARY AFTER A BROKEN LINK).

A reader reports each of the codes of items 1 to 3 at most once for each frame, at the frame's `position`, however many terms of
the frame cause it. A row, a binding or a suppression entry that names an unreadable term, directly or inside a `triple`, or that
holds an unreadable leaf (6.3, POSITIONS IN A LEAF), is HELD
(4.3): it is reported `opaque_term`, hides and states nothing, and the content digest is `absent` (8.1).

THE DICTIONARY IN DOUBT. A segment begins only at an opener (`P1`, "Structure"), and two segments whose openers are
byte-equal have one `h0`, so the frames of a segment whose opener damage has made into another item can link as frames of the
segment before it (`P1`, "Healing"). In a segment whose opener outcome is `readable`, the dictionary of the segment is IN DOUBT
from the first item after the opener that is an opaque item that is not a frame (the class `opaque` of `P1`, "Classification of
an item") or a damaged region (`P5`, "The one recovery procedure") to the next opener of the file, or to the end of the
input when no opener follows; an item of the class `frame`, `offsets` or `locator` puts no dictionary in doubt. A FRAME IN DOUBT
is a frame that stands where the dictionary of its segment is in doubt. For a frame in doubt:

- a `terms` frame defines no term for the fold: it reserves its ids by 2.1 as every `terms` frame does, so the expected base of
  each later `terms` frame of the segment is that of 2.1, and each of its terms is unreadable by item 7 (items 1 to 6 are
  judged first, in the order of the list: a term that holds one of them has that item's finding, and a term that reaches item 5
  names its pair for `missing_dependency`; item 7 decides the term that none of them holds), gives no pair of VALUES
  (5.1) and, when it is a `blank` term, holds no label for a pair (6.3);
- every term id that a row, a binding or a suppression entry names, directly or inside a leaf of tag `3`, and that an earlier
  `terms` frame of the segment reserves names an unreadable term for that row, binding or entry by item 7, so the row is HELD by
  4.2, condition 2, and the binding or the entry by 7.1 and P3-47, each with `opaque_term`; an id that no earlier `terms` frame of
  the segment reserves is not defined at the frame, and 4.2, condition 1, and 7.1 decide it.

The doubt leaves alone what is named by digest (a value and a unit, 2.6 and `P4`, and a declaration, a referent and a leaf of tag
`2`, which name no term id); the links, the commits, the parity and the signatures of every item; and every frame before the item
that begins the doubt. A segment that holds no such item has no dictionary in doubt, and a repaired copy of `P5` that holds no
such item is read as any file is.

THE DICTIONARY AFTER A BROKEN LINK. A torn frame followed by a joined file absorbs that file's header, and the joined frames,
whose first link is then `broken` and whose later links hold, would otherwise be resolved against the dictionary of the segment
before without any finding. In a segment whose opener outcome is `readable`, after an ordinary frame whose link state is `broken`
(`P1`, "Healing"), every term id that a `terms` frame before that frame reserves names an unreadable term, by item 7, for each
row, binding and suppression entry of a later frame of the segment, to the next opener; a later `terms` frame defines its own ids.
A transparent frame starts no such rule. A frame whose ordinary position is below that of a valid commit of the segment (`P5`,
"The checks of a commit") is not subject to the rule after a broken link; it stays subject to the doubt. A read with the access
`forward` does not apply that exception (`P7`, "Access: the steps by access"). Where the dictionary is in doubt, the doubt
decides.

The two rules are two triggers and two strengths. The doubt, begun by an opaque item that is not a frame or by a damaged region,
makes the ids reserved before it unreadable and makes every later `terms` frame define nothing; the rule after a broken link,
begun by an ordinary frame whose link state is `broken`, makes only the ids reserved before it unreadable. Both apply in every
segment whose opener outcome is `readable`, whatever opened it, a header or a resync item (`P1`, "The resync item"). The end at
a valid commit belongs to the rule after a broken link alone and never ends the doubt.

**P3-19.** A reader MUST treat as unreadable every term that meets a condition of 2.8, MUST report `ill_formed_term`,
`forward_reference` and `position_constraint` at most once each for a frame, and MUST NOT renumber any id.

**P3-72.** A reader MUST judge the terms of a frame in id order and the conditions of 2.8 for one term in the order listed, MUST
give the term the finding of the first condition that holds, and MUST NOT report a second finding for that term.

**P3-116.** A reader MUST put the dictionary of a segment whose opener outcome is `readable` in doubt from the first opaque
item that is not a frame, or damaged region, after its opener to the next opener of the file (2.8, THE DICTIONARY IN DOUBT),
MUST let no `terms` frame in doubt define a term for the fold, and MUST hold with `opaque_term` every row, binding and suppression
entry of a frame in doubt that names a term id that an earlier `terms` frame of its segment reserves.
Vector `doubt-misplaced-locator-none` (a `locator` item after the header, then a `terms` frame and a `bindings` frame whose
`state` referent names a commit that names no state, read with `rebuilt_bytes_per_read` `2`: the `locator` is
`malformed_value` with `unordered_or_repeated`, no dictionary is in doubt, and the binding has `missing_dependency` with
`state_unavailable` and no `opaque_term`).
Vector `doubt-terms-conditions-judged` (`H T X T2` with `X` an opaque item that is not a frame, from which the dictionary is
in doubt, and `T2` a `terms` frame whose first term is an `iri` whose text is not valid UTF-8: items `1` to `6` of 2.8 are
still judged before item `7`; `X` has `malformed_value` with `unregistered_value` in the findings of the segment, `T2`, at
position `3`, has `ill_formed_term`, and its terms are unreadable).

**P3-117.** A reader MUST apply the rule of THE DICTIONARY AFTER A BROKEN LINK (2.8) in every segment whose opener outcome is
`readable`; MUST hold with `opaque_term` each row, binding and suppression entry of a later frame of the segment that names an
id that a `terms` frame before the broken frame reserves; MUST let a later `terms` frame define its own ids; MUST start no such
rule at a transparent frame; MUST end the rule after a broken link, and the rule alone, never the doubt, for the frames whose
ordinary position is below that of a valid commit of the segment when its access is `whole`; MUST NOT end it for them when its
access is `forward`; and MUST let the doubt decide where both rules apply. The clauses are the one rule of 2.8, which the vectors `broken-link-*` and
`dictionary-doubt-decides` test together, each clause by a case of its own.
Vector `broken-link-no-effective-identifier` (an ordinary frame, its `transparent` not a `bool`, whose link is `broken` and
whose `identifier` is not `bytes`, so that it has no effective identifier, after a `terms` frame, then a row that names an
id of that `terms` frame: the row is held with `opaque_term`, and the content digest of the segment and of the file is
`absent`).

### 2.9 The declared order of terms

`order` is `canonical` or `arrival`. `arrival` declares nothing. `canonical` declares that the terms of the frame are
strictly ascending under the order of 11.1, and that the first term of the frame follows the last term of the nearest
earlier `terms` frame of the segment when that frame also declares `canonical`. The nearest earlier `terms` frame is the nearest
in item order, whatever its state. A frame whose nearest earlier `terms` frame declares `arrival`, or which has none, or which is
not decoded (opaque, malformed or above the reader's capacity, so that its last term is unknown), has no earlier term to follow. A frame that breaks a declaration it makes is
`declared_order_violated` at its position and still folds. Every reader that decodes the frame checks a `canonical` declaration,
in one linear pass that holds the previous term.

A frame that declares `canonical` defines each term once. One literal can be stored in more than one kind (a `literal` and a
`literal_value`, or a `literal_ref` and a `literal_value_ref`, of the same typed literal; 2.2, 2.6), and the kind order of 11.1
orders the two definitions without refusing them: a frame that defines one literal twice, in two kinds, breaks its declaration.
A reader that checks the declaration reports `declared_order_violated` for it, at the position of the frame, and still folds the
frame; the two definitions are one term (2.7). The reader judges this when it has resolved the terms of the frame (2.6), by
comparing the literals whole; a term that is unreadable is not compared. The canonical check of `P7` (STRUCTURE) is a separate rule, made on the file as a whole.

**P3-89.** A reader that checks a `canonical` declaration MUST report `declared_order_violated` for a frame that defines one
literal twice, in two kinds of term, and MUST fold the frame all the same.

**P3-20.** A reader that decodes a `terms` frame declaring `canonical` MUST check the declaration and MUST report
`declared_order_violated` when it is broken, and MUST fold the frame all the same.

**P3-21.** A writer MUST NOT declare `canonical` for a frame whose terms are not strictly ascending under the order of 11.1.

**P3-79.** A reader MUST take as the earlier frame that a `canonical` frame follows the nearest earlier frame of its type in item
order, whatever its state, and MUST give a frame whose nearest earlier frame of its type is not decoded no earlier term or row to
follow.

THE TERMS THAT THE CHECK COMPARES. The check of a `canonical` declaration compares the keys of 11.1, which are formed from the
frame and the `terms` frames before it, with no value rebuilt: a text that is not valid `UTF-8` is compared as its bytes, and a
`literal_ref` or `literal_value_ref` term whose value is missing has its digest and its length. A term whose key cannot be
formed (a `datatype` id or a `triple` component id that is not smaller than the term's own id, or a `datatype` id that names a
term that is not an `iri`) is SKIPPED: it is compared with no term, and the term after it is compared with the last term before
it that was not skipped, in the frame or, at the junction, in the earlier frame. The check for one literal defined in two kinds
(P3-89) compares readable terms only.

## 3. The row frames

### 3.1 Columns and their fixed codings

```text
graph_runs   = list([ graph: graph_name, rows: uint ])   ; graph_name: [1] (default_graph) / [2, term_id: uint] (named), P1
delta_column = list(int)                                 ; value[i] = value[i - 1] + d[i], with value[-1] = 0
run_column   = list([ value: uint, count: uint ])        ; the value repeated count times
```

A frame's rows are the concatenation of its graph runs, each naming its graph by the typed variant `graph_name`; the graph is
never a column. Term ids are written as they are, with no shift and no sentinel. The default graph is `[1]` and a named graph
`[2, id]`. The coding of each column is fixed by the frame type, for every file, so that no writer chooses a coding.

- `quads`; `content`: `[order: enum(row_order), row_count: uint]`; decoded payload:
  `[graph_runs, predicates: run_column, objects: delta_column, subjects: delta_column]`
- `reifier_bindings`; `content`: `[order, row_count]`; decoded payload:
  `[graph_runs, reifiers: delta_column, triples: delta_column]`
- `annotations`; `content`: `[order, row_count]`; decoded payload: `[graph_runs, reifiers, predicates, objects, counts]`, every
  column a `delta_column`
- `empty_graphs`; `content`: `[row_count]`; decoded payload: `[graphs: delta_column]`

Row `i` of a frame (counted from `0`) has the graph of the graph run that holds index `i` and, in each column, the decoded value
at index `i`. The `predicates` column of `quads` is run-length coded because the predicate repeats over long stretches of sorted
rows; every other column is delta coded because object, subject, reifier and triple ids move by small steps. The `counts` column
holds the annotation counts. An `empty_graphs` row has no graph run: its column holds term ids of graph names.

**P3-22.** A writer MUST write the columns of each row frame with the coding of the table above, and a reader MUST take the graph
of a row from the graph run that holds its index, the default graph being the variant `default_graph` and never a column value.

### 3.2 What a reader checks in the payload

A decoded value is the integer that a delta column or a run column gives; it is judged against the range of a `uint`.

**P3-23.** A reader MUST report `malformed_value` with `wrong_length` for a row frame whose payload is not the record of the table
above, and with `out_of_range` for a `row_count` of `0`, for a graph run whose `rows` is `0`, for a run of a `run_column` whose
`count` is `0`, and for a delta column in which any decoded value is negative or not below `2^64`.

**P3-24.** A reader MUST report `malformed_value` with `inconsistent_counts` when the `rows` of the graph runs do not sum to
`row_count`, when a delta column does not hold exactly `row_count` elements, and when the `count` fields of a `run_column` do
not sum to `row_count`.

**P3-25.** A reader MUST report `malformed_value` with `not_deterministic` for two adjacent graph runs that name one graph and for
two adjacent runs of a `run_column` that hold one value: one run is the one encoding of such rows.

**P3-26.** A reader MUST report `malformed_value` with `out_of_range` for an `annotations` row whose decoded `count` is `0`, and
`malformed_value` with `unregistered_value` for an `order` that is not a `row_order` value of its registry version, at its
place in step 1 of 1.4 and only for a frame whose link state is `linked` or `healed`; an `order` of a newer registry version than
the reader's, in a segment of that version, has the outcome that `P2-62` gives, at the same place.

A frame that fails P3-23 to P3-26, or P3-27, is opaque with `malformed` and is not folded as a whole.

### 3.3 Rows are sets, annotations are counted

Within one frame two rows that are equal after their ids are resolved to terms (4.1) are one row: an exact repeat of a `quads`,
`reifier_bindings` or `empty_graphs` row collapses and is no finding. Two `annotations` rows that are equal in every field other
than the count, equal counts included, make the frame malformed (`L1`, paragraph "WELL-FORMED INPUT", condition W3). This
rule is judged when the frame is decoded, from the frame and the `terms` frames before it, with no value rebuilt: two ids are
equal when they are one id or name terms that are written alike: a term of a storage form (`literal_ref`, `literal_value`,
`literal_value_ref`) is written like another term of its own kind with equal fields, and never like a term of another kind.
WRITTEN ALIKE is defined by kind: two `iri` terms with equal texts; two `blank` terms with equal labels; two `literal` terms
with equal lexical forms, equal forms and, by the form, datatypes that are written alike or equal language texts; two
`literal_ref` terms with equal digests, lengths and forms and the same last component; two `literal_value` terms with equal
typed values; two `literal_value_ref` terms with equal digests and lengths; two `triple` terms whose three components are
written alike, component by component. Two
rows that only a rebuilt value or a generated text makes equal are equal lines of the
fold. The fold adds the counts of equal annotation lines, of different frames or of one frame (5.1).

**P3-27.** A reader MUST report `malformed_value` with `unordered_or_repeated` for an `annotations` frame that holds two rows
equal, by the rule of 3.3, in every field other than the count.

### 3.4 The declared order of rows

`order` is `canonical` or `unordered`; `unordered` declares nothing. `canonical` declares that the rows of the frame are strictly
ascending under the key of 11.2, comparing term ids, and that the first row follows the last row of the nearest earlier frame
of the same type in the segment, the nearest in item order whatever its state, when that frame also declares `canonical`; a frame
whose nearest earlier frame of its type is not decoded (opaque, malformed or above the reader's capacity, so that its last row is
unknown) has no earlier row to follow. A canonical frame has exactly one graph run for each
graph present, in ascending graph order, because the rows ascend by graph first. The `empty_graphs` frame has no `order` field
and declares nothing; its canonical form is the sorted column of 11.2.

**P3-28.** A reader that decodes a `quads`, `reifier_bindings` or `annotations` frame declaring `canonical` MUST check the
declaration in one linear pass, MUST report `declared_order_violated` when it is broken and MUST fold the frame all the same.

**P3-29.** A writer MUST NOT declare `canonical` for a frame whose rows are not strictly ascending under the key of 11.2.

## 4. From ids to typed lines

### 4.1 Resolution

An id is DEFINED at a frame when an earlier `terms` frame of the segment reserves it (2.1). The terms of a segment are
resolved to typed values by their ids: an `iri` to `iri(text)`; a `literal` or `literal_ref` term to `literal(...)` when its
form is `typed` and to `lang_literal(...)` when its form is a language form; a `literal_value` or `literal_value_ref` term to the
`literal(...)` of its generated lexical form and datatype (1.5); a `blank` to
`blank(ordinal, label)` with `ordinal` the ordinal of the segment in the file read, and a `triple` to `triple(s, p, o)` of the
resolved components. A row, a binding and a suppression entry are resolved by resolving every id they hold, in their own segment.

### 4.2 What happens to a row

For each row of a folded row frame, the first condition that holds decides:

1. an id of the row is not defined at the frame: the row is DROPPED, finding `forward_reference` at the frame;
2. an id of the row names an unreadable term (2.8): the row is HELD, finding `opaque_term` at the frame;
3. a field of the row breaks a position rule of 1.2 (a graph, a reifier or a subject that is not an `iri` or a `blank`; a
   predicate that is not an `iri`; a `triples` entry that is not a `triple`): the row is DROPPED,
   finding `position_constraint` at the frame;
4. otherwise the row is a LINE of the fold (5.1).

A frame reports each of `forward_reference`, `opaque_term` and `position_constraint` at most once, at its position, however many
rows cause it. A DROPPED row is not a line: it breaks a position rule and states nothing, and the content digest is not affected.
A
HELD row is a statement that the reader could not read, so the content digest is `absent` (8.1). Rows that remain are folded.

**P3-30.** A reader MUST apply the conditions of 4.2 in their order to every row of a folded row frame, MUST drop or hold a row as
the first condition says, and MUST report each of the three codes of 4.2 at most once for a frame.

### 4.3 Held

A HELD row, binding or suppression entry hides and states nothing, is not a line, binding or entry of the fold, and is reported
`opaque_term` once for the frame that holds it. The content digest of a fold with a held item is `absent`; the reader still
produces the fold of everything else.

## 5. The fold

### 5.1 Definition

THE FOLD of a file is a function of the contributions of its folded frames and of nothing else. Which frames are folded is decided
by the reading procedure of `P7`; a frame that fails a check, a frame after an `unsupported_critical`, a frame of a segment with
an unreadable opener and a frame that is opaque for any reason contribute nothing. The fold is the quintuple of

1. its LINES: the union of the lines of every folded row frame (4.2). `quad`, `reifier_binding` and `empty_graph` lines are
   united as sets. An `annotation` line whose fields other than the count equal those of another annotation line adds its
   count to that line's count (multiset union), so the fold holds one `annotation` line for each distinct (reifier, predicate,
   object, graph) and its count is the sum;
2. its BINDINGS: the union of the bindings of every folded `bindings` frame, each resolved to a pair of a term value and a
   referent (7.1);
3. its BLOB SET: the set that `P4` defines over the declarations of the folded frames;
4. its SUPPRESSION RECORD: the set of the resolved entries of every folded `suppress` frame (9.3);
5. its VALUES: the set of the pairs of a typed literal and a typed value, one for each readable `literal_value` and
   `literal_value_ref` term of every folded `terms` frame, the literal being the term's literal and the value its typed value
   (1.5). A literal is in at most one pair, since it is the literal of at most one typed value (1.5). A literal stored as text
   only gives no pair; a literal stored both as text and as a typed value has the pair.

An `empty_graph` line whose name another line of the fold CARRIES (1.3) is not in the fold: the graph is not empty. Nothing is
inferred from the absence of a line. The test is made against every line of the fold, hidden or not.

WHICH FOLD. The fold above is the fold of the file. A segment's OWN fold is the fold of that segment read alone as a file:
a pair `[frame, label]`, a value named by a `literal_ref` or `literal_value_ref` term, or a binding referent that lies in another segment is
unresolved there. The file's fold resolves across segments exactly in those three cases (6.3, 2.6, 7.1). A recipe resolves
the units of its entries in its own segment only (`P4`). For a segment's own fold a reader reports exactly two things: its
content digest (8.1) and the finding `labelling_refused` when the labelling refuses (6.5). Every other finding about content
(`missing_dependency`, `suppression_unmatched`, `binding_unanchored`, `opaque_term`, `conflicting_declaration` and the
rest) is reported ONCE, for the file's fold.

A fold holds no `value` declaration: in the fold a literal is a literal, whatever form stored it, and what a typed value adds
is its pair of VALUES. The values of the CANONICAL FILE, the octets of its `value` frames, are defined
once, in `P7`, in its section "The construction, from a fold to the item sequence".

THE ORDER OF DELIVERY. The fold is a set, and every listing of it (the lines, the bindings, the suppression record, VALUES, the
visible dataset and its named graphs) is delivered, by a reader with the access `whole`, in the order of the canonical file of the
fold, which `P7` states once, in its paragraph THE ORDER OF THE CONTENT, over the orders of 11.1 to 11.4; a reader with the access
`forward` delivers the content in arrival order (`P7`, "Reading as a stream"), and a reader with the access `sparse` delivers
an answer in the order that `P7`, "Reading by range", THE ANSWER, states. One case is decided here. The ids of the canonical
dictionary give a blank node its place by its final position, and when the labelling is refused (6.5) there is no final position:
the blank nodes are then compared with one another, in the place of their positions, bytewise on the source identifier
`E([ordinal, label])` (6.1), and every other comparison of 11.1 to 11.4 is unchanged. The finding `labelling_refused` in the
findings of the file is the sign that this order was used (`P7`, THE SIGN), and this order is not invariant
under a renaming of labels. In a fold with nested terms, the final positions of its blank nodes depend on the source
identifiers of its term nodes where the labelling orders top components of equal identity or breaks a tie among twins, and those
identifiers are digests (6.2a): the order is defined and the same in every reader, and it is no function of the labels alone.

**P3-75.** A reader with the access `whole` MUST deliver every listing of the fold in the order of `P7`, THE ORDER OF THE CONTENT, and MUST compare blank
nodes bytewise on `E([ordinal, label])` when the labelling is refused.

Suppression removes nothing from the fold (section 9): every line, binding and blob declaration of every folded frame is in it.
Removal and change of content are recorded as content (tombstone statements, versioned graphs) and never by suppression. Quotation
does not imply assertion; a `reifier_binding` line states that its reifier stands for its triple term, and an `annotation` line
states one property of a reifier, each in the graph its slot names (`L1`, paragraph "WHAT THE KINDS DENOTE").

**P3-31.** A reader MUST compute the lines of the fold as the union of 5.1 item 1 with annotation counts added, MUST remove an
`empty_graph` line from the fold when another line of the fold carries its name, and MUST treat a sum of annotation counts
above `2^64 - 1` as the refusal `width` (6.5), and MUST then deliver that line with the count `2^64 - 1`.

**P3-32.** A reader MUST NOT let the order of frames or the position of a line in a frame change which
lines, bindings, blob declarations (the length of an entry is the smallest declared, `P4`), record entries and pairs of VALUES
are in the fold,
given which rows, bindings and entries are valid and which terms are readable (the validity of 4.2 depends on the order of
frames, because an id is defined before it is used, and the readability of a `literal_ref` or `literal_value_ref` term depends on
it, because a value serves only the terms of the `terms` frames after it, in its segment or a later one, 2.6, as a unit serves only
the declarations in its frame or after it, `P4`). A reader MUST take the ordinal of each segment, its lineage, the holder of each
frame, the segment to which a `state` referent resolves and the values that serve a term from the order of segments as 5.3
defines it, the values that serve a term also from the order of the items of its segment (2.6), and from nothing else.

### 5.2 The fold is well formed for `L1`

By construction the lines of the fold satisfy the well-formedness conditions of `L1` (3.3): every slot holds a variant that the
position rules of 1.2 admit (W1, W4); every annotation count is at least `1` (W2, section 3.2 and the sum of 5.1); no two lines
agree in every field other than the count (W3, because 5.1 unites equal lines and adds the counts of equal annotations); no line
carries a term that an `empty_graph` line holds (W5, because 5.1 removes such a line). The width conditions (W6) can fail only by
a sum of annotation counts or a quantity above `2^64 - 1`, which is the refusal `width` (6.5). The `record` lines add no repeated
line (6.2: two equal records are one) and carry no graph
name, so they affect none of W1 to W5. The lowering of 6.2a keeps every one of them: it changes no graph slot and no count, it puts
a blank node only where a triple term stood, inside a triple term, where every variant is admitted, so the `triple` slot of a
`reifier_binding` line still holds a `triple`; and it maps two different lines to two different lines, because two different
nested terms have two different term nodes (6.2a).

### 5.3 What the order of frames and the order of segments decide

The union, the sum of counts and the set of entries are commutative and associative, the removal of an `empty_graph` line is
decided by the union as a whole, and the length of a blob entry is the smallest length declared for its name (`P4`). So the order
of frames, and of rows within a frame, decides nothing about which lines, bindings, blob declarations, entries and pairs
of VALUES are in the fold,
beyond the validity of 4.2 and what stands before what: a value serves only the terms of the `terms` frames after it (2.6), and a
unit only the declarations in its frame or after it (`P4`, "The unit index and the unit used"); a later identical frame adds no line, a repeated annotation adds its count, and nothing in a later
segment can revive a line or withdraw a line from the fold, an `empty_graph` line excepted (5.1). This is the property that makes a prefix of a file a file (`P1`) and
makes the content digest a function of the fold (8.2).

The order of segments is part of a file. Segments stand in file order, the order of their openers in the bytes, and that
order is defined by the bytes alone. It decides exactly five things: the ORDINAL of each segment (zero-based in file order),
which is the first part of the name of each of its blank nodes (1.1); the LINEAGE of each segment, which is judged against the
segment before it (`P1`); the HOLDER of a frame, which is the first segment in file order, other than the segment that holds
the entry, that holds it (6.3), and so the node that a pair names; and the RESOLUTION of a `state` referent, which is the first
segment in file order that holds a commit of the referent's effective identifier that names a state (7.2); and the VALUES that
serve a term, which stand in the segment of its `terms` frame or in an earlier one (2.6). The states that
several such segments name are equal (7.2, claim ST2), so the resolution of a `state` referent fixes only the ordinals that the
blank nodes of the resolved state carry.

## 6. The labelled set and what the format takes from the labelling

THREE STATEMENTS OF `L1` AS THIS FORMAT TAKES THEM. Where the text of `L1` says otherwise, these three govern for every part of
this format. None changes a result of the labelling: a position, a label, a certificate, an identity, a digest or a refusal.

1. RENAMING. Equal canonical line sequences for isomorphic inputs hold without assumption only for an input with at most one
   class of top components. For an input with two or more top components that are not isomorphic they hold under the hash
   assumption that `L1` states in its paragraph THE HASH, THE IDENTIFIER AND THE SECURITY ASSUMPTION. Equal certificates and
   equal identities hold without assumption. Claim D2 (8.2) is stated under that assumption.
2. MEMORY. The working state of the labelling is `O(O + Z)` integers, `O` being the number of label elements of the input: a
   record line whose list names one blank node several times has one integer for each occurrence. It is not bounded by the
   number of lines.
3. TIME. The units that Lemma T of `L1` bounds are at most six times the units of its part (b) plus twice the units of its
   part (c). The bound is a bound on time and on nothing else.

### 6.1 The labelled set

The format calls `L1` ONCE per fold, on the LABELLED SET of the fold: its RDF lines (every line, hidden or not) together
with one `record` line for each binding, each suppression entry, each blob declaration and each pair of VALUES
of the fold (6.2) and one for each nested term of the fold (6.2a), every term in its lowered form (6.2a). There is no
second call, no labelling of a prefix, no labelling of the RDF lines alone and no other dataset. Every stored or reported
final position comes from that one run.

- Every RDF line of the fold is a typed line of `L1`, in the slots of 1.3, with the terms of 1.1 mapped to the terms of
  `L1` (section "Typed lines, masking and encoding", paragraph "TERMS"): `iri(text)` to `iri` with the bytes of the text; a
  typed literal, in every storage form, to `literal` with the bytes of the lexical form, the bytes of the datatype IRI as
  `datatype`, the language `absent` and the direction `absent` (for a typed value, its generated lexical form and datatype,
  1.5); a language literal to `literal` with the bytes of the lexical form, the generated datatype of 1.1 as `datatype`, the
  bytes of the language text as the language `present`, and the direction as it stands; `triple` to `triple`; and
  `blank(ordinal, label)` to `blank(b)` with `b = E([ordinal, label])`, the deterministic encoding of the typed pair, as `bytes`.
  `L1` compares `b` whole and reads nothing in it.
- The `annotation` count is the field `count` of the fold's annotation line.
- The `record` lines are those of 6.2, with the terms mapped in the same way.
- Every term of a line and of a record is mapped in its lowered form (6.2a), and the term node of a nested term is mapped to
  `blank(b)` with `b` its source identifier of 6.2a.

The source identifier orders the nodes only for the canonical map (`L1`, paragraph "THE CANONICAL MAP"): it
decides which twin takes which position and the order of copies of one top component. It decides nothing in the
canonical line sequence or its digest.

**P3-33.** A reader MUST call `L1` once per fold, on the labelled set of 6.1, and MUST use `E([ordinal, label])` as the source
identifier of each blank node of the fold and the source identifier of 6.2a as that of each term node.

### 6.2 Record lines

A BINDING, a SUPPRESSION ENTRY, a BLOB DECLARATION, a PAIR OF VALUES and a NESTED TERM (6.2a) are not RDF
lines. Each is carried into the labelled set by one `record`
line, so that a blank node that a binding or an entry names takes part in the labelling with the structure that the binding or the
entry gives it. The record of an item is `record(payload, terms)`: the payload holds the constants of the item and `terms` holds
its terms, both in the order of the item's wire record; a nested term has no wire record, and its record is that of 6.2a.

```text
record_kind        = enum(record_kind)       ; 1 binding, 2 suppression, 3 blob_declaration, 4 value, 5 nested_term (P2)
payload            = E([ record_kind, body ])
body               = binding_body / suppression_body / blob_declaration_body / value_body
                   / nested_term_body                                             ; by record_kind
value_body         = [ kind: enum(value_kind), literal: slot ]                    ; the slot is [1]
nested_term_body   = [ node: slot, subject: slot, predicate: slot, object: slot ] ; every slot is [1]
binding_body       = [ term: slot, referent: referent ]
suppression_body   = [1, term: slot]                                              ; kind term
                   / [2, s: slot, p: slot, o: slot, g: option(slot)]              ; kind quad
                   / [3, reifier: slot]                                           ; kind reifier
                   / [4, graph: slot]                                             ; kind graph
                   / [5, blob: qualified_name]                                    ; kind blob
                   / [6, r: slot, t: slot, g: option(slot)]                       ; kind reifier_binding
                   / [7, r: slot, p: slot, o: slot, g: option(slot)]              ; kind annotation
                   / [8, term: slot, referent: referent]                          ; kind binding
blob_declaration_body = [ presence: enum(blob_presence), name: qualified_name, length: uint, media_types: list(text) ]
slot               = [1]                                                          ; slot_kind term: the next member of terms
                   / [2, frame: digest, label: bytes]                             ; slot_kind unnamed: a pair that names no node
                   / [3, subject: slot, predicate: slot, object: slot]            ; slot_kind triple: one unnamed at least
```

The tag of `suppression_body` is the `suppression_kind` of the entry. `option(slot)` is `[1]` or `[2, slot]`, so that the
presence of an optional term slot is a constant of the payload and the term list never shifts. `slot_kind` is an enumeration of
the registry (`term`, `unnamed`, `triple`).

THE TERM LIST. `terms` is the list of the term values of the slots tagged `term`, taken in a depth-first walk of the body in the
order of its fields, `option(slot)` entering its slot when `present`, and a slot tagged `triple` entering its three components
in order. A term value is the typed value of 1.1 resolved from the term id or the leaf (6.3); a blank node in it is the node
`blank(ordinal, label)`. A `blob` entry and a `blob_declaration` have an empty term list, a `value`
record has its one literal, and a `nested_term` record has the four terms of 6.2a. The term list of a record of an item is that
of its values as the item holds them; the labelled set holds each of them in its lowered form (6.2a). A named component of a
slot tagged `triple` stands directly in the term list and is of depth one there.

EVERY RECORD OF THE FOLD. A record is built for each of:

1. each binding of the fold, with `record_kind` `binding`;
2. each entry of the suppression record (9.3), with `record_kind` `suppression`;
3. each entry of the blob set (`P4`), with `record_kind` `blob_declaration` and the body `[presence, name, length, media_types]`,
   the four fields of the `blob_entry` of `P4` as they stand. The blob set itself is defined by `P4`; this part only
   turns each of its entries into one record.
4. each pair of VALUES (5.1), with `record_kind` `value`, the body `[kind, [1]]` with the `value_kind` of its typed value, and
   the term list that holds its literal. The value is not in the payload: the literal fixes it (1.5). The payload is `82 04 82 02
   81 01` for a value of kind `uint` and `82 04 82 04 81 01` for one of kind `time`, six bytes for every kind.
5. each nested term of the fold (6.2a), with `record_kind` `nested_term`, the body `[[1], [1], [1], [1]]` and the term list of
   6.2a. The payload is `82 05 84 81 01 81 01 81 01 81 01`, eleven bytes, for every nested term.

Two records equal in payload and `terms` are one record. A hidden binding, entry or declaration has its record like any other:
hidden content is content.

**P3-34.** A reader MUST add to the labelled set one `record` line for each binding, each suppression
entry, each blob-set entry, each pair of VALUES and each nested term of the fold, with the payload and the term list of 6.2, MUST
add no other line, and MUST treat two
records equal in payload and terms as one.

**P3-35.** A writer and a reader MUST build the payload of a record as the deterministic encoding of `[record_kind, body]`
with the body of the kind of 6.2, with every optional term slot written as `[1]` or `[2, slot]`, and the term list in the order of
the walk of 6.2.

### 6.2a Nested terms, lowering and term nodes

A term is HANDED TO `L1` when it stands in a slot of an RDF line of the fold or in the term list of a record of 6.2.

NESTED TERMS. A NESTED TERM of the fold is a triple term that is the object of another triple term, at any depth, in a term
handed to `L1`. Two nested terms that are equal as typed values (1.1, IDENTITY) are ONE nested
term, wherever and however often they occur. The position rules of 1.2 bind a dictionary term and a leaf of tag `3` alike (2.8;
6.3, POSITIONS IN A LEAF), so a nested term is always the OBJECT of the term that holds it. The terms that leaves build and the
terms of the dictionaries are interned in ONE table by typed value, so a triple term that a leaf builds and that equals a term of
a dictionary is that term.

LOWERING. The LOWERED FORM of a term `T` is `T` when `T` is not a triple term, and for a triple term `(s, p, o)` it is the
triple term `(N(s), N(p), N(o))`, where `N(x)` is `x` when `x` is not a triple term and the term node of `x` (below) when `x` is
a triple term. Every term handed to `L1` is replaced by its lowered form before the labelled set is formed, so no triple term of
the labelled set holds a triple term: `L1` meets triple terms of depth one only. ONE VALUE AT TWO DEPTHS. Where a triple term
stands in a slot of a line or directly in the term list of a record, it is a triple term of depth one there, with its own
components lowered; where the same value is a component of another triple term, it is a nested term, with its node and its
record. The two are independent: the value that stands in the slot or in the list is not replaced by the node. A reader lowers
the terms of a fold from the ids of its triple terms and from its leaves, interning each nested term once, and forms no triple
term that holds a triple term for the labelled set.

THE TERM NODE. Each nested term `x = (s, p, o)` of the fold has one TERM NODE, a blank node of the labelled set that is no blank
node of the fold, and the labelled set holds one record line for it, its NESTED-TERM RECORD
`record(P, [node of x, N(s), N(p), N(o)])`, with `P` the payload `82 05 84 81 01 81 01 81 01 81 01` (6.2, item 5). A nested
term has one node and one record, however many lines, records and terms hold it.

THE SOURCE OF A COMPONENT. The SOURCE `K(c)` of a component `c` of a nested term is a byte string:

- a blank node `b = blank(ordinal, label)`: `E([0, h(b)])`, an array of the `uint` `0` and the digest
  `h(b) = H_ctx(nested_term, E([ordinal, label]))` of its source identifier (6.1); `h(b)` is computed once for the fold;
- a triple term: `E([d(c)])`, the source identifier of its term node (below);
- any other term: `E(k)`, the deterministic encoding of the byte string `k` whose bytes are `E(v)`. For `iri(text)`, `v` is
  `[1, text]`, the IRI as a `text` item. For a literal, `v` is `[2, lexical, form, x]`, with `lexical` and `x` as `text` items and
  `form` as the `uint` of its code: `form` is its `literal_form`, `typed` for a typed literal and
  `language`, `language_ltr` or `language_rtl` for a language literal whose direction is `absent`, `ltr` or `rtl`; `x` is the
  text of the datatype IRI of a typed literal and the language of a language literal. The tag of `v` is the `term_kind` `iri` or
  `literal` (`P2`); the record `v` is of the variant `term_source` of `P2`. `v` holds every text as the fold holds it, whole: no letter case is folded and no text is judged (1.1, WHAT
  THE FORMAT DOES NOT JUDGE), so two languages that differ in the case of a letter give two sources. A literal stored as a typed
  value has the source of its generated lexical form and datatype (1.5), which is the source of the equal literal in every
  other storage form.

THE DIGEST AND THE SOURCE IDENTIFIER. The digest of a nested term `x = (s, p, o)` is
`d(x) = H_ctx(nested_term, E([K(s), K(p), K(o)]))`, the message being the array of the three sources as byte strings and the
context that of `P1` ("The one construction and the hash context table"). `d` and `h` are computed with the `blake3_256`
construction of `H_ctx` under the context `nested_term` (code `15`) in every fold, whatever `hash_algorithm` its segments declare:
they are identifiers of nodes for the labelling and enter no hash of the format, and a registry version that changes this says
so. The SOURCE IDENTIFIER of the term node of `x` is
`E([d(x)])`, the deterministic encoding of an array of one element. The three kinds of source never collide, because a source of
a blank node begins with the bytes `82 00` (an array of two whose first element is `0`), a source of a triple term with `81` (an
array of one) and every other source with the head of a byte string (`40` to `5b`), and within each kind the deterministic
encoding is injective. So the source identifier of a term node, `E([d])`, is never that of a blank node of the fold,
`E([ordinal, label])`, which `L1` receives for that node (6.1); only the source `K` of a blank node is formed from `h`. The two uses of the
context `nested_term` share no preimage: the message of `h` is an array of two whose first element is a `uint` (first byte `82`)
and the message of `d` an array of three byte strings (first byte `83`).

USE OF THE DIGEST. `d` and `h` are used only in the source identifier of a term node, which orders nodes where `L1` reads source
identifiers (`L1`, paragraph "THE CANONICAL MAP", and its walk); they enter no line, no payload, no stored field and no digest
of the format. A reader computes `h` once for each blank node that is a component of a nested term, and `d` once for each
distinct nested term, from the sources of its three components, the components first, so the number of hashes is at most the
number of those blank nodes plus the number of distinct nested terms, whatever their depth and however often they occur. THE
BYTES HASHED. The messages of `h` hold the labels of the fold once each, so their bytes are bounded by the decoded bytes of the
`terms` frames. The digests `d` and `h` are of `32` bytes, and the message of each `d` is at most the weight of the record line of its nested term
plus `51` bytes: the
message holds each component as a byte string of at most `29` bytes more than the weight of that component (a source of a blank
node or of a triple term is `38` or `37` bytes against a weight of `9`, and an IRI or a literal is at most `20` bytes more than
its weight, which counts its texts whole and `9` bytes or more besides), the record line weighs `37` plus the weights of its components, and the array head
is `1` byte.

WHAT A FOLD CAN HOLD. A nested term stands only as the object of the term that holds it, so a triple term, however it is built,
names at most one nested term directly, and no fold holds a term that uses a nested term twice. The number of distinct nested
terms that the terms of a dictionary name is at most the number of its `triple` terms whose object is a `triple` term, and a leaf
of tag `3` writes each of its components out (6.3), so the nested terms of a leaf are bounded by its bytes. The statistics of the `index` (`P5`) and every count of a result that describes the data count no term node and no `nested_term`
record; the size and the weight (6.5) count them, and the statistics record and the structural record of `L1` describe the
labelled set and so include them (`P7`, "The typed result of reading"). A canonical map or a stable identity that an engine
offers to a consumer is offered for the blank nodes of the fold only.

SOURCE ORDER ACROSS KINDS. To `L1` the source identifiers of term nodes and of the blank nodes of the fold are byte strings of one
kind. Where `L1` orders nodes by source identifier (its source order, which its canonical map and its walk read), they are
compared bytewise and whole, with no regard to whether a node is a term node. Since `E([d])` begins with the byte `81` and
`E([ordinal, label])` with `82`, every term node precedes every blank node of the fold in that order.

A COLLISION. Two different nested terms of one fold whose digests `d` are equal would give two term nodes one source identifier.
A reader interns the nested terms by value and so meets every such pair. The labelling of that fold is refused with the reason
`refusal_term_node` (6.5).

THREE CASES. (1) A fold with no triple term and (2) a fold whose triple terms, in its dictionaries and its leaves, are all of
depth one, such as a reifier of a triple of IRIs, literals and blank nodes with annotations on it, have no nested term: the
lowered form of every term is the term itself, the labelled set holds no term node and no `nested_term` record and is the set of
6.1 and 6.2, and both nested-candidate numbers of its size are `0` (6.5). (3) A fold with a nested term has one term node and one
record line for each of its distinct nested terms, and its labelled set, final positions and content digest are those of its
lowered set.

CLAIM L (the lowering keeps isomorphism). Two folds have isomorphic labelled sets exactly when their lines and records before
lowering are isomorphic. Proof. A term node is exactly a blank node that stands first in the term list of a `nested_term`
record, and it stands first in one record only, whose other three terms give its nested term with every nested component
replaced by its node. An isomorphism of two labelled sets keeps every payload, so it maps term nodes to term nodes; by induction
on depth it maps the nested term of each node to the nested term of the image node, and replacing every node by its nested term
gives an isomorphism of the lines and records before lowering. Conversely an isomorphism of the lines and records before
lowering maps nested terms one to one onto nested terms, and extended to their nodes it maps one labelled set onto the other.
End of proof.

**P3-107.** A reader MUST replace every term handed to `L1` by its lowered form of 6.2a before it forms the labelled set, MUST
treat as nested every triple term that is the object of another triple term in a term handed to `L1`, and MUST treat two nested
terms equal as typed values as one.

**P3-108.** A reader MUST give each distinct nested term of a fold one term node and one `nested_term` record of 6.2a, and MUST
NOT replace by a node a triple term that stands in a slot of a line or directly in the term list of a record.

**P3-109.** A writer and a reader MUST give the term node of a nested term `x` the source identifier `E([d(x)])`, with `d` and
the sources `K` and the digests `h` of 6.2a, and MUST NOT use `d` or `h` in any line, payload, stored field or digest of the
format.

**P3-110.** A reader MUST refuse the labelling of a fold that holds two different nested terms with equal digests `d`, with
`labelling_refused` and the reason `refusal_term_node`, after the size and the weight of the fold are admitted and before it
builds a record line or calls `L1`.

### 6.3 Naming a blank node in a binding or an entry

A LEAF is the wire form of a term in a binding or an entry (7.1, 9.1):

```text
leaf = [1, term: uint]                                  ; leaf_kind term: a term id of the entry's own segment, of any kind
     / [2, blank: [frame: digest, label: bytes]]        ; leaf_kind blank: a blank node of another segment
     / [3, triple: [leaf, leaf, leaf]]                  ; leaf_kind triple: a triple term that holds a leaf of tag 2 at some depth
```

A leaf of kind `triple` holds leaves to any depth, and no depth is refused (`P1`, "Primitive types"): a reader builds the triple
term of a leaf at depth `d` as it builds one at depth `1`. A blank node of the entry's own segment is named by its term id, as
content does. A blank node of another segment is named by the
typed pair of a FRAME of that segment and its label. The frame is named by its effective identifier; the effective identifier and
the ordinary frames are those of `P1`, in its section "The predecessor and transparent frames", and this part states neither. A
final position is a result of labelling and is never written as a name; no non-canonical file stores one.

A segment HOLDS a frame `f` when its opener outcome is `readable` and it has an ordinary frame whose effective identifier is `f`
and whose link state is `linked` or `healed` (`P1`, "The link check" and "Healing"). A frame whose link is `broken` is held by no
segment, a transparent frame is held by no segment, and a header identifier is the identifier of no frame. The HOLDER of a frame
`f` for an entry is the first segment in file order, other than the segment that holds the entry, that holds `f`. The holder of a
frame is one segment, and a segment that comes after the holder and holds the same frame is not searched.

RESOLVING a leaf gives one resolution or none:

- tag `1`: the one value of the term id (4.1);
- tag `2`: the node `blank(ordinal, label)`, where `ordinal` is the ordinal of the HOLDER of `frame` and the dictionary of the
  holder holds a `blank` term with that `label`. This is at most one node: the holder is one segment, and two blank terms of one
  segment with equal labels are one node (2.4). No holder, or a holder whose dictionary holds no such label, gives no resolution:
  the leaf is UNNAMED, and the dictionary of a later segment that holds the same frame is not consulted. In a segment's own fold
  (5.1) the segment is the only one, and it is excluded as the holder, so a pair is UNNAMED there;
- tag `3`: the triple term of the resolutions of its three components, when every component has a resolution; otherwise the leaf
  is UNNAMED as a whole.

POSITIONS IN A LEAF. The position rules of 1.2 bind a leaf of tag `3` as they bind a term of a dictionary: its subject is a leaf
of tag `1` that names an `iri` or a `blank` term, or a leaf of tag `2`; its predicate is a leaf of tag `1` that names an `iri`;
its object is any leaf. A leaf of tag `3` that breaks them, at any depth, is an UNREADABLE LEAF, and the binding or the entry that
holds it is HELD with `opaque_term` (4.3), as one that names an unreadable term (2.8). The rule reads the tags of the leaves and
the kinds of the terms that their ids name, and it is judged whether or not the leaf is named.

**P3-112.** A reader MUST hold, with `opaque_term`, a binding or an entry that holds a leaf of tag `3`, at any depth, whose subject
is not a leaf of tag `2` or a leaf of tag `1` that names an `iri` or a `blank` term, or whose predicate is not a leaf of tag `1`
that names an `iri`.

A node is NAMED by a pair when a folded `terms` frame of the holder holds a blank term of that label, whether or not any line
holds it: a blank node that occurs only in records is a node of the labelled set like any other. An entry has one record line.

An UNNAMED leaf has no node and no position. The entry (or binding) that holds it is a GROUND record: its slot for the leaf is
`[2, frame, label]` with the pair as written (for a triple leaf: `[3, ...]` with the components that are named as `[1]` and
the unnamed ones as `[2, ...]`), and its `terms` list holds the other terms. An entry with an unnamed leaf has one record line,
a ground record. A ground record has no node of the pair in it, so the labelled set is a function of the fold.

**P3-36.** A reader MUST resolve a leaf by the rules of 6.3, MUST give a pair at most one node and an entry one record
line, and MUST write an unnamed leaf as the slot `[2, frame, label]` with the pair as written.

**P3-59.** A reader MUST take as the holder of a frame the first segment in file order, other than the segment that holds the
entry, that holds the frame as 6.3 defines holding, MUST resolve a pair only in the dictionary of that segment, and MUST NOT
let a frame whose link state is `broken`, a transparent frame or a header identifier name any segment.

### 6.4 What the format uses of the result

- the canonical line sequence and its digest (paragraph "THE CANONICAL LINE SEQUENCE AND ITS DIGEST"), computed with `L1`'s own
  context, over the labelled set; where the format uses it: the content digest (8.1)
- the final position of every blank node of the fold (paragraph "THE CANONICAL MAP", with the section "Final positions");
  where the format uses it: blank labels of the canonical file (11.1) and the order of delivery (5.1), and nothing else. The
  final position of a term node (6.2a)
  is used for nothing: it is not stored, and no label is written for it
- the predicate `guaranteed` (paragraph "THE GUARANTEE, THE PREDICATE `guaranteed` AND THE SEARCH-FREE RUN"); where the format
  uses it: whether a reader's own work limit can stop the labelling (6.5). It is judged on the lowered labelled set (6.2a), as
  every result of `L1` is, so two blank nodes that only nested terms hold are not twins there: each stands in the record of its
  own term node
- the refusals (paragraph "OUTCOMES AND REFUSALS"); where the format uses it: the finding `labelling_refused` (6.5)
- the stable identity and the canonical map (paragraphs "STABLE IDENTITY" and "THE CANONICAL MAP"); where the format uses it:
  not stored; an engine can offer them to consumers, for the blank nodes of the fold only (6.2a, WHAT A FOLD CAN HOLD)

The format stores no label text and writes no position into any hash. `L1` states a label as the position in decimal (7.14) for
document formats that need text; this format has no use for it. A canonical file stores a blank node's label as `E(position)`
(11.1), which no reader reads back.

**P3-37.** A reader MUST NOT store, parse or compare the label spelling of `L1` paragraph "LABEL SPELLING (the one place)" (7.14)

### 6.5 Refusals and the reader's limit

The labelled set is well formed for `L1` (5.2, 6.1), so the refusals `input` and `graph` cannot occur; an engine whose `L1`
call returns one is wrong. The refusal `width` can occur and is the finding `labelling_refused` with reason `refusal_width`.
When the sum of the counts of an annotation line exceeds `2^64 - 1`, the labelling is refused with `refusal_width`, and the line
is delivered with the count `2^64 - 1`; the refusal says that the count is not exact. The refusal `size` is the format's own,
is made before `L1` is called (below) and is the finding `labelling_refused` with reason
`refusal_size`. The refusal `weight` is the format's own too, is made after the size and before `L1` is called (below) and is
the finding `labelling_refused` with reason `refusal_weight`. The refusal `term_node` is the format's own as well, is made after
the weight and before `L1` is called, when two different nested terms of the fold have equal digests (6.2a, A COLLISION), and is
the finding `labelling_refused` with reason `refusal_term_node`. A
reader has a work limit, `labelling_units` of its capacity (`P7`), which is the limit `L` of the walk of `L1` (section "The walk
and the outcome of a limit"): whether the limit trips, and at which charge, is decided by that walk and is the same in every
reader that has this value. By `L1` paragraph "THE GUARANTEE, THE PREDICATE
`guaranteed` AND THE SEARCH-FREE RUN" (7.10) a reader's limit is never applied to an input for which the predicate `guaranteed`
holds: every conforming reader labels every `guaranteed` input, and the bound of `L1` for such an input is a function of the
input, so no single number is the minimum. `labelling_units` is the limit the reader applies to every other input. A reader
MUST perform the search-free run on every top component before it begins the search of any top component (`L1`, paragraph
"THE GUARANTEE, THE PREDICATE `guaranteed` AND THE SEARCH-FREE RUN"), so that a refusal `limit` carries the first failure
of every component that is not `guaranteed`. A limit that trips is the refusal `limit` and the finding `labelling_refused`
with reason `refusal_limit`. THE ONE REFUSAL. A fold that meets more than one refusal gives one `labelling_refused`. The refusal
`size` is judged first, the refusal `weight` second (below) and the refusal `term_node` third; the first of the three that
applies is the one. Each is judged before the work that the next needs: the size needs content records and a count over
decoded payloads, the weight needs the lengths of texts and the structure of terms and no hash, and the digests of the nested
terms, which the refusal `term_node` needs, are computed only for a fold whose size and weight are admitted. Otherwise the
refusal is the one of the smallest code among `width` and `limit`, as `L1` selects in its paragraph "OUTCOMES AND REFUSALS",
so that `width` is chosen before `limit`; the refusal `width` for a sum of annotation counts above `2^64 - 1`, which the format
makes at the fold, is judged with them, after `term_node`. The finding carries the reason of that refusal.
In every refusal the content digest is `absent`, the labelling level of the canonical check
is `unchecked` (`P7`) and nothing falls back to another labelling or digest. A writer that cannot label does not claim the
canonical form; that is no finding of the file.

THE SIZE OF THE LABELLED SET. The SIZE of a fold is the sum of six numbers over its folded frames: the `row_count` of each
folded row frame; the `entry_count` of each folded `bindings` frame and each folded `suppress` frame; the number of its
folded declarations of a blob (`P4`, "Identity": a `blob`, `recipe` or `external` frame, and each `pack` member of kind `blob` or
`recipe`); the `count` of each run of kind `literal_value` or `literal_value_ref` of each folded `terms` frame; the number of
the NESTED CANDIDATES of each folded `terms` frame, the terms of its `triple` blocks whose object id is an id that a folded
`terms` frame of the same segment reserves with the kind `triple` (the kinds are those that the runs of the content records give,
2.1), whether that frame stands before or after it and whether or not either term is readable, one for each such term, since it
names one nested term (6.2a, WHAT A FOLD CAN HOLD); and the number of the nested candidates of each folded `bindings` frame and
each folded `suppress` frame: for every leaf of tag `3`, at every depth, of every binding and every entry, each of its three
components that is a leaf of tag `3`, or a leaf of tag `1` whose id is reserved with the kind `triple` in the same way (6.3). An
object id that no folded `terms` frame of the segment reserves counts nothing. The first four numbers are functions of the
content records of the folded frames and the last two of their decoded payloads and of the runs of the folded `terms` frames, an
upper bound that needs no term to be formed or resolved, so a reader has the size before it forms the union of the lines,
builds a record or calls `L1`. The last two are an upper bound of
the distinct nested terms of the fold, since every nested term is the object of a `triple` term of a dictionary or a component
of a leaf of tag `3`; and the size is an upper bound of the number of lines and records of the labelled set, since the union
only merges and two equal nested terms are one. A fold whose dictionaries and leaves hold no triple term inside a triple term
has both last numbers equal to `0`. A reader has a capacity `labelled_lines` (`P7`). When the size of the fold exceeds it, the
labelling is refused with the reason `refusal_size`: no record is built and `L1` is not called. A size equal to the capacity is
labelled. The size is judged first: when it exceeds the capacity the refusal is `size`, and no other refusal of the labelling is judged
for that fold. The refusal does not depend on `guaranteed`: that predicate bounds the search work of a set the reader accepts. The
refusal withholds the labelled set and nothing else: the lines, bindings, suppression record, hiding and visible dataset are
those of the fold and are reported as for any file, and the content digest is `absent` as in every refusal. In particular the
suppression record of the fold (9.3, the resolved entries) is reported; what is not built is a record LINE (6.2): no record
line and no labelled set is built. The size of a
segment's own fold (5.1) is taken over that segment's folded frames alone.

THE WEIGHT OF THE LABELLED SET. The size counts lines and records, not bytes. A line of the labelled set holds each of its terms
whole (`L1`, "Typed lines, masking and encoding", paragraph ENCODING), and one long text may stand in many lines. A triple term
of the labelled set has depth one (6.2a): a nested term stands in it as its term node, and its components are written once, in
its own record line. So the lines that name the terms of a chain of triple terms that each name the one before grow with the
length of the chain and not with its square. The weight bounds the bytes that the labelling encodes and hashes, and it is judged
before that work.

- The WEIGHT of a line or of a record line is the length of its encoding by `L1` (paragraph ENCODING), in its lowered form
  (6.2a), with every blank leaf written as `fixed`, which takes `9` bytes whatever its position: the bytes that the digest of the
  canonical line sequence hashes for it.
- The WEIGHT of a term is the length of the encoding of its lowered form so written. It is a function of the term alone, the same
  in every line that holds it, and a reader computes it from the lengths of the term's texts and the weights of its components,
  forming no encoding: an `iri`, `9` plus the length of its text; a typed literal, `19` plus the lengths of its lexical form and
  of its datatype's text; a language literal, `27` plus the lengths of its lexical form, of its generated datatype (1.1) and of
  its language, and `1` more when its direction is `present`; a blank node, `9`; a triple term, `1` plus the weights of its three
  components, where a component that is a triple term weighs `9`, the weight of its term node. The record line of a nested
  term weighs `37` plus the weights of its three components so counted: `1` for the tag, `19` for the payload of eleven bytes and
  its length, `8` for the length of the term list and `9` for the term node. The length of a front-coded text is `prefix_lengths[i]` plus `suffix_lengths[i]`; of the lexical form of a
  `literal_ref` term, its `lengths` element; of a `literal_value_ref` term, twice its `lengths` element; of a `literal_value`
  term, the length of the text that 1.5 generates.
- The WEIGHT OF A FOLD is the sum, over the lines of the fold and over its bindings, suppression entries, blob-set entries,
  pairs of VALUES and distinct nested terms, of the weight of the line or of the record line (6.2, 6.2a) that each gives. It is a
  function of the fold, so a reader has it before it builds a record line or calls `L1`; it is at least the weight of the
  labelled set, since two equal records are one record. A reader counts the distinct nested terms by interning them from the ids
  of the triple terms and from the leaves (6.2a, LOWERING), and forms no digest for the weight.

**P3-106.** A reader MUST hold a triple term of a `terms` frame by the ids of its three components, as the frame writes it, and
MUST NOT form, for the labelling, the whole of a term that names other terms, nor the encoding of a line, before the weight of
the fold that holds it is admitted; for a fold that the size or the weight refuses it forms neither for the labelling (vector
`lowering-deep`).

The work and the memory of a reader on a fold up to the admission of its weight are bounded by the decoded bytes of the frames
and by the counts of its capacity (`P7`, "Capacity"), and never by the weight: a chain of `n` triple terms that each name the one
before costs a reader `n` entries, not the `n` squared nodes of their written forms, and its lowering gives at most `n - 1` term
nodes and record lines (6.2a).

THE TOTAL LABELLED WEIGHT. A bound on each fold would not bound a read: every segment has an own fold, so a file of many small
segments could give each own fold the whole bound. The reader keeps the TOTAL LABELLED WEIGHT of the read: the sum of the weights
of the folds whose labelling it has admitted, in the order in which it labels them: the own fold of each segment in file order, at
OWN FOLD, then the fold of the file, at LABELLING (Canonical form and reading, "The reader procedure"). A fold that the size
refuses is not weighed and adds nothing. Every other fold is judged after its size and before any record line is built or `L1` is
called: when the total kept so far plus its weight is at most `labelled_bytes_per_read` (Canonical form and reading, "Capacity"),
the weight is added and stays added whatever follows, and the fold is labelled unless a later refusal applies: a fold then refused
with `refusal_term_node`, `refusal_width` or `refusal_limit` was admitted by weight and its weight stays in the total; otherwise
the labelling is refused with the reason `refusal_weight`: no record line
is built, `L1` is not called and the weight is not added, and every later fold of the read whose size is admitted is refused in
the same way, whatever its weight. The refusal by weight withholds the labelled set and nothing else, as the refusal by size does:
the lines, bindings, suppression record, hiding and visible dataset of the fold are reported, and the content digest is `absent`.
It does not depend on `guaranteed`.

Worked example (typed values). A segment with no blank node holds the IRI `X` of `1024` bytes (weight `1033`), the triple terms
`t_1 = <<X X X>>` (weight `3100`) and `t_2 = <<X X t_1>>`, and the rows `(X, X, t_1)` and `(X, X, t_2)`. `t_1` is a nested term,
a component of `t_2`, so `t_2` weighs `1 + 1033 + 1033 + 9 = 2076`. A `quad` line weighs `1` plus its three terms plus `1` for
the `absent` graph: `5168` and `4144`. The record line of `t_1` weighs `37 + 3 * 1033 = 3136`, so the fold weighs `12448`. Its
own fold and the fold of the file are equal: the total labelled weight of the read is `24896` when both are labelled. With
`labelled_bytes_per_read` `24895` the own fold is labelled and the fold of the file is refused with `refusal_weight`; with
`12447` the own fold is refused too.

**P3-104.** A reader MUST compute the weight of a fold as 6.5 defines it, from the lengths of texts and the weights of terms,
before it builds a record line or calls `L1`, and MUST NOT weigh a fold that the size refuses.

**P3-105.** A reader MUST admit the labelling of a fold only when the total labelled weight kept so far plus the weight of the
fold is at most its `labelled_bytes_per_read`, taking the own folds in file order and then the fold of the file, and MUST keep
the weight of an admitted fold in the total whatever refusal follows it; MUST refuse every
other fold, and every later fold whose size is admitted, with `labelling_refused` and the reason `refusal_weight`; and MUST NOT
build a record line or call `L1` for a fold so refused.

**P3-60.** A reader MUST compute the size of a fold as 6.5 defines it, MUST refuse the labelling with `labelling_refused` and
the reason `refusal_size` when the size exceeds its capacity `labelled_lines`, and MUST NOT build a record line or call `L1` for a
fold so refused, and MUST report the suppression record of that fold.

**P3-61.** A reader MUST NOT refuse by size a fold whose size is at most its capacity `labelled_lines`, whether or not
`guaranteed` holds for it.

**P3-38.** A reader MUST NOT apply its labelling limit to an input for which `guaranteed` holds, MUST report
`labelling_refused` with `refusal_width` for the refusal `width`, with `refusal_size` for the refusal `size`, with
`refusal_weight` for the refusal `weight`, with `refusal_term_node` for the refusal `term_node` and with `refusal_limit` for the
refusal `limit`, and MUST then report the content digest `absent` and no substitute.

**P3-39.** A reader MUST report `labelling_refused` at most once for a fold and with no `position`.

**P3-76.** A reader MUST report, for a fold that meets several refusals, the refusal `size` when it applies, otherwise the
refusal `weight` when it applies, otherwise the refusal `term_node` when it applies, and otherwise the refusal of the smallest
code in `L1` ("OUTCOMES AND REFUSALS"), and no other.

## 7. Typed references: the `bindings` frame

A reference from RDF content to a blob, a frame or a graph state is not a term and is never recognised by a spelling. It is a
BINDING: a typed record that pairs a term of the content with a typed referent.

### 7.1 The frame, the referents and what a reader does with a binding

```text
bindings.content = [ entry_count: uint ]
bindings_payload = list(binding)                       ; entry_count elements
binding          = [ term: leaf, referent: referent ]  ; leaf: 6.3
referent         = [1, name: qualified_name]           ; blob: a blob named by algorithm and digest (P1, P4)
                 / [2, frame: digest]                  ; frame: the effective identifier of a frame of the file
                 / [3, state: digest]                  ; state: the effective identifier of a commit frame (7.2)
```

The term "stands for" the referent: an IRI, a literal, a triple term or a blank node. A consumer's vocabulary can say anything
about that term in ordinary statements; the format reads none of it and defines no vocabulary for references.

A reader judges a `bindings` frame as follows.

- The frame, in the order of 1.4. A referent tag or a leaf tag outside the registry is `malformed_value` with
  `unregistered_value`; a `digest` whose length is not the length of the segment's `hash_algorithm`, or a `qualified_name` digest
  whose length is not that of its algorithm, is `malformed_value` with `wrong_length`; a `qualified_name` whose algorithm is
  outside the registry is `malformed_value` with `unregistered_value`; the first of these in the order of the bytes decides. Then
  `entry_count` is the number of elements of the payload, else `malformed_value` with `inconsistent_counts`. Then a leaf of tag
  `3` that holds no leaf of tag `2` at any depth is `malformed_value` with `forbidden_combination`. A frame so malformed is
  opaque with `malformed` and not folded.
- The term: two conditions are judged in this order, and the first that holds decides. (1) An id of the term leaf, at any depth,
  is not defined at the frame (4.1): the binding is dropped with `forward_reference`. (2) Otherwise an id of the leaf names an
  unreadable term (2.8), or the leaf is an unreadable leaf (6.3, POSITIONS IN A LEAF): the binding is held with `opaque_term`
  (4.3). A binding that holds both kinds of id is dropped. Each
  finding is reported at most once for the frame.
- Repeats: two bindings that are equal after resolution are one binding.
- Naming: the term leaf is resolved by 6.3. A binding whose leaf is UNNAMED (a pair that names no node) is kept in the fold as a
  ground record and is reported `binding_unanchored` at the frame, at most once for the frame. This is the only case of that
  finding: a blank node that occurs in no RDF line is a node of the labelled set and its binding is in the fold with no finding.
- Resolving the referent, against the whole file and not by the position of the binding: a `blob` referent resolves when the blob set of the fold holds a
  declaration of that name (`P4`); a `frame` referent resolves to the first ordinary frame of the file, the segments in file order and the frames of a segment in
  item order, whose effective identifier is that digest; a `state` referent resolves to the state that a `commit` frame whose effective identifier is that digest names
  (7.2; the first such segment in file order when several hold one). A referent that does not resolve is, for the file's fold,
  `missing_dependency` with reason `blob`, `frame`, `state` or `state_unavailable`, once for each distinct referent, at the first
  frame in file order that holds a binding with it. The bindings that count are those of the fold: a binding that is dropped or
  held, and the
  referent inside a `binding` entry of a `suppress` frame, give no such finding. A `state` referent does not resolve for the
  reader when no commit of the file has that effective identifier, and the reason is then `state`; it does not resolve either
  when commits of the file have it and every one of them names no state to that reader (7.2: the state is UNAVAILABLE to it),
  and the reason is then `state_unavailable`. The findings and the fold states of the frames and of the segment say why a
  commit names none. A referent of another
  file is legitimate and is reported the same way: the format resolves only within the file it reads.
- A binding stays in the fold whether or not it resolves. A suppression entry of kind `binding` or `term` hides it from the
  visible dataset (9.3). Its record is in the labelled set whether it is hidden or not (6.2).

**P3-40.** A reader MUST judge a `bindings` frame by the rules of 7.1, MUST keep in the fold every binding that is not dropped or
held, resolved or not, and MUST report `binding_unanchored` only for a binding whose leaf is unnamed.

**P3-78.** A reader MUST resolve a `frame` referent to the first ordinary frame of the file in file order whose effective
identifier is the digest of the referent.

**P3-41.** A reader MUST report `missing_dependency` with the reason of the referent kind (`blob`, `frame` or `state`) once for
each distinct referent that does not resolve in the file, and with the reason `state_unavailable` in place of `state` for a
`state` referent whose identifier a commit of the file has, every such commit naming no state to the reader (7.2).

### 7.2 Graph states

There is no graph-state frame. A GRAPH STATE is the fold of the frames of a RUN of segments up to a commit of the last of them.

- THE RUN of a segment is a list of consecutive segments of the file that ends with that segment. A segment whose opener outcome
  is not `readable` has no run. When the segment is opened by a resync item, the run is that segment alone, whether its lineage
  is checked or not. When its `lineage` is `absent`, the run is that segment alone. When its lineage is CHECKED (`P1`,
  "The header"), the run is the run of the previous segment followed by the segment, and it is undefined when the previous
  segment has no run. When its `lineage` is `present` and not checked, it has no run.
- A `commit` frame NAMES A STATE TO A READER when all four hold. (1) It is a valid commit (`P5`, "The checks of a commit") and
  its own fold state is `not_content`. (2) The run of its segment is defined. (3) Every ordinary frame before it in its run has
  the link state `linked` or `healed` and a fold state `folded` or `not_content` (`P7`, "How each state value is decided").
  (4) No term of the fold of its state is unreadable while the reader's capacity refused the rebuild of some candidate of
  its value (2.6; `P4`, "Rebuilding a blob or a value"). The ordinary frames before it in its run are the ordinary frames of its
  own segment that stand before it, and every ordinary frame of each earlier segment of the run. Apart from a frame of type
  `parity`, a frame with the fold state `folded` or `not_content` has the payload state `verified`, a link state that is not
  `broken` and no `malformed_value` finding, the grammar of its `signatures` field included (`P6`, "The signature record"). The
  link state is named in clause (3) beside the fold state because a frame of type `parity` whose `transparent` field is `false`
  is an ordinary frame and has the fold state `not_content` whatever its link state (`P1`, "The frame envelope", THE EXCEPTION OF PARITY FRAMES).
  Otherwise the commit names NO state to that reader and the state is UNAVAILABLE to it: a commit that fails its checks, is itself
  opaque, or stands in a segment with no run, and a commit after a frame whose link state is `broken` or whose fold state is
  `opaque` for any reason (malformed, damaged, unchecked for capacity or codec, of an unknown type, after a stop, a sealed frame
  not opened). UNAVAILABLE is an outcome of the reading and not a value: no fold, no partial fold and no name stands for it. The
  findings and the fold states of that frame, of the commit and of the segment say why (`chain_break` and `malformed_value`, the
  payload findings of `P1` 5.4, `exceeds_capacity`, `unsupported_critical`, `segment_key_not_held`, `key_conflict`, `seal_failed`,
  `lineage_mismatch`, `lineage_begins`, `commit_count_mismatch`, `commit_root_mismatch`); a commit that names no state gets no
  finding of its own for it.
- A damaged region before the commit (`P1`, "Framing: torn, damaged and undelimitable input") is not an ordinary frame and does
  not stop the commit from naming a state, because a valid commit's `count` and `root` agree with the ordinary frames present
  (`P5`, "The checks of a commit"): the root binds the sequence of the effective identifiers of the ordinary frames before the
  commit, so no ordinary frame of the prefix is missing. A region that took an ordinary frame away makes the commit invalid.
- A frame above the capacity of the reader (`stored_bytes_per_frame`, `decoded_bytes_per_frame`, `decoded_bytes_per_read`, and
  for a `terms` frame `formed_bytes_per_read`, 2.3), a frame whose codec the reader does not hold, a frame of a type or of a registry version that the reader does not hold, a frame
  after a stop, and a sealed frame that the reader does not open are `opaque` (`P7`, "How each state value is decided"; `P8`).
  Each makes every later commit of its run UNAVAILABLE to that reader. So whether a commit names a state depends on the
  capacity, the codecs, the registry version and the sealing input of the reader. The capacity and the registry version stand in
  the typed result (`P7`, "Capacity": the capacity is part of the result); the sealing input holds secrets and is no field of it, and its
  effect stands in the fold states of the sealed frames and in `unopened_sealed`. For one commit of one file, a reader of one
  capacity or one key set can find a state where another finds none. Two readers that each find a state find equal states
  (claim ST3).
- THE STATE that a commit `c` names is the fold of the frames of the segments of the run of its segment: every segment of the run
  before the segment of `c` whole, and the segment of `c` up to and including `c`. The state holds the frames of its run up to
  the commit and none after it: no frame of the segment of `c` that stands after `c`, and no frame of a segment after it, is in
  the state. Each segment keeps the ordinal it has in the
  file read, so a blank node of the state is the node of the same pair in the fold of the file when the holder of the frame is
  the same segment there, and a pair of 6.3 in a frame of the state is resolved among the segments of the run only, the holder
  being the first of them in file order other than the segment that holds the entry. Every prefix of a file that ends at an item
  boundary is a file (`P1`); the state of a run that begins at a segment after the first is the fold of that part of the file, and
  this part computes no digest for it.
- THE UNITS OF A STATE. In a state a `recipe` or `value` declaration is served only by the units of the run that stand before
  the commit `c`: the units of its own segment (`P4`, P4-36) that stand before `c`, which are every unit of a segment of the
  run before the segment of `c` and the units of the segment of `c` at positions below that of `c`. These units are a prefix
  of the unit index of their segment (`P4`, "The unit index and the unit used"), so the unit used for a digest in the state
  is the unit used in the read when that unit stands before `c`, and there is none otherwise. The outcome of a declaration in
  the state therefore follows from the unit index without producing a byte: it is `incomplete` with the reason `chunk` when
  one of its entries, taken in entry order up to the entry at which its rebuild in the read stops (every entry, for a
  declaration that is verified or refused), has a unit used that stands after `c` or has none, and otherwise it is its
  rebuild state in the read (`P4`, "The total rebuilt length"). A state never depends on a frame after its commit.
- A state is LOCAL to its segment when the run is that segment alone, which is the case whenever its `lineage` is `absent`. It
  extends over several segments only through a checked lineage.
- THE NAME of a state is the effective identifier (`P1`) of its commit, and a `state` referent is that identifier. The name
  names a VALUE, not an occurrence and not a view: it says what the state holds, not where in the file the commit stands and not
  which reader reads it. To every reader to which it names a state it names the one value that ST3 states; to another reader it
  names none.
- A `state` referent RESOLVES, for a reader, to the state that a commit names to it in the first segment in file order, among
  the segments that hold a commit of that effective identifier which names a state to it. Its blank nodes carry the ordinals of
  that segment and of the earlier segments of its run. When no segment holds such a commit, the referent is unresolved for that
  reader, and the reader reports `missing_dependency` (7.1) with the reason `state` when no commit of the file has that
  identifier, and with the reason `state_unavailable` when every commit that has it names no state to the reader.

A range proof over the frames of a state is the proof of `P5`. A profile that needs exports names each export by the commit that
closes it.

CLAIM ST1 (the name binds its state). Under the assumptions (A1) and (A2) of `P1` "What the identifier commits to", let two
commits have one effective identifier, each naming a state to a reader, the two readers being one or two and of any capacity,
codecs and sealing input. Then the opener identifiers (`h0`) of their two segments are equal; the ordinary frames that precede
each commit in its segment are equal, in order, in `frame_type`, `critical`, `transparent`, `content`, `decoded_length` and
`payload_digest`, and, for each frame whose fold state is `folded`, in the decoded payload bytes, a sealed frame that a reader
opened being equal in these fields of its inner frame; when the openers of the segments are headers whose lineage is checked,
the head of the previous segment is equal in both and the same holds for the previous segment, back to the first segment of the
run; and the two runs have the same number of segments. Proof. Let `c` name a state to its reader. By clause (1) of 7.2 the link
state of `c` is `linked` or `healed`, and by clause (3) so is the link state of every ordinary frame before `c` in its run; so
the ordinary frames of the segment of `c` up to `c` are one unbroken run that begins at the first ordinary frame of the segment,
and Claim 2 of `P1` applies to `c`: equal identifiers of the two commits give equal identity records (Claim 1), hence equal
fields of the commits and of every ordinary frame before them, in order, and equal `h0`; `h0` fixes the opener bytes, so equal
`lineage`. A frame whose fold state is `folded` is not opaque, so its payload state is `verified` (`P7`, "How each state value
is decided", gives `damaged` and `unchecked` an opaque reason): its decoded payload hashes to its `payload_digest`, and equal
digests give equal bytes under (A1). A sealed frame that a reader opened has the fold state of its inner frame; its stored
payload is equal in both files (equal `payload_digest`), and a sealed frame opens, up to a collision of SHA-256, to one
plaintext (`P8`, "Commitment"), so the inner frames are equal in type, content, decoded length, payload
digest and decoded payload. When `lineage` is `present` and checked, `previous_head` is equal; it is the head of the previous
segment, the effective identifier of its last ordinary frame or, when it has none, its opener identifier. In the first case that
frame is in the run, so by clause (3) the frames before it in its segment are an unbroken run from the first frame, Claim 2
applies to it and gives all frames before it in that segment and its `h0`; in the second case the opener identifier fixes the
opener of that segment. Both segments are equal in the opener, so both openers are headers with `lineage` absent, or both are
headers with a checked lineage, or both are resync items (the preimages of a header and of a resync item are distinct). In the
second case the run of each is the run of the previous segment followed by the segment, and the argument repeats on the previous
segment. A `lineage` `absent` ends it, and so does an opener that is a resync item, whose run is its segment alone whether or
not its lineage is checked (THE RUN): its `previous_head` is equal in both, as part of the equal opener bytes, and no frame
before it is in the run, so nothing about those frames is claimed. By induction on the number of segments of the run the claim
holds. The `storage`, the stored bytes and the `signatures` of a frame are in no identifier and are not compared; they reach the
fold only through its fold state (the grammar of `storage` and of `signatures`, the capacity, the codec), which clause (3)
requires to be `folded` or `not_content` in both runs. End of proof.

CLAIM ST2 (a name names a value, in one file). In one file and for one reader, let two segments each hold a commit that names a
state to that reader, the two commits having one effective identifier. Then the two states are equal as values: their folds are
isomorphic labelled sets and have equal content digests. Proof. By ST1 the opener identifiers are equal, so the openers are of
one kind (the preimages of a header and of a resync item are distinct) and their bytes are equal, `lineage` included. (a) Both
openers are resync items: the run of each is its segment alone, so each state is the fold of its segment up to the commit. (b)
Both are headers with `lineage` `absent`: the same. (c) Both are headers with `lineage` `present`: a segment whose lineage is
present and not checked has no run, so its commit names no state, which contradicts the hypothesis; so both lineages are
checked, each run is the run of the previous segment followed by the segment, and by ST1 the head of the previous segment is
equal in both and so, back to the first segment of the run, are the segments, which also carry equal lineages (the `ordinal` is in
the equal bytes); a run never passes a segment whose opener is not readable, since the lineage after such a segment is not
checked (`P1`, "The header", LINEAGE), so every step back compares readable openers and no step uses a position; the two runs
are consecutive stretches of equal length whose segments are equal frame by frame. In every case each state is the fold of the
frames of a run up to the commit, and by ST1 these frames are equal in the two runs in every field that the fold reads
(`frame_type`, `critical`, `transparent`, `content`, `decoded_length` and decoded payload bytes, for a sealed frame those of its
inner frame, and nothing from a frame that is not folded); the grammar of `storage` and of `signatures` leaves no violating
frame in either run, by clause (3) of 7.2. The reader's inputs act on equal frames alike, being one reader, and the rebuild of a
value is a function of the units of the run and of the capacity, with no term unreadable for the capacity, by clause (4). The
commits are equal in `count` and `root`. A pair of 6.3 in a frame of a run is resolved among the segments of that run only, and
the runs are equal, so the pair names the same node in both, up to the renaming of ordinals. The two folds differ only in the
ordinals of the segments in the file read, which rename the blank nodes by one consistent bijection (the stretches are
consecutive and of equal length, the `i`-th segment of one for the `i`-th of the other); by `D2` (8.2) the labelled sets are
isomorphic and the content digests are equal. End of proof. Consequently one name for two segments is correct, and the
resolution rule of this section is a choice among equal values; it fixes only which ordinals the blank nodes carry, and takes
the first segment in file order.

CLAIM ST3 (a name names one value for every reader). Let one commit of one file, or two commits with one effective identifier
in one file or in two, name a state to a reader `R1` and to a reader `R2`, whatever their capacities, codecs, registry versions
and sealing inputs. Then the two states are equal as values (ST2 states what that means), up to the ordinals of the blank nodes
when the two segments stand at other places. Proof. By ST1 the frames before the two commits are equal in every field that the
fold reads. A reader's input reaches the fold of a state only by one of these ways, and each way is closed for the two readers.
(a) The capacity (`stored_bytes_per_frame`, `decoded_bytes_per_frame`, `decoded_bytes_per_read`, `formed_bytes_per_read`), the
codecs and the registry version act through the payload state, the refusal of a `terms` frame by the total formed length, the
frame type and the stop of a fold, that is through the fold state of a frame; by
clause (3) every frame of both runs is `folded` or `not_content` for both readers. `items_per_read` ends a read: the frames
before the commit are read in full in both. `labelling_units`, `labelled_lines` and `labelled_bytes_per_read` bound a labelling, and a state is
not labelled. (b) The sealing input acts through whether a sealed frame is opened; by clause (3) every sealed frame of both runs is
opened by both readers, and the inner frames are equal (ST1). (c) The capacity of a rebuild acts through whether a term that names
a value is readable. By clause (4) a term is unreadable in neither fold for the capacity, so it is unreadable only for a reason
of the bytes (2.8, items 1 to 4, and a value that no candidate provides or that no candidate rebuilds); those are functions of the
frames and are the same in both. When a term is readable, its octets are those of the first candidate that rebuilds, every
candidate has the digest and the length of the term, and a candidate that rebuilds has the bytes that the digest names (`P4`,
property 3), so the two readers find the same octets even when they find them at different candidates. (d) The trusted keys, the
claims, the profiles and the encoder are not read by the fold: no signature is consulted before the fold is formed (`P6`,
"Verification: the procedure and its typed outcomes"; `P7`, step `R20`). So the two folds are equal. End of proof.

WHAT THE NAME DOES NOT BIND: a transparent frame, the storage form, the stored bytes, the signatures, and every frame after the
commit. A `signatures` field outside its grammar is not ignored: it makes its frame opaque, and the state is then unavailable
(clause (3) of 7.2). A signature whose value has the wrong length, does not verify or names a key that is not trusted is a
signature outcome (`P6`), reaches no fold state and changes no state. Two files in which one segment is the same (equal header
bytes, equal frames up to the commit, no checked lineage) give one name to one state, whatever stands before the segment in either
file; that is the meaning of a local state.

**P3-42.** A reader MUST resolve a `state` referent only to the state that a `commit` frame of that effective identifier names to
it (7.2), and MUST define the state as the fold of the frames of the run of the segment of that commit, up to the commit.

**P3-62.** A reader MUST determine the run of a segment as 7.2 says, and MUST regard a `commit` frame as naming a state to it
only when the four clauses of 7.2 hold: the commit is valid and has the fold state `not_content`; the run of its segment is
defined; every ordinary frame before it in its run has the link state `linked` or `healed` and the fold state `folded` or
`not_content`; and no term of the fold of its state is unreadable for the reader's capacity.

**P3-63.** A reader MUST define the state that a commit names as the fold of the frames of the segments of its run up to the
commit, with each segment keeping its ordinal in the file read and a pair of 6.3 resolved among the segments of the run only.

**P3-65.** A reader MUST resolve a `state` referent to the state that a commit names to it in the first segment in file order
among the segments that hold a commit of the referent's effective identifier that names a state to it, and, when there is
none, MUST report `missing_dependency` with `state` if no commit of the file has that identifier and with `state_unavailable`
if a commit of the file has it.

**P3-66.** A reader MUST NOT give a segment a run when its `lineage` is `present` and not checked, unless a resync item opens the
segment, whose run is then that segment alone (7.2, THE RUN), and MUST NOT let a state
include a segment before the first segment of the run.

**P3-90.** A reader MUST give a commit that names no state to it the outcome UNAVAILABLE, a typed outcome of the reading: it MUST
NOT fold, offer or name a state from the frames that it folded while another frame of the run is opaque, MUST NOT give a state
that another reader found in its place, and MUST report a `state` referent to the identifier of such a commit as
`missing_dependency` with `state_unavailable` unless another commit of that identifier names a state to it.

**P3-91.** A reader MUST NOT regard a commit as naming a state when a term of the fold of its state is unreadable and the
reader's capacity refused the rebuild of some candidate of the value of that term, and MUST take the outcome of a declaration
in a state only from the units of the run that stand before the commit (7.2, THE UNITS OF A STATE).

## 8. The content digest

### 8.1 Definition

THE CONTENT DIGEST of a fold IS the digest of the canonical line sequence of its labelled set (6.1) that `L1` defines (`L1`
paragraph "THE CANONICAL LINE SEQUENCE AND ITS DIGEST"), computed with `L1`'s own context. The format writes nothing around it:
no record of components, no digest of bindings, suppression or blobs, no preimage and no position. The digest of the labelling is
already bound to the identifier of the labelling, `L1` version `1` (`labelling` of `P2`), and the format adds nothing to it.

- It covers every RDF line of the fold, hidden or not, graph names, empty graphs and annotation counts included, and one record
  for each binding, each suppression entry and each blob declaration, hidden or not, for each pair
  of VALUES and for each distinct nested term (6.2a), every term in its lowered form.
- A fold with no blank node goes through `L1` as well; a fold with no line and no record has the digest of the empty sequence that
  `L1` defines.
- The same rule gives the digest of a segment's own fold (5.1): it is `absent` exactly when that fold has none, because the
  labelling refused or a row, binding or entry is held in it, and `P5` and `P7` use it for the index and the segment result.
- The content digest is `absent` in exactly two cases: the labelling of the fold is refused (6.5: by size, by weight, by a
  collision of the digests of nested terms or by `L1`),
  and the fold has a HELD row, binding
  or entry (4.3). A reader never substitutes another digest. A fold that has a held item is labelled all the same, on the
  labelled set of what it holds: its final positions order the delivery (5.1), and a refusal of that labelling is reported
  with its finding. The digest is `absent` in both cases, and a held item alone adds no finding beyond its `opaque_term`.

**P3-43.** A writer and a reader MUST compute the content digest as the digest of the canonical line sequence of the labelled set
of 6.1 under `L1`'s own context, and MUST NOT combine it with any other value.

**P3-44.** A reader MUST report the content digest `absent` when the labelling of the fold is refused (6.5) or the fold has a held
row, binding
or entry, and MUST NOT report any other digest in its place.

### 8.2 What the digest is a function of

CLAIM D1 (a function of the fold). The content digest is a function of the fold: of its set of RDF lines (annotation
counts included), its bindings, its suppression record, its blob set and its VALUES, with each
blank node identified by its segment
and label (1.1). It does not depend on frame boundaries, ids, storage, signatures or parity, nor on the order of frames or rows beyond what 5.3
states (the validity of 4.2, and which value or unit stands before what names it); the blob set does not depend on the order of
declarations. It depends on the order of segments through three of the five things
that 5.3 names: the ordinal of each segment, which names its blank nodes and is a renaming (D2), the holder of a frame (6.3),
which decides the node a pair names, and the values that serve a term (2.6), which decide whether a term is readable. The lineage of a segment is no part of the fold. Where a pair
names a frame that two segments hold, the pair names the node of the first of them, and the same node of the other segment is
its isomorphic counterpart.

Proof. The labelled set is built from those and from nothing else (6.1, 6.2, 6.2a, 6.3). The canonical line sequence is a
function of the set of typed lines (`L1`, section "Scope and result", item "ROUTE INDEPENDENCE"), and its digest is
the digest of that sequence. End of proof.

CLAIM D2 (invariance under renaming, under the hash assumption of `L1`). Let two folds have labelled sets that are isomorphic:
some bijection of their blank nodes maps the lines of one onto the lines of the other, every constant and every annotation count
being kept, and every record onto a record of equal payload and of equal term list under the bijection. Then, unless the hash
of `L1` collides on the certificate encodings of two non-isomorphic top components of one of the two sets, their content digests
are equal. What holds with no assumption is narrower: the certificate of each top component is invariant under the bijection
(`L1`, section "Scope and result"); the order of two top components whose identities collide is decided by source identifiers,
so the whole sequence is invariant only where no such collision occurs. In particular, under that hypothesis, renaming the labels or the segment ordinals of blank nodes, all consistently, and the source
identifiers with them, changes no content digest, and this holds whether or not a binding or a suppression entry names
a blank node that has automorphic twins.

Proof. The payload of a record holds no label, no ordinal and no position: it holds the kinds, the referents, the names of blobs
and the written pairs of unnamed leaves, which are constants; every node a record names is a member of its `terms`, where the
bijection acts. The renaming, with each term node mapped to the term node of the renamed nested term (claim L of 6.2a), therefore
gives an isomorphism of the labelled sets. By `L1`, section "Scope and result", item 3, two
inputs have equal canonical line sequences exactly when they are isomorphic, so their digests are equal. End of proof.

The claim rests on the invariance of the labelling, which `L1` proves for the certificates without assumption and for the whole
sequence under the collision resistance of its hash, and on nothing else. A binding or an entry that names one of several twin
nodes puts that node in a record line, which distinguishes it from its twins in the incidence structure (6.2), so the position
that the node receives is fixed by the structure and not by a source label.
What the source identifiers still decide is which twin of an unbound orbit takes which position, and
no digest or stored value depends on it.

CLAIM D3 (what an equal digest says). Under the assumption of `L1` (paragraph "THE HASH, THE IDENTIFIER AND THE SECURITY
ASSUMPTION"), equal content digests of two folds mean that their labelled sets are isomorphic. Proof. This is the second half of
item 3 of `L1`, section "Scope and result".
End of proof.

**P3-45.** Two folds have equal content if and only if their labelled sets are isomorphic, and a reader MUST decide the content of
two folds as equal exactly when their content digests are equal.

## 9. Suppression hides

Suppression HIDES content from the visible dataset and removes nothing from the fold. The labelled set covers hidden content as it
covers the rest. Suppression is MONOTONE under append: the suppression record is the SET of the entries of all
folded `suppress` frames of the file, by value; an entry has the same effect whether it stands before or after what it names, in
its own segment or in another; a later identical assertion does not revive hidden content. The order of frames and of entries
never enters but through what stands before what in the rules of values and units: a value serves only the terms of the `terms`
frames after it (2.6) and a unit only the declarations in its frame or after it (`P4`, "The unit index and the unit used"),
which decide whether a term is readable and so whether an entry or a line that names it is held. The order of segments enters in
two places: the holder of a frame, which is the first segment in file order that holds it, decides the node that a pair names
(6.3, 5.3), and the values that serve a term (2.6) decide whether a term is readable. Removal
and change of content are not suppression: they are RDF content (5.1).

An ENTRY is exactly three things: (a) a wire record (9.1), (b) a record line of the labelled set (6.2), and (c) a hiding rule
(9.2). It is nothing else: it has no note, no author, no frame target and no position.

### 9.1 The wire record

```text
suppress.content   = [ entry_count: uint ]
suppress_payload   = list(suppression_entry)            ; entry_count elements
suppression_entry  = [1, term: leaf]                                             ; kind term
                   / [2, quad: [s: leaf, p: leaf, o: leaf, g: option(leaf)]]     ; kind quad: one quad line
                   / [3, reifier: leaf]                                          ; kind reifier
                   / [4, graph: leaf]                                            ; kind graph
                   / [5, blob: qualified_name]                                   ; kind blob
                   / [6, reifier_binding: [r: leaf, t: leaf, g: option(leaf)]]   ; kind reifier_binding
                   / [7, annotation: [r: leaf, p: leaf, o: leaf, g: option(leaf)]] ; kind annotation
                   / [8, binding: [term: leaf, referent: referent]]              ; kind binding
```

The leaf is that of 6.3. The tags of `suppression_entry` are the `suppression_kind` values and the tags of `leaf` are the
`leaf_kind` values of `P2`, in the order of the tables. A free-text reason, a note and an author are not part of an entry: who
suppressed what is the segment that holds the entry, and a human reason is RDF content if anyone wants it.

**P3-46.** A reader MUST judge a `suppress` frame so, in the order of 1.4: an entry or a leaf tag outside the registry is
`malformed_value` with `unregistered_value`, and a `digest` whose length is not that of the segment's `hash_algorithm` is
`malformed_value` with `wrong_length`, the first of them in the order of the bytes; then `entry_count` has to equal the number of
entries, else `malformed_value` with `inconsistent_counts`; then a leaf of tag `3` that holds no leaf of tag `2` at any depth is
`malformed_value` with `forbidden_combination`. A frame so malformed is opaque with `malformed` and is not folded.

**P3-47.** A reader MUST drop an entry that holds a term id not defined at its frame, with `forward_reference`, and MUST hold an
entry that holds a term id of an unreadable term (2.8) or an unreadable leaf (6.3), with `opaque_term` (4.3), each reported at
most once for the frame; the
first condition is judged before the second, so that an entry that holds both is dropped.

**P3-73.** A reader MUST judge a binding or an entry by the two conditions of 7.1 in the order written there, the undefined id
before the unreadable term or the unreadable leaf, and MUST give it the one finding of the first that holds.

### 9.2 The hiding rule

An entry RESOLVES (6.3) to one resolved entry, a suppression kind with term values, blob names and referents.
A term OCCURS in a line when it equals the value of a slot of the line, the graph slot included, or equals a component, at
any depth, of a triple value that is a slot; the datatype and the language of a literal are fields of the literal and are not
occurrences. The rule compares typed values and nothing else: no position, no frame, no order.

- `term` `T`; hides: every RDF line in which `T` occurs; every binding whose term value is `T` or holds `T` at any depth; and the
  `empty_graph` line whose graph is `T`
- `quad` `(s, p, o, g)`; hides: the `quad` line with those fields (`g` absent: the default graph)
- `reifier` `R`; hides: every `reifier_binding` line and every `annotation` line whose reifier is `R`, in every graph
- `graph` `G`; hides: every RDF line, of every kind, whose graph slot holds `G`, and the `empty_graph` line of that name; nothing
  that merely mentions `G`
- `blob` `N`; hides: every declaration of the blob named `N` (`P4`)
- `reifier_binding` `(r, t, g)`; hides: the `reifier_binding` line with those fields
- `annotation` `(r, p, o, g)`; hides: the `annotation` line with those fields, whatever its count
- `binding` `(t, referent)`; hides: the binding of that term value and that referent

An entry hides what its resolved entry hides. A line, a binding or a declaration is HIDDEN when some entry of the record
hides it. An entry is UNMATCHED when it hides nothing in the fold: no such term, line, blob, binding, segment or label, and
every entry with an unnamed leaf (6.3), which matches nothing. An unmatched entry is reported `suppression_unmatched` with its
`suppression_kind` as reason, at the frame that holds it, at most once for the frame and kind; it stays in the record and has its
record line. A record line hides nothing and is hidden by nothing: records are not RDF lines. No entry hides a pair of
VALUES: a `term` entry that names a literal hides the lines in which it occurs and leaves its pair and its record.

**P3-48.** A reader MUST hide a line, a binding or a declaration exactly when an entry of the record hides it by the rule of 9.2,
and MUST report `suppression_unmatched` with the kind of the entry as reason for each kind of unmatched entry of a folded frame.

**P3-49.** A reader MUST NOT let the order of frames or entries, or the segment that holds an entry, change what the entry
hides, given which terms are readable (2.6), and MUST let the order of segments decide what an entry hides through the holder of
a frame (6.3) and the values that serve a term (2.6), and through nothing else.

### 9.3 The record

The SUPPRESSION RECORD of the fold is the set of the entries of all folded `suppress` frames, listed in the order of delivery (5.1), by value after resolution (6.3): two
entries that resolve to the same values are one entry, whether one named a node by a term id and the other by a pair. An entry
that is dropped, held or in a frame that is not folded is not in it.

### 9.4 The visible dataset and visibility

THE VISIBLE DATASET is a function of the fold: its RDF lines, bindings and blob declarations that no entry of the record hides.
VALUES is a part of the fold and not of the visible dataset: a profile that asks for the typed value of a literal of the
visible dataset asks VALUES of the fold.
Its named graphs are the graphs that a visible line carries and the graphs of visible `empty_graph` lines; a graph whose lines
are all hidden is not a named graph of the visible dataset, so hiding never creates an empty graph. A blob declaration that is
hidden stays in the blob set, and an extraction of the visible dataset does not deliver the bytes of that blob. The reading result
(`P7`) gives the fold, the suppression record and the visibility state below; `visible` is defined here and nowhere else. There
is no mode, no `all` view to select and no second content digest.

NOT FOLDED, AND NOT AUTHENTICATED. A `suppress` frame that is not folded (damage, an unknown codec, capacity, a fold that
stopped before it) hides nothing. A reader knows a `suppress` frame by its clear type, `frame_type` `suppress` in the envelope.
A folded `suppress` frame that is not an AUTHENTICATED frame, as `P6` defines authentication in its section "Authentication",
hides content all the same, so that unauthenticated content can hide authenticated content: the reader says so in the visibility
state. The visibility state is a variant:

```text
visibility = [1, unauthenticated_suppress: uint]                                          ; complete
           / [2, unfolded_suppress: uint, unopened_sealed: uint, unauthenticated_suppress: uint]   ; incomplete
```

It is `complete` when every `suppress` frame of the file is folded and no sealed frame is unopened, and otherwise `incomplete`
with `unfolded_suppress` the number of items of the file that pass the envelope test, have `frame_type` `suppress` and are
not folded, and `unopened_sealed` the number of sealed frames not opened (`P8`; `0` in registry version `1`). In both tags
`unauthenticated_suppress` is the number of folded `suppress` frames that are not authenticated frames, zero when every one is.
It counts every folded `suppress` frame, clear or opened from a sealed frame: a `suppress` frame opened from a sealed frame is a
folded frame like any other, and is authenticated exactly when the sealed frame that carries it is an authenticated frame. The
claim fails closed, the data does not. Hiding is content and is authenticated only like any content.

**P3-50.** A reader MUST give the visibility state `complete` exactly when every `suppress` frame of the file is folded and
no sealed frame is unopened, and otherwise `incomplete` with the counts of 9.4, and MUST state in both the number of folded
`suppress` frames that are not authenticated frames.

**P3-51.** A reader MUST NOT hide anything by an entry of a `suppress` frame that is not folded.

CLAIM S1 (the record line is a function of the fold). The records and the visible dataset are functions of the fold: the wire
entries, the dictionaries, the frame identifiers and the order of the segments of the file determine the resolutions (6.3), and
the hiding rule compares values. Two folds with equal entries, lines and dictionaries, and equal holders for the frames their
pairs name, give equal records.

CLAIM S2 (monotone under append; at most one resolution). An APPEND of whole items to a file `F` gives a file `F'` whose bytes
begin with the bytes of `F`, `F` ending at an item boundary (`P1`): the added items are frames of the last segment of `F`, or
whole segments after it. Then, for every leaf of tag `2` of an entry of `F`:

- (a) the leaf has at most one resolution in `F` and in `F'`;
- (b) when it resolves in `F`, it resolves in `F'` to the same node;
- (c) when it is unnamed in `F`, it is unnamed or named in `F'`;
- (d) a line, binding or declaration hidden in `F` is hidden in `F'`, or is an `empty_graph` line that is no longer a line of the
  fold because a line of `F'` carries its name (5.1).

Proof. (a) The holder of a frame is one segment (6.3) and two blank terms of one segment with equal labels are one node (2.4).
(b) By `P1`, "Healing", the link state of a frame depends on the frame, on its predecessor and, for `healed`, on the frame after
it. So the link state and the effective identifier of every ordinary frame of `F` are the same in `F'`, with one exception: the
last ordinary frame of the last segment of `F`, when it is the last item of `F` and `broken`, can become `healed` when the first
item appended is a frame that explains it (`P1`, "Healing": the witness is the item directly after the frame), and its
effective identifier is then the computed one. A `broken` frame is held by no segment, so no segment stops holding a frame. The
segments of `F` are the first segments of `F'`, in the same order and with the same ordinals. If the holder `h` of a frame in `F`
exists, a segment of `F'` before `h` that holds the frame in `F'` and did not in `F` would have to be a segment of `F` that gained
a frame, and only the last segment of `F` can, which is not before `h`; the segments added follow every segment of `F`. So `h` is
the holder in `F'`. The dictionary of `h` in `F'` holds every term of its dictionary in `F`: a frame that is folded stays folded,
since what stops a fold stands before the frames it stops, and frames are added after those that stand. So the label is found and
the node `blank(ordinal of h, label)` is the same. (c) A leaf unnamed in `F` has no holder or a holder without the label; in
`F'` a holder can appear (a segment added, or a last frame healed) or the label can appear in the dictionary of the holder (a
`terms` frame added to the last segment). This is the only way a leaf changes. (d) By 9.2 an entry hides by comparing values and
nothing else. An entry whose leaves are all named in `F` has the same resolved entry in `F'` by (b), and every line, binding and
declaration of `F` is an item of `F'`, except an `empty_graph` line removed by 5.1; an entry with an unnamed leaf in `F` hides
nothing in `F`, so nothing hidden is lost when it becomes named. End of proof.

WHAT AN APPEND CAN STILL CHANGE. (1) A leaf unnamed in `F` becomes named in `F'`: the entry that held it then hides what its
resolved entry hides, its record line changes from a ground record to a record with terms, and so the labelled set and the
content digest change. This is the only change of a resolution, and it is the case that CLAIM S3 describes. (2) The content of the
added frames, and what the entries of `F` hide in it. (3) The removal of an `empty_graph` line (d). An append never removes a
resolution, never moves a pair to another node, and never makes a pair resolve twice. WHAT IS NOT PROMISED. Concatenation of
two files `F` and `G`: an entry of `G` is resolved with `F` before it, so a frame that `F` holds takes the pair to `F`. Prepending
a segment, removing one, and changing the order of two segments that both hold a named frame. Compaction is covered by 11.4: it
writes one segment, in which no pair names any node.

CLAIM S3 (what an unmatched entry says). An entry that is unmatched in a fold can match in a larger fold: its unnamed leaf can
become named when a segment that holds its frame is appended, or when a `terms` frame that defines its label is appended to the
holder, and its record line then changes; the finding is information about this fold.

## 10. Metadata is RDF

There is no `meta` frame, no header metadata and no public envelope. File-level and statement-level metadata are RDF content:
rows, annotations, a named graph if a consumer wants it kept apart. Blob metadata is the `media_type` field of the declaring
frames (`P4`), which the blob-set entry carries as its `media_types` (6.2); any other property of a blob is RDF about a term bound to it (7.1). A profile that needs metadata defines an RDF
vocabulary in its own specification. No rule of this part reads a vocabulary.

**P3-52.** A reader MUST NOT give a term, a graph, a predicate or a literal any meaning for the fold, the content digest or the
visible dataset because of its spelling or its vocabulary.

## 10a. The RDF projection of a fold

The model of 1.1 is broader than RDF: an `iri`, a lexical form and a language are texts compared whole and bytewise, and the fold
holds things that a set of RDF statements does not (VALUES, bindings, suppression, blob declarations, annotation counts). RDF 1.2
Concepts requires an IRI to be absolute, a language tag to be a well-formed tag and treats two language tags that differ only in
the case of a letter as equal. This section defines the one function from a fold to RDF terms. It judges no text.

### 10a.1 The function

THE RDF PROJECTION is a total function `rdf(F, v)` from a fold `F` and a variant `v` to a pair `(Q, G0)`:

- `Q` is a set of QUADS `(s, p, o, g)`, each of the four a term of 1.1 (`iri`, `literal`, `lang_literal`, `blank`, `triple`),
  `g` being `absent` for the default graph;
- `G0` is a set of GRAPH NAMES, each an `iri` or a `blank` term: the named graphs that hold no statement.

The variant `visible` takes the visible dataset of `F` (9.4). The variant `whole` takes every line of `F`, hidden or not (5.1 item
1). The two variants differ in that one set of lines and in nothing else. In both, the lines are mapped one by one:

| line | gives |
|---|---|
| `quad(s, p, o, g)` | the quad `(s, p, o, g)` |
| `reifier_binding(r, t, g)` | the quad `(r, rdf:reifies, t, g)`, with `rdf:reifies` the `iri` text `http://www.w3.org/1999/02/22-rdf-syntax-ns#reifies` |
| `annotation(n, r, p, o, g)` | the quad `(r, p, o, g)`; the count `n` is not carried |
| `empty_graph(g)` | the name `g` in `G0` |

The text `http://www.w3.org/1999/02/22-rdf-syntax-ns#reifies` is a constant of this part: it is written, never parsed, and never
compared with a text of a file except as one whole term with another. The terms are carried as they are: a typed literal in every
storage form is the one `literal(lexical, datatype)` of 1.1 and 1.5; a `lang_literal` keeps its language text, byte for byte, and
its direction; a `triple` keeps its three components; a `blank(ordinal, label)` is one blank node of the result, two blank terms
being one node exactly when they are equal in the fold, and the result is defined up to a renaming of its blank nodes, as `M(SF)`
is in `P9`. The position rules of 1.2 are the positions that RDF 1.2 allows for a subject, a predicate, a graph name, a reifier and
the parts of a triple term, so no line is dropped for its position. A blank node that occurs only in a binding, a suppression
entry or a pair of VALUES is in no quad and is not in the result.

`Q` is a set: two lines that give one quad give it once. `Q` has no order. A tool that lists it lists the lines it came from in
the order of 5.1.

### 10a.2 What each construct gives and what it loses

| construct | the projection gives | the projection loses |
|---|---|---|
| language texts | each `lang_literal` keeps its language text byte for byte; `"x"@EN` and `"x"@en` are two terms of `Q` | RDF compares the two tags without case and reads them as one literal; `Q` can hold two quads that an RDF tool reads as one, and nothing in `Q` marks them. The projection makes no two terms equal |
| a typed value and the text literal of the same lexical form | one literal (lexical form, datatype): the literal of a typed value is generated from it (1.5) and is equal to the literal stored as text | the pair of VALUES. Two folds that differ only by that pair have different content digests and one projection |
| VALUES | nothing | every pair |
| `reifier_binding` | the quad `(r, rdf:reifies, t, g)`; `t` stays a triple term and no quad is added for the quoted triple (quotation is not assertion) | the line kind: an ordinary `quad` line with the same four terms gives the same quad |
| `annotation` | the quad `(r, p, o, g)`, in the graph its slot names | the count: counts `1` and `3` give one quad; an annotation line and a `quad` line with the same four terms give one quad. No reifier is invented to carry a count |
| `empty_graph` | the name in `G0` | nothing, while `G0` is kept; an interchange form of quads alone (N-Quads) cannot write `G0`, and whoever writes `Q` alone drops the empty named graphs |
| bindings | nothing | every binding, whatever its referent; a term that is bound stays a term of `Q` if a line uses it, and its referent is not RDF |
| suppression record | nothing | every entry |
| hidden content | `visible`: the hidden lines are absent; `whole`: they are present as ordinary quads | `visible` does not say what was hidden; `whole` does not say that a quad is hidden. A graph whose lines are all hidden is no named graph of `visible` (9.4) and gives no name in `G0` |
| blob declarations and the blob set | nothing | every declaration and every byte |
| texts that RDF does not admit | carried as they are (10a.3) | nothing |
| blank nodes | nodes, up to renaming | the label text, the segment ordinal, and the nodes that occur in no line |

### 10a.3 Texts that RDF does not admit

The projection judges no syntax. It does not test that an `iri` is absolute or well formed, that a language text is a language tag
of any standard, that a lexical form suits its datatype, or that a datatype text is not `rdf:langString`; it resolves, normalises,
lower-cases and escapes nothing. A text that RDF 1.2 does not admit therefore gives a quad that is not RDF: a relative or
malformed IRI, a language that is not a tag, a typed literal whose datatype text equals `rdf:langString`, a lexical form outside
the lexical space. The result is then NOT AN RDF DATASET, and the RDF tool that the consumer uses to write or read it judges it.
This part requires no such judgment, and a tool that writes `Q` in a syntax also owns the escaping that the syntax asks. The
projection never drops a quad, a name or a term because of its text.

### 10a.4 The content digest is not an RDF digest

The content digest of a fold (section 8) is the digest of the canonical line sequence of its labelled set. It is not a digest of
RDF dataset equivalence and is not computed by an RDF canonicalization. It is finer than that equivalence in the ways 10a.2 lists
(the pair of VALUES, the kind of a line, the count, a binding, an entry, a hidden line, a blob declaration, the case of a
language text), and it is coarser in none: equal content digests mean isomorphic labelled sets (D3), hence isomorphic lines, hence
isomorphic `whole` projections. Isomorphic projections, or two datasets that an RDF tool reads as equal, do not give equal content
digests.

### 10a.5 Import and the round trip

IMPORT takes a dataset `D = (Q, G0)` written as terms of 1.1 (`G0` holding no name that a quad of `Q` carries) and gives the fold
with one `quad` line for each quad of `Q`, one `empty_graph` line for each name of `G0`, and no `reifier_binding` line, no
`annotation` line, no binding, no suppression entry, no blob declaration and no pair of VALUES. Its blank nodes are the blank nodes
of `D`, in one segment.

CLAIM R1 (what import then projection preserves). For every such `D`, `rdf(IMPORT(D), visible) = rdf(IMPORT(D), whole) = D`, up to
a renaming of blank nodes, as sets of terms. Proof. Every line of `IMPORT(D)` is a `quad` or an `empty_graph` line, nothing is
hidden, and the projection of such a line is the quad or the name it came from (10a.1). End of proof. The claim holds for the
terms as terms, whatever their texts (10a.3).

CLAIM R2 (projection then import then projection). For every fold `F` and variant `v`, `rdf(IMPORT(rdf(F, v)), v) = rdf(F, v)`.
Proof. R1 applied to `D = rdf(F, v)`. End of proof.

WHAT IS NOT PRESERVED. `IMPORT(rdf(F, v))` equals `F` only when `F` holds `quad` and `empty_graph` lines alone, no binding, no
entry, no blob declaration, no pair of VALUES, and, for `v` `visible`, no hidden line. Otherwise it differs from `F` by exactly
the losses of 10a.2: a `reifier_binding` line returns as a `quad` line with the predicate `rdf:reifies`, an `annotation` line as
a `quad` line with count `1` implied, and the bindings, entries, declarations and pairs of VALUES are gone. The content digest of
`IMPORT(rdf(F, v))` then differs from that of `F`. Two datasets that RDF reads as equal (a language tag in two cases) import to
folds with different content digests.

**P3-93.** A tool that offers the RDF projection MUST compute `rdf(F, v)` as 10a.1 states, line by line, for the variant it names,
and MUST name the variant.

**P3-94.** The projection MUST carry every text byte for byte and MUST NOT parse, resolve, normalise, case-fold, escape or test
any text, and MUST NOT drop a quad, a name or a term because of its text.

**P3-95.** The variant `visible` MUST omit exactly the lines that the record hides (9.2) and the variant `whole` MUST omit none.

**P3-96.** The projection MUST give the set `G0` beside `Q`, MUST NOT write an empty named graph into `Q`, and MUST leave a name
out of `G0` when a line of the variant carries it.

**P3-97.** A tool MUST NOT report a projection as preserving bindings, the suppression record, VALUES, blob declarations,
annotation counts, the kind of a line or the difference between language texts that RDF reads as equal, and a tool that reports a
projection MUST state the variant and the losses of 10a.2.

**P3-98.** A tool MUST NOT present a content digest as a digest of RDF dataset equivalence, and MUST NOT compare it with a digest
that an RDF canonicalization gives.

**P3-99.** A tool that imports RDF into a fold MUST give the fold of 10a.5 and no other: one `quad` line for each quad, one
`empty_graph` line for each name of `G0`, and nothing else.

**P3-100.** A tool that writes `Q` in an RDF syntax MUST NOT claim that the output is RDF when a text of `Q` is a text that RDF
does not admit, and MUST NOT change the text to make it so.

## 11. The canonical dictionary, rows and suppression frames

This section states what the canonical file of a fold holds in the frames of this part, and in what order. The constants that
cut the frames (`Bt`, `Nr`, `Ng` and the literal threshold), the order of regions and frames, and the check that a segment is
canonical are `P7`. Everything here is a function of the fold.

### 11.1 The canonical dictionary

MEMBERSHIP. The dictionary of a canonical file is exactly the set of terms that the fold uses, each once:

- every term of a line of the fold, hidden or not, in every slot, graph names included;
- every component, at any depth, of a `triple` term that is in the dictionary;
- every datatype IRI of a typed literal of the dictionary that is a `literal` or `literal_ref` term (a language literal has
  none, and the datatype of a typed value is generated);
- every term value that the record of a binding, of an entry or of a pair of VALUES holds in its `terms` (6.2), before
  lowering (6.2a): the terms of the bindings and of the resolved
  entries and the literals of the pairs of VALUES, with their components at any depth, a blank node that occurs only in a record included. The dictionary holds no term
  of a pair that names no node: such a pair stays written in its entry (11.4).

THE STORAGE FORM OF A LITERAL. A typed literal that VALUES holds is a `literal_value` term that holds the typed value of its pair,
except that one whose value is of the kind `octets` with at least the value threshold of `P7` in octets is a `literal_value_ref`
term. Every other literal, typed or language, is a `literal_ref` term when its lexical form has at least the literal threshold of
`P7` in bytes, and a `literal` term otherwise (2.6). The form is decided by VALUES and by a length, and no text is tested. In the
canonical file that a writer forms, a blank node of the fold is the term `blank` with the label `E(position)`, the deterministic
encoding of its final position in the one labelling of the labelled set (6.1, records included): opaque bytes to every reader,
compared whole.
A term node (6.2a) is no term of the dictionary and has no label. The label is generated from the position and is never read
back to a position by any step. For positions the bytewise order of these labels is the numeric order of the positions, since the shortest head
orders by length first and then by the argument. Two terms whose octets are equal share one value: the canonical file holds one
`value` for each distinct octet string, and a `literal_ref` or `literal_value_ref` term names it by its digest and length.

ORDER. The order of terms is TOTAL. Two terms of different kinds are ordered by the kind order, which is `iri`, `literal`,
`literal_ref`, `literal_value`, `literal_value_ref`, `blank`, `triple`; two terms of one kind are ordered by the key of the kind.
"Bytewise" in this section and in 11.2 to 11.4 is the bytewise order of `P1` (Notation): bytes compared as unsigned integers from
the first byte, a proper prefix before the longer string. Within a kind the order is:

- `iri`; order, ascending, each comparison component by component: bytewise on the text
- `literal`; order, ascending, each comparison component by component: `(lexical form, form, datatype or language)`: the lexical
  form bytewise; the form by its code in `literal_form` (`typed`, `language`, `language_ltr`, `language_rtl`); then, between two
  terms of the form `typed`, the datatype by the text of its IRI bytewise, and between two terms of one language form, the
  language text bytewise
- `literal_ref`; order, ascending, each comparison component by component: `(value digest, length, form, datatype or
  language)`: the digest bytewise, the length as an integer, then the form and the last component as for a `literal`
- `literal_value`; order, ascending, each comparison component by component: `(kind, value)`: the kind by its code in
  `value_kind`; then the value: of `bool`, `false` before `true`; of `uint` and `int`, as integers; of `time`, `seconds` and then
  `nanoseconds` as integers; of `octets`, bytewise
- `literal_value_ref`; order, ascending, each comparison component by component: `(value digest, length)`: the digest bytewise,
  then the length as an integer
- `blank`; order, ascending, each comparison component by component: bytewise on the label;
  in the canonical file that a writer forms the label is `E(position)`, so this is the order of the final positions, and the
  canonical check forms the dictionary in the bytewise order of the stored labels (`P7`, "The canonical check", STRUCTURE)
- `triple`; order, ascending, each comparison component by component: by DEPTH, then by the ids of `(subject, predicate, object)`
  compared in that order as integers

The depth of a `triple` whose components hold no `triple` term is `1`, and otherwise one more than the deepest component. Depth
order guarantees that every component and every datatype has a smaller id than the term that uses it, so no canonical file holds
a forward reference.

THE ORDER IS TOTAL. Each key is a function of the fields that the block of its kind holds and of the texts that its ids name, and
the dictionary holds each term once and in one kind (THE STORAGE FORM OF A LITERAL), so no two terms of a dictionary have one kind
and one key, and every two terms have exactly one order. Two terms of one reference kind with equal keys are written alike (3.3)
and are one term. Examples, for one lexical form `x` and one datatype `d`: the typed literal `x` with `d`, then `x` with the
language `EN`, `x` with `en`, `x` with `en-US`, then `x` with `en` and the direction `ltr`; and the typed literal `y` with `d`
after all of them.

**P3-88.** A writer of the canonical file and a reader that checks a `canonical` declaration MUST order terms by the kind order
first and the key of the kind second, and MUST NOT order two terms by their ids, their frames or any property that is not in the
key.

FRAMES. The terms are cut into `terms` frames by the budget `Bt` of `P7`; consecutive terms of one kind in a frame
form one run; every frame has `order` `canonical`, `restart_group` the constant `Ng` of `P7`, and `id_base` equal to
its expected base (2.1). The ids are `0, 1, 2, ...` in the order above. The front coding is that of 2.3, with prefix
lengths the longest common bytewise prefix.

**P3-53.** A writer of the canonical file MUST write the dictionary with the membership, the kind order, the order within each
kind and the label `E(position)` of this section, each term once.

**P3-54.** A reader that decodes a canonical dictionary MUST apply the order of this section to the terms of every frame declaring
`canonical`, as 2.9 states, including the order of datatype IRIs and of the ids of triples.

### 11.2 The canonical rows

Rows of each type are sorted under their key, every term compared by its id (the ids follow 11.1):

- `quads`; key, ascending: `(graph, predicate, object, subject)`
- `reifier_bindings`; key, ascending: `(graph, reifier, triple)`
- `annotations`; key, ascending: `(graph, reifier, predicate, object)`; the count is not in the key and every key is distinct,
  since the fold adds the counts of equal annotations
- `empty_graphs`; key, ascending: `(graph)`

The graph key puts the default graph first and then the named graphs by their ids. The rows of the fold are cut into frames of
`Nr` rows, in order, the last frame of a type holding the rest; each frame has `order` `canonical` (except `empty_graphs`, which
has no order field) and one graph run for each graph present in it. The annotation `count` is the sum of the fold. A type with
no line has no frame. The frame types follow one another in the order of `P7`.

**P3-55.** A writer of the canonical file MUST sort the rows of each type under its key, MUST cut them into
frames as 11.2 says, and MUST write each frame with `order` `canonical`; a reader that decodes a frame declaring
`canonical` MUST apply this key as 3.4 states.

### 11.3 The canonical bindings frames

The fold's bindings are written in the `bindings` frames, none when the fold has no binding. Each binding is `[leaf, referent]`
with the leaf `[1, term id]` of 11.1 for every binding whose leaf is named, and the leaf `[2, [frame, label]]` (or the tag
`3` form) as written for a binding whose leaf is unnamed. The bindings are sorted bytewise on the deterministic encoding of each
binding, with no repeat, and cut in that order into frames of `Nr` entries (`P7`, "The constants"), the last frame holding the
rest. The `entry_count` of a frame is the number of its entries. No canonical frame of bindings grows with the fold.

**P3-56.** A writer of the canonical file MUST write the bindings of the fold in the `bindings` frames of 11.3:
sorted bytewise on the encoding of each binding, after the term ids of 11.1 are assigned, with no repeat, cut
into frames of `Nr` entries in that order.

### 11.4 The canonical suppression frames

The fold's suppression record is written in the `suppress` frames, none when the record is empty. A canonical file is one segment,
so every entry uses term ids, with one exception.

- An entry whose leaves are all named is written once, with the leaf `[1, canonical id]`
  for every value, a blank node included. The record lines of the canonical file are those of the fold before compaction, as a
  set: the final positions are the same up to an automorphism of the labelled set (THE POSITIONS OF THE CANONICAL FILE, below),
  and the payload holds no label; and what it hid before it
  hides after: compaction keeps hidden content, because hidden content is content. A canonical file therefore produces no
  `suppression_unmatched` finding for an entry that matched.
- An entry that holds an unnamed leaf keeps the pair in its wire record exactly as it stood, `[2, [frame, label]]`, so that
  compaction never changes the labelled set. No pair names a node in a canonical file: the file is one segment, and a pair never
  names a node of the segment that holds the entry (6.3).

The entries are sorted bytewise on the deterministic encoding of the entry, with no repeat, and cut in that order into frames of
`Nr` entries (`P7`, "The constants"), the last frame holding the rest. The `entry_count` of a frame is the number of its entries.

THE POSITIONS OF THE CANONICAL FILE. The canonical file stores the final position of each blank node of the fold, from
the run of 6.1, as the label `E(position)` (11.1), and stores nothing for a term node (6.2a). `L1` states what a second labelling of such a file returns, in its paragraph
"THE CANONICAL MAP", as "LEMMA I (IDEMPOTENCE: THE MAP OF A RELABELLED DATASET)": if every blank node `b` of a dataset is
renamed `name(pos(b))` with `name` increasing in the source order, the renamed dataset is labelled by the identity map, with the
same canonical line sequence and digest. Its condition is that `name` is increasing in the source order. The canonical file
of a fold with no nested term meets it. The source identifier of the node of position `i` in the canonical file is `E([k, L_i])`, where `k` is the
segment's ordinal (`0` for the canonical file read alone) and `L_i` is the byte string whose bytes are `E(i)`; the head of `L_i` is `40` plus its length, which
grows with the length of `E(i)` (`41`, `42`, `43`, `45`, `49`), and among labels of one length the bytes of `E(i)` compare as
the integers `i`. So, for a fold with no nested term, the source order of the canonical file is the order of the positions,
and the labelling of the canonical file returns each position as its label, records included. In the canonical file a term node
has the source identifier that 6.2a gives it there, which is not the name of its position; when its nested term holds a blank
node at any depth it is not its identifier in the fold, since the `h` of that blank node is computed from its label in the
canonical file, and when its nested term holds none it is the same. So the condition holds for the blank nodes of the fold and
not for the term nodes. The stored labels of a canonical file whose fold has nested terms are not consecutive: the term nodes
take positions and are not stored.

What a second labelling of the canonical file returns is this. (1) Under the assumption that statement 1 of section 6 names, its
final positions are those stored up to an automorphism `sigma` of the labelled set: each blank node of the fold takes the position
stored as the label of its image under `sigma`. (2) For a fold in which no nested term holds a blank node at any depth they are
exactly those stored. With no nested term this is Lemma I. Otherwise every term node has the same source identifier in the fold
and in its canonical file, and by induction on the depth of its nested term refinement gives it a cell of its own: a nested term
of depth one has a record whose three other terms are constants, so its record line is alone in its line cell and its node in a
cell of its own; at depth `k` the other terms of its record are constants and nodes of nested terms of smaller depth, each already
in a cell of its own. So the node is fixed before any choice: no rule 3 call chooses it, no two components with equal
certificates or identities hold it, and M1 and M2 read the source identifiers of the blank nodes only, as in Lemma I. (3) The canonical line
sequence and the content digest are the same in every case, under the assumption that statement 1 of section 6 names. The reason
for (1) and (3): the two labellings are canonical maps of isomorphic inputs with one canonical line sequence, so the first
composed with the inverse of the second is an automorphism of the labelled set. The bytes of the canonical file are a function
of the labelled set with positions as names, which an automorphism leaves unchanged, so the canonical file is the same for either
labelling. The canonical check of `P7` relies on exactly this (`P7`, "The canonical check", LABELLING).

**P3-57.** A writer of the canonical file MUST write the suppression record of the fold in the `suppress` frames of 11.4, with
entries sorted bytewise on their encoding, no repeat, cut into frames of `Nr` entries in that order.

**P3-58.** A reader MUST NOT report `suppression_unmatched` for an entry of a canonical file that matches, and MUST report it,
as for any file, for an entry that matches nothing.

content = [0, [[1, 4], [2, 1], [3, 1]], 1, 2]      ; id_base 0; runs: iri 4, literal 1, blank 1; order canonical; restart_group 2
