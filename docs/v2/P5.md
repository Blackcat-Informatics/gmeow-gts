# Part P5: Durability

## 0. Scope, dependencies and notation

This part defines the `commit` frame and the RANGE of a segment, a Merkle mountain range over the segment's opener identifier
and the identifiers of its ordinary frames (section 1); the committed prefix (section 2); inclusion, consistency, range and file
proofs and their verification (section 3); the `index` frame and its payload, and the use of the `offsets` item and the locator
as accelerators (section 4); the stored check as a scrub (section 5); the `parity` frame with its one geometry, a Reed-Solomon
code over `GF(2^8)`, the parity stream and the placement of spans (section 6); and the one recovery procedure (section 7).

It depends on:

- part `P2` (Registry) for every enumeration, every finding code with its severity and present fields, and every reason. This
  part uses `P2`'s labels and codes exactly and assigns none.
- part `P1` (Model and wire), by section title: "The typed model and its one serialization" for the notation and `E(x)`;
  "File, segment, items, header, offsets and locator" for the segment, the header, `h0`, the `offsets` item and the locator;
  "The frame envelope" for the envelope and the content records; "The predecessor and transparent frames" for the predecessor,
  transparent frames and the effective identifier; "Identifiers, hashing and
  every preimage" for `H_ctx` and the context table; "The checks of every frame" for the link check, healing, the stored check
  and the payload check; "Framing: torn, damaged and undelimitable input" for delimiting items.
- part `P3` (RDF content) for the fold, the content digest and the rows that the index directory counts; part `P4` (Blobs)
  for the `pack` frame and its members; part `P6` (Signatures) for what a signature on a `commit` authenticates; part `P7`
  (Canonical form and reading) for the canonical placement checks and the result records that carry this part's findings.

## 1. The range

### 1.1 Leaves, nodes and root

The RANGE of a prefix of a segment is a Merkle mountain range over a sequence of leaves. For the prefix that ends before an
ordinary frame `f` (or at the end of the segment), the leaf sequence is:

- leaf `0`: `H_ctx(range_leaf, E([h0]))`, where `h0` is the identifier of the opener of the segment, a header or a resync item
  (`P1`, "The resync item");
- leaf `i`, for `i` from `1`: `H_ctx(range_leaf, E([e_i]))`, where `e_i` is the effective identifier of the ordinary frame of
  ordinary position `i - 1`.

The LEAF COUNT `n` of the prefix is the number of its leaves: one more than the number of ordinary frames in it. Transparent
frames and opaque items are never leaves. A leaf `i` is called the leaf of the frame of ordinary position `i - 1`.

The preimages, typed:

```text
leaf_preimage = [ identifier: digest ]                 ; H_ctx(range_leaf, E(leaf_preimage))
node_preimage = [ left: digest, right: digest ]         ; H_ctx(range_node, E(node_preimage))
root_preimage = [ count: uint, peaks: list(digest) ]    ; H_ctx(range_root, E(root_preimage)); count is the leaf count
```

With 32-byte digests, `E(leaf_preimage)` is `81 58 20` and the 32 bytes (35 bytes), `E(node_preimage)` is `82 58 20`, the
left digest, `58 20` and the right digest (69 bytes), and `E(root_preimage)` is `82`, the shortest `uint` head of `count`, the
list head of the number of peaks, and `58 20` with 32 bytes for each peak.

A PERFECT SUBTREE of height `h` over the leaves `a .. a + 2^h - 1` has the hash `T(a, 0) = leaf a` for `h = 0`, and
`T(a, h) = H_ctx(range_node, E([T(a, h - 1), T(a + 2^(h - 1), h - 1)]))` for `h >= 1`.

THE PEAKS of a leaf count `n >= 1`: write `n` in binary as the sum of distinct powers `2^(h_0) + 2^(h_1) + ... + 2^(h_(q-1))`
with `h_0 > h_1 > ... > h_(q-1)`. Peak `t` is the perfect subtree of height `h_t` whose first leaf is `p_t`, where `p_0 = 0`
and `p_(t+1) = p_t + 2^(h_t)`. `q` is the number of ones in the binary form of `n`. The peaks stand tallest first, which is
left to right.

THE ROOT of a leaf count `n` is `H_ctx(range_root, E([n, [T(p_0, h_0), ..., T(p_(q-1), h_(q-1))]]))`.

**P5-1.** A writer and a reader MUST compute a leaf, a node and a root only by the three preimages of 1.1, each encoded by `E`,
under the contexts `range_leaf`, `range_node` and `range_root`, and MUST NOT assemble a preimage from hand-built heads.

**P5-2.** The leaf sequence of a prefix MUST be leaf `0` over `h0` followed by one leaf for each ordinary frame of the prefix in
item order, over its effective identifier; a writer and a reader MUST NOT take as a leaf a transparent frame, an opaque item or a
frame that `P1` gives no effective identifier.

**P5-3.** The `count` of a root preimage MUST be the leaf count of the range, and `peaks` MUST be the peak hashes of 1.1 in order,
tallest first; the heights of the peaks are never written, since they follow from `count`.

### 1.2 Decomposition and aggregation

DECOMPOSITION. For `0 <= a <= b`, `decomp(a, b)` is the list, left to right, of the perfect subtrees that exactly cover the
leaves `a .. b - 1` and that a full tree would hold:

1. Let the list be empty.
2. While `a < b`: let `h` be the largest integer such that `a mod 2^h = 0` and `a + 2^h <= b`; append `(a, h)`; set
   `a = a + 2^h`.
3. The list is the result.

`decomp(0, n)` is the list of the peaks of `n`. The heights of `decomp(a, b)` rise and then fall; it has at most
`2 * floor(log2(b - a)) + 1` entries for `b > a`.

AGGREGATION. A STACK is a list of entries `(h, x)`: a height and a hash. `push(stack, h, x)` is:

1. Append `(h, x)` at the top.
2. While the stack has two or more entries and the top two have the same height `g`: remove the top two, `(g, l)` below and
   `(g, r)` on top, and append `(g + 1, H_ctx(range_node, E([l, r])))`.

ONE-PASS CONSTRUCTION. A prover (a writer, a server, a scrub) computes the root of `n` leaves by `push(stack, 0, leaf i)` for
`i` from `0` to `n - 1` in order, starting from the empty stack; the stack then holds the peaks of `n`, tallest at the bottom,
and the root is `H_ctx(range_root, E([n, the hashes of the stack from bottom to top]))`. The stack never holds more than
`floor(log2(n)) + 1` entries, so the memory is bounded by the logarithm of the leaf count and the pass reads each leaf once. A
writer that continues a segment keeps the stack between commits.

**P5-4.** A prover MUST obtain the same root as the definition of 1.1 for every leaf count; the one-pass construction of 1.2 is
the construction this part states, and any other that gives the same root for every leaf sequence is equal to it.

### 1.3 What the root binds

Assumptions. (A1) `H_ctx` is collision resistant. (A2) Two different contexts give unrelated functions (`P1`, "Identifiers,
hashing and every preimage"). Both are assumptions, not theorems of this format.

Claim. Two ranges with equal roots have equal leaf counts and equal leaf sequences, except with the probability of a collision
under (A1) and (A2).

Consequence. The root of a commit binds `h0`, so the opener, and the SEQUENCE OF THE EFFECTIVE IDENTIFIERS of every ordinary
frame before it in order. It binds the type, flags, content, decoded length and payload digest of an earlier frame only when that
frame's own link state is `linked` or `healed` (`P1`, "What the identifier commits to"), and the payload bytes of an earlier frame
only when its payload check holds as well. It does not bind a transparent frame, the storage form, the stored bytes, the
signatures, the `offsets` item or the locator. A root that is valid for a prefix (the commit's `count` and `root` agree with the
identifiers the reader has) is a valid range commitment, and is neither an intact prefix nor an authenticated one: a frame whose
link is broken keeps its stored identifier as a leaf, so its fields can differ from those that the identifier was computed over
while the root and every later identifier are unchanged; the frame's own finding says so. The root binds the sequence of
identifiers it commits, across a broken link; the identifier of a frame binds earlier frames only through its unbroken run
(`P1`, "What the identifier commits to", Claim 2).

### 1.4 Worked example: the peaks and root of five and of eight leaves

Let the effective identifiers be `h0` and `I1` to `I7`, and the leaves `L0 = H_ctx(range_leaf, E([h0]))` and
`Lk = H_ctx(range_leaf, E([Ik]))`. Write `N01 = H_ctx(range_node, E([L0, L1]))`, likewise `N23`, `N45`, `N67`; then
`N0123 = H_ctx(range_node, E([N01, N23]))`, `N4567` likewise, and `N0_7 = H_ctx(range_node, E([N0123, N4567]))`.

Leaf count `5` (the header and four ordinary frames: the root a `commit` after the fourth frame carries). `5 = 4 + 1`: two
peaks, `T(0, 2) = N0123` and `T(4, 0) = L4`. The root is `R5 = H_ctx(range_root, E([5, [N0123, L4]]))`, and the preimage is
`82 05 82 58 20 <N0123> 58 20 <L4>`: `1 + 1 + 1 + 34 + 34 = 71` bytes.

The one-pass construction: push `L0`: `(0, L0)`. Push `L1`: the top two have height `0`, giving `(1, N01)`. Push `L2`:
`(1, N01), (0, L2)`. Push `L3`: `(1, N01), (1, N23)`, then `(2, N0123)`. Push `L4`: `(2, N0123), (0, L4)`. The stack is the
peak list of `5`.

Leaf count `8`: one peak `N0_7`; `R8 = H_ctx(range_root, E([8, [N0_7]]))`, the preimage `82 08 81 58 20 <N0_7>` (37 bytes).
`decomp(5, 8)` is `(5, 0), (6, 1)`: `5 mod 2 = 1` allows height `0` only; `6 mod 2 = 0` and `6 + 2 = 8` allows height `1`.

## 2. The `commit` frame and the committed prefix

### 2.1 The record

A `commit` frame is ordinary, not critical and not transparent (`P2`). Its content record and payload:

```text
commit.content = [ count: uint, root: digest ]   ; decoded payload: empty
```

`count` is the number of ordinary frames of the segment that stand before the commit, which is its ordinary position. `root` is
the root of the range of the prefix before the commit: leaf count `count + 1`, over `h0` and the effective identifiers of those
`count` frames. Both are stored. `count` lets a reader find the prefix a commit closes before it recomputes anything; it adds no
second meaning, because the root preimage holds the leaf count and a disagreement between the two is a finding.

**P5-5.** A writer MUST write a `commit` frame with `critical` and `transparent` `false`, an empty decoded payload, `count` equal
to its ordinary position and `root` equal to the root of 1.1 over the leaf count `count + 1`.

### 2.2 The checks of a commit

For each `commit` frame `c` of a segment that carries no `malformed_value` finding, taken in item order, with `o` its ordinary
position:

1. COUNT. When `count` differs from `o`, the finding is `commit_count_mismatch` with the `position` of `c` and `count` equal to
   `o`, the number of ordinary frames that stand before it.
2. ROOT. Compute the root over leaf count `o + 1`: `h0` and the effective identifiers of the `o` ordinary frames before `c`.
   When it differs from `root`, the finding is `commit_root_mismatch` with the `position` of `c`.
3. A commit is VALID when its link state is `linked` or `healed` and neither step 1 nor step 2 gave a finding.

A `commit` frame that carries a `malformed_value` finding of any reason (a content outside its grammar, flags that differ from
the registry columns, a `decoded_length` that is not `0`) is opaque with `malformed` (`P1`) and is not a commit for the checks of
a commit, the committed prefix or a graph state; an opaque frame closes no graph state. A `commit` frame
whose `decoded_length` is not `0` is `malformed_value` with the reason `out_of_range` (P5-44): this is a field rule, judged with
the
field rules of `P1` and after them.

**P5-44.** A reader MUST report a `commit` frame whose `decoded_length` is not `0` as `malformed_value` with the reason
`out_of_range`.

**P5-6.** A reader MUST run steps 1 and 2 of 2.2 on every `commit` frame that carries no `malformed_value` finding, and MUST
report `commit_count_mismatch` and `commit_root_mismatch` with exactly the fields that `P2` marks present for them.

**P5-7.** A reader MUST compute the root of step 2 over the ordinary frames that it observed before
the commit, whatever `count` states.

### 2.3 The committed prefix

The COMMITTED PREFIX of a segment is the run of its items from the opener up to and including its last valid commit. A segment
with no valid commit has an empty committed prefix. A commit that is not valid commits nothing and closes no graph state.

`committed_frames` (the field of the segment result, `P7`) is the number of ordinary frames in the committed prefix: `o + 1` for
a last valid commit of ordinary position `o`, the commit included, and `0` when there is none.

THE UNCOMMITTED TAIL is the ordinary frames after the committed prefix other than `index` frames. An `index` frame is an
accelerator and holds no content (section 4), so a file that ends `commit`, `index`, as every canonical file does, has no tail.
When the tail holds at least one frame, the finding is `uncommitted_tail`, once for the segment, with `count` the number of its
frames and `position` the position of the first of them.

The fold of a segment (`P3`) covers committed and uncommitted frames alike. There is no reading mode. A consumer that wants only
committed content reads the fold of the committed prefix, which is a GRAPH STATE: the fold of the frames of the run of segments
that a commit closes when it names a state (`P3`, "Graph states"), named by that commit's effective identifier (`P3` names it as
a `state` referent). A valid commit names a state to a reader only when every ordinary frame before it in its run is linked or
healed and has the fold state `folded` or `not_content` for that reader (`P3`); otherwise the state is unavailable to it. A
state is local to its segment unless the checked lineage of a segment opened by a header joins it to the segments before it; a
segment opened by a resync item is joined to none, whether or not its lineage is checked (`P3`, "Graph states", THE RUN).

A graph state is a property of the APPEND HISTORY of a file, not of its content: the commit that names it exists only in the file
whose frames were appended up to it. A copy of the fold that is written again (a compacted or canonical copy, whose frames and
commit are new) holds no commit with that identifier, so a `state` referent of the source file is unresolved in the compacted
copy (`missing_dependency` with reason `state`, `P3`); a tool that compacts a file does not carry its graph states.

**P5-8.** A reader MUST give `committed_frames` the value of 2.3, and MUST treat as valid only a commit that meets step 3 of 2.2.

**P5-9.** A reader MUST report `uncommitted_tail` exactly when the uncommitted tail of 2.3 holds at least one frame, once for the
segment, with the count and the position of 2.3.

**P5-10.** A reader MUST fold the frames of the uncommitted tail as it folds the committed ones, and MUST NOT offer a mode that
folds the committed prefix only; a graph state MUST be named only by a valid commit that names a state (`P3`).

### 2.4 What a commit does not show

A commit is a logical commitment, not evidence that a durable write completed. A whole round of frames missing at the end of a
file, and a rollback to any earlier valid commit, cannot be detected from the file alone: a valid earlier prefix cannot be told
from a file whose later suffix was lost. A party that recorded an earlier root detects a rollback or a fork by a consistency
proof (3.3) against the root it recorded. A segment with no commit still has order integrity by the chain of identifiers
(`P1`): an insertion, a deletion, a reordering or a splice of frames is a failed link at the next frame.

### 2.5 Worked example

A segment holds, in item order: the header (position `0`), `T` a `terms` frame (position `1`, ordinary position `0`) that
defines every term id the rows use, `F1` a `quads` frame (position `2`, ordinary position `1`), `X` a `parity` frame (position
`3`, transparent), `F2` a `commit` (position `4`, ordinary position `2`), `F3` a `quads` frame (position `5`), `F4` an `index`
frame (position `6`). Let `IT` and `I1` be the effective identifiers of `T` and `F1`.

- `F2` is written with `count` `2` and `root` `R3 = H_ctx(range_root, E([3, [N01, L2]]))`, where `N01` is the node over
  `L0 = H_ctx(range_leaf, E([h0]))` and `L1 = H_ctx(range_leaf, E([IT]))`, and `L2 = H_ctx(range_leaf, E([I1]))`. `X` is not a
  leaf: removing it changes nothing.
- When `F2` links and both checks hold, it is valid: `committed_frames` is `3` (`T`, `F1` and `F2`). The tail is `F3` alone
  (`F4` is an `index`): `uncommitted_tail` with `count` `1` and `position` `5`.
- When a defective writer wrote `F2` with `count` `3` and computed its identifier over that content, `F2` links; step 1 gives
  `commit_count_mismatch` with `count` `2`, step 2 compares `root` with `R3`, and `F2` is not valid whatever step 2 gives.
  `committed_frames` is `0`, and the tail is `T`, `F1`, `F2`, `F3`: `uncommitted_tail` with `count` `4` and `position` `1`.

## 3. Proofs

### 3.1 The records

A proof is a value of the reading interface, not an item of a file: nothing in a file stores one. Its bytes are the
deterministic encoding of one of these records, whose fields are `uint`, `digest`, lists of `digest` and one option:

```text
inclusion_proof   = [ count: uint, index: uint, path: list(digest) ]
consistency_proof = [ old_count: uint, new_count: uint, path: list(digest) ]
range_proof       = [ first: uint, end: uint, count: uint, path: list(digest) ]
file_proof        = list(file_proof_entry)
file_proof_entry  = [ segment_ordinal: uint, count: uint, head: digest, proof: option(inclusion_proof) ]
```

Every count of a proof is a leaf count and every index a leaf index, zero-based: leaf `0` is the leaf of the opener and the frame
of
ordinary position `o` is leaf `o + 1`. A root that a proof is checked against comes with its leaf count: a `commit` with
`count` `c` gives its `root` and the leaf count `c + 1`; an `index` with `frame_count` `c` gives its `range_root` and `c + 1`;
a party that publishes a root gives both. No side, height or list split is carried: each is a function of the counts and
indexes, and the verifier derives it.

THE OUTCOME of verifying one proof is exactly one finding: `proof_holds`, `proof_failed` or `proof_malformed`, with its reason
and no other field present (`P2`). The reasons of `proof_malformed`, as this part uses them:

- `length`: the bytes are not exactly one deterministic encoding of the record (another item, a trailing byte, a field of
  another type, an array of another length, a digest that is not 32 bytes), or a list holds another number of entries than the
  procedure derives, or the verifier was given another number of identifiers than the proof covers;
- `count`: a count or a value that the proof states differs from the count of the root it is checked against, or from the
  value that the procedure fixes for it;
- `order`: an index or a pair of counts out of order: an `index` not below `count`, an `old_count` of `0` or above
  `new_count`, a `first` not below `end`, an `end` above `count`, a count of `0`, or segment ordinals that are not `0`, `1`, `2`
  and so on in order.

The checks of a procedure run in its step order, and within one step in the order in which the sentences of the step state them;
the first that fails decides the finding; no hash is computed before the shape, count and order steps hold.

**P5-11.** A proof MUST be the deterministic encoding of one record of 3.1, and a verifier MUST judge it by the procedure of its
kind and report exactly one finding with the reason that the first failing step names.

**P5-12.** A verifier MUST derive every height, side and list split from the counts and indexes of the proof and MUST NOT accept a
proof that states any of them.

### 3.2 Inclusion

Input: a root `R` with leaf count `n`, the identifier `id` claimed at a leaf (`h0` for leaf `0`, else the
effective identifier of the frame), and an `inclusion_proof`. The proof says that leaf `index` of the range with root `R` is
`H_ctx(range_leaf, E([id]))`. `path` holds the siblings from that leaf up to its peak, then the other peaks tallest first.

1. Decode the proof; a failure is `proof_malformed` `length`.
2. When `count` is `0` or `index >= count`: `proof_malformed` `order`. Otherwise, when `count` differs from `n`:
   `proof_malformed` `count`.
3. Compute the peaks of `count` (1.1): heights `h_0 .. h_(q-1)`, first leaves `p_0 .. p_(q-1)`. Let `t` be the peak with
   `p_t <= index < p_t + 2^(h_t)`. When `|path|` differs from `h_t + q - 1`: `proof_malformed` `length`.
4. `x = H_ctx(range_leaf, E([id]))`, `r = index - p_t`. For `l` from `0` to `h_t - 1`: when `bit(l, r)` is `0`,
   `x = H_ctx(range_node, E([x, path[l]]))`; otherwise `x = H_ctx(range_node, E([path[l], x]))`.
5. The peaks are `path[h_t] .. path[h_t + q - 2]` in order, with `x` inserted at place `t` (zero-based).
6. When `H_ctx(range_root, E([count, peaks]))` equals `R`: `proof_holds` `inclusion`; otherwise `proof_failed` `inclusion`.

The side of a sibling is the bit of the leaf's offset in its peak at that level, as in the inclusion proof
verification of RFC 9162 section 2.1.3.2.

**P5-13.** A verifier MUST verify an inclusion proof by the six steps of 3.2, and a prover MUST write `path` as those steps
read it.

### 3.3 Consistency

Input: a root `R1` with leaf count `n1`, a root `R2` with leaf count `n2`, and a `consistency_proof`. The proof says that the
first `old_count` leaves of the range of `R2` are the leaves of the range of `R1`. `path` holds the peaks of `old_count`,
tallest first, then the hashes of the subtrees of `decomp(old_count, new_count)` in order.

1. Decode the proof; a failure is `proof_malformed` `length`.
2. When `old_count` is `0` or above `new_count`: `proof_malformed` `order`. Otherwise, when `old_count` differs from `n1` or
   `new_count` from `n2`: `proof_malformed` `count`.
3. Let `q1` be the number of peaks of `old_count` and `d` the number of entries of `decomp(old_count, new_count)`. When `|path|`
   differs from `q1 + d`: `proof_malformed` `length`.
4. When `H_ctx(range_root, E([old_count, path[0 .. q1 - 1]]))` differs from `R1`: `proof_failed` `consistency_old_root`.
5. When `old_count` equals `new_count`: `proof_holds` `consistency` when `R1` equals `R2`, else `proof_failed`
   `consistency_diverged` (two roots for one leaf count: a fork). Stop.
6. Form the stack of the peaks of `old_count`, `(h_t, path[t])` for `t` from `0` to `q1 - 1`, bottom to top. For the `j`-th
   entry `(a, h)` of `decomp(old_count, new_count)`: `push(stack, h, path[q1 + j])`.
7. The stack now holds the peaks of `new_count` (each pushed subtree is aligned, so the merges are the carries of adding its
   size). When `H_ctx(range_root, E([new_count, the stack's hashes bottom to top]))` equals `R2`: `proof_holds`
   `consistency`; otherwise `proof_failed` `consistency_new_root`.

**P5-14.** A verifier MUST verify a consistency proof by the seven steps of 3.3, and MUST compare the old root (step 4) before it
computes any hash of the new range.

### 3.4 Range

Input: a root `R` with leaf count `n`, the identifiers of the leaves `first .. end - 1` in order (`h0` for leaf
`0`), and a `range_proof`. The proof says that those leaves of the range of `R` are the leaves of the given identifiers. `path`
holds the hashes of the subtrees of `decomp(0, first)` then those of `decomp(end, count)`. The frames of a graph state that stand
in the segment of its commit, or any
sequence of consecutive ordinary frames of one segment, are a range.

1. Decode the proof; a failure is `proof_malformed` `length`.
2. When `count` is `0`, `first >= end` or `end > count`: `proof_malformed` `order`. Otherwise, when `count` differs from `n`:
   `proof_malformed` `count`.
3. When the number of identifiers given differs from `end - first`, or `|path|` differs from the number of entries of
   `decomp(0, first)` plus that of `decomp(end, count)`: `proof_malformed` `length`.
4. Start from the empty stack. Push the subtrees of `decomp(0, first)` with their hashes from `path`, in order; push
   `(0, H_ctx(range_leaf, E([id_i])))` for `i` from `first` to `end - 1`; push the subtrees of `decomp(end, count)` with the
   rest of `path`, in order.
5. When `H_ctx(range_root, E([count, the stack's hashes bottom to top]))` equals `R`: `proof_holds` `range`; otherwise
   `proof_failed` `range`.

**P5-15.** A verifier MUST verify a range proof by the five steps of 3.4.

### 3.5 File

A file proof says that a file `F2` extends a file `F1`: every segment of `F1`, by ordinal, stands in `F2` at the same ordinal
with the same header identifier and the same effective identifiers at the same ordinary positions, and every segment of `F1`
but the last holds no further ordinary frame in `F2`. It is the proposition that an append-only writer's later file contains
its earlier one; it is a statement about identifiers, not about stored bytes.

Let `F1` have `p` segments, and for its segment `k` let `c_k` be the number of its ordinary frames and `g_k` its HEAD, as `P1`
defines it ("The predecessor and transparent frames"); it is `h0` when `c_k` is `0`. `g_k` is the identifier of leaf `c_k` of
segment `k`. The proof has one entry for each segment of `F1`, in order: `[k, c_k, g_k, proof]`. `proof` is `present` when
segment `k` of `F2` has a valid commit whose leaf count exceeds `c_k`: it is then the inclusion proof of leaf `c_k` under the
root of the FIRST such commit in item order. It is `absent` when there is none.

Input: the file proof; the values `c_k` and `g_k` of `F1` (read from `F1`, or held by the verifier from an earlier read); and
`F2` read by the procedure of `P7`.

1. Decode the proof; a failure is `proof_malformed` `length`. When the number of entries differs from `p`: `proof_malformed`
   `length`. When the `segment_ordinal` values are not `0` to `p - 1` in order: `proof_malformed` `order`.
2. When an entry's `count` or `head` differs from `c_k` or `g_k`: `proof_malformed` `count`. When an entry's `proof` is
   `present` and no valid commit of segment `k` of `F2` has the leaf count it states, or it is not the first commit of 3.5, or
   its `index` is not `c_k`; or when it is `absent` and such a commit exists: `proof_malformed` `count`. A segment `k` that `F2`
   does not hold has no valid commit, so a `present` entry for it ends here, at step 2, before step 3 is judged.
3. When `F2` has fewer than `p` segments: `proof_failed` `consistency_diverged`.
4. For each `k` from `0` to `p - 1`: when one of the first `c_k` ordinary frames of segment `k` of `F2` is `broken`, or the
   segment has fewer than `c_k` ordinary frames: `proof_failed` `consistency_diverged`.
5. For each `k` below `p - 1`: when segment `k` of `F2` has another number of ordinary frames than `c_k`, or its head differs
   from `g_k`: `proof_failed` `consistency_diverged`.
6. For `k = p - 1`: when `proof` is `present`, verify it by 3.2 with `id = g_k` against the commit's root and leaf
   count; when its outcome is `proof_failed`, `proof_failed` `consistency_diverged`; when its outcome is `proof_malformed`, that
   finding with its reason. When `proof` is `absent`, compare `g_k` with the
   effective identifier at ordinary position `c_k - 1` of segment `k` of `F2` (its `h0` when `c_k` is `0`); a difference
   is `proof_failed` `consistency_diverged`.
7. Otherwise `proof_holds` `consistency`.

A segment of `F2` with an ordinal of `p` or more is not covered by the proof; its own lineage field is judged by `P1`.

**P5-16.** A prover MUST write one entry of 3.5 for each segment of `F1` in order, with the `proof` that 3.5 fixes, and a
verifier MUST verify a file proof by the seven steps of 3.5.

### 3.6 What a proof proves, and what it does not

- inclusion; proved, under (A1) and (A2) of 1.3: the range of `R` holds at leaf `index` the leaf of `id`; so the frame of that
  identifier stands at ordinary position `index - 1` of the prefix that `R` closes and, when its own link state is `linked` or
  `healed`, with the type, content and payload digest its identifier fixes; not proved: that `R` is the signer's (see below);
  anything about stored bytes, storage, signatures or
  transparent frames; that the verifier holds the payload
- consistency; proved, under (A1) and (A2) of 1.3: the first `old_count` leaves of the range of `R2` are those of `R1`: the later
  prefix extends the earlier; not proved: anything about leaves `old_count` and later; that either root is current (a rollback to
  a valid older root is not detected unless the verifier recorded a later one)
- range; proved, under (A1) and (A2) of 1.3: leaves `first .. end - 1` of the range of `R` are the given identifiers, in order;
  not proved: the same as inclusion
- file; proved, under (A1) and (A2) of 1.3: every ordinary frame of `F1` has its effective identifier at the same segment ordinal
  and ordinary position in `F2`, given the links of `F2` that step 4 checks; not proved: equality of bytes (a storage rewrite, a
  signature added or parity rebuilt change bytes and no identifier); any segment of `F2` beyond those of `F1`

AUTHENTICATION. A proof is a statement about identifiers. Checked against the root of a commit on which a signature authenticates
(`P6`, "What a signature binds and authenticates"), an inclusion proof shows that the frame stood in the signed prefix, and
a consistency proof between two roots that one trusted key signed shows that the signer's later state extends the earlier; a
consistency failure between two such roots is evidence that the key signed two histories. Against a root with no authenticating
signature, a proof shows only agreement with that root.

COST. A verifier holds a stack of at most `floor(log2(count)) + 1` entries. An inclusion path has at most `h_t + q - 1` entries,
fewer than `2 * floor(log2(count)) + 1`; a verifier computes one hash per entry and two more. No proof holds a field that a
verifier has to trust to choose a structure, so no proof makes a verifier do work beyond these bounds.

**P5-17.** A reader MUST NOT report a frame as authenticated on the strength of a proof alone;
authentication is the typed fact of `P6`.

A prover needs the effective identifiers of the segment and no payload byte: elements `1` to `6` of each frame and its stored
`identifier` give its effective identifier (`P1`, "The link check" and "Healing"), so the inclusion, consistency and range proofs
of
this section are computed from those fields of the items alone.

### 3.7 Worked examples

The names are those of 1.4: `R5` is the root of leaf count `5` and `R8` of leaf count `8`.

INCLUSION of leaf `2` (the frame of ordinary position `1`, identifier `I2`) under `R5`: the proof is `[5, 2, [L3, N01, L4]]`,
encoded `83 05 02 83 58 20 <L3> 58 20 <N01> 58 20 <L4>` (`4 + 3 * 34 = 106` bytes). Steps: (2) `5 = n`, `2 < 5`. (3) peaks of

`x = node(N01, N23) = N0123`. (5) peaks `[N0123, L4]`. (6) the root of `E([5, [N0123, L4]])` is `R5`: `proof_holds`
`inclusion`. With `path` `[L3, N01]`: step 3, `proof_malformed` `length`. With `index` `5`: step 2, `order`. Checked against

CONSISTENCY from leaf count `5` to `8`: `decomp(5, 8)` is `(5, 0), (6, 1)`, so the proof is `[5, 8, [N0123, L4, L5, N67]]`
(`4 + 4 * 34 = 140` bytes). Steps: (3) `q1 = 2`, `d = 2`, `|path| = 4`. (4) the root of `E([5, [N0123, L4]])` is `R1 = R5`.
(6) the stack `(2, N0123), (0, L4)`; push `(0, L5)`: the top two have height `0`, giving `(1, N45)`; push `(1, N67)`: height

`proof_failed` `consistency_new_root`; had the first root been of another history, step 4 gives `consistency_old_root`.

RANGE of leaves `2` and `3` under `R5`: `decomp(0, 2)` is `(0, 1)` and `decomp(4, 5)` is `(4, 0)`, so the proof is
`[2, 4, 5, [N01, L4]]` with the identifiers `I2`, `I3`. Step 4: push `(1, N01)`; push `(0, L2)`; push `(0, L3)`, which merges
to `(1, N23)` and then `(2, N0123)`; push `(0, L4)`. Step 5: the root over `[N0123, L4]` is `R5`: `proof_holds` `range`.

FILE. `F1` is one segment with three ordinary frames, effective identifiers `I1`, `I2`, `I3`, so `c_0 = 3` and `g_0 = I3`

compares `I3` with the effective identifier of ordinary position `2` of `F2`.

## 4. The index and the accelerators

### 4.1 The `index` frame

An `index` frame is ordinary, not critical and not transparent. It is an accelerator: it states, for the frames of its segment
that stand before it, facts that a reader can also obtain by reading those frames. It is never a dependency: the fold, the
content digest and every answer that a reader gives about content are the same whether the reader uses the index, ignores it,
or meets a false one, under the access `whole` or `forward`; the answer of a read with the access `sparse` is reached through the
index and states what it takes from it as claims of the index (`P7`, "Reading by range"). The index is itself an ordinary frame,
so it is a leaf and is counted in `committed_frames` and in the
links, and the findings of 4.3 are the only findings it adds. A file from which the index frame has been physically removed is
another file: it has another count of ordinary frames, another head, another `committed_frames` and other roots and identifiers
after the place of the index, and a commit that followed the index no longer agrees with them. What is equal with and without
an index is the fold and the answers about content, not the counts, heads or roots of the frames.

```text
index.content = [ frame_count: uint, head: digest ]
index_payload = [ type_runs:       list([ frame_type: enum(frame_type), run: uint ]),
                  graph_directory: list(graph_entry),
                  statistics:      statistics,
                  range_root:      digest,
                  content_digest:  option(digest),
                  pack_members:    list([ unit_digest: digest, ordinary_position: uint, member_index: uint ]) ]
graph_entry   = [ graph: graph_name, ranges: list([ frame_type: enum(frame_type), first_frame: uint, first_row: uint,
                                                    row_count: uint ]) ]
statistics    = [ lines: uint, records: uint, blank_nodes: uint, per_predicate: list([ term_id: uint, rows: uint ]) ]
```

The COVERED frames of an index are the ordinary frames of its segment before it; the COVERED FOLD is the fold (`P3`) of the
covered frames of this segment and of no other segment: the index describes the fold of its own segment. The result of reading
carries the segment's own `content_digest` (compared with the index's claim) and, separately, the content digest of the whole
file (`P7`). Every field is complete for what it covers:

- `frame_count`; value: the number of covered frames: the index's ordinary position
- `head`; value: the head of the covered frames, as `P1` defines the head of a segment taken over them
- `type_runs`; value: the `frame_type` of the covered frames in item order, as maximal runs: each `run` at least `1`, two
  consecutive entries never of one type, the runs adding up to `frame_count`. These three properties are part of the claim and
  not of the grammar: a `type_runs` with a `run` of `0`, with two consecutive entries of one type, or whose runs add up to
  another number is in the grammar of the payload and disagrees with the covered frames (4.3). A frame item of no frame type
  (`P1`, "The locator", A FRAME ITEM HAS A TYPE ONLY BY A
  VALUE) is counted in `frame_count` exactly when it is an ordinary frame (its `transparent` field is not `true`) that has an
  effective identifier (`P1`, "The predecessor and transparent frames": a frame with no effective identifier is counted by no
  count of ordinary frames), and it belongs to no run of `type_runs`; so no `type_runs` states the covered frames of an index that
  covers one: the claim `type_runs` of that index disagrees with the covered frames (4.3), and the index has `index_claim_failed`
  with `index_directory`. A frame item of no frame type that is transparent or has no effective identifier is no covered frame and
  affects neither `frame_count` nor `type_runs`. A writer never writes such a frame, so a canonical file holds none
- `graph_directory`; value: one entry for each graph that has a row in a covered `quads`, `reifier_bindings`, `annotations` or
  `empty_graphs` frame, ordered by graph: the default graph first, then named graphs by ascending term id, no graph twice.
  `ranges` are the maximal runs of consecutive rows of that graph in the rows of the covered frames of one type, taken frame after
  frame in item order and each frame's rows in its own order; `first_frame` is the ordinary position of the frame of the run's
  first row, `first_row` the zero-based index of that row in its frame, `row_count` at least `1`; a run continues into the next
  covered frame of the same type. `ranges` are ordered by `first_frame`, then `first_row`. There is no digest of a graph name: the
  name is a term of the dictionary
- `statistics`; value: the description of the LABELLED SET of the covered fold (`P3`, "The labelled set": the RDF lines of
  the fold together with one record line for each binding, each suppression entry, each blob declaration and each pair of
  VALUES, and one for each nested term, which `records` does not count); `lines`: the number
  of RDF lines, hidden or not, since hidden content is content; `records`: the number of record lines other than the `nested_term`
  records (`P3`, "Nested terms, lowering and term nodes");
  `blank_nodes`: the number of distinct blank nodes of the fold, those that occur only in record lines included, a term node
  being no blank node of the fold; `per_predicate`: one entry for each
  term id that is the predicate of a row of a `quads` frame of the segment, ascending, with `rows` the number of such rows. The
  statistics record of the labelling is not stored: every reader that labels computes it, and the index holds nothing that a
  reader does not need
- `range_root`; value: the root of the range of leaf count `frame_count + 1` (section 1)
- `content_digest`; value: the content digest of the labelled set of the covered fold, as `P3` defines it (this part states no
  preimage); `absent` exactly when `P3` gives the covered fold no content digest (the labelling refused, or a row, binding
  or entry is held)
- `pack_members`; value: one entry for every member of kind `chunk` or `blob` (`P4`) of every covered `pack` frame: its unit
  digest, the ordinary position of the pack frame and the zero-based index of the member in the pack's directory; ordered by
  `unit_digest` bytewise, then `ordinary_position`, then `member_index`; no entry twice

The list is complete, so a reader that finds no entry for a unit digest in a VERIFIED `pack_members` claim (4.3) knows that no
covered pack holds a member with it: a lookup needs no decoding of a pack. In a claim that is not verified, a missing entry is
not absence. `ordinary_position` and `first_frame` count ordinary frames, so inserting or removing a
transparent frame changes no index.

**P5-18.** A writer MUST write an `index` frame with `critical` and `transparent` `false`, its content and payload as the list of
4.1 states for its covered frames, and every list in the order and with the completeness that the list states.

**P5-19.** A reader with the access `whole` or `forward` MUST NOT let an index change any result other than the findings of 4.3:
the fold, the content digest, every
answer about content, and the counts, the roots, the positions and every state of the frames, are those that the items give, the
index frame among them. A file with the index frame removed is another file, and only its fold and its answers about content
are equal to those of the file with the index.

### 4.2 The `offsets` item and the locator

The `offsets` item and the locator are defined by `P1` ("File, segment, items, header, offsets and locator"). Both are claims
about storage outside every hash: the `offsets` item lists the encoded length of every earlier item of the segment, and the
locator gives the distance back from its own first byte to the first byte of the last `index` frame of the segment in item order,
the CLOSING INDEX (6.3). A frame item counts as an `index` frame for the locator by its `frame_type` alone, whatever its
payload, so that a frame item of type `index` that fails a rule of another field is still the last `index` frame that the locator
can name; a frame item whose `frame_type` element is itself outside its one form has no frame type and is not one
(`P1`, "The locator"). They are accelerators in the same sense as the
index: a reader uses the `offsets` item to start link checks of many frames in parallel and to fetch one frame by its range, and
the locator to find the index from the end of a file without reading it forward; each offset is a hint, and an item boundary is
established only by walking from a known boundary (`P1`, "Complete items that are not items of this format"): a local parse at
an offset that yields a complete item establishes nothing, and no result computed from a hint is used before the walk has
arrived at it. A read with the access `sparse` (`P7`, "Reading by range") reads the frames at the offsets that the `offsets`
item gives and states those offsets as claims of that item.

Both are items of the sequence, so every reader that reads a segment meets them in order and checks them there: an `offsets`
item against the item lengths the reader has seen (`index_offsets`), and a locator against the offset of the segment's closing
index, at the locator's own position (`index_locator`). Reading the last 11 bytes of a file first is only a faster way to find
the index; when those bytes do not lead to an `index` frame a reader with the access `whole` reads the file from its
start, and that attempt gives no
finding of its own.

They stand after the last parity frame of the segment, outside the parity stream (6.3), and are never reported by
`unprotected_tail`: they are rebuilt from the items, so their loss costs nothing that the file cannot give again.

**P5-20.** A writer that writes a locator MUST write an `index` frame in the same segment for the locator to name, MUST make the
locator name the last `index` frame of the segment in item order, and MUST write the `offsets` item and the locator after every
frame of the segment, parity frames included.

**P5-21.** A reader MUST check an `offsets` item and a locator in sequence, where it meets them, MUST NOT let either change any
result other than the findings `index_claim_failed` with `index_offsets` and with `index_locator`, and MUST NOT report a finding
for an attempt to read a locator from the last 11 bytes of the input that does not lead to an `index` frame.

### 4.3 Checking an index

An index is CHECKED when its link state is `linked` or `healed`, its payload state is `verified` and its payload is in the
grammar of 4.1. An index that stands after a frame that stopped the fold (`unsupported_critical`) is checked and judged like any
other index: the link check and the payload check are made whatever the fold (`P7`, "The wire phase of a segment", `R7` PAYLOAD
CHECK), so the reader decodes its
payload for this definition, its fold state stays `opaque` with the reason `stopped`, and the claims below are compared with the
covered frames as they stand, a claim that depends on a fold being compared only where this section says it is. A reader checks
every checked index of a segment, in item order, against the covered frames, field by field, and reports
`index_claim_failed` at the position of the index once for each reason that applies:

When a read stops at `items_per_read` or fails, no fold is formed and the content digests are `absent` (`P7`, "The reader
procedure"): the claims that are judged against a fold, `content_digest` and `statistics`, are then not judged, and every
other claim of a checked index is judged over the items read.

| field that disagrees with the covered frames | reason |
|---|---|
| `frame_count` or `head` | `index_head` |
| `range_root` | `index_root` |
| `type_runs`, `graph_directory` or `statistics` | `index_directory` |
| `content_digest` | `index_digest` |
| `pack_members` | `index_members` |

The finding is made once for each reason, not once for each field: an index whose `frame_count` and `head` are both wrong has one
`index_claim_failed` with `index_head`.

An index states its claims about the ordinary frames BEFORE it, and a reader checks them as follows.

STRUCTURAL CLAIMS (`frame_count`, `head`, `type_runs`, `graph_directory`, `range_root`, `pack_members`) are checked wherever the
index stands in its segment, against the covered frames. A claim that depends on a fold (`graph_directory`, `pack_members`) is
compared when every covered frame of the kinds it reads has the fold state `folded`. `graph_directory` reads `quads`,
`reifier_bindings`, `annotations` and `empty_graphs` frames; `pack_members` reads `pack` frames; an opaque covered frame of
another type does not stop the comparison.

DIGEST CLAIMS (`content_digest`, `statistics`) describe the segment's own fold. They are compared ONLY when the index is the
last ordinary frame of its segment, where the frames before it are the segment, and EVERY ordinary frame before it has the fold
state `folded` or `not_content`; they are then compared against the segment's own fold: its labelled set, labelled once. When
one of those frames has another fold state (`opaque`, whatever its reason) they
are not compared and no finding is made: the frames' own findings say why. For an index that is followed by an ordinary frame
they are not compared and no finding is made; they stay unverified (below). No labelling of a prefix is ever run, so a
reader never computes a digest or statistics for the frames before an index that is not last. When the reader's
own content digest is `absent` because a row, binding or entry of the fold is held (`P3`), which is a function of the bytes, the
claim `content_digest` is compared for presence: an `absent` claim agrees and a `present` claim is `index_digest`; `statistics`
is compared as always. When it is `absent` because the labelling refused, neither `content_digest` nor `statistics` is
compared: the reader reports
`labelling_refused` once for the segment (`P3`), and no finding of this section.

VERIFICATION IS PER CLAIM. Each of the claims `frame_count` with `head`, `type_runs`, `graph_directory`, `statistics`,
`range_root`, `content_digest` and `pack_members` of a checked index is VERIFIED when the reader compared it against the covered
frames, by the rules above, and it held. A claim is UNVERIFIED when it was not compared (the digest claims of an index that is
not the last ordinary frame; a covered frame of a kind that a fold-dependent claim reads that has a fold state other than
`folded`; the digest claims when the labelling refused), when its comparison gave a finding, and every claim of an index that
is not checked. An unverified claim answers nothing: no absence, no count, no directory entry and no digest is taken from it. A
reader that answers a question from an index (a lookup, a count, a directory) uses a claim only when that claim is verified,
and the claim speaks only of the covered frames of its index; for every frame after the index, and for every claim that is
unverified, the reader reads the frames, or reports the answer as UNKNOWN, which is never reported as zero, as absent or as
false. A claim whose comparison gave a finding is used for nothing, and a torn or damaged index is never used. A read with the
access
`sparse` is the one reading that reads frames that an unverified claim names: it verifies each frame it reads by itself and
states the claim as the index's (`P7`, "Reading by range"), and its answer is never an absence, a count of the content or a digest
of the file.
Among the indexes of a
segment, the claim of the last index in which that claim is verified answers for the frames that the index covers. A later
index never makes verified a claim that it does not state, and an earlier index's verified claim is not withdrawn by a later
index whose own claim is unverified. The answers that a verified claim gives are those that reading the covered frames gives, so
no result of the fold changes (P5-19).

**P5-22.** A reader MUST check every checked index of a segment by the table of 4.3, and MUST report `index_claim_failed` once for
each reason that applies, with the position of the index frame. It MUST check the structural claims wherever the index stands,
MUST compare `content_digest` and `statistics` only when the index
is the last ordinary frame of its segment and every ordinary frame before it has the fold state `folded` or `not_content`, and
MUST NOT label a prefix of a segment.

**P5-23.** A reader that uses an index MUST use a claim of it only when that claim is verified (4.3): the index is checked, the
claim was compared against the covered frames and held. It MUST NOT take an answer from a claim that was not compared or that
has a finding, MUST read the frames or report the answer as unknown instead, and MUST NOT report an unknown answer as zero,
absent or false; a read with the access `sparse` reads the frames that a claim names and answers as `P7`, "Reading by range",
states. Under the access `whole` or `forward` an index never changes a result (P5-19).

### 4.4 Worked example

The segment of 2.5 ends with `F4`, an `index`. `T` (ordinary position `0`) is the `terms` frame; `F1` (ordinary position `1`)
is a `quads` frame of three rows, in graphs default, default and the named graph of term id `7`; `F2` is the `commit`; `F3`
(ordinary position `3`) is a `quads` frame of two rows, both in graph `7`. Then:

## 5. The stored check

### 5.1 What it is

Every `stored_compressed` frame carries a `stored_check` of 16 bytes, the first 16 bytes of
`H_ctx(stored, E([codec]) followed by payload)`; a `stored_plain` frame carries none, because its `payload_digest` is over its
stored bytes (`P1`, "The frame envelope" and "The checks of every frame"). The check is computed over the stored bytes, so a
storage rewrite (a recompression) recomputes it and leaves the identifier unchanged.

It locates ACCIDENTAL damage of stored bytes without a codec. It is not authentication: a party that writes the stored bytes
writes the check too. When the stored check and the payload check disagree, both are reported and the payload check decides the
content (`P1`). Its length is fixed: one length for every frame and every file, a check of 128 bits against random damage.

**P5-24.** A writer MUST write a `stored_check` of 16 bytes on every `stored_compressed` frame and MUST recompute it whenever it
rewrites the stored bytes or the codec of a frame.

### 5.2 The scrub

A SCRUB checks every stored byte of a segment once, judging the frames in item order and reporting them in that order, with no
codec, no key and memory bounded by the state of one streaming hash per frame in progress:

1. Delimit the items (`P1`, "Framing: torn, damaged and undelimitable input") and check the link of every frame (`P1`).
2. For a `stored_compressed` frame, compute its stored check over the stored bytes and compare: the stored state is `matches`
   or `mismatch` (`stored_check_mismatch`).
3. For a `stored_plain` frame, compute `H_ctx(payload, payload)` over the stored bytes and compare it with `payload_digest`, and
   the length with `decoded_length`: this is the payload check of `P1`, which for a plain frame needs no codec.
4. Report each frame's link state, stored state and, for a plain frame, payload state, the frames in item order (ascending
   position).

The scrub checks every byte of the segment other than these, which no keyless check covers: the bytes of a signature record that
still parse as a signature record (a signature check with the key covers them, `P6`). Every other byte is covered: the header by
`h0` through the link of the first ordinary frame; the envelope fields of identity order by the link; the identifier by the link
and by the next frame's link; the storage form and the stored payload by the stored check (the codec through `E([codec])`) or by
the payload digest; the CBOR heads by delimitation and the grammar of `P1`; a parity frame by its own checks (6.6); the `offsets`
item and the locator by their claim checks.

**P5-25.** A scrub MUST check every frame of a segment by the four steps of 5.2 and MUST NOT sample frames or bytes.

## 6. Parity: one geometry

Parity detects, locates and repairs accidental damage to the stored bytes of a segment. It proves nothing about content or
authorship. The enumeration `parity_geometry` has one value, `geometry_1`; every constant of this section is a constant of that
geometry, and no frame states another.

### 6.1 The field

`GF(2^8)` is the field of polynomials over `GF(2)` modulo the FIELD POLYNOMIAL `x^8 + x^4 + x^3 + x^2 + 1` (`0x11D`). An element
is a byte; bit `t` is the coefficient of `x^t`.

- ADDITION is `XOR`; every element is its own negative, so subtraction is `XOR` too.
- THE TABLES. The GENERATOR is `2` (the polynomial `x`), which is primitive for `0x11D`. `exp[0] = 1`; `exp[t + 1]` is
  `exp[t]` shifted left by one bit, `XOR` `0x11D` when the shifted value is `256` or more; for `t` from `0` to `254`,
  `log[exp[t]] = t`. `exp[0 .. 11]` is `01 02 04 08 10 20 40 80 1d 3a 74 e8`. `log[0]` is not defined.
- MULTIPLICATION. `a * b = 0` when `a` or `b` is `0`; otherwise `a * b = exp[(log[a] + log[b]) mod 255]`.
- INVERSE. For `a` not `0`, `a^-1 = exp[(255 - log[a]) mod 255]`; `0` has no inverse.

Worked arithmetic. `02 * 80`: `0x80` shifted left is `0x100`, and `0x100 XOR 0x11D = 0x1D`, so `02 * 80 = 1d` (`exp[8]`).
`03 * 0e`: `03 = 02 XOR 01`, so the product is `(02 * 0e) XOR 0e = 1c XOR 0e = 12`. `ff * fd`: the table gives `log[ff] = 175`
and `log[fd] = 80`; `175 + 80 = 255`, `255 mod 255 = 0` and `exp[0] = 01`, so `fd` is the inverse of `ff`.

### 6.2 The coding matrix

`geometry_1` has `k = 64` data shards and `m = 4` parity shards to a stripe. The CAUCHY MATRIX `C` has `m` rows and `k` columns:

```text
x_i = 255 - i           for i from 0 to 3     (ff, fe, fd, fc)
y_j = j                 for j from 0 to 63
C[i][j] = (x_i XOR y_j)^-1
```

Every `x_i` is at least `252` and every `y_j` at most `63`, so `x_i XOR y_j` is never `0` and every entry is defined; the `x_i`
are distinct, the `y_j` are distinct and no `x_i` equals a `y_j`, so `C` is a Cauchy matrix and every square submatrix of it is
invertible. That is the property repair needs: any `m` of the `k + m` shards of a stripe can be lost and solved for. Row `0`
begins `fd 7e ff 7f` and row `3` begins `7f ff 7e fd` (for example `C[3][1] = (fc XOR 01)^-1 = fd^-1 = ff`).

The code is SYSTEMATIC: the data shards are the stored bytes themselves, and only the parity shards are new.

### 6.3 The stream and the spans

THE STREAM `S` of a segment is the concatenation, in item order, of the bytes of every item of the segment other than its
transparent frames, its `offsets` item and its locator: the opener, every ordinary frame and every opaque item. In a segment
whose items are all well formed, `S` runs from the first byte of the opener to the last byte of the last ordinary frame (the
`index` frame of a canonical file). A torn last item is not an item and is not in `S`. Offsets in `S` count from `0` at the
first byte of the opener. The S-POSITION of an item is the number of bytes of `S` that stand before it.

WHEN A RESYNC ITEM IS ACCEPTED INSIDE THE COMPLETE ITEM `F` THAT PRECEDES THE LOSS OF FRAMING, the stream of the previous segment
holds the whole of `F` and runs to the end of `F`, although the accepted item begins inside `F` (`P1`, "Finding a resync item
where framing is lost"); the spans tile that stream, and a parity span that would need bytes after the end of `F` is not reached:
its bytes of `S` are in the unprotected tail of the
segment (6.7), the span has no parity frame to be judged by, and no repair of it is made.

THE SPAN LENGTH is `4194304` bytes. Spans tile `S` from offset `0`: span `t` is the run `[first, end)` of `S` with
`first = t * 4194304`. A FULL span has `end = first + 4194304`. The FINAL span of a segment that has its closing index (PLACEMENT)
is SHORT
when the length of `S` up to the end of the closing index is not a multiple of the span length: it ends at the end of the closing
index.

PLACEMENT. The parity frame of a span with end `e` stands immediately after the first non-transparent item whose end in `S`
(its S-position plus its length) is at least `e`; when one item ends at or after the ends of several spans, their parity frames
follow it in span order. The CLOSING INDEX of a segment is its last `index` frame in item order (`P1`, "The locator"). The FINAL
span of a segment with a
closing index is the span of the tiling that holds the last byte of the closing index, with `end` at the end of the closing index.
Its parity frame, full or short, stands after the closing index, after the parity frames of any other spans that the closing index
reaches; a segment in which no such frame stands there has no parity of its final span. No span begins after a short span. The
OPEN span of a segment being written is the span of
the tiling that holds the end of `S`. Items written
after the parity frame of a short span are in the unprotected tail (6.7).

APPEND never changes existing parity: a span is closed when its parity frame is written, and a parity frame is never edited. A
STORAGE REWRITE that changes the bytes of a covered item changes the bytes, or the length, of `S` there, and the parity of every
span from that point to the end of the segment is then STALE; a tool that rewrites storage drops those parity frames and writes
them again over the new `S`, and a file in which it did not is reported `parity_stale` (6.6). Inserting or removing a parity
frame changes no identifier, no root and no signature, because a transparent frame is never a predecessor or a leaf.

**P5-26.** A writer of parity MUST write one parity frame for every full span of `S` that it reaches while it writes, placed by
the
rule of 6.3, immediately after the item that reaches the end of the span and before it writes another non-transparent item, as the
segment written at once has it, and, when it writes the closing `index` frame of the segment (`P1`, "Growing a file", CLOSING A
SEGMENT), the parity frame of the final span after it, placed by the same rule.

**P5-27.** A writer MUST NOT change or remove an existing parity frame when it appends, and a tool that rewrites stored bytes
MUST drop every parity frame from the first span whose bytes changed to the end of the segment and write them again over the
new `S`.

**P5-28.** A writer that is not writing a canonical file MAY write no parity frame in a segment; a segment then holds none. Its
stated value: a live writer saves the cost of 6.8. A canonical file carries parity (`P7`), and a profile can require it.

### 6.4 Shards, stripes and parity shards

For a span `[first, end)` with `n = end - first`:

0. THE SHARD LENGTH `z` of the span is a function of `n` alone: `z = 4096` when `n >= 262144` (the span holds at least `64`
   full shards), and otherwise `z` is the smallest power of two that is at least `64` and at least `ceil(n / 64)`. So `z` is
   `4096` for every span of `262144` bytes or more, a full span included, and for a span of fewer bytes it lies between `64` and
   `4096` (it is `4096` for `n` from `131073` to `262143`), and the span holds at most `64` data shards. A reader computes `z`
   from `first` and `end`; no frame states it. A reader or writer that meets the bytes of a span before its end is known (a
   forward reader, a one-pass writer) does not know `z` while the span holds fewer than `262144` bytes: it holds those bytes, at
   most `262143`, and computes the shard digests with the `z` of the span once its end is known, or with `z = 4096` once the span
   holds `262144` bytes, from which point it accumulates shards of `4096` bytes (`P7`, "Reading as a stream", THE MEMORY OF A
   FORWARD READER). A writer that closes the segment with the parity of its final span holds the bytes of a span until its end
   is known, since its
   parity depends on `D` as well (6.8).
1. The DATA SHARDS: `N = ceil(n / z)`. Data shard `g`, for `g` from `0` to `N - 1`, is the bytes `[first + z * g,
   min(first + z * (g + 1), end))` of `S`; the last is padded with zero bytes to `z` bytes. Every data shard is `z`
   bytes.
2. The STRIPES: the DEPTH is `D = ceil(N / 64)`; it is `1` for every span of fewer than `262144` bytes. Data shard `g` belongs to
   stripe `s = g mod D` at column `j = g div D`. A stripe
   holds at most `64` data shards, `D` apart in the span, so a run of consecutive damaged shards touches each stripe as seldom as
   possible. The span has `D` stripes. A column `j` with `s + j * D >= N` holds no shard and counts as a shard of zero bytes.
3. The PARITY SHARDS: stripe `s` has the `m = 4` parity shards `P[s][i]`, `i` from `0` to `3`, each of `z` bytes:
   `P[s][i][b] = XOR over j from 0 to 63 of C[i][j] * d(s + j * D)[b]`, byte by byte, where `d(g)` is data shard `g` (zero for a
   column with no shard). Parity shard `P[s][i]` has the number `p = 4 * s + i`, from `0` to `4 * D - 1`.
4. The SHARD DIGEST of a shard (data, padded, or parity) is the first 16 bytes of `H_ctx(shard, the z bytes of the shard)`.

A full span has `z = 4096`, `N = 1024`, `D = 16`, `64` parity shards and `262144` parity bytes. A span of `n` bytes with
`262144 <= n` has `z = 4096` and `D = ceil(ceil(n / 4096) / 64)`. A span of `n < 262144` bytes has `D = 1`, `4` parity shards
and `4 * z` parity bytes: `4` parity shards to `N <= 64` data shards, so the parity of such a span is `4 / N` of the length of
its data shards, `4 / 64` when `N = 64`, and its table holds `16 * (N + 4)` digest bytes. The cost of such a span is `4 * z`
parity bytes and `16 * (N + 4)` digest bytes: for `n = 4096`, `z = 64`, `N = 64`: `256 + 1088 = 1344` bytes; for `n = 65536`,
`z = 1024`, `N = 64`: `4096 + 1088 = 5184` bytes; for `n = 262143`, `z = 4096` (`ceil(262143 / 64) = 4096`), `N = 64`:
`16384 + 1088 = 17472` bytes. The guarantee of 6.9 is per stripe and is stated in shards of `z` bytes.

DECODING A STRIPE. Let stripe `s` have its `n_s` data shards (the columns `j` with `s + j * D < N`) and its `4` parity shards, of
which a set `E` is UNKNOWN (to be solved for; the bytes of an unknown shard as read are not used), `|E| <= 4`: `e_d` unknown
data columns and `e_p` unknown parity rows. The unknown data shards are the explained and the unexplained failing data shards
of 6.6; the unknown parity shards are the failing parity shards. Take as `I`
the first `e_d` rows `i`, in ascending order, whose parity shard is not unknown. For each byte offset `b`, solve the `e_d` by
`e_d` system `XOR over unknown j of C[i][j] * u_j = P[s][i][b] XOR (XOR over the other j of C[i][j] * d(s + j * D)[b])`, for `i`
in `I`, for the unknown bytes `u_j`. The matrix of the system is a square submatrix of `C`, so the solution exists and is unique,
and any method of solving the system of the rows `I` gives the same bytes. Then compute each unknown parity shard by step 3. The
solved
bytes of an unexplained data shard are never written (7.2, step 5).

**P5-29.** A writer MUST compute the data shards, stripes, parity shards and shard digests of every span by the four steps of 6.4,
over the field of 6.1 and the matrix of 6.2, and a reader MUST solve a stripe by the decoding of 6.4.

### 6.5 The `parity` frame

A `parity` frame is not critical, is transparent and is `stored_plain`; it is never in a pack, never sealed, and carries no
signature. Its content and payload:

```text
parity.content = [ geometry: enum(parity_geometry), first: uint, end: uint, anchor: digest, table_digest: digest ]
parity_payload = [ data_shard_digests: list(bytes), parity_shard_digests: list(bytes), parity: bytes ]
```

| field | value |
|---|---|
| `geometry` | `geometry_1` |
| `first`, `end` | the span, as offsets in `S` |
| `anchor` | the frame's predecessor: the effective identifier of the last ordinary frame before it, or `h0` when there is none |
| `table_digest` | `H_ctx(parity_table, E([h0, data_shard_digests, parity_shard_digests]))`, with the `h0` of this segment |
| `data_shard_digests` | the `N` shard digests of the data shards (`z` of 6.4), `16` bytes each, in the order of `g` |
| `parity_shard_digests` | the `4 * D` shard digests of the parity shards, in the order of `p` |
| `parity` | the `4 * D` parity shards concatenated in the order of `p`: `4 * D * z` bytes |

`anchor` equals the predecessor, so the link of a parity frame is checked from the frame alone, with `anchor` as the
predecessor: that is what makes a parity frame a place to resume reading after damage (section 7). `table_digest` binds the
frame to its segment through `h0`: a parity frame of a segment, or of a file stored inside a blob, whose opener differs from this
segment's opener does not verify under this segment's `h0`; one whose opener is byte-equal to it has the same `h0` and verifies,
and the frames read after it are kept from the terms of this segment by `P3`, "Unreadable terms", THE DICTIONARY IN DOUBT. The
shard digests are not bound to an offset; their place in the table names them.

A full span's payload is `E(parity_payload)`: the array head `83`; the list head `99 04 00` and `1024` entries of `50` and 16
bytes; the list head `98 40` and `64` such entries; the head `5a 00 04 00 00` and `262144` bytes. That is
`1 + 3 + 17408 + 2 + 1088 + 5 + 262144 = 280651` bytes, `6.69` per cent of the span, and the envelope adds under `200` bytes.
The payload of a span of fewer than `262144` bytes has `D = 1`: the array head `83`; the list of the `N` data digests (the
head `80 + N` for `N` below `24`, `98 N` for `N` from `24` to `255`, then `17` bytes an entry); the list of `4` parity digests
(`84` and `68` bytes); and the head `59` with two length bytes and the `4 * z` parity bytes, `4 * z` being between `256` and
`16384`. For `n = 4096` (`z = 64`, `N = 64`) it is `1 + 2 + 1088 + 1 + 68 + 3 + 256 = 1419` bytes; for `n = 65536` (`z = 1024`,
`N = 64`) it is `1 + 2 + 1088 + 1 + 68 + 3 + 4096 = 5259` bytes; for `n = 262143` (`z = 4096`, `N = 64`) it is
`1 + 2 + 1088 + 1 + 68 + 3 + 16384 = 17547` bytes; for `n = 1` (`z = 64`, `N = 1`) it is `1 + 1 + 17 + 1 + 68 + 3 + 256 = 347`
bytes. The largest payload of any span is the `280651` bytes of a full span.

**P5-42.** A writer and a reader MUST take the shard length `z` of a span from `n = end - first` alone, by 6.4 step 0: `4096`
when `n` is at least `262144`, otherwise the smallest power of two that is at least `64` and at least `ceil(n / 64)`, and MUST
compute `N`, the data shards, the parity shards, the shard digests and the payload length of the parity frame with that `z`.

**P5-30.** A writer MUST write a parity frame with `critical` `false`, `transparent` `true`, `stored_plain`, no signature, and the
content and payload of the table of 6.5 for its span.

### 6.6 Checking a parity frame

A frame IS OF TYPE `parity` when it passes the envelope test and its `frame_type` element is well typed, in the
deterministic form, with the value `parity` (`P1`, "The record, in identity order"). An item whose `frame_type` is ill
typed has no known type: the general field rule of `P1` applies to it, and this section does not. The type is identified
first, and what follows comes from the type.

WHAT A LATER VERSION CAN CHANGE IN A PARITY FRAME, AND WHAT IT CANNOT. A reader that holds the type `parity` holds its columns
and its envelope rules: `critical` `false`, `transparent` `true`, `stored_plain`, no signature. A later registry version adds
values and never changes the columns or the envelope rules of a type that an earlier version defines. So in a segment of ANY
registry version an envelope field of a frame of type `parity` that is not as the type states (`critical` `true`, a `storage`
other than `stored_plain`, a codec or a storage tag the reader does not hold included, a signature) is damage, `parity_damaged`
(condition 2 below); it is never `unsupported_critical` and never `unknown_codec`, and a parity frame never stops a fold. A later
version never changes the SHAPE of the content record of a type that an earlier version defines (the number and the types of its
elements); it adds enumeration values. So the content record of a frame of type `parity` is judged for its shape first, in a
segment of any version: a record that is longer, shorter or ill typed is damage, whatever values it holds. The later-version
case is ONLY this: the envelope is as the type states, the content record has the shape the reader holds, the link state of
the frame is not `broken` (the content of a `broken` frame is not interpreted), the segment's `registry_version` is above the
reader's, and an enumeration value in the record is one the reader does not hold (a `geometry`). That frame is a frame of
unknown type by
the row for an enumeration inside a content record (`P1`, "Values the reader does not know"): opaque, with
`unknown_enumeration_value` and reason `unknown_frame_type`, not used for repair, and the fold continues. Being transparent by its
envelope field it enters neither `integrity_state` nor the chain. In a segment of the reader's own version an unknown value is
damage too. A stop of the fold is no condition of this paragraph. A frame handled by this paragraph is
not judged by the rest of this section. The payload state of the frame is no condition of this paragraph.

A reader judges every frame of type `parity` of a segment, for which the paragraph above does not apply, in item order, in three
stages. What is transparent is decided by the envelope field alone (section 0). For such a frame the fold state is always
`not_content`, and a transparent frame, of any type, never enters `integrity_state` or the chain.

STAGE 1, THE FRAME ITSELF. The frame is DAMAGED when any of these holds. Conditions 1, 2, 3, 6 and 7 need no payload and are
judged for every frame of type `parity`, above the reader's capacity or not (conditions 4 and 5 are judged only when the payload
check ran: the table digest is a hash over two lists of the payload); a frame that any of them makes damaged gives
`parity_damaged` and nothing else of the wire part (the capacity report is one of the findings it replaces), and is not used:

1. its `content` is outside the record of 6.5, `geometry` is not `geometry_1`, `first` is not a multiple of `4194304`, or
   `end - first` is not between `1` and `4194304`;
2. its `critical`, `transparent` or `storage` is not as 6.5 states, or its `signatures` list is not empty (a transparent
   frame carries no signature);
3. its link state is not `linked`, or `anchor` differs from its predecessor;
4. its payload check (`P1`) ran and its payload state is `damaged`, or, when the payload check ran, its stored payload is not one
   deterministic item of `parity_payload` with `N` and `4 * D` digests of 16 bytes and `parity` of `4 * D * z` bytes for its
   span (`N`, `D` and `z` of 6.4 for `end - first`). A frame whose payload state is `unchecked` with `exceeds_capacity`, above
   capacity and with no other damage (none of
   conditions 1, 2, 3, 6 and 7 holds), is NOT damaged, whatever its `table_digest` holds, since the payload was not read: it gives
   the finding `exceeds_capacity` of `P1` and no `parity_damaged`, it is a frame that this section judges (the segment is not, for
   the tail, a segment without parity), and it is not used, so it protects nothing for this reader;
5. its `table_digest` differs from `H_ctx(parity_table, E([h0, data_shard_digests, parity_shard_digests]))` under this
   segment's `h0`;
6. an earlier frame of type `parity` of the segment, in item order, that this section judges and whatever its own state
   (damaged, above capacity or used), has the same `first`; a frame whose content record is ill typed, or whose link state is
   `broken`, has no `first` and matches no span, while a frame whose content record is well typed, whose link state is not
   `broken` and whose `first` is out of range (not a multiple of the span) has a `first` and matches, a `geometry` of no
   registry version in a segment of the reader's own version included (damage by condition 1); a frame handled by the
   paragraph on a later version is not judged by this section, so it has no `first` and matches no span, whatever it holds;
7. any other field rule of the envelope fails once the type is known, a second-form `identifier` head or `signatures` head
   included, and any other failure that `P1` reports for the frame and that conditions 1 to 6 do not list (the capacity report
   is decided by condition 4 and is not among them).

A damaged frame gives exactly one `parity_damaged`, with its position. That finding replaces, for this frame, every finding of
`P1`, so that damage to the repair layer never voids what it protects. It does not replace the findings of
this part about the use of a frame (`parity_mismatch`, `parity_stale`, `parity_unexplained`, the repair outcome and
`unprotected_tail`): a frame whose payload check fails and whose table holds is damaged AND used, and gives `parity_damaged`
together with what stage 3 finds. Transparent frames do not enter `integrity_state`: it is a function of the ordinary frames only.
The frame is USED when none of 1, 2, 3, 5, 6 and 7 holds, its payload state is not `unchecked`, and its payload decodes into the
grammar of 6.5: a payload whose parity bytes are damaged and whose table holds is used, and its failing parity shards are
erasures.

STAGE 2, THE PLACEMENT. For a used frame with S-position `u`, the last non-transparent item before it is the item that ends at
`u`. The placement holds when `end <= u`, that item begins before `end` in `S`, and, for a short span (`end - first` below
`4194304`), `end = u`. Otherwise the span's bytes are not where its parity frame says, which a storage rewrite causes: the finding
is `parity_stale` with its position, and the frame is no longer used.

STAGE 3, THE SHARDS. For a used frame, compute the digest of every data shard from the bytes of `S` as read, and of every parity
shard from `parity`, and compare each with its entry in the table. Let `F_d` be the failing data shards and `F_p` the failing
parity shards. When `F_d` is not empty the finding is `parity_mismatch` with reason `data` and `count` `|F_d|`; when `F_p` is not
empty, `parity_mismatch` with reason `parity` and `count` `|F_p|`; both carry the frame's position.

PASSING ITEMS. Recovery consults no signature and no key: it is a function of the bytes alone, so two readers with different
trust inputs recover the same file. An item of `S` PASSES when every check of `P1` that covers its bytes ran and held: the
opener, when the first ordinary frame of the segment exists and its link state is `linked` or `healed`; an ordinary frame, when
its link state is `linked`, its stored state is `matches` (for `stored_compressed`) or its payload state is `verified` (for
`stored_plain`). An opaque item, a frame that fails one of these, the opener of a segment with no ordinary frame, and bytes that
could not be delimited do not pass.

A failing data shard is EXPLAINED when it overlaps at least one byte of an item that does not pass, and UNEXPLAINED
when every byte of it lies in items that pass. A shard that fails while every item it overlaps passes is
unexplained; in solving a stripe it is an unknown like an erasure (6.4), and its solved bytes are never written. Then:

- when `F_d` is not empty and every shard of it is unexplained, the table describes other bytes than the span holds: the finding
  is `parity_stale`, and nothing of the span is repaired;
- otherwise, when some shards of `F_d` are unexplained, the finding is `parity_unexplained` with `count` their number; they are
  never written.

**P5-31.** A reader MUST judge every parity frame by the three stages of 6.6, MUST report `parity_damaged` for a damaged frame in
place of every finding of `P1` for that frame, the capacity report included, and MUST use only a frame that 6.6 calls used.

**P5-32.** A reader MUST report `parity_stale` for a used frame whose placement fails, or whose failing data shards are all
unexplained, and `parity_unexplained` with the count of the unexplained shards in every other span that has any; a failing
shard that parity cannot explain is `parity_unexplained` and its solved bytes are never written. A reader MUST NOT consult a
signature or a key in this classification or anywhere in recovery.

**P5-33.** A reader MUST report `parity_mismatch` with reason `data`, and with reason `parity`, each with the count of failing
shards of that kind, for every used parity frame whose span has failing shards of that kind.

### 6.7 The unprotected tail

A frame handled by the paragraph on a later version in 6.6 is not judged by that section and does not count as a parity
frame here: the tail is computed from the frames that 6.6 judges, and a segment in which it judges none is, for the tail,
a segment without parity. In a segment that holds at least one frame that 6.6 judges, let `e*` be the largest `end` of
a used parity frame whose span is not stale, or `0` when there is none. THE UNPROTECTED TAIL is the bytes of `S` from
`max(e*, the length of the opener)` to the end of `S`: the bytes after the largest end of a used span that is not stale; with
no used span, `e*` is `0` and the tail runs from the end of the opener. A span BEFORE that end whose parity frame is damaged or
stale is reported by the findings of that frame. A span before that end whose parity frame is MISSING is reported by nothing of
this part; a profile can require parity frames and then reports it. A span before that end whose frame is not used because it is
above capacity, or because it is handled by the later-version case of 6.6, is likewise reported by nothing of this part beyond
that frame's own finding. When the tail holds at least one byte, the finding is `unprotected_tail`, once for the segment, with
`offset` the input offset of its first byte (when that byte is the first byte of an item that follows a parity frame, the offset
lies after the parity frame) and `length` its number of bytes of `S`. The `offsets` item and the locator are not
in `S` and are never in the tail. A segment with no parity frame reports no tail: it makes no claim of parity. A canonical file,
whose final span ends at the end of `S`, has none while its parity frame is used.

**P5-34.** A reader MUST report `unprotected_tail` exactly when 6.7 gives a tail of at least one byte, once for the segment, with
the offset and length of 6.7.

### 6.8 Cost

The parity payload of a full span is `280651` bytes for `4194304` bytes of span, 6.69 per cent. A span of fewer than `262144`
bytes restores at most `4 * z` bytes (6.9).

A span that reaches `4194304` bytes has `z = 4096`, `N = 1024` and `D = 16`, known in advance. A one-pass writer can keep, from
the first byte of a span, `4 * 16` accumulators of `z = 4096` bytes and one shard in progress, `266240` bytes; on completing data
shard `g` it computes its digest and adds `C[i][g div 16] * shard` into accumulator `(g mod 16, i)` for each `i`, and when the
span reaches `4194304` bytes the accumulators are its parity. They serve full spans only: the depth `D` of a span that ends short
is known only at its end, and below `262144` bytes its `z` too, and its shards fall in other stripes and columns. A writer
that closes the segment with the parity of its final span therefore holds the bytes of the open span, at most `4194304`, and
encodes the final
span from those bytes when it writes the closing index; a writer that holds them pays up to `4194304` bytes of memory where the
accumulators of a full span cost `266240`. It holds each finished parity frame until the item that reaches its span's end is
written, so behind an item of
`f` stored bytes it holds at most `ceil(f / 4194304)` finished frames.

### 6.9 What parity guarantees, and what it does not

GUARANTEED, for a used parity frame and with its digests as the judge: a span in which every stripe has at most `4` unknown
shards, and which step 5 of 7.2 accepts, is restored exactly (the explained failing data shards and the failing parity shards are
written); the unknowns are
the explained and the unexplained failing data shards and the failing parity shards. Because a stripe takes
every `D`-th shard, a run of up to `4 * D` consecutive damaged shards (`64` shards, `262144` bytes, in a full span; `4` shards,
`4 * z` bytes, in a span of fewer than `262144` bytes, where `D = 1`) touches no stripe more than `4` times and is restored.
A span of fewer than `262144` bytes is one stripe of at most `64` shards of `z` bytes (6.4 step 0): any `4` of its shards are
restored: a run of at most `3 * z + 1` damaged bytes is restored wherever it falls in the span, a longer run is restored exactly
when it touches at most `4` shards, and a run that touches `5` shards is not. A change of any byte of `S` changes the digest of
its shard except with probability
about `2^-128` for damage that is not chosen against the digest.

NOT GUARANTEED. Parity is not authentication: a party that can write the file can write a parity frame. It cannot make a repair
accepted unless the repaired frames pass their link, stored and payload checks (section 7), so a forged or stale parity frame can
waste a reader's work, bounded by one span, and cannot make a frame read as one the chain does not name. Not repaired: more than
`4` unknowns in a stripe. Parity written over bytes that are already damaged protects
those bytes as they stand: when a frame among them is damaged, the parity frame carries the digests
of the damaged bytes, the frame's `damaged_frame` stands, and parity repairs nothing of it.

LIMIT, A DAMAGED OPENER. An opener whose bytes changed and that the reader still reads gives another `h0`. The link of the first
ordinary frame fails, because its identifier was computed over the old `h0`, and so does every `table_digest`, which is computed
over `h0`; the link of every later ordinary frame still holds, because it is checked against the identifier that its predecessor
stores (`P1`, "Healing"). No parity frame is used. Consequence: parity alone does not repair an opener; the items of the segment
are still delimited and counted, the first ordinary frame is reported `broken`, no span is repaired, and every parity frame is
`parity_damaged`. An opener that the reader cannot read gives no `h0` and no link is checked (`P1`, "The header").

LIMIT, A PARITY FRAME THAT NO LONGER PASSES THE ENVELOPE TEST. It becomes an opaque item of `S` and is counted in it. Consequence:
`S` is longer by the bytes of that item, so the bytes of every later span are not the bytes that its table describes. Each
later parity frame is judged by 6.6 as it stands: `parity_stale` when its placement fails or every failing data shard of its
span is unexplained, `parity_unexplained` otherwise; and a repair is accepted only by step 5 of 7.2.

LIMIT, A RESUMPTION POINT. Reading resumes only at a parity frame whose `anchor` gives it a link that holds and whose
`table_digest` holds under the `h0` of the segment being recovered. A parity frame of another segment, or of a file stored in a
blob, whose opener differs from this segment's opener does not hold under this `h0` and is never a resumption point. A party that
can write a byte-identical opener inside a blob that lies in the damaged region can write a parity frame that holds under this
`h0`, and a later segment of the same file whose opener is byte-equal to this one holds such parity frames. Consequence, exactly:
reading resumes at that frame, and the frames after it are read with their own checks, so a frame is
accepted only when its link holds against the `anchor` it names. That party cannot make a frame pass as one the chain of this
segment names, cannot change any identifier, head, root or signature, and cannot make a repair accepted (step 5 of 7.2), but it
can make the reader resume inside the blob, so that frames of the nested file are read as the remainder of this segment: their
`commit` frames then give `commit_count_mismatch` or `commit_root_mismatch`, and no frame after the point is authenticated by the
resumption (`P6`). Reading also resumes at a resync item (`P1`, "Finding a resync item where framing is lost"), which opens a new
segment; it is found by its claim of place and its identifier, not by this segment's `h0`.

### 6.10 Worked examples

A TOY GEOMETRY, for the arithmetic only. It is NOT `geometry_1`: `k = 3`, `m = 2`, shards of 4 bytes, the same field and the
same construction of `C` (`x_0 = ff`, `x_1 = fe`, `y_j = j`). The rows are `C[0] = (ff^-1, fe^-1, fd^-1) = (fd, 7e, ff)` and
`C[1] = (fe^-1, ff^-1, fc^-1) = (7e, fd, 7f)`. With `d0 = 11 22 33 44`, `d1 = 55 66 77 88`, `d2 = 99 aa bb cc`, the parity
shards are `P0 = 35 19 f6 67` and `P1 = 4b df 58 2e`. Byte `0` of `P0` by hand: `fd * 11 = 96`, `7e * 55 = de`,
`ff * 99 = 7d`, and `96 XOR de XOR 7d = 48 XOR 7d = 35`. Erasing `d0` and `d2` leaves the two-by-two system of rows `0` and `1`
over columns `0` and `2`, a Cauchy submatrix, whose unique solution is `11 22 33 44` and `99 aa bb cc`.

SPAN PLACEMENT. A segment's items, by length in bytes: the header `11`; `F1` `3000000`; `F2` `2000000`; `F3` `5000000`; `F4`, its
closing `index`, `300`. `S` is `10000311` bytes. `F2` ends at `5000011`, the first end at or after `4194304`: the parity frame
of span `[0, 4194304)` follows `F2`. `F3` ends at `10000011`, at or after `8388608`: the parity frame of `[4194304, 8388608)`
follows `F3`, and it stands at S-position `10000011`, since the first parity frame is not in `S`. The final span
`[8388608, 10000311)` is short and its parity frame follows `F4`. Its `n` is `1611703`: `N = 394` (the last shard holds `1975`
bytes and `2121` bytes of padding), `D = ceil(394 / 64) = 7`, stripes `0` and `1` hold `57` data shards and stripes `2` to `6`
hold `56`, and there are `28` parity shards (`114688` bytes). Had `F1` been rewritten to `2999999` bytes, every span's bytes
shift by one: the first parity frame still follows `F2`, but its shards no longer match while every item checks, so it is
`parity_stale`, and the later ones fail their placement or their shards the same way.

## 7. The one recovery procedure

### 7.1 Input, output and the rule of one behaviour

Input: the bytes of a file and the reader's capacity (`P7`). Output: the typed result of reading those bytes (`P7`), which holds
the findings of this part; and, when a span is repaired, the REPAIRED COPY: the input with the bytes of every accepted repair
written in place. The result reports the input as it is: a repair does not change the fold, the states or the findings of the
input, it adds the finding `repaired`. The repaired copy is a new file; read again, it gives the result of the input with the
accepted spans as their parity writer covered them. The result is the same whether or not a reader emits the copy's bytes.

There is one procedure, with no mode and no option: every step runs, in the order of 7.2 (steps 1, 2 and 6 until the
segment is read to its last item, then steps 3, 4 and 5 once, then step 7), for every segment, the segments taken in file
order, except as the reader's `access` states (`P7`, "Access: the steps by access": under `forward` steps 4, 5 and 6 are not
run and step 3 runs at each parity frame, so a forward reader resumes at no parity frame after a torn or damaged region, and
resumes only at a resync item (`P1`, "Finding a
resync item where framing is lost"); a read with the access
`sparse` runs none of these steps, `P7`, "Reading by range"), and two readers of one
registry version, one capacity and one access give the same result and the same repaired copy from the same bytes. A completed
repair is the
finding `repaired`; a failed one is `repair_failed`. Recovery never changes an identifier, a head or a root, and a `signatures`
list that it
writes is the one that the parity writer covered: a repaired byte is a byte a writer wrote, restored and confirmed by the checks
of step 5.

### 7.2 The steps

1. DELIMIT. Read the items of the segment forward by `P1` ("Framing: torn, damaged and undelimitable input"). Where the input
   ends inside an item, `torn_tail`; where no next item can be delimited, `undelimitable_input`, and the bytes from that offset
   form the DAMAGED REGION of the segment. Both findings are final only after step 6: see 7.2A.
2. CHECK. For every item before the damaged region: classify it, apply the envelope test, and run the link check with healing, the
   stored check and the payload check (`P1`, "The checks of every frame"); judge every `commit` (2.2) and every `index` (4.3),
   each in item order.
3. LOCATE THE SHARDS. Judge every parity frame by 6.6: the frames used, the stale spans, and in every used span that is not
   stale the failing data shards, explained or not, and the failing parity shards.
4. ELIGIBILITY. In a used span that is not stale, the UNKNOWNS of a stripe are its explained failing data shards, its
   unexplained failing data shards and its failing parity shards; the bytes of an unexplained shard as read are not used in
   solving, and its solved bytes are never written. A span with no explained failing data shard and no failing parity shard needs
   no repair. A span in which a stripe has more than `4` unknowns is `repair_failed` with reason `too_many_erasures` and the
   position of its parity frame, and nothing of it is repaired.
5. REPAIR, THEN RE-CHECK.
   1. For every other span with a shard to write, solve each stripe by the decoding of 6.4 and write the solved explained data
      shards and parity shards into a working copy of the input; the solved bytes of an unexplained shard are not written.
   2. THE TOUCHED ITEMS are the items of the segment as steps 1 and 6 delimit them in the input, the damaged region counting
      as one item, that hold a byte of a solved and written data shard of some span. Damage moves the ends of items: a frame
      whose tail is overwritten is delimited short, and the bytes after it are delimited as items that are not the frames they
      were (a range of zero bytes is delimited as one item for each byte). The working copy is therefore delimited again, in
      RUNS, found in ascending offset. A run BEGINS at the first byte of the first touched item whose first byte no earlier run
      holds. From there the bytes of the working copy are WALKED as a sequence of items, each delimited by `P1` from the byte
      after the item before it. The run ENDS at the first offset that the walk reaches, after at least one item, that is the
      first byte of an item of the input, or the end of the last item of the segment; the run holds the bytes from its
      beginning to its end. A run CHECKS when the walk reaches the end of the run and every item walked passes the envelope
      test (or is the header) and checks: its link state is `linked` with no healing, the predecessor of the first frame being
      the effective identifier of the ordinary frame before the run, and that of each later frame the effective identifier of
      the ordinary frame before it in the walk, or, when the walk holds none before it, of the ordinary frame before the run; its
      stored state `matches` when it is `stored_compressed`; and its payload
      state `verified`, or, when the reader's capacity leaves the payload `unchecked`, its stored state `matches`. The header of
      the
      segment, when it is a walked item, checks when its bytes in the working copy are its bytes in the input, and it is not
      a frame: the first ordinary frame after it is judged as every walked frame is, its predecessor being `h0`. The conditions of
      one walked item are
      judged in this order: the envelope test, the link, the stored check, the payload check; the first that fails ends the
      walk, and a payload check that is not reached is not run and not charged. THE ORDINARY FRAME BEFORE A RUN is the last
      ordinary frame that
      ends at or before the first byte of the run, taken among the items walked by the earlier runs that check and the items
      of the input that no run holds; a run that does not check gives no frame. The effective
      identifier of a frame of an earlier walk is the identifier it stores; that of an item of the input that no run holds
      is the one that step 2 gave it in the read of the input, and it is not judged again against the working copy. When no
      ordinary frame stands before the run, the predecessor of its first frame is `h0` (`P1`), and no run gives that
      predecessor. The items of the input are every item that
      steps 1 and 6 delimit in the segment: the header, frames of every type, items that are not frames (each zero byte of a
      zeroed range is one), the `offsets` item, the locator and a damaged region. A run does
      NOT check when an item of its walk does not delimit, extends beyond the last item of the segment, or fails one of the
      conditions above; its walk stops at the first such item, and the run then holds the bytes from its beginning to the
      last byte that the walk examined: the last byte of that item when it delimits within the items of the segment; else the
      last byte of the last head that its delimiting read, the heads being read in the order of their bytes until the
      delimiting fails (`P1`, "Framing: torn, damaged and undelimitable input"), or a `bytes` or `text` head has a length
      that passes the last item of the segment, or the last item of the segment ends; the head at which it fails counts as
      read; extended to the end of
      the item of the input that holds that byte. A touched item whose first byte such a run holds begins no run, so no byte
      of the working copy is examined by two walks. A run of an input whose damage moved no end
      of an item is one touched item, walked alone, when every solved shard written into it matches its digest. A span TOUCHES a
      run that holds a byte of a solved and written data shard
      of the span, and DEPENDS on the runs it touches and, for each of them, on the run whose walk gives the ordinary frame before
      it, if
      a run gives it, and on every run that does not check and holds a byte between that frame, or the first byte of the header
      when no ordinary
      frame stands before the run (the run of the header included), and the run.
   3. ONE CHECK. Each run is walked ONCE, in ascending offset, in the working copy in which the solved shards of every span of
      substep 1 are written, and each solved shard, explained or not, written or not, is compared with its digest once. A span
      PASSES when every
      solved shard of it matches its digest and every run it touches checks. The bytes of an item that neither its identity nor
      its payload
      covers (the array head of the envelope, the heads of its last four elements, the `signatures` list, and the stored
      bytes of a payload that the capacity leaves `unchecked`, which the stored check also covers) are confirmed by the shard
      digest alone, whose table the parity frame holds: a repair restores them to what the parity writer covered, so
      a repair from a table that a rewrite of the file left stale restores the `signatures` list that the table covered.
   4. PROPAGATION. Every span that does not pass is REMOVED, and every span that depends on a run that a removed span touches
      is removed, until no span is removed. The removed spans are the smallest set that holds every span that does not pass and
      every span that depends on a run that one of its members touches, so the set does not depend on the order in which the
      removals are made. A removed span is `repair_failed` with reason `verify_failed` and the position of its parity frame;
      nothing of it is written. A span that is not removed is ACCEPTED: no removed span touches a run it depends on, so in the
      repaired copy those runs hold the bytes they held when they were walked, and they check there.
   5. An accepted span is `repaired` with `count` the number of its shards solved and written, data and parity, and the
      position of its parity frame. The repaired copy is the input with the solved shards of the accepted spans in place; padding
      is never written.
   6. THE COST. The runs are disjoint, a walk examines no byte outside its run, every run is walked once and every solved
      shard compared once, so the work of this step
      is linear in the bytes of the segment and of the payloads checked. Each payload check of substep 3 is charged to the total
      decoded
      length of the read (`P1`, "The payload check", THE TOTAL) at its capacity step, before it decodes, the checks in the order
      of substep 3 after the last item of the segment; a check that the total refuses leaves that payload `unchecked`.
6. RESUME. When the segment has a damaged region or ends in an incomplete item, scan forward from the first byte of the region or
   of that item, to the end of the input and with no bound on
   the distance, for the first offset at which a CANDIDATE passes. Two kinds of candidate are tested in one pass in ascending
   offset, the first that passes deciding. A PARITY CANDIDATE is an item that delimits, passes the envelope test, is of type
   `parity`, and passes every condition of stage 1 of 6.6 with its `anchor` as its predecessor and its table under this segment's
   `h0`, a condition that compares it with earlier frames of type `parity` being judged against those before the region; parity
   candidates are tested from the first byte of the damaged region or of the incomplete item. A RESYNC CANDIDATE is an
   occurrence of the pattern of a resync item that passes the test of `P1`, "Finding a resync item where framing is lost"; resync
   candidates are tested over the run of that section. A resync candidate that passes ends the segment and opens a new one, and
   the bytes before it are
   reported by that section; a parity candidate that passes continues the segment. At each offset the test of a parity candidate
   is made in this order and stops at the first part that fails:
   1. THE ENVELOPE, read element by element from the offset in the order of the envelope, each element judged when it is
      reached against what a frame of type `parity` holds, so that no element is walked past the first that fails: an array of
      eleven elements; `item_kind`, `frame_type`, `critical`, `transparent`, `storage` and an empty `signatures` list as 6.5
      states (condition 2); a `content` of the record of 6.5 that meets condition 1; `decoded_length` and `payload_digest` well
      typed; a `payload` that is a `bytes` item whose length equals `decoded_length` and equals the length of the deterministic
      encoding of a `parity_payload` with `N` and `4 * D` digests and `4 * D * z` parity bytes for the span `[first, end)`
      (`N`, `D` and `z` of 6.4 for `end - first`), which is at most `280651`; an `identifier` of the digest length; every head in
      the deterministic form (condition 7). A
      payload of another length is not the payload of its span, and the candidate fails here whatever the reader's capacity;
   2. THE LINK, with its `anchor` as its predecessor (condition 3): one hash of the identity preimage;
   3. THE CHARGE: a candidate whose `payload` is longer than `stored_bytes_per_frame`, or whose `decoded_length` is above
      `decoded_bytes_per_frame`, judged in that order (`P1`, "The payload check", step 1), is above capacity and not damaged:
      it passes, reading resumes at it, its payload state is `unchecked` with `exceeds_capacity` and the reason `stored_size`
      or `decoded_size`, it is not used, and it is not charged. Otherwise the candidate's `decoded_length` is charged to the total
      decoded length of the read (`P1`, "The payload
      check", THE TOTAL), at the place of the damaged region, the candidates in ascending offset. A candidate that the total
      refuses is above capacity and not damaged (condition 4): it passes, reading resumes at it, its payload state is
      `unchecked` with `exceeds_capacity`, it is not used, and every later frame of the read is refused by the total. A
      candidate that passes, at which reading resumes, is not charged again as an item: its charge in the scan is its
      charge in the read, and its payload check is the check of part 4;
   4. THE PAYLOAD: its payload check and the grammar of 6.5 (condition 4);
   5. THE TABLE under this segment's `h0` (condition 5).
   At an offset where the envelope fails the test reads a fixed number of heads, where the link fails one identity preimage,
   and otherwise one payload that the total counts and one table no longer than that payload: the scan is linear in the bytes
   of the region, and the payloads it hashes are bounded by `decoded_bytes_per_read`. When a parity candidate passes, the damaged
   region ends at its first byte: reading resumes there by steps 1, 2 and 6 for the rest of the segment, as often as a region is
   met. When a resync candidate passes, the damaged region ends at its first byte, the segment ends there, and reading resumes at
   the item that the candidate is, which opens the next segment. Steps 3, 4 and 5 run ONCE for the segment, after its last item
   is read, over every item that steps 1 and 6
   delimit and every parity frame of the segment, in one working copy. The S-position of the bytes after
   the region counts every byte of the region as a byte of `S`, so the frame's span can cover the region, whose bytes
   pass no check and whose shards are therefore explained erasures. When none is found, the rest of the input is the remainder
   of the `undelimitable_input` finding and is not read.
7. REPORT. Every item that is complete, delimitable and checks stays in the result. A damaged frame is opaque with the reason
   `damaged` or `chain_break` (`P1`, `P2`); the term ids that a damaged `terms` frame would define stay reserved by its content,
   so no row is renumbered (`P3`).

7.2A. THE FINDINGS OF STEP 1 WHEN RECOVERY RESUMES. The `length` of an `undelimitable_input` finding runs from its `offset` to the
first byte of the resumption point of step 6 when there is one, else to the end of the input. When the resumption point is a
resync item, the bytes before it are reported by `P1`, "Finding a resync item where framing is lost". An item that is
incomplete at the
end of the input is `torn_tail` only when no resumption follows it; when a resumption point lies after the first byte of an
incomplete item, the bytes up to it are the damaged region and the finding is `undelimitable_input`.  For the check of an
`offsets` item the damaged region is one item whose length is its number of
bytes, as it is one item for positions.

RESUMPTION, what it gives. The first ordinary frame after the parity frame at which reading resumes has that frame's `anchor` as
its predecessor and is `linked` when its link holds. Every frame read after the point has its own checks and nothing more: it is
a frame in doubt (`P3`, "Unreadable terms", THE DICTIONARY IN DOUBT), so no term id that it names is resolved, it is
not authenticated by the resumption, the leaves of the frames lost in the region are unknown, so, when the region held an ordinary
frame, every later `commit` gives
`commit_count_mismatch` or `commit_root_mismatch`, and every later `index` gives `index_claim_failed` for the structural claims
that count or hash the lost frames (`index_head`, `index_root`, `index_directory`). The damaged region counts as ONE item for
the positions of the items after it.

**P5-35.** A reader MUST run the seven steps of 7.2 on every segment, steps 1, 2 and 6 until the segment is read to its last
item, then steps 3, 4 and 5 once over the whole segment, then step 7, and MUST NOT offer a mode that skips a step or
changes its outcome, except as `P7`, "Access: the steps by access", states for the access `forward` and for the access
`sparse`.

**P5-36.** A reader MUST treat as unknowns only the shards that step 4 names, and MUST NOT write any byte of an unexplained shard.

**P5-37.** A reader MUST accept a repair only by step 5, MUST report `repaired` or `repair_failed` with the reason of steps 4 and
5 for every span that has unknowns to write, and MUST write into the repaired copy no byte of a span that is not accepted.

**P5-38.** A reader MUST scan for a resumption point to the end of the input with no bound on the distance, MUST test the two
kinds of candidate of step 6 in one pass in ascending offset and resume at the first that passes, MUST make the test of a parity
candidate at each offset in its order and stop it at the first part that fails, MUST charge every parity candidate that reaches
part 3 and is within `stored_bytes_per_frame` and `decoded_bytes_per_frame` to the total decoded length before it hashes the
candidate's payload, and MUST resume only at a candidate that passes its
test.

**P5-41.** A reader MUST delimit the working copy again in the runs of substep 2 of step 5, MUST walk each run at most once, in
the working copy of substep 3, MUST
compare each solved shard with its digest at most once, MUST remove exactly the spans of the propagation of substep 4, and MUST
charge every payload check of substep 3 to the total decoded length before it decodes.

**P5-39.** A reader MUST report the input as it is: the fold, the states and every finding other than `repaired` MUST be those of
the input, whatever the repaired copy holds. The payload checks that recovery runs count in the total decoded length of the read
(`P1`, "The payload check"), so a reader's capacity can refuse a later frame of the input that it would have checked had no
candidate and no repair been checked; the result is still a function of the bytes and of the capacity alone.

**P5-40.** A reader MUST give an `undelimitable_input` finding the length of 7.2A, MUST report the bytes before an accepted resync
item as `P1`, "Finding a resync item where framing is lost", states, MUST report `torn_tail` for
an incomplete last item only when no resumption point follows it, and MUST report a non-empty `signatures` list
on a parity frame as `parity_damaged`.

### 7.3 What recovery holds in memory

A repair solves one span at a time (at most `4194304` bytes, and its parity frame of `280651` bytes of payload for a full span)
and keeps the solved shards of the spans of the segment beside the input, which with them is the working copy; it walks each
run once, a frame larger than a span streamed through its checks and not held, and its propagation holds one bit for each
span and the runs each span depends on. The scan holds one candidate item at a time, whose payload is at most `280651` bytes,
and, while it tests a resync
candidate, a candidate buffer of `4096` bytes.
Every quantity is within the minimum capacity of every reader (`P7`). The scan has
no distance bound, because a fixed bound loses everything behind a damaged frame that is longer than the bound.

**P5-43.** A reader MUST hold at most `4096` bytes of candidate beyond its other recovery memory while it tests a resync
candidate.

### 7.4 Worked example

The segment of the placement example of 6.10, with the parity frames `Q0` after `F2`, `Q1` after `F3` and `Q2` after `F4`; `F3`
is `stored_compressed`.

- CASE A. 12 KiB from S-offset `5001216` (a shard boundary of span `1`, a full span with `z = 4096`:
  `5001216 - 4194304 = 806912 = 197 * z`) are flipped,
  inside the stored payload of `F3`. Step 2: `F3` links (its identifier does not cover stored bytes) and its stored state is
  `mismatch` (`stored_check_mismatch`); its payload is `damaged` (`damaged_frame`). Step 3: `Q1` is used; data shards `197`,
  `198`, `199` of span `1` fail: `parity_mismatch` with `data` and `count` `3`; they overlap `F3`, which does not pass, so they
  are explained. Step 4: with `D = 16` they fall in stripes `5`, `6` and `7`, one erasure each. Step 5: each stripe is solved
  from parity row `0`; each shard matches its digest; `F3` in the working copy links, its stored state matches and its payload
  verifies: `repaired` with `count` `3` at the position of `Q1`. The result keeps `F3` as damaged; the repaired copy reads intact.
- CASE B. 70 consecutive shards of span `1` are destroyed: `70 > 4 * 16`, so some stripe has `5` erasures. Step 4:
  `repair_failed` with `too_many_erasures` at the position of `Q1`, beside the `parity_mismatch` with `data` and `count` `70` of
  stage 3; nothing of span `1` is written.
- CASE C. The first byte of `F2`, its array head, is damaged so that no item can be delimited there. Step 1:
  `undelimitable_input` at the offset of `F2`. Step 6: the scan finds `Q0`, whose `anchor` is the effective identifier of `F2`
  and whose table holds under `h0`; reading resumes at `Q0`, and `F3` links with that `anchor` as its predecessor. The byte of
  `F2` lies in span `0`, one explained erasure; step 5 solves it, the working copy delimits `F2` again and `F2` checks:
  `repaired` with `count` `1` at the position of `Q0`. The result of the input still has `undelimitable_input`, and the region
  counts as one item, so `F3` has position `4` (the header, `F1`, the region and `Q0` stand before it). Stage 3 of 6.6 gives
  `parity_mismatch` with `data` and `count` `1` at the position of `Q0`. Every later `commit` and `index` of the input gives the
  findings that RESUMPTION states (7.2A).

## 8. Constants

- hash contexts of this part; value: `stored`, `range_leaf`, `range_node`, `range_root`, `shard`, `parity_table`; fixed by: the
  context table (`P2`)
- stored check length; value: `16` bytes
- field polynomial, generator; value: `0x11D`, `2`
- Cauchy points; value: `x_i = 255 - i`, `y_j = j`
- data shards a stripe `k`, parity shards a stripe `m`; value: `64`, `4`
- shard length; value: `4096` bytes for a span of `262144` bytes or more; for a shorter span the smallest power of two that is at
  least `64` and at least `ceil(n / 64)`
- shard length threshold; value: `262144` bytes (`64` full shards)
- shortest shard length; value: `64` bytes
- shard digest length; value: `16` bytes, the same strength as the stored check
- span length; value: `4194304` bytes (`1024` shards, depth `16`)
- full-span parity payload; value: `280651` bytes; fixed by: arithmetic of 6.5
- scan distance of recovery; value: no bound; the work of the scan is bounded by the test order of 7.2 step 6 and by the total
  decoded length, which counts every candidate payload it hashes
