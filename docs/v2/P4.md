# Part P4: Blobs

## 0. Scope, dependencies and notation

This part defines how bytes that are not RDF content are named, cut, stored and rebuilt. It defines the identity of a unit, a
blob and a value (section 1); the one chunker, with its gear table, its rolling fingerprint, its two masks and its cut rule
(section 2); the frames `chunk`, `blob`, `recipe`, `value` and `external` (section 3); the `pack` frame and its typed directory
(section 4); the blob set of the fold, as far as declarations go (section 5); the rebuilding of a blob or a value with its typed
outcomes and the total rebuilt length of a read (section 6); and the value region and the blob region of the canonical file
(section 7). Section 8 lists the constants
and the measurements that fix them, section 9 the vectors.

Dependencies, by part and section title.

- `P1` (Model and wire): the typed model and its one serialization, the frame envelope and its content records, the shared
  records `qualified_name` and `graph_name`, the context table and `H_ctx`, the three checks of every frame (link, stored check,
  payload), and the zstd constraint. This part adds no field to the envelope.
- `P2` (Registry): every enumeration, finding code and reason used below, by label. `P2` is the authority for every code.
- `P3` (RDF content): the `literal_ref` and `literal_value_ref` terms, which name a value by `value_digest` and `length`; the
  `bindings` frame, whose
  `blob` referent names a blob; the `blob` suppression entry, which hides the declarations of a name; the fold and the labelled
  set, which holds one `record` line for each entry of the blob set of section 5.
- `P5` (Durability): the `index` frame, whose `pack_members` list is a claim about the packs of this part, and the stored check.
- `P7` (Canonical form and reading): every constant of the canonical form other than those of this part, the order of the
  regions among the other frames of a canonical segment, the storage rule of a canonical frame, the canonical check that
  reports a difference as `canonical_violated` with `value_region` or `blob_region`, and the typed result of reading.
- `P8` (Sealing): sealed packs. This part defines no sealed form.

Notation. `E(x)` is the deterministic encoding of `x` (`P1`). `H_ctx` is the construction of `P1`. A byte string is written in
hexadecimal with a space between bytes. `<D>` followed by a name stands for 32 bytes of a digest whose value the vectors give;
an example never states a digest it cannot show by hand. A field is written `name: type` in the notation of `P1`. A position in
a finding is the zero-based index of the item among all items of its segment, the header being `0`, transparent frames counted
(`P2`). "Segment" and "file" are as `P1` defines them. The words MUST, MUST NOT and MAY have their meaning in BCP 14 (RFC 2119,
RFC 8174) and carry it only in capitals. A requirement has an identifier `P4-n`; each states one thing and is testable by a
vector of section 9. Registry version `1` is in force for every rule of this part. A requirement of a reader in this part applies under the access `forward` or `sparse` as `P7`, "Access: the steps by
access", THE SCOPE OF A READER REQUIREMENT, states.

External documents cited, by identifier, title and date.

- RFC 8949; title: Concise Binary Object Representation (CBOR); date: December 2020
- RFC 8878; title: Zstandard Compression and the `application/zstd` Media Type; date: February 2021
- BLAKE3; title: BLAKE3: one function, fast everywhere (derive-key mode and extended output); date: 2020

Terms used throughout.

- A UNIT is a byte string stored once under its digest, as a `chunk` frame, a `blob` frame or a member of kind `chunk` or `blob`
  of a `pack`. The bytes of a unit are its octets, its length their count.
- A BLOB is a byte string that the fold declares by a name (section 5). A VALUE is the octet string that gives the lexical form
  of a `literal_ref` term or the typed value of a `literal_value_ref` term (`P3`). A blob and a value are different declarations
  even when their bytes are equal.
- A DECLARATION of a blob is a `blob` frame, a `recipe` frame, an `external` frame, or a `pack` member of kind `blob` or
  `recipe`. A DECLARATION of a value is a `value` frame or a `pack` member of kind `value`. A `chunk` declares neither.
- ONE-UNIT BLOB: a blob stored as one unit. MULTI-UNIT BLOB: a blob stored as a list of two or more units by a recipe.

## 1. Identity

1. A unit is named by its digest, `H_ctx(unit, bytes)`: the context `unit` of the context table of `P1`, the message the bytes
   of the unit. The unit digest is a different value from the `payload_digest` of the envelope, which uses the context `payload`.
2. A blob is named by the record `qualified_name = [algorithm, digest]` (`P1`), where `algorithm` is the `hash_algorithm` of the
   header of the segment that holds the declaration, and `digest` is `H_ctx(unit, bytes of the whole blob)`. A value is named by
   `value_digest = H_ctx(unit, octets of the whole value)`. A blob that is one unit has the digest of that unit. The empty blob
   has a name: the digest of the empty byte string.
3. Every name of a blob has the one form of `qualified_name`, whether the bytes are in the file or not. A reader never tells a
   present name from an external one by the type of a value.

**P4-1.** A writer MUST compute every unit digest, blob digest and value digest as `H_ctx(unit, bytes)` over the whole byte
string named, and MUST NOT compute any of them with another context.

**P4-2.** A writer MUST write the `qualified_name` of every blob, in every frame that names one, as the array of two fields
`[algorithm, digest]`, with `digest` of the length that `algorithm` fixes.

**P4-3.** A chunk MUST NOT be treated as a declaration of a blob or of a value: a blob exists only by one of the declarations of
the terms above.

**P4-4.** The `media_type` of a declaration MUST be carried and compared bytewise only. A reader MUST NOT parse it, MUST NOT
normalise it, and MUST NOT use it to recognise a blob that is itself a file of this format; the fold never opens a blob.

**P4-5.** The `unit_digest` of a `chunk` or `blob` frame MUST equal `H_ctx(unit, payload)` over the decoded payload. A reader
MUST report a frame that has verified its `payload_digest` and whose `unit_digest` differs as `damaged_frame` with the reason
`payload_digest`; a frame whose `payload_digest` check has already failed has that one finding and no second one.

Why two digests. The envelope digest covers the decoded payload of every frame under one context so that a frame never stands
in for another kind of frame; the unit digest is the name that recipes, values, bindings and suppression entries use for the
bytes. They are computed over the same octets, once each, and the second is stored in the content record, which the identifier
covers, so the name of a unit is bound into the chain.

Worked example (typed values). A `chunk` frame holding the three bytes `61 62 63` has `content = [U]` with
`U = H_ctx(unit, 61 62 63)`, `decoded_length` `3`, `payload_digest = H_ctx(payload, 61 62 63)` and the stored payload
`61 62 63` under `stored_plain`. `U` and `payload_digest` differ because the contexts differ. The content record is the array
`81 58 20 <U>` (an array of one field, a `bytes` of 32). The value of `U` is given by the vectors.

## 2. The chunker

The chunker is a function from a byte string to a sequence of lengths that sum to the length of the string. A reader never runs
it: every recipe lists its units explicitly. It matters to a writer that wants its units to agree with another writer's, and to
the canonical file, which cuts every blob and every value with it (section 7). It has no identifier, no declared parameter, no
keyed variant and no registry entry: it is one function with the constants below.

### 2.1 Constants

The procedure of 2.3 is stated over named constants so that the worked example of 2.5 can run it on a small instance. The format
fixes every constant.

- `R`: the width in bits of the fingerprint register; fixed at `64`
- `W`: the window of bytes that the fingerprint depends on; fixed at `64`
- `min`: the shortest chunk that is cut before the end of input; fixed at `16384`
- `b`: the base-2 logarithm of the scale of the masks; fixed at `16`, so `avg = 2^b = 65536`
- `max`: the longest chunk; fixed at `262144`
- `n`: the normalisation level; fixed at `2`
- `G`: the gear table, 256 words of `R` bits (2.2)

The constants satisfy `W <= min`, `min < 2^b < max`, `0 <= n < b` and `b + n <= R`; the procedure is defined only for constants
that do. The measurements that fix the values are cited in section 8.

### 2.2 The gear table

`T` is the first `2048` bytes of the extended output of the key-derivation mode of `BLAKE3` whose context string is the ASCII
spelling of the context `gear_table` of the context table of `P1` and whose key material is the empty byte string. The first
`32` bytes of `T` are `H_ctx(gear_table, empty message)`; the table continues the same output stream. `G[i]` for `i` from `0`
to `255` is the unsigned integer that the eight bytes `T[8 * i]` to `T[8 * i + 7]` give in big-endian order. The table is
derived with `BLAKE3` and depends on no field of any header.

**P4-6.** An implementation MUST derive the gear table as the 256 big-endian unsigned 64-bit words of the first `2048` bytes of
the extended output of `BLAKE3` in key-derivation mode with the context string `gts2 gear table` and an empty key material, and
MUST NOT use any other table.

### 2.3 The fingerprint, the masks and the cut rule

THE FINGERPRINT. For a byte `c`, `update(fp, c) = ((fp << 1) + G[c]) mod 2^R`, where `fp` is an unsigned integer below `2^R`.

THE WINDOW PROPERTY. Let `fp` be `0` and let the bytes `x[q]` to `x[p]` be applied in order. The contribution of `x[p - k]` to
the result is `G[x[p - k]]` shifted left by `k` places, modulo `2^R`. For `k >= R` it is `0`. Hence, once at least `R` bytes
have been applied, the register is `sum over k from 0 to R - 1 of (G[x[p - k]] << k) mod 2^R`, which depends on the `R` bytes
that end at `p` and on nothing else. With `W = R`, this is the sense in which the window is `W` bytes.

THE MASKS. `strict(fp)` holds exactly when `fp >> (R - (b + n)) = 0`: the top `b + n` bits of `fp` are zero. `loose(fp)` holds
exactly when `fp >> (R - (b - n)) = 0`: the top `b - n` bits are zero. With the fixed constants `strict` tests the top `18` bits
and `loose` the top `14` bits; they are equivalent to `fp < 2^46` and `fp < 2^50`. The tests read the high bits: a byte at
distance `k` from `p` reaches only the bits `k` to `R - 1` of `fp`.

THE CUT RULE. `cut(x, s)` returns the end `e`, exclusive, of the chunk that starts at offset `s` of a byte string `x` of length
`N`, with `s < N`:

1. If `N - s <= min`, return `N`.
2. Set `fp` to `0`. For `p` from `s + min - W` to `s + min - 2` inclusive, set `fp` to `update(fp, x[p])`. These are `W - 1`
   updates; no test is made.
3. For `p` from `s + min - 1` upward, in order:
   1. set `fp` to `update(fp, x[p])` and `l` to `p - s + 1`, the length the chunk has if it ends after `x[p]`;
   2. if `p = N - 1`, return `N`;
   3. otherwise, if `l = max`, return `p + 1`;
   4. otherwise, if `l < 2^b` and `strict(fp)`, return `p + 1`;
   5. otherwise, if `l >= 2^b` and `loose(fp)`, return `p + 1`;
   6. otherwise continue with the next `p`.

The chunking of `x` is the sequence of lengths `e - s` for `s = 0`, then `s = e` of the previous result, until `s = N`. The
empty string has no chunk. One byte is consumed per step, exactly as written; a computation that consumes two bytes per step or
keeps a different register gives other cut points and is not this rule.

**P4-7.** An implementation of the chunker MUST use the constants of 2.1 with the values fixed there.

**P4-8.** An implementation MUST compute the fingerprint as `update` of 2.3, with the register of `64` bits and the addition and
the shift taken modulo `2^64`, one byte at a time.

**P4-9.** An implementation MUST apply `strict` for a chunk length below `2^b`, and `loose` for a chunk length of at least
`2^b`, each on the register after the byte that ends the candidate chunk.

**P4-10.** An implementation MUST find the end of each chunk by the steps of `cut` in 2.3, in that order, and the sequence of
the lengths it produces for a byte string MUST equal the chunking defined there.

### 2.4 End of input, short input, bounds

- A string of length `0` has no chunk. A string of length `N` with `1 <= N <= min` is one chunk of length `N`.
- Every chunk but the last has a length from `min` to `max` inclusive. The last chunk has a length from `1` to `max`.
- The first byte that can end a chunk is the one at length `min`: a chunk of length below `min` is never cut before the end of the
  string. At that position `p = s + min - 1` is at most `N - 2`, because step 1 returned otherwise, so the first test is never
  the end-of-input test.
- When the end of the string is reached before a mask cuts, the chunk ends at `N` whatever the register holds (step 3.2): no mask
  is tested at the last byte.
- Where no mask cuts, the chunk ends at `max` (step 3.3), whether or not `p` is the last byte; the end-of-input test comes
  first, and both give the same end when `l = max` and `p = N - 1`.

THE WINDOW PROPERTY OF THE CUT. Because `min >= W`, every register that step 3 tests was built by `R` updates or more over bytes
of the current chunk only, so by the window property a mask test at `p` is a function of the `W` bytes that end at `p` and of the
length `l` since the previous cut, and of nothing earlier. That is all that is guaranteed. The cut points that follow an edit
need not coincide with those of the unedited string again, within `max` bytes or at all. Counterexample: let the string be a
long run of the zero byte, on which no mask cuts, so that every chunk is cut at `max` (the figure of `A1`, FE6-2, for one mebibyte
of zero bytes). Prepend one zero byte: both strings are cut at multiples of `max` from their own starts, so the cut points of
the two strings differ by one byte and never meet, however long the run.

LOCALITY AS AN EXPECTATION. On input in which masks cut, a cut at `p` that depends on the window and on `l` makes the cut points
after an edit, in the usual case, coincide with those of the unedited string again a short distance after the edit, which is why
the units of two versions of a blob can agree. This is a statistical expectation, measured in `A1` (FE6-2), and not a bound: no
distance after which the cut points coincide is guaranteed. The canonical chunking of a string is a deterministic function of its
bytes whatever the input; the expectation concerns only how many units two versions share.

**P4-11.** An implementation MUST treat a string of length `0` as having no chunk, a string of length `N <= min` as one chunk of
length `N`, and MUST end the last chunk at the end of the string without testing a mask at its last byte.

**P4-12.** A writer MUST chunk each blob and each value on its own, restarting `cut` at offset `0` of the string: no chunk spans
two blobs, two values, or a blob and a value.

**P4-13.** A canonical writer MUST chunk every present blob and every value with this chunker. Any other writer MAY cut by any
rule, because a recipe lists its units. A reader MUST NOT run the chunker, MUST NOT depend on the way a recipe was cut, and MUST
NOT report a recipe whose cut differs from the chunking of its bytes.

### 2.5 Worked example of the cut rule on a small instance

The instance below runs the procedure of 2.3 with other constants, small enough to follow by hand; it shows the rule and does not
define bytes of the format. Constants: `R = 4`, `W = 4`, `min = 4`, `b = 3` (so `2^b = 8`), `n = 1`, `max = 12`. They satisfy the
conditions of 2.1: `4 <= 4`, `4 < 8 < 12`, `0 <= 1 < 3`, `3 + 1 <= 4`. The register has `4` bits, so `update(fp, c) =
(2 * fp + G[c]) mod 16`. `strict` has shift `R - (b + n) = 0`: it holds when `fp = 0`. `loose` has shift `R - (b - n) = 2`: it
holds when `fp < 4`. The input uses only the byte values `0` to `3`, so only these table words matter:
`G[0] = 3`, `G[1] = 9`, `G[2] = 6`, `G[3] = 12`.

Input, `N = 19` bytes, by offset: `x[0..5] = 0 0 2 2 1 2`, `x[6..13] = 1 3 3 0 1 0 0 1`, `x[14..18] = 2 0 0 3 1`.

Chunk from `s = 0`. `N - s = 19 > 4`. Warm-up for `p = 0, 1, 2`: `fp` is `3`, then `2 * 3 + 3 = 9`, then
`2 * 9 + 6 = 24 mod 16 = 8`.

| p | byte | fp | l | test | result |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3 | 2 | `2 * 8 + 6 = 22 mod 16 = 6` | 4 | strict, `6 != 0` | continue |
| 4 | 1 | `2 * 6 + 9 = 21 mod 16 = 5` | 5 | strict, `5 != 0` | continue |
| 5 | 2 | `2 * 5 + 6 = 16 mod 16 = 0` | 6 | strict, `0 = 0` | cut: end `6` |

Chunk from `s = 6`. Warm-up for `p = 6, 7, 8` (bytes `1 3 3`): `fp` is `9`, then `2 * 9 + 12 = 30 mod 16 = 14`, then
`2 * 14 + 12 = 40 mod 16 = 8`.

| p | byte | fp | l | test | result |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 9 | 0 | `2 * 8 + 3 = 19 mod 16 = 3` | 4 | strict, `3 != 0` | continue |
| 10 | 1 | `2 * 3 + 9 = 15` | 5 | strict | continue |
| 11 | 0 | `2 * 15 + 3 = 33 mod 16 = 1` | 6 | strict, `1 != 0` | continue |
| 12 | 0 | `2 * 1 + 3 = 5` | 7 | strict | continue |
| 13 | 1 | `2 * 5 + 9 = 19 mod 16 = 3` | 8 | loose, `3 >> 2 = 0` | cut: end `14` |

At `p = 13` the length is `8 = 2^b`, so `loose` applies, and the register `3` that `strict` would have refused is accepted.

Chunk from `s = 14`. `N - s = 5 > 4`. Warm-up for `p = 14, 15, 16` (bytes `2 0 0`): `fp` is `6`, then `2 * 6 + 3 = 15`, then
`2 * 15 + 3 = 33 mod 16 = 1`.

| p | byte | fp | l | test | result |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 17 | 3 | `2 * 1 + 12 = 14` | 4 | strict, `14 != 0` | continue |
| 18 | 1 | not needed | 5 | `p = N - 1` | end of input: end `19` |

The chunking is `6, 8, 5`, which sums to `19`. Had no mask cut by `l = 12`, step 3.3 would have ended the chunk there. The first
chunk of the example is the one that the window property predicts: the register at `p = 5` is
`G[2] + 2 * G[1] + 4 * G[2] + 8 * G[2] = 6 + 18 + 24 + 48 = 96 mod 16 = 0`, the sum over the four bytes `2 2 1 2` that end at `5`,
taken from the newest byte to the oldest: bytes `x[5] = 2`, `x[4] = 1`, `x[3] = 2`, `x[2] = 2`.

This instance is a worked example only: it uses constants other than the fixed ones and no vector uses them. The boundaries of
real inputs under the fixed constants are given by the vectors of section 9.

## 3. The frames

The content record of every frame of this part is fixed by `P1` (Model and wire, "The content record of every frame type of
registry version 1"); this section gives the
meaning of its fields, the decoded payload and the rules. An entry of a recipe or a value is the record
`entry = [unit_digest: digest, length: uint]`, and the decoded payload of a `recipe` or a `value` frame is `E(list(entry))`.

`entry_count` of a `recipe` or `value`, and `member_count` and `unit_length` of a `pack`, repeat what the payload states. Their
value is stated: a reader bounds its allocation from the envelope, before it decodes the payload, and a party that cannot
decode the payload (an unknown codec, a capacity limit) still checks the declaration. A disagreement is `malformed_value` with
the reason `inconsistent_counts` (`P1`).

- `chunk`; content: `[unit_digest]`; decoded payload: the bytes of one unit; declares: nothing
- `blob`; content: `[unit_digest, media_type: option(text)]`; decoded payload: the bytes of a whole blob, possibly none;
  declares: the blob `[algorithm, unit_digest]` of length `decoded_length`
- `recipe`; content: `[blob_digest, total_length, media_type: option(text), entry_count]`; decoded payload: `E(list(entry))`;
  declares: the blob `[algorithm, blob_digest]` of length `total_length`
- `value`; content: `[value_digest, total_length, entry_count]`; decoded payload: `E(list(entry))`; declares: a value
- `external`; content: `[name: qualified_name, length: uint, media_type: option(text)]`; decoded payload: empty;
  declares: the blob `name`, without bytes; the frame does not say that the bytes are absent from the file

`algorithm` of a blob declared by `blob` or `recipe` is the `hash_algorithm` of the header of the segment that holds the frame.

A rule of this section that reads only fields of the envelope (P4-14, and the `decoded_length` and `storage` of P4-18) is a
field rule: a reader judges it with the field rules of `P1`, after them, at the step ITEMS of the reader procedure, and the one
`malformed_value` of an item is the first violation in that order. It needs no payload and is judged whatever the payload check
gives.

Every other rule of this section on a frame (P4-5, P4-16, P4-17 and P4-21 to P4-24, and the disagreement of a count with the list
or the payload that it counts) is a rule on a value of the content record or on the payload against it. It is judged only for a
frame whose link state is `linked` or `healed`, at the step CONTENT of the reader procedure and, for the units, at the step UNITS
AND VALUES: the content of a frame whose link is `broken` is not interpreted (`P1`, "The record, in identity order", ORDER OF THE
FIELD CHECKS), and the frame declares nothing and has no unit.

**P4-14.** A `chunk` frame MUST have `decoded_length` at least `1`. A reader MUST report a `chunk` frame with `decoded_length`
`0` as `malformed_value` with the reason `out_of_range`.

**P4-15.** A `blob` frame declares the blob whose digest is its `unit_digest`, whose length is its `decoded_length` and whose
media type is its `media_type`. A `blob` frame MAY have `decoded_length` `0`; it then declares the empty blob, with
`stored_plain` and an empty payload (`P1`).

**P4-16.** A `recipe` frame MUST be checked in this order, and the first violation is the one `malformed_value` of the frame:
`entry_count` below `2`, reason `out_of_range`; the decoded payload against its type `list(entry)`, the first violation in the
order of its bytes with the reason `P1` gives (a digest of an entry whose length is not the digest length of the segment's
`hash_algorithm` is `wrong_length`); a decoded list whose length differs from
`entry_count`, reason `inconsistent_counts`; an entry with `length` `0`, reason `out_of_range`; a running sum of the entry
lengths above `2^64 - 1`, reason `out_of_range`; a sum that differs from `total_length`, reason `inconsistent_counts`. The
checks after the second read a payload that is of its type.

**P4-17.** A `value` frame MUST be checked in the order of P4-16 with `entry_count` below `1` as the first violation (reason
`out_of_range`). A `value` declares a value and no blob: it is never in the blob set (section 5).

**P4-18.** An `external` frame is a declaration without bytes (a name, a length and a media type), and a reader MUST NOT read
it as a statement that the bytes are absent from the file. An `external` frame MUST have `decoded_length` `0`, an empty payload
and `stored_plain`; a reader MUST report a `decoded_length` other than `0` or a `storage` other than `stored_plain` as
`malformed_value` with the reason `out_of_range`, judging `decoded_length` first and then `storage`, so that the one
`malformed_value` of the frame is the first violation of that order. A payload that is not empty under `decoded_length` `0` is the `damaged_frame`
with `decoded_length` of the payload check of `P1` and has no second finding. A writer MUST write the length of the bytes it
declares.

**P4-19.** A writer MUST write the digest and the length of each recipe or value entry from the bytes it stored, never from a
claim of its source, and MUST write a blob of exactly one unit as a `blob` frame or a `pack` member of kind `blob`, never as a
recipe, which has at least two entries.

**P4-20.** A writer MAY store a unit more than once in a segment, in any frames. Each copy is checked on its own and the unit
used is chosen by 6.1; a copy serves the declarations that stand in its frame or after it (6.1, A UNIT SERVES ONLY WHAT STANDS
AFTER IT). The value stated: in a file that is only appended to, a damaged unit is repaired by appending an intact copy and then
the declarations that name it again, and, for a value, the `terms` frames and the rows that name its terms again (`P3`,
"`literal_ref`: a lexical form held by a `value` frame"). The earlier declaration keeps its finding and its rebuild state; for a
value, the earlier definition of the term stays unreadable and the rows that name its id stay held, so the content digest of the file stays `absent`
(`P3`, "The content digest").
Vectors `rebuild-unit-after-recipe` and `rebuild-unit-after-value` (section 9) hold units on each side of the declaration that
names them.

Worked example (typed values and bytes). A recipe over two units `A` of `6` bytes and `B` of `5` bytes, whose concatenation is a
blob of `11` bytes, with `UA = H_ctx(unit, A)`, `UB = H_ctx(unit, B)` and `BD = H_ctx(unit, A followed by B)`:
`content = [BD, 11, [1], 2]` and the decoded payload

```text
82                      array of 2 entries
  82 58 20 <UA> 06      entry [UA, 6]
  82 58 20 <UB> 05      entry [UB, 5]
```

which is `1 + 36 + 36 = 73` bytes; `decoded_length` is `73`. The sum `6 + 5` equals `total_length` `11`, `entry_count` equals the
length of the list. A `value` over the same units has `content = [BD, 11, 2]` and the same payload.

## 4. The pack

A `pack` frame stores several units, recipes and values under one compression context, so that the unit of identity (one digest)
and the unit of compression (one zstd frame) are separate. Its decoded payload is the one record

```text
pack_payload = [ directory: list(member), bodies: bytes ]
member       = [1, content: chunk_content,  body_length: uint]     ; member_kind: chunk
             / [2, content: blob_content,   body_length: uint]     ; blob
             / [3, content: recipe_content, body_length: uint]     ; recipe
             / [4, content: value_content,  body_length: uint]     ; value
chunk_content  = [unit_digest: digest]
blob_content   = [unit_digest: digest, media_type: option(text)]
recipe_content = [blob_digest: digest, total_length: uint, media_type: option(text), entry_count: uint]
value_content  = [value_digest: digest, total_length: uint, entry_count: uint]
```

The content records of the members are the content records of the frames of the same name (section 3). `bodies` is the
concatenation of the bodies of the members in directory order; the body of member `k` has `body_length` bytes and starts at the
sum of the `body_length` of the members before it. The body of a `chunk` or `blob` member is the bytes of the unit; the body of
a `recipe` or `value` member is the encoding of its entry list, the decoded payload that the frame of that kind would have. The
`content` of the pack is `[member_count: uint, unit_length: uint]`. The byte strings of the pack are data and not structure: every
length, count, digest and entry is a typed field, and `bodies` is the only part with no structure of its own.

**P4-21.** A reader MUST check a `pack` frame in this order, and the first violation is the one `malformed_value` of the frame:
`member_count` `0`, reason `out_of_range`; the decoded payload against its type `pack_payload`, the first violation in the
order of its bytes with the reason `P1` gives, the tag of a member (P4-23) included; `member_count` differing from the length of
the directory, reason `inconsistent_counts`; the sum of all `body_length` differing from the length of `bodies`, reason
`inconsistent_counts`; `unit_length` differing from the sum of the `body_length` of the `chunk` and `blob` members, reason
`inconsistent_counts`; then the rules of P4-22 over the members in directory order. The checks after the second read a
payload that is of its type, so every member has a kind and a `body_length`.

**P4-22.** A reader MUST judge each member as the frame of its kind is judged, with the body as the decoded payload: a `chunk`
member with `body_length` `0`, reason `out_of_range`; a `recipe` or `value` member by P4-16 and P4-17 over its body, with the
reason of the first violation in byte order (a body whose item is not a list is `wrong_type`), and a body that decodes as one
list with bytes remaining after it, reason `wrong_length`. The test for bytes remaining belongs to the second check of P4-16,
the body against its type: the bytes after the one list are a violation at the offset where the list ends, so it follows
every violation inside the list and precedes the comparison of the length of the list with `entry_count`. The members are
judged in directory order, and the first member that violates gives the one reason of the pack. A pack that has a member
outside these rules is `malformed_value` and opaque with the reason `malformed`: no member of it is folded.

**P4-23.** A pack MUST NOT hold an `external` frame, a pack or any other frame as a member: a tag outside `member_kind` is
`malformed_value` with the reason `unregistered_value`.

**P4-24.** A reader MUST check each `chunk` and `blob` member against its own digest: `H_ctx(unit, body)` MUST equal the
`unit_digest` of its content. A member that fails is `damaged_frame` with the reason `payload_digest`, at the position of the
pack frame, with the offset and length of that frame, one finding for each failing member; its unit stands in the unit index
and is not verified (6.1), exactly as
the unit of a `chunk` or `blob` frame that fails P4-5; the pack stays folded, every member of it declares what it declares, and
a `blob` member that fails still declares its blob. A member's authority is its own digest:
a claim of the `index` frame about a pack (`P5`) is never evidence of a unit's identity.

**P4-25.** A `recipe` member declares its blob and a `value` member its value exactly as the frame of that kind does; the
declaration's position, for a finding, is the position of the pack frame.

**P4-26.** The stored form of a `pack` frame is the form of every frame (`P1`): `stored_plain`, or `stored_compressed` as one
zstd frame under the constraint of `P1`. No pack is block-framed, and no member has a stored locator. The cost that follows,
stated as its value: one frame is the smallest form that every engine decodes with identical bytes, and a lookup of one member
decodes the pack up to that member's body, on average half the pack (`FE6-8`).

Worked example (typed value and bytes). A pack with one `chunk` member whose body is the three bytes `61 62 63`, and
`U = H_ctx(unit, 61 62 63)`: `content = [1, 3]` (`member_count` `1`, `unit_length` `3`), and the decoded payload

```text
82                      pack_payload: array of 2
  81                    directory: list of 1 member
    83 01 81 58 20 <U> 03   member [1, [U], 3]
  43 61 62 63           bodies: bytes of length 3
```

which is `1 + 1 + (1 + 1 + 35 + 1) + 4 = 44` bytes. A pack of the members of the example of
section 7 is given there in typed values.

## 5. The blob set

The BLOB SET of the fold is the list of the blobs that its folded frames declare, as `blob_entry` records:

```text
blob_entry = [ presence:    enum(blob_presence),   ; present, external
               name:        qualified_name,
               length:      uint,
               media_types: list(text) ]           ; every distinct declared media type, sorted bytewise on E, no repeat
```

Only folded frames declare (RDF content, "The fold"): a frame that is damaged, opaque or after a stop declares nothing. A hidden
declaration is a declaration (RDF content, "Suppression hides"): it is in the set.

**P4-27.** A blob MUST be in the blob set if and only if a folded `blob` frame, `recipe` frame, `external` frame, or `pack`
member of kind `blob` or `recipe` declares it. A `value`, a `chunk` and a member of kind `chunk` or `value` MUST NOT put a name
in the set.

**P4-28.** The set MUST hold one entry for each distinct name, the name being the whole `qualified_name`. The `presence` of the
entry is `present` when any declaration of the name carries or lists its bytes (a `blob` frame, a `recipe` frame, or a `pack`
member of kind `blob` or `recipe`), and `external` otherwise. Presence is a function of the declarations and never of whether
the bytes are found.

**P4-29.** The `length` of an entry MUST be the smallest length stated by the declarations of its name: the `decoded_length` of
a `blob` frame, the `body_length` of a `blob` member, the `total_length` of a `recipe` frame or member, and the `length` of an
`external` frame. Every declaration states a length, and the entry does not depend on the order of the declarations in the file.

**P4-30.** A reader MUST report `conflicting_declaration` once for each declaration whose stated length is not the `length` of
the entry of its name (the smallest), with the `position` of that declaration (of the pack frame, for a member). The finding does
not name the length. The set of findings does not depend on the order of the declarations.

**P4-31.** The `media_types` of an entry MUST be the distinct values `present` in the `media_type` fields of the declarations of
the name, sorted ascending by the bytes of `E(media_type)`, with no repeat; a declaration whose `media_type` is `absent`
contributes nothing. The list does not depend on the order of the declarations.

**P4-32.** The blob set MUST be the list of entries sorted ascending by the bytes of `E(name)`. Each entry is the source of one
`blob_declaration` record line of the labelled set, whose payload body is `[presence, name, length, media_types]` and whose term
list is empty (RDF content, "Record lines"); the blob set has no digest of its own.

**P4-33.** A suppression entry of kind `blob` hides the declarations of its name from the visible dataset (`P3`) and MUST NOT
remove the name from the blob set, change any of its fields, or remove its `blob_declaration` record line from the labelled set.

Worked example (typed value and bytes). A blob of `5` bytes with digest `D`, declared by a `blob` frame with media type `a/b` and
by an `external` frame with length `5` and media type `c/d`, has one entry `[1, [1, D], 5, ["a/b", "c/d"]]`, in bytes

```text
84 01 82 01 58 20 <D> 05 82 63 61 2f 62 63 63 2f 64
```

(array of 4; presence `1`; name `[1, D]`; length `5`; two texts of three bytes; `48` bytes in all). The presence is
`present` because a `blob` frame declares it. A second `external` frame that states length `6` for the same name gives one
`conflicting_declaration` at its position and changes no field of the entry, because the entry takes the smallest length, `5`; the
same two frames in the other order give the same entry and the same finding at the frame that states `6`.

## 6. Rebuilding a blob or a value

A recipe or a value names its units by digest and length; a rebuild finds them, concatenates their bytes and checks the
whole. The fold never reads blob bytes: a rebuild is a separate operation on the units of a segment. A value is rebuilt
before the fold resolves the `literal_ref` and `literal_value_ref` terms that name it. A `value` declaration (a `value` frame or
a `pack` member of kind `value`, folded) PROVIDES a value for a term exactly when its `value_digest` equals the term's digest
and its `total_length` equals the term's length, and its frame stands before the `terms` frame that holds the term (RDF content, "`literal_ref`: a lexical form held by a `value` frame"); a
declaration of the same digest and another length provides nothing for that term. THE VALUE USED for a term is the first
VERIFIED declaration (6.2) that provides a value for it, taking the declarations that stand before the term's `terms` frame in
file order, the frames of a segment in item order and the members of a pack in directory order (P4-54). When no `value` that
stands before the term's `terms` frame provides a value for a `literal_ref` or `literal_value_ref` term, the finding is
`missing_dependency` with `value`, once for each pair of a digest and a length (RDF content states where); when some `value`
that stands there provides one and no declaration that stands there and provides one is verified, the finding
is the blobs part's finding at each such declaration, at its own position (the one finding of a failed rebuild, 6.2, or the
refusal of 6.3), and no `missing_dependency` is added. In both cases the term is unreadable and whatever names it is held with
`opaque_term`. Every declaration, whether or not it is the value used, is charged by 6.3 and, when admitted, rebuilt by 6.2, and
its own failure or refusal is reported once, at its own position.

### 6.1 The unit index and the unit used

THE UNIT INDEX of a segment holds one record `[digest, length, location]` for each stored unit, in file order: for a folded
`chunk` or `blob` frame, its `unit_digest`, its `decoded_length` and the position of the frame; for each `chunk` or `blob` member
of a folded `pack`, its `unit_digest`, its `body_length`, and the position of the pack with the index of the member in the
directory. Members of one pack are in directory order, and a pack stands at its position among the frames.

A unit is VERIFIED when the payload check of its frame holds (`P1`: the decoded payload is intact) and its bytes satisfy P4-5 for
a frame or P4-24 for a member. A frame that is opaque (damaged, malformed, over the reader's capacity, of an unknown codec) has
no verified unit and a pack that is opaque has no member in the index.

THE UNIT USED for a digest `d` is the first verified unit of the index with digest `d`, in the order of the index. Every
verified unit of one digest has the same bytes (Property 3); the findings about copies that are not verified are reported at
their own frames. A UNIT SERVES ONLY WHAT STANDS AFTER IT: the unit used serves an entry of a declaration only when its frame is
the frame of the declaration or stands before it in item order (the unit and the declaration may be members of one pack). When
the unit used stands after the declaration, no verified unit stands at or before the declaration, since the unit used is the
first in the order of the index, and the entry has no unit: the declaration is incomplete with `missing_dependency` and the
reason `chunk` (6.2). A canonical file holds every unit before the recipe or value that names it, within its region.

In a graph state a declaration is served only by the units of its run that stand before the commit, which are a prefix of the
index: the unit used for a digest in the state is the unit used of the index when it stands before the commit, and there is
none otherwise (RDF content, "Graph states", THE UNITS OF A STATE).

**P4-34.** A reader MUST build the unit index of a segment from its folded frames and from the members of its folded packs, in
file order, before it rebuilds a blob or a value of that segment.

**P4-35.** A reader MUST use, for a digest `d`, the first verified unit of the index with digest `d` in the order of the index,
and MUST NOT use an unverified unit.

**P4-36.** A reader MUST resolve the entries of a recipe or a value only among the units that stand in the segment of the
declaration, in its frame or before it, and MUST NOT look in another segment, at a unit that
stands after the declaration, or outside the file. An entry whose unit stands in another segment or after the declaration is
unresolved. This confines the units of an entry and not the place of a declaration: the `value` frame (or `pack` member of
kind `value`) that provides the octets of a `literal_ref` or `literal_value_ref` term may stand in any segment at or before the
segment of the `terms` frame, and in that segment before the `terms` frame, as RDF content states in "`literal_ref`: a lexical form held by a `value` frame",
while a recipe resolves the units of its entries in its own segment only.

**P4-54.** A reader MUST take as the bytes of a `literal_ref` or `literal_value_ref` term the output of the value used for the
term: the first verified declaration, in file order (frames of a segment in item order, members of a pack in directory order),
whose `value_digest` equals the term's digest and whose `total_length` equals the term's length and that stands before the
`terms` frame that holds the term. It MUST judge the term
unreadable when no declaration with both that stands there is verified, whatever a declaration of the same digest and another
length, or a declaration that stands at or after the `terms` frame, gives. It MUST report the failure or the refusal of each
declaration that is not verified once, at that declaration, and `missing_dependency` with `value` only for a pair for which a
term has no folded declaration of that digest and length standing before its `terms` frame.
Vector `value-opaque-declares-nothing` (a `value` frame whose link is `broken`, so that it is opaque, then a `terms` frame
with a `literal_ref` term of its digest and length and a frame that names the term, read with `rebuilt_bytes_per_read` `2`:
the `value` frame declares nothing; the `terms` frame has `missing_dependency` with `value` and the later frame has
`opaque_term`).

### 6.2 The rebuild

Input: a declaration of a blob by `recipe` (a frame or a member) or of a value by `value` (a frame or a member), folded and
ADMITTED by the total rebuilt length of the read (6.3), with its digest `D`, its `total_length` `T` and its entries `(d_1, l_1)`
to `(d_m, l_m)`, which satisfy P4-16 or P4-17. Let `q` be the position of the frame (of the pack frame, for a member). A
declaration that 6.3 refuses does not enter this procedure. The rebuild starts a hash state for `H_ctx(unit, ...)` and an empty
output, and runs:

1. For `k` from `1` to `m` in order: let `u` be the unit used for `d_k` (6.1).
   1. If there is none, or the frame of the unit used stands after the frame of the declaration (6.1, A UNIT SERVES ONLY WHAT
      STANDS AFTER IT), the declaration is INCOMPLETE; report `missing_dependency` with the reason `chunk` at `q`; stop.
   2. If the length of `u` differs from `l_k`, the declaration is INCOMPLETE; report `damaged_frame` with the reason
      `blob_length` at `q`, with the offset and length of that frame; stop.
   3. Append the bytes of `u` to the output and to the hash state.
2. The output has length `T`, because the entry lengths sum to `T` (P4-16) and each unit has the length of its entry.
3. If the hash of the output differs from `D`, the declaration is INCOMPLETE; report `damaged_frame` with the reason
   `blob_digest` at `q`, with the offset and length of that frame; stop.
4. Otherwise the declaration is VERIFIED and the output is the blob or the value.

A `blob` frame or member is VERIFIED when its unit is verified (6.1); it has no entries. An `external` declaration is never
rebuilt and is never missing.

THE STATE OF A DECLARED BLOB, in the sense of this part, is a result of its declarations: it is COMPLETE when some declaration of
its name is a verified `blob` frame or member or a verified rebuild; it is UNCHECKED when its presence is `present`, no
declaration is verified and some declaration of it is refused by the total rebuilt length (6.3): the reader did not look; it is
INCOMPLETE when its presence is `present`, no declaration is verified and none is refused (the bytes are declared to be in the
file, and they are not); it is EXTERNAL when its presence is `external`. A blob that is incomplete or unchecked is in the blob
set (P4-28). The state is a function of the fold and of the reader's `rebuilt_bytes_per_read`: for the fold of the
file, over the declarations of every segment (each recipe rebuilt in its own segment, P4-36); for a segment's own fold, over
the declarations of that segment. One state belongs to one name whatever the number of its declarations.

**P4-37.** A reader that rebuilds a recipe or a value MUST run the steps of 6.2 in that order, MUST stop a declaration at its
first failing step, and MUST report for it exactly the one finding that step gives.

**P4-38.** A reader MUST report `missing_dependency` with the reason `chunk` for a declaration whose entry has no verified unit,
whether no unit of that digest exists or every unit of it has failed its own check; a unit that failed its check is reported
once, at its own frame, as P4-5 and P4-24 say, and is not reported again at the declaration.

**P4-39.** A reader MUST NOT deliver the bytes of a blob or of a value as the blob or the value before its rebuild is verified.
A reader that cannot hold the output spools it; the memory that the procedure itself needs is one unit and the hash state.
Where the output is held (in memory, spooled, or streamed to a caller and discarded) changes no charge and no refusal of 6.3.

**P4-40.** A rebuild that fails leaves every other declaration, every other blob and every other value unaffected. A rebuild
that fails with `damaged_frame` (`blob_length` or `blob_digest`), like every other `damaged_frame` finding of this part on a
frame (P4-5, P4-24), makes the `integrity_state` of the segment `damaged` (Canonical form and reading, "How each state value is
decided"). A `missing_dependency` finding does not.

**P4-41.** A reader MUST report `unreferenced_unit` once for each folded `chunk` frame and each `chunk` member of a folded pack
whose digest is named by no entry of any folded recipe or value of the segment and is the digest of no declared `blob` frame or
`blob` member of the segment, at the position of the frame (of the pack frame, for a member). The rule reads the digest that
the unit states, verified or not: a lone `chunk` frame whose bytes disagree with its digest has its `damaged_frame` and this
finding.

**P4-42.** A reader MUST NOT use the `media_type` of any declaration, and MUST NOT open any blob, to rebuild; a blob that is
itself a file of this format is read by a separate call and its result is not part of the fold of the file that holds it.

Worked example (typed values). A segment holds, in this order: `F1` a `chunk` frame with digest `UA` whose payload has been
altered so that `H_ctx(unit, payload)` differs from `UA` (and whose `payload_digest` was altered with it, so that the envelope
check holds); `F2` a `chunk` frame with digest `UB` and intact bytes of length `5`; `F3` a `chunk` frame with digest `UA` and
intact bytes of length `6`; `F4` a `recipe` over `[(UA, 6), (UB, 5)]`. The unit index holds `F1`, `F2`, `F3` in this order. The
unit used for `UA` is `F3`, the only verified unit of that digest, because `F1` is not verified; the unit used for `UB` is `F2`.
The rebuild is verified when the concatenation hashes to the recipe's digest. The findings are one `damaged_frame` with the reason
`payload_digest` at `F1`, and none at `F4`. If `F2` were absent, the findings would be that one and `missing_dependency` with the
reason `chunk` at `F4`, and the blob of `F4` would be INCOMPLETE. If `F4` stated the length `7` for `UA`, the finding at `F4`
would be `damaged_frame` with `blob_length`, and the declaration would stop there.

### 6.3 The total rebuilt length

A rebuild produces `T` bytes from units already in the input, and the entries of one recipe may name one unit many times, so the
bytes that the rebuilds of a read produce are not bounded by the bytes that the read decodes. The total rebuilt length bounds
them, and it is judged before the work it bounds.

THE CHARGE of a declaration of a blob by `recipe` or of a value by `value`, frame or member, folded, is its `total_length` `T`.
It is a function of the declaration alone: a typed field of its content record, known before any unit is looked up and before
any byte is produced or hashed. It does not depend on which units the segment holds, on whether the rebuild will succeed, on any
other declaration, or on anything an implementation keeps: two declarations of one digest are two charges, and a reader that
rebuilds a digest once and uses the output for both still makes both charges. A `blob` frame or member is not charged, because
its bytes are its decoded payload, which the total decoded length of the read counts (`P1`, "The payload check"); an `external`
frame is not charged, because it has no bytes.

THE ORDER OF THE CHARGES. A reader charges the declarations of a read in one order: the segments in file order, and within a
segment, at the step UNITS AND VALUES of the reader procedure (Canonical form and reading, "The reader procedure"), the
declarations in item order, the members of a pack in directory order at the position of the pack, and the members of an opened
sealed pack (`P8`) in directory order at the position of the sealed frame. It is the order of the unit index (6.1) restricted to
the charged declarations. A sealed pack that the reader does not open charges nothing.

THE TOTAL. The reader keeps the TOTAL REBUILT LENGTH of the read: the sum of the charges of the declarations that it has
admitted. A declaration is ADMITTED when the total kept so far plus its charge is at most `rebuilt_bytes_per_read` (Canonical
form and reading, "Capacity"); its charge is added, and 6.2 runs on it. Otherwise the declaration is REFUSED: 6.2 does not run,
no unit is looked up for it and no byte of it is produced or hashed; the finding is `exceeds_capacity` with the reason
`total_rebuilt`, at the position of the frame (of the pack frame for a member, of the sealed frame for a member of a sealed
pack), with the `offset` and `length` of that frame and, as its `count`, the total kept so far plus its charge, or `2^64 - 1`
when that sum is above `2^64 - 1` (`P7`, "Capacity"); its charge is not
added; and every later declaration of the read, in the order of the charges, is refused in the same way, whatever its charge.

THE TYPED OUTCOME OF A DECLARATION. Every declaration that is charged has exactly one REBUILD STATE: `verified` (step 4 of 6.2),
`incomplete` with the reason of the one finding that 6.2 gives it (`chunk`, `blob_length` or `blob_digest`), or `refused`. A
reader whose `access` does not run the rebuild charges nothing and gives each declaration that this section would charge the
rebuild state `not_run` (Canonical form and reading, "Access: the steps by access"). The result of reading lists it in the frame result of the frame that holds the declaration (Canonical form and reading, "The typed
result of reading"), so the outcome of each declaration, each member of a pack and each candidate of a value included, is
visible.

WHAT A REFUSAL DOES, AND WHAT IT DOES NOT.

- The frame that holds the declaration keeps its fold state: a refusal is not an opaque reason. The declaration declares what it
  declares: it stays in the blob set with its presence, its length and its media types (P4-27 to P4-31), and its length is judged
  by P4-30 as any declaration's. Its entries still name their digests for P4-41 and for the seal scope of `P8`.
- The declaration is neither verified nor incomplete. It produces no `missing_dependency` with `chunk` and no `damaged_frame`,
  and it does not make the segment's integrity `damaged`: it makes it at best `intact_unchecked` (Canonical form and reading, "How
  each state value is decided").
- The blob it declares is UNCHECKED when no other declaration of the name is verified (6.2, THE STATE OF A DECLARED BLOB).
- A `value` declaration that is refused provides no octets. A term whose candidates are all incomplete or refused is unreadable,
  with the finding of each candidate at its own position and no `missing_dependency` (RDF content, "`literal_ref`: a lexical form
  held by a `value` frame"); the rows, bindings and entries that name it are held, and the content digest is `absent`.
- What a refusal does to a graph state, to the canonical check, to compaction and to conversion is stated where each is defined
  (RDF content, "Graph states"; Canonical form and reading, "Compaction" and "The canonical check"; `P9`).

Worked example (typed values). A segment holds `F1` a `chunk` frame of `16384` bytes with digest `U`, and `F2` a `recipe` frame
with `entry_count` `1024`, every entry `[U, 16384]`, and `total_length` `16777216`. Its charge is `16777216`. A reader whose
`rebuilt_bytes_per_read` is `16777215` refuses `F2` before it looks up `U`: one finding `exceeds_capacity` with `total_rebuilt`
and `count` `16777216` at `F2`, the rebuild state `refused`, no `damaged_frame` whatever the blob digest of `F2` holds, the blob
UNCHECKED and in the blob set. A reader whose `rebuilt_bytes_per_read` is `16777216` or more admits `F2`, rebuilds `16777216`
bytes and judges the digest. A recipe of `1000000` entries that each name one unit of `67108864` bytes has the charge
`67108864000000`, above the minimum `2^37` of every conforming reader, and every reader at that minimum refuses it before its
first entry is looked up.

**P4-55.** A reader MUST charge every folded `recipe` or `value` declaration, frame or member, its `total_length`, MUST make
the charge before it looks up a unit for the declaration or produces or hashes a byte of it, and MUST NOT charge a `blob` frame
or member or an `external` frame.

**P4-56.** A reader MUST make the charges in the order of 6.3: the segments in file order, within a segment the declarations in
item order, the members of a pack in directory order at the position of the pack, and the members of an opened sealed pack in
directory order at the position of the sealed frame.

**P4-57.** A reader MUST admit a declaration only when the total rebuilt length kept so far plus its charge is at most its
`rebuilt_bytes_per_read`, MUST refuse every other declaration and every later declaration of the read, MUST NOT run 6.2 on a
refused declaration, and MUST report for each refused declaration one `exceeds_capacity` with the reason `total_rebuilt`, the
`position`, `offset` and `length` of the frame that holds it and the `count` of 6.3.

**P4-58.** A reader MUST give each charged declaration the rebuild state `verified`, `incomplete` with its reason, or
`refused`, and a declaration that its `access` does not rebuild the state `not_run`, MUST keep a refused declaration in the blob set with every field, and MUST NOT report `missing_dependency` or
`damaged_frame` for a refused declaration or count it as damage.

**P4-59.** A reader MUST make the charges as a function of the declarations alone: it MUST charge two declarations of one digest
twice, and MUST NOT let a result it keeps from an earlier rebuild, a cache, or the place where it holds an output change a
charge, an admission or a refusal.

## 7. The canonical value region and blob region

The canonical file places every unit, recipe and value in packs, in two regions that are each a function of the fold alone. This
section defines the regions; the place of the regions among the other frames of a canonical segment, the storage of each frame and
the check that reports a difference are defined by Canonical form and reading ("The construction, from a fold to the item
sequence", "The constants" and "The canonical check"). The pack size and the chunker are the
constants of section 8.

Inputs. The canonical file of a fold is a function of the fold, of the bytes of its present blobs, and of the values of the
canonical file. The bytes of a blob are determined by its name: a name fixes its bytes, because the digest is collision resistant
(section 8). A canonical file exists only for a fold whose present blobs are all verified by 6.2; a writer MUST NOT claim the
canonical form for any other. What a compaction does when a unit of a present blob is missing or damaged is stated, as a typed
outcome, by Canonical form and reading, "Compaction".

Definitions used below.

- The PRESENT BLOBS of the fold are the entries of the blob set whose `presence` is `present`; each has a name, a digest `d`, the
  bytes `x` of that digest, and a list `M` of media types (P4-31).
- Chunk each present blob and each value with the chunker (section 2). Its UNITS are the chunks of its bytes, each as
  `(digest, length)`. A blob with exactly one unit, and the empty blob, are ONE-UNIT BLOBS (the unit of the empty blob is the
  empty byte string, of length `0`, with digest `H_ctx(unit, empty)`); a present blob with two or more units is a MULTI-UNIT
  BLOB. A chunk of a blob never exceeds `262144` bytes (2.4).
- `least(M)` is the smallest media type of `M` by the bytes of `E(media_type)`, as `present`, and `absent` when `M` is empty;
  it does not depend on the order of the declarations in the file.
- THE VALUES OF THE CANONICAL FILE are defined once, by Canonical form and reading, "The construction, from a fold to the item
  sequence" (with "The constants" for the two thresholds): the UTF-8 bytes of the lexical form of each literal that the canonical
  file stores as a `literal_ref` term, and the octets of each typed value that it stores as a `literal_value_ref` term. A fold
  holds no `value` declaration: in the fold a literal is a literal, whatever form stored it. The value region holds exactly these
  values in exactly these octets, and nothing else, one value for each distinct octet string, so that a text and a typed value
  of equal octets share one; the `value` frames of a file that is not canonical are whatever its writer stored. The octets of a
  value are a function of the fold's literal and of its pair of VALUES, and not of any `value` frame, so no rebuild is needed to
  write the region.

THE VALUE REGION. The value region holds ONE value for each distinct octet string, shared by every `literal_ref` and
`literal_value_ref` term whose octets
are equal. The values of the canonical file are taken in ascending order of `value_digest`, compared as byte strings; two octet
strings that are equal have one digest and so one value, and two that differ have two. The region emits members:

1. For each value `v` in that order, with units `(d_1, l_1)` to `(d_m, l_m)`:
   1. for `k` from `1` to `m`: if `d_k` has not been emitted in this region, emit a member of kind `chunk` with `unit_digest`
      `d_k` and the bytes of the unit as its body, and mark `d_k` emitted;
   2. emit a member of kind `value` with content `[value_digest, total_length, m]` and the body `E(list(entry))`.

THE BLOB REGION. Present blobs are taken in ascending order of `E(name)`. The region emits members by the procedure `emit(d)` and
the steps below.

- `emit(d)`: if `d` has not been emitted in this region, mark it emitted and emit one member for the unit with digest `d`: of kind
  `blob`, with content `[d, least(M)]` where `M` is the media type list of the one-unit blob whose digest is `d`, if there is one
  in the fold; otherwise of kind `chunk`, with content `[d]`. The body is the bytes of the unit.
- For each present blob in that order: if it is a one-unit blob, `emit(d)` for its digest. If it is a multi-unit blob with units
  `(d_1, l_1)` to `(d_m, l_m)`: `emit(d_k)` for `k` from `1` to `m`, then emit a member of kind `recipe` with content
  `[d, total_length, least(M), m]` and the body `E(list(entry))`.

Every recipe member therefore follows every unit it names, within its region; a unit shared by two blobs, or by a one-unit blob
and a multi-unit one, is stored once, at its first emission, and a one-unit blob whose digest is emitted first as a unit of an
earlier multi-unit blob is declared by that member.

THE PACKS of a region. The members of a region in emission order are cut into packs by the length of their bodies. Let `P` be the
pack size (section 8), `S` the sum of the `body_length` of the members of the pack being built, initially empty and `0`. For each
member in order, with body length `w`: if the pack is not empty and `S + w > P`, close the pack and start an empty one with `S`
`0`; then add the member and set `S` to `S + w`. At the end of the region close the pack if it is not empty. A member with `w`
above `P` is thereby alone in its pack. The cut uses decoded lengths only, so no boundary depends on an encoder or on a codec.
A pack has `member_count` the number of its members and `unit_length` the sum of the lengths of the bodies of its `chunk` and
`blob` members. The value packs come first, then the blob packs.

THE REGION OF A PACK is read from the segment. A `pack` frame that is folded and holds a `value` member is of the value region;
one that holds a `blob` or `recipe` member is of the blob region; one that holds both is of the region of the first such member
in its directory. A `pack` frame that holds `chunk` members only, and a `pack` frame that is not folded, is of the region of the
first later `pack` frame of the segment that is folded and holds a `value`, `blob` or `recipe` member, and of the blob region
when there is none. The canonical check (`P7`, "The canonical check") uses this region for the kind of a `pack` frame.

THE EXTERNAL FRAMES. After the blob packs, for each pair `(name, media_type)` of declarations in the fold that the packs do not
declare (that is, each name of the fold of `external` presence with each of its distinct media types, or with `absent` if it has
none, and each present name with each of its distinct media types other than `least(M)`), the segment holds one `external` frame
with that `name`, the `length` of the entry, and that `media_type`.
These frames are sorted ascending by the bytes of `E(name)` and then by the bytes of `E(media_type)`.

**P4-43.** A canonical writer MUST emit the value region as section 7 gives it: values in ascending order of `value_digest`,
each distinct unit digest once as a `chunk` member, then the `value` member of each value after its units.

**P4-44.** A canonical writer MUST emit the blob region as section 7 gives it: present blobs in ascending order of `E(name)`,
each distinct unit digest once, as a `blob` member when the digest is that of a one-unit blob of the fold and as a `chunk`
member otherwise, a `recipe` member after the units of each multi-unit blob, and no `recipe` member for a one-unit blob.

**P4-45.** A canonical writer MUST emit the two regions in separate packs: a pack holds members of one region only. A unit
needed in both regions is stored in both.

**P4-46.** A canonical writer MUST cut each region into packs by the rule of section 7 over the lengths of the bodies, with `P`
the pack size of section 8, and MUST write for each pack the `member_count` and `unit_length` that follow.

**P4-47.** A canonical writer MUST write the `external` frames after the blob packs, one for each pair of section 7, in the
order of section 7, and MUST NOT write a `chunk`, `blob`, `recipe` or `value` frame outside a pack.

**P4-48.** A canonical writer MUST write each declaration of the fold, hidden or not, exactly once in its region: compaction
keeps hidden content. A declaration of a blob name hidden by suppression is written as any other.

Worked example (typed values; no vector uses it). The illustration uses the pack size `P = 100` in place of the

`80`; one pack of three members, `member_count` `3`, `unit_length` `7`.

`d(u2) = d(n2)`
is the digest of the one-unit blob `n2`, so a `blob` member `blob(u2, "a/b")` (`5`), with `least(M)` of `n2`; then `recipe(n1, 11, "text/plain",

## 8. Constants, properties and what the format does not have

### 8.1 Constants

- chunker: `W` `64`, `R` `64`, `min` `16384`, `b` `16`, `max` `262144`, `n` `2`, the gear table of 2.2; measurement `FE6-2`: two
  independent implementations gave identical boundaries on every case of the golden set, and insertion of one byte lost no cut
  under the constants of the format; the words of the table are pinned by the vector `chunker-table`, whose bytes the reference
  engine produces and two other computations reproduce (`FE6-1`). The mean chunk on a random stream is `75061` bytes, `1.15`
  times `2^b`, because no cut is tested below `min`, with no chunk at `max`; on the 19 blobs of the reference bundle the mean
  is `107936` bytes and `7.1` per cent of the chunks are cut at `max` (`A1`, "OW-3: chunk-size distribution under the gear
  table of the format")
- pack size `P`: `16777216` bytes of body length; measurements `U-B` and `FE6-8`: on the real bundle 64 MiB packs are `12.4` per
  cent smaller and 4 MiB packs `3.7` per cent larger than 16 MiB packs; a lookup of one member decodes on average half a pack,
  which at 16 MiB is `8.5` MB of output, `17.1` MB of decoder memory and `4.7` ms; neither size dominates on both axes, and the
  canonical form takes the size whose lookup cost is measured and stays within a quarter of the reader's minimum capacity of
  `2^26` bytes; there is one value and no second
- each distinct unit stored once in a region: measurement `U-B`: `27.3` per cent of the blob region
- blob order, ascending `E(name)`: measurements `FE6-7` and `FE5-6`: the four orders measured are within `2.3` per cent; the
  digest order is within `0.6` per cent of the order of first reference and needs no scan of the rows
- one zstd frame for a pack, no block framing: measurement `FE6-8`: independent frames of 1 MiB cost `7.5` per cent more stored
  bytes over the blob region of the real bundle; and `U-C`: one engine decodes dependent blocks to wrong bytes with no error
- hash: `blake3_256` only (registry version `1`); no other algorithm is defined for these names

**P4-49.** A canonical writer MUST cut packs with `P = 16777216`.

### 8.2 Properties

PROPERTY 1, the canonical regions are a function of the fold. The inputs of section 7 are: the set of present blobs with their
names, lengths and media types (a function of the blob set, P4-27 to P4-31, which depends on the declarations by value and not
on their order: the length is the smallest declared and the media types are a set); the values of the canonical file (7), a
function of the fold's literals and the threshold; the bytes of each
blob and value (a function of its name, by the assumption below); the chunker, a deterministic function of bytes (2.3); the
ascending orders of names and digests, which are total orders on distinct byte strings; the emission procedure, which depends on
nothing else; and `P`. No input is a frame boundary, a position, an arrival order, a storage form or a segment. Hence two folds
with equal inputs have the same value region and the same blob region.

PROPERTY 2, compaction keeps the blob set. Let the fold have a blob set `B`, and let every present blob be verified with a length
equal to the `length` of its entry. The canonical file declares each present name by exactly one member, with the length of its
bytes (the entry's length), and its media types as `least(M)` of the member together with the media types of the `external` frames
of that name, which are the remaining members of `M`; so the media types of the name are `M`, `presence` is `present`, and the
`length` is that of the entry. Each external name has `external` frames that carry every one of its media types, or one with
`absent`, and the entry's `length`; presence is `external` and the fields are those of the entry. No other name is declared. The
blob set of the canonical file is therefore `B`, and the `blob_declaration` record lines of the labelled set do not change.

PROPERTY 3, a verified rebuild yields the named bytes. Assumption: `BLAKE3` in key-derivation mode with a context string is a
collision-resistant and second-preimage-resistant function of its message for each context of the context table. Then a unit whose
digest verifies has the bytes that the name fixes; a rebuild whose output hashes to `D` has the bytes that `D` names; and two
distinct byte strings are not both verified under one name. A rebuild that verifies proves that the units were the right ones,
not that the cut was the canonical one: a reader never needs the cut.

PROPERTY 4, the unit used is the same for every reader. Two readers that decode the same frames and members (the same registry
version and the same stated capacity) build the same unit index and use the same first verified unit of each digest and the
same value for each pair of a digest and a length that a term names (P4-54); by Property 3 the copies of one digest that are verified have the same bytes, so they rebuild the
same bytes and report the same findings.

PROPERTY 5, the window property of the cut (2.4): a mask test at `p` depends only on the `W` bytes that end at `p` and on the
length since the previous cut. Resynchronisation after an edit is not guaranteed within `max` or at all: a run of bytes on which
no mask cuts is cut only at `max`, and a one-byte shift of such a run never realigns the cut points. Locality of the cut points
after an edit is a statistical expectation measured in `A1` (FE6-2), not a bound.

**P4-50.** A difference of a canonical segment from the canonical file of its fold that is located in a `pack` of the value
region, or is a `value` frame outside a pack, MUST be reported as `canonical_violated` with the reason `value_region`; one located
in a `pack` of the blob region, in an `external` frame, or in a `chunk`, `blob` or `recipe` frame outside a pack, with the reason
`blob_region`. A `pack` frame or an `external` frame is so reported when the canonical file holds an item of its own kind at its
position and the two differ; one that stands where the canonical file holds an item of another kind, or none, is a difference
of `frame_sequence`, as is a value pack exchanged with a blob pack. A `value`, `chunk`, `blob` or `recipe` frame outside a pack
is reported with its region wherever it stands. Which difference is the first is decided by item order (Canonical form and
reading, "The canonical check").

**P4-51.** A writer MUST NOT claim the canonical form for a fold that holds a present blob that 6.2 does not verify, or a present
blob whose length differs from the `length` of its entry; the outcome of a compaction asked for such a fold is stated by
Canonical form and reading, "Compaction".

**P4-52.** The blob set of a canonical file MUST equal the blob set of the fold of which it is the canonical file.

### 8.3 What the format does not have

**P4-53.** No frame, member or storage form of this part holds a unit as a difference against another unit: the decoded payload of
a `chunk` or `blob` frame, and the body of a `chunk` or `blob` member, are the bytes of the unit itself. The chunker has no
identifier, no declared parameter, no keyed variant and no registry entry. A recipe has no continuation, no external flag and no
chunker declaration; a frame has no extras map; a blob has no reference rank; a blob name has no spelling in an RDF literal.
Every value of every field of a frame of this part is read from its typed field and never from a spelling. No
literal is taken for a typed value because of its text (`P3`, "Typed values").
