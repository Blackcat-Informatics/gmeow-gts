# Canonical labelling of blank nodes: version 1

This text defines version `1` of a canonical labelling of the blank nodes of a set of typed lines of five kinds. Its identifier
is the pair of the name `nested-refinement-labelling` and the version `1` (7.15).

Conventions. `MUST` and `never` state requirements on a conforming engine. A word in capitals is defined where it first
appears. Identifiers and formulas are written in backticks. `u64(n)` is the eight-byte big-endian form of an unsigned integer
`n`. `bits(v)` is the number of binary digits of an unsigned integer `v` (`bits(0) = 0`, `bits(7) = 3`, `bits(8) = 4`).
`ceil(x / 8)` is integer division by eight rounded up. A section number such as 5.5 names a part of this text; a term may be used before
the section that defines it, and its number is then given at the use. The notation of the
examples (`<p>`, `<<(s p o)>>`, `quad(s, p, o, g)` and the like) is defined at the head of section 12.

## 1. Scope and result

1. The input is a finite SEQUENCE of typed lines (section 3) over a set of blank nodes, in which by (W3) no line occurs twice; so
   the lines form a SET, and the order of the sequence is never read. A blank node of the input is named by its
   SOURCE IDENTIFIER, an opaque byte string: two blank leaves denote one node exactly when their source identifiers are equal
   as byte strings. The definition never reads the form of a source identifier; it only compares two whole identifiers
   bytewise (7.9). The labelling never sees text, and no rule of this text parses a string.
2. The RESULT of the labelling is three typed values.
   (a) The CANONICAL MAP: for every blank node of the input, its `position` (an unsigned integer, 6.5) and its `identity`
   (the typed triple `(component: 32 bytes, local position: unsigned, copy index: unsigned)`, 7.8). It is a typed map from
   source identifiers to these values (7.9). A listing of the map that is written or reported as a sequence has its entries in
   ascending `position`; the positions of the nodes are distinct (6.5), so this order is total.
   (b) The CANONICAL LINE SEQUENCE: every input line, those with no blank node included, with each blank leaf replaced by
   `fixed(position)`, in ascending bytewise order of the line encoding (3.7), together with its DIGEST (7.6).
   (c) The STATISTICS RECORD (7.7), a typed record, which states among other values whether the input is `guaranteed` (7.10). The CLAIM of
   the labelling is (b). It is defined by the smallest certificate over the leaves of a search tree fixed by a normative refinement
   (section 5) and a normative selector (section 6). It is never defined by pruning, by traversal order or by the order of the input. The
   map (a) is a second normative object (7.9); the claim about (b) holds for any map that yields (b).
3. Two inputs have equal canonical line sequences exactly when they are isomorphic: some bijection of their blank nodes maps
   the lines of one onto the lines of the other, every constant and every annotation count being kept (section 9, property
   2). This rests on the completeness of certificates (6.8) and, for inputs with two or more non-isomorphic top components, on the
   assumption of 7.15.
4. The definition has no limit on work. An engine declares a limit `L` of limited units (8.3); whether it refuses an input with
   `limit` is a function of the input as given and of `L`, subject to the guarantee of 7.10. A claim of canonicity is
   checkable by a reader (7.13) and is not a property of the data.
5. ROUTE INDEPENDENCE. The canonical line sequence, its digest, the positions, the identities and the structural record
   depend only on the SET of typed lines, not on the route by which they were produced: two routes that yield the same set
   of typed lines yield the same result. This holds by construction, since every step that produces typed lines is outside
   the definition (section 11). The one exception is the canonical map (7.9): it, and with it which node of an orbit receives
   which identity and every copy index, depends also on the source identifiers.
6. RECORDS AND POSITIONS. Labels and positions are those of the WHOLE input. The same lines of the four kinds of RDF statements, with
   and without `record` lines (3.2), get different positions and different canonical line sequences, and the digest of the lines
   alone is another value than the digest of the lines and the records. The positions, identities and digest of one labelling are
   never mixed into another. For an input with no `record` line no rule of this text that concerns records applies (the payload ranks
   of 7.12 are a class of their own and are empty), so its results are those of the four kinds of RDF statements alone.
7. No label is ever read back. The label spelling of 7.14 is an output for document formats that require text; no rule of
   this text, and no conforming implementation, parses a label to recover a position, an identity or any other meaning.

REQUIREMENT LANGUAGE. The key words "MUST", "MUST NOT", "REQUIRED", "SHALL", "SHALL NOT", "SHOULD", "SHOULD NOT",
"RECOMMENDED", "NOT RECOMMENDED", "MAY", and "OPTIONAL" in this document are to be interpreted as described in BCP 14
[RFC2119] [RFC8174] when, and only when, they appear in all capitals, as shown here. This document uses the key words MUST,
MUST NOT and MAY, and no other.

## 2. Values and their order

1. Every value compared is an unsigned 64-bit integer, a byte string, a finite list of values, or a typed value with its
   encoding (3.7). All integers in this text are unsigned 64-bit integers. An engine that would need a larger value refuses
   with the typed refusal `width` (7.11) and never wraps or saturates; the two exceptions are the allowance (7.10) and the running total of the walk (8.3),
   which saturate.
2. Integers compare numerically. Byte strings compare bytewise, each byte an unsigned integer from `0` to `255`, a proper prefix
   before its extensions: this is the order of source identifiers, which are compared whole as byte strings (7.9). A byte string
   inside a typed value is compared through the encoding of the value (3.7), hence by its length first (3.7(c)). Lists compare element
   by element, a proper prefix before its extensions; in particular the empty list is smaller than every other list. Two
   values of different kinds are never compared: every list position that is compared holds one kind. Typed values compare by
   their encodings (3.7), bytewise.
3. No floating-point number, no locale and no container iteration order is used. No hash decides anything inside the search
   of sections 5 and 6. The hash `H` (7.15) is used only for the two shared values of 7.6 and 7.8 and, through the component
   identity, for the order of the top components (7.3). No sort of this text depends on its stability. Every sort is by a total order of its elements, and elements that compare equal
   are equal values: the labels of a key (5.2), the entries of one `E` (6.3), the failure records and the factor records (7.7), the
   encodings of lines (7.6) and the ranks (7.12). Where different elements may tie, the text orders them: nested calls and top
   components with equal certificates or identities by 7.9 (M2), and the children of a rule 3 call by source identifier (6.1).
   Vertices put in one fragment by a split (5.3) form one cell, which has no inner order (5.1).

## 3. Typed lines, masking and encoding

3.1 TERMS. A TERM is one of four variants: `iri(bytes)`; `literal(lexical: bytes, datatype: bytes, language: absent |
present(bytes), direction: absent | present(ltr | rtl))`; `blank(b)`, with `b` the source identifier of a blank node of the
input; `triple(s, p, o)`, three terms, nesting allowed (written `<<(s p o)>>` in this text). Absence is always the explicit variant
`absent`, never an empty string
or a sentinel. A source identifier is any byte string, the empty one included. Every literal carries its datatype; its
language and direction are options, and the labelling gives no meaning to their combination: every combination is a distinct
value, encoded as given (3.7). Language tags are compared as bytes; a format that compares them without regard to letter case
writes them in one case before lines reach the labelling. A term of another kind (for example a triple term with no content) has
no variant in this version; an input that holds one, a direction that is neither `ltr` nor `rtl`, or a `free` or `fixed` leaf
presented as input, is refused as `input` with the reason `slot` (W1, 7.11).

3.2 LINES. A LINE is a tagged variant of a closed list of five KINDS. The tag of a kind (3.7) is its one integer code; no
other number identifies a kind.

| tag | kind | slots, in order |
|---|---|---|
| `0x40` | `quad` | `subject`, `predicate`, `object`, `graph: absent \| present(term)` |
| `0x41` | `reifier-binding` | `reifier`, `triple`, `graph: absent \| present(term)` |
| `0x42` | `annotation` | `count: unsigned`, `reifier`, `predicate`, `object`, `graph: absent \| present(term)` |
| `0x43` | `empty-graph` | `graph` (a term) |
| `0x44` | `record` | `payload: bytes`, `terms: list(term)` |

SLOTS ADMIT VARIANTS. A `predicate` slot admits only `iri`. The `triple` slot of a `reifier-binding` admits only `triple`. Every other
slot, every term of the list of a `record`, and every position inside a `triple` term, admits every variant of 3.1; any further
restriction a data model places on terms is applied before lines reach the labelling. No rule of the labelling reads either restriction: a
line's predicate is an `iri` because a line is a statement of a dataset, whose predicates are IRIs, and the positions inside a triple term
are left open so that a format with less restricted triple terms loses nothing. WHAT THE KINDS DENOTE. A dataset is a default graph and a
set of named graphs, each named graph a pair of a name and a graph, and a graph may be empty. A `quad` line is one statement; its graph
slot is `absent` for the default graph and holds the name otherwise. A `reifier-binding` line states that its reifier stands for its
triple term, and an `annotation` line states one property of a reifier; each is a statement of the graph its graph slot names. A format
that carries reifiers and annotations as rows of their own supplies these kinds, and one that does not supplies none. A `record` line
denotes nothing (below). A line CARRIES a name when its kind has a graph slot (`quad`, `reifier-binding`, `annotation`) and that slot is
`present` and holds the name; a name held anywhere else in a line (as a subject, an object, a reifier, or inside a triple term) is not
carried. An `empty-graph` line denotes that the pair (its name, the empty graph) is in the dataset. Every named graph is therefore denoted
exactly once: a non-empty one by the lines that carry its name, an empty one by its `empty-graph` line. An `empty-graph` line is supplied
for each empty named graph and for nothing else. Two consequences, stated so that they cannot be read otherwise: (i) a name carried ONLY
by `reifier-binding` or `annotation` lines, with no `quad` line, names a NON-EMPTY graph (a reifier binding or an annotation in a graph is
a statement in that graph), so it has no `empty-graph` line, and supplying one for it is ill formed (3.3, W5); (ii) a name that occurs in
lines only as a subject, an object, a reifier or inside a triple term, and is carried by no line, is not thereby a named graph: if the
dataset has a named graph of that name and the graph is empty, its `empty-graph` line is supplied (12.7 shows a blank node that is the
name of an empty graph and also a subject). Nothing is inferred from the absence of a line. With these rules the set of lines that denotes
a dataset is a function of the abstract dataset, once a format has fixed which statements it supplies as `reifier-binding` and
`annotation` lines. Two formats share the results of this labelling for a dataset only if they supply the same lines for it: a format that
writes a reifier binding as a `quad` line and one that writes it as a `reifier-binding` line obtain different results, and that choice is
the format's (section 11). THE RECORD KIND. A `record` line holds a byte string, its PAYLOAD, and a finite LIST of terms in a fixed order.
The payload may be empty and the list may be empty. The payload is ONE CONSTANT: the labelling compares it whole, charges it by its length
like any constant, never reads it, gives it no meaning, and reserves no value and no prefix of it. A record DENOTES NOTHING in RDF: it is
no statement of a graph, it has no graph slot and carries no name, it takes no part in (W5) or in the rule for `empty-graph` lines, and a
name that occurs only inside records is not thereby a named graph. It takes FULL part in everything else: its blank leaves are
occurrences, in list order and at any depth inside its terms, as the slots of any other kind are (3.4); it is a line of its top component,
a vertex of the incidence graph, a line of every refinement, of every twin test, of every certificate, of the canonical line sequence and
of both digests. A blank node that occurs only in records is a node like any other (12.12). What a payload means is the meaning a format
gives it (section 11); a line is supplied by a format that has such lines, and a format that has none supplies none. For an input with no
`record` line no rule that concerns records applies, and its results are those of the four kinds of RDF statements alone (section 12,
blocks 12.1 to 12.7). THE COUNT. Only the `annotation` kind has a `count`, an unsigned integer at least `1`: the multiplicity with which a
format carries that annotation (a format whose annotations form a set always supplies `1`; a caller whose annotations form a multiset
supplies ONE `annotation` line whose count is the number of identical rows, and `k` identical lines are ill formed (W3)). The labelling
treats the count as a field of the line: it takes part in the encoding and therefore in every order, and has no further meaning here.
Reason for one line with a count rather than `k` identical lines: the input stays a set, so the incidence graph is simple, no identical
line vertices add automorphisms that carry no information, and certificates are smaller. A multiplicity on another kind would be a new
kind or a new version, never a field of an existing kind. A blank node may occur in any slot, at any depth inside a `triple` term, as a
reifier and as a graph name; every such occurrence takes part in the labelling in the same way (3.4). The blank name of an empty graph is
an occurrence like any other.

3.3 WELL-FORMED INPUT. An input is WELL FORMED when all of the following hold:
(W1) SLOT: every slot, every term of the list of a `record`, and every position inside a term, holds a variant of 3.1 that the place
admits (3.2), and the payload of a `record` is a byte string. A refusal for an input value that a typed interface cannot represent
(a direction that is neither `ltr` nor `rtl`, a `free` or `fixed` leaf supplied as input) binds only an engine whose input type can
carry that value;
(W2) COUNT: every `annotation` count is at least `1`;
(W3) REPEATED: no two lines of one kind agree in every field (two identical lines included); the annotation count is the ONLY field that
this condition ignores. The kind is part of the line: `quad(a, <p>, c)` and `annotation(1, a, <p>, c)` are two lines of two kinds and an
input that holds both is well formed. A `record` is compared WHOLE, its payload and its entire list of terms, the order of the list
included: `record("m", [a, b])` and `record("m", [b, a])` are two records. The interface receives a SEQUENCE of lines, and this condition
makes it a set; an engine that collected the lines into a set before checking would have repaired the input;
(W4) KIND: every line is of one of the five kinds (an interface that receives a kind as a number receives one of the five tags). (W3)
compares the lines of
the five known kinds only: a line of no known kind is refused for its kind and takes part in no comparison of (W3). This sentence
says which lines (W3) compares; it does not decide an outcome. When an input violates (W4) and another condition, the outcome is
decided by 7.11 like any other: the input [a line of no known kind, `quad(b, <p>, c)`, `quad(b, <p>, c)`] is refused as `repeated`;
(W5) GRAPH: no line carries (3.2) a term that an `empty-graph` line holds. Two terms are equal exactly when they are the same
typed value: equal variants, equal constant fields, and at every depth equal source identifiers at the blank leaves. No encoding
is used (a term that holds a blank leaf has none, 3.7). Examples, each a well-formed input that is labelled:
`empty-graph(<<(a <p> b)>>)` with `quad(a, <p>, b, <<(b <p> a)>>)`, and `empty-graph(<<(a <p> <o>)>>)` with
`quad(x, <p>, y, <<(b <p> <o>)>>)`: in each pair the two triple terms are different typed values, although they are equal
when every blank leaf is read as `free`;
(W6) WIDTH: every byte string length (a payload included), every number of terms of a `record` and every count is at most `2^64 - 1`,
and every quantity of 7.11 fits in 64 bits (the allowance saturates instead, 7.10). A count above `2^64 - 1` is the refusal `width`
with the quantity `count`, not the refusal `input`.
An input that is not well formed is REFUSED, with the typed refusal `input` for (W1) to (W4), `graph` for (W5) and `width` for
(W6) (7.11). These conditions are checked on the input before any labelling; the labelling detects every one of them itself,
since it sees every line, and (W5) in particular needs no outside knowledge. An engine never repairs an input: it never sums
counts, removes a line, drops a duplicate or changes a term.
THE EMPTY INPUT, with no line, is well formed and is labelled: it has no top component, its canonical line sequence is empty, its
digest is `H(context(lines), u64(0))` (7.6, 7.15), the 32 bytes `bf4b40a613c1cc97ababeccefc1c1c810dde52818d8928603f2180376f06fb5e`,
and its record (7.7) holds the number `0` in every field of numbers, the empty list in every list and `guaranteed` true.

3.4 OCCURRENCES. The OCCURRENCES of a line are its `blank` leaves in a depth-first, left-to-right walk of its slots in the order of the
table of 3.2 (the `count` and the payload are no slot; the terms of a `record` are its slots, in list order), descending into `triple`
terms in the order `s`, `p`, `o`. A node that occurs `r` times in a line has `r` occurrences. Every operation that changes a line replaces
leaves of the typed value, never bytes.

3.5 FIXED AND FREE. In a call (section 6) some nodes are FIXED: each has a PATH POSITION `q`, an unsigned integer (6.2). The
other nodes of the call are FREE. The FREE OCCURRENCES of a line are the occurrences of free nodes, numbered `0, 1, ...` in
walk order; fixed occurrences carry no number.

3.6 MASKED LINE. The MASKED LINE of a line in a call is the same typed value with each `blank` leaf replaced by one of two
variants: `free` for a free node, `fixed(q)` for a fixed node of path position `q`. Every other field is unchanged, the
annotation count and the payload of a record included. A masked line with no `free` leaf is FULLY FIXED. A line with no blank node is its
own masked line.

3.7 ENCODING. Every masked line has ONE normative byte encoding, built from three primitives: `u64(n)`; `bytes(b)`, which is
`u64(length of b)` followed by the bytes of `b`; and the one-byte TAG of a variant. The tags are fixed by this table; no other
tag value is used.

| tag | variant | fields after the tag, in order |
|---|---|---|
| `0x01` | `iri` | `bytes(value)` |
| `0x02` | `literal` | `bytes(lexical)`, `bytes(datatype)`, option of `bytes(language)`, option of a direction |
| `0x03` | `triple` | three terms |
| `0x04` | `free` | none |
| `0x05` | `fixed` | `u64(q)` |
| `0x20` | `absent` | none |
| `0x21` | `present` | one value of the type the option holds |
| `0x30` | `ltr` | none |
| `0x31` | `rtl` | none |
| `0x40` | `quad` | `subject`, `predicate`, `object`, option of a term (the graph) |
| `0x41` | `reifier-binding` | `reifier`, `triple` term, option of a term (the graph) |
| `0x42` | `annotation` | `u64(count)`, `reifier`, `predicate`, `object`, option of a term (the graph) |
| `0x43` | `empty-graph` | one term |
| `0x44` | `record` | `bytes(payload)`, `u64(n)`, then `n` terms, `n` the length of the list |

An option is `absent` (`0x20`, no field) or `present` (`0x21`, then one value). The direction option holds `absent`, or
`present` followed by `ltr` or `rtl`. Example: the masked line `quad(free, iri(p), free)` with the graph `absent` is the
fourteen bytes `40 04 01 0000000000000001 70 04 20`. The masked line `record("m", [free])`, payload the byte `m` (`0x6d`) and a list of
one term, is the nineteen bytes `44 0000000000000001 6d 0000000000000001 04`.
LEMMA E (the encoding is injective and prefix-free). PROVED. Induction on the structure of the value. The first byte is the
tag; it names the variant, and the variant fixes the sequence of the types of its fields. A field of type `u64` is the next
eight bytes; a field of type `bytes` is read by its length, so its content, which may hold any byte value including a tag value,
is never read as a tag; an option is read by its first byte and then, if `present`, by the type it holds; a term is read by
this same procedure; a `record` is read as its tag, then `bytes(payload)` by its length (the payload may be empty and may hold any
byte values), then `u64(n)`, then exactly `n` terms, each by this procedure, so a decoder knows where the record ends, the empty list
included. A decoder that follows these rules needs no look-ahead and consumes exactly the bytes of the encoding.
Hence no encoding is a proper prefix of another, and the decoder inverts the encoding, so distinct values have distinct
encodings. A leaf `fixed(q)` is the byte `0x05` and eight bytes; no other value produces those bytes where a decoder expects a
term, because the content of every `bytes` field is skipped by its length. End of proof.
CONSEQUENCES. (a) Two distinct masked lines never have one encoding as a prefix of the other, so their bytewise order is decided
at the first differing byte, inside both. (b) The order of two masked lines is NORMATIVELY the bytewise order of their encodings,
wherever the definition orders masked lines: the start of a top component (5.6), the entries of a certificate (6.3) and the
canonical line sequence (7.6). (c) Every integer has fixed width and big-endian form, so integers inside an encoding compare
numerically (path position `10` follows `9`); a byte string is preceded by its length, so a shorter byte string precedes a
longer one whatever their contents. (d) The count of an annotation precedes its terms, so annotation lines order first by
count. (e) The payload of a record precedes its list, and its length precedes its bytes and the length of the list its terms, so
records order by payload (a shorter payload first, then bytewise), then by the number of terms, then by the terms in order.

3.8 NO SPELLING CARRIES MEANING. Every comparison is on typed values or on their encodings; a source identifier is compared
whole and never inspected; a hash context is hashed whole and never inspected; the label spelling of 7.14 is output only.
Nothing a rule produces is parsed by another rule. The table lists every rule that consumes a value the definition produced.

| rule | what it reads | typed or opaque |
|---|---|---|
| start of a top component (5.6) | encodings of masked lines with every leaf `free` | typed, compared bytewise |
| `KEY` and `SPLIT` (5.2, 5.3) | labels: lists of free occurrence numbers | typed integers |
| the queue (5.4, 5.5) | side numbers, colours, cell sizes | typed integers |
| FIX (6.2) | colours of singleton node cells | typed integers |
| entries (6.3) | `m` and the encoding of the fully fixed masked line | typed integer and typed encoding |
| order of certificates (6.3) | `s`, then the entries of `E`, then `C` | typed |
| final positions (6.5) | the call structure: offsets, arguments `p`, path positions, `s`, sizes `n_K` | typed integers; nothing is recovered from a certificate's contents |
| REBUILD (6.8) | a certificate | typed |
| order of top components (7.3) | the component identity (32 bytes, compared whole), then the smallest source identifier | opaque bytes compared whole |
| the canonical map (7.9) | source identifiers | opaque bytes compared whole |
| component identity (7.8) | the certificate encoding, hashed whole | typed encoding, opaque digest |
| digest (7.6) | the number of lines and the line encodings, hashed whole | typed encoding, opaque digest |
| twin test (7.10) | typed lines, compared as values | typed |
| record (7.7) | integers of the steps above | typed integers |
| label spelling (7.14) | a position | typed integer; the label is output only |

## 4. The incidence graph

1. The INCIDENCE GRAPH of a set of free nodes and the lines holding at least one of them has the nodes and the lines as
   vertices. A node `u` and a line `l` are INCIDENT when `u` occurs in `l`. The LABEL of the incidence is the ascending list of
   the free occurrence numbers of `u` in `l` (one element for each free occurrence of `u` in `l`).
2. Two free nodes are CONNECTED when one line holds both; a fixed node connects nothing. Given a set `F` of fixed nodes, a FREE
   COMPONENT is a connected component of the incidence graph of the nodes outside `F` and of the lines that hold at least one
   of them. A line holds free nodes of exactly one free component.
3. The NODES of a component are its free nodes; its LINES are the lines holding at least one of them. `n_K` is the number of
   nodes of a component `K`, `m_K` its number of lines, and `M_K` its number of INCIDENCES (a node that occurs twice in a line
   is one incidence). `O_K`, the number of LABEL ELEMENTS, is the number of free occurrences in its lines. Colours, cells and
   offsets are relative to one component.

## 5. Refinement

5.1 ORDERED PARTITIONS. A call holds two ordered partitions: of the nodes of its component (SIDE `0`) and of its lines (SIDE
`1`). A CELL is a part of one of them. The COLOUR of a cell is the number of vertices of its side in the cells before it. A cell
of colour `c` and size `s` therefore occupies the positions `c .. c + s - 1` of its side, and splitting one cell never changes
the colour of another. A partition is DISCRETE when every cell has one vertex. The vertices inside one cell have no order.

5.2 KEY. For a vertex `x` and a set `W` of vertices of the other side, `KEY(x, W)` is the list, sorted ascending, of the labels
(4.1) of the incidences between `x` and members of `W`, one item for each incidence. It is the empty list when there is none.
Items are lists of integers and compare as in section 2.

5.3 SPLIT. `SPLIT(X, W)`, for a cell `X` of colour `c` and a set `W` of the other side:

1. Group the vertices of `X` by equal `KEY(x, W)`.
2. If there is one group, `X` is unchanged.
3. Otherwise the groups are the FRAGMENTS of `X`, placed in ascending order of key (the group with the empty key, if any,
   therefore comes first).
4. The fragments replace `X` and take the colours `c`, `c + s_1`, `c + s_1 + s_2`, ... in that order, `s_i` being their sizes.
   The first fragment keeps the colour `c`.

5.4 QUEUE. The QUEUE is a set of pairs `(side, colour)`, each naming a cell of the current partition. Its NEXT pair is its
smallest, compared by side first and then by colour. The queue is keyed by colour, never by cell identity: a pair `(s, c)` names
whichever cell of side `s` has colour `c` at the moment it is read. THE QUEUE RULE, applied when a cell `X` of side `s` and colour
`c` has been split into fragments `F_1 .. F_k` (`k` at least 2):

1. If `(s, c)` is in the queue, the pairs of all fragments are added (`(s, c)` already names `F_1`).
2. Otherwise the pairs of all fragments are added except the pair of the LARGEST fragment. Among fragments of maximal size the
   one of smallest colour is the largest.

5.5 REFINE. While the queue is not empty:

1. Remove the next pair `(s, c)` from the queue, and let `W` be the cell of side `s` and colour `c`.
2. List the cells of the other side, `1 - s`, that have at least two vertices and hold a vertex incident to a member of `W`. Order
   the list by ascending colour.
3. Visit the listed cells in that order. For each listed cell `X`, apply `SPLIT(X, W)` and, if `X` split, the queue rule to its
   fragments. A split changes the colour of no other cell (5.1), so each listed colour still names its listed cell when that cell
   is visited; `W` lies on the other side and is not changed during the visits.
When the queue is empty the pair of partitions is STABLE: for every cell `W` of either side, all vertices of each cell `X` of the
other side have one key with respect to `W`. Remark: a cell with no vertex incident to `W` has the empty key for every vertex,
and a cell of one vertex has one key, so SPLIT leaves both unchanged; visiting every cell of the other side in step 2 therefore
gives the same result, and step 2 only saves work. Within one pop the listed cells may also be visited in ANY order with the same
result: `W` does not change during the visits, a split changes no other cell's colour, each split reads only `W` and the cell
split, and the queue is a set, so after the last visit the partitions and the queue are the same whatever the order.

5.6 FRESH START OF A TOP COMPONENT. A top component (7.1) starts alone, from nothing inherited. Its node partition has one cell,
of colour `0`. Its lines are placed in cells in ascending order of the ENCODING (3.7) of their masked lines with every leaf
`free`, equal encodings forming one cell. The count of an annotation is part of that encoding (3.7(d)); no other key is used.
The queue holds the pair of every cell of both sides. Then REFINE. Nothing is refined over the whole input (7.5).

5.7 INHERITED START OF A NESTED CALL. A call on a free component that is not a top component does not start from one cell. It
takes the ordered partitions of its parent restricted to its own nodes and lines, with the order of the cells kept and the
colours re-offset by the convention of 5.1 (the first cell of each side has colour `0`). The queue is empty: the inherited pair
is stable (section 9, property 7). Only the ORDER of the cells matters: an engine may keep the parent's colours, gaps included,
or any order-preserving renaming of them (8.2).

5.8 INDIVIDUALISATION. To individualise the node `v` of a node cell `X` of colour `c` and size at least 2, in a stable pair of
partitions with an empty queue:

1. `X` is split into the fragments `{v}` (colour `c`) and `X` without `v` (colour `c + 1`).
2. The queue rule (5.4) is applied as for a cell that is not in the queue: of two fragments of equal size the one of smaller
   colour is the largest and is left out. Hence with `|X| = 2` the fragment `{v}` is left out and the pair `(0, c + 1)` is
   queued; with `|X|` greater than 2 the largest is `X` without `v` and the pair `(0, c)` is queued.
3. REFINE. During this REFINE `v` is a vertex of a singleton cell and its occurrences are free occurrences; it is fixed by the
   FIX that follows (6.1).
The result is a function of the input alone: the queue is empty before step 2, and step 2 reads only sizes and colours.

5.9 LEMMAS (all with respect to one call).

LEMMA 1 (the result is stable). PROVED. Keys are additive: `KEY(x, A union B)` is the sorted merge of `KEY(x, A)` and
`KEY(x, B)` for disjoint `A`, `B`; so stability with respect to `A` and to a subset `B` of `A` gives stability with respect to `A`
without `B` (the multiset difference of two sorted lists is determined by them). Invariant: for every cell `Y` not in the queue
there is a set `A(Y)` containing `Y` such that the other side is stable with respect to `A(Y)` and `A(Y)` without `Y` is a union
of queued cells. At the fresh start every cell is queued. Processing `W` makes the other side stable with respect to `W`, so
`A(W) = W`; any `Y` whose `A(Y)` contains `W` replaces `A(Y)` by `A(Y)` without `W`. A split of an unqueued `X` gives the unqueued
largest fragment `L` the set `A(X)`, and `A(X)` without `L` is `A(X)` without `X` together with the queued fragments. A split of a
queued `X` queues every fragment. Splitting never destroys the stability of a set, because the fragments of a cell with one key
each have one key. When the queue is empty `A(Y) = Y` for every cell. The same argument holds from a stable pair after
individualisation, where the invariant starts with the unsplit `X` as `A` of its unqueued largest fragment. End of proof.

LEMMA 2 (coarsest stable). PROVED. At every moment of REFINE, every stable pair `rho` of partitions that refines the start also
refines the current pair. A cell `W` of the current pair is a union of cells of `rho`; vertices of `X` in one cell of `rho` have
equal keys for each cell of `rho`, hence for `W` by additivity, so `rho` refines `SPLIT(X, W)`. With Lemma 1 the end of REFINE is
the COARSEST stable refinement of the start. In it two nodes share a cell only if they have equal multisets of (line cell, own
label), and lines in one line cell have equal masked lines and equal node cells at each free occurrence number. End of proof.

LEMMA 3 (the order is a function of the input alone). PROVED. Each decision reads only side numbers, colours, cell sizes, labels
and encodings of masked lines: the next pair, which cells are split, the order of the fragments, which fragment is left out.
None reads a source identifier or a storage order. By induction on the steps, an isomorphism between two inputs maps the cells at
each step onto the cells at the same step with the same colours. Individualisation is equivariant in the same way. End of proof.

LEMMA 4 (work of refinement). PROVED. Let REFINE run on a component either from the fresh start (every cell queued) or from a
stable pair with the fragments of one individualisation queued. `deg(x)` is the number of incidences of a vertex `x`. The label
elements read by REFINE are the sum, over incidences, of the length of the label times the number of popped cells that contain
the line of the incidence plus the number of popped cells that contain its node.
(a) One REFINE. A vertex `x` lies in at most `bits(t)` popped cells, `t` the size of its cell at its first pop. Proof. At any
moment `x` is in one cell and the queue holds at most one pair naming that cell. Let `W_1, W_2, ...` be the popped cells that
contain `x`, in order. Right after `W_i` is popped the cell of `x` is not in the queue. A pair is added only by the queue rule; the
first re-queueing of the cell of `x` after the pop of `W_i` is therefore a split of its cell `X` at that time, a subset of `W_i`
(cells only shrink), of the case where `X` was not queued and the fragment `F` of `x` is not the largest. The largest fragment
has maximal size, so `|F|` plus that size is at most `|X|`, hence `|F| <= |X| / 2 <= |W_i| / 2`. Later splits while queued keep
`x` in a queued subset of `F`, so `W_(i+1)` is contained in `F` and `|W_(i+1)| <= |W_i| / 2`. Every size is at least 1, so the
chain has at most `1 + floor(log_2 |W_1|) = bits(|W_1|)` terms. Hence the label elements read are at most `2 O bits(V)`, with
`O` the label elements and `V = n + m` the vertices of the component.
(b) Along the result path. Along a top call's fresh start followed by any sequence of individualisations, fixings and inherited
starts, the label elements read by all REFINEs total at most `2 O bits(V)`, with `O` and `V` those of the top component. The
same holds for the whole result path (7.7), because every vertex lies in one chain of calls of it. Proof. The argument of (a)
applies to the whole sequence: an inherited start queues nothing; an individualisation queues only a non-largest fragment of the
split cell `X`, and `X` is a subset of the cell of `x` at its previous pop (cells only shrink, restriction removes vertices); the
first pop of `x` is in the fresh start; a nested component's incidences are a subset of its parent's. End of proof.
(c) Sorting keys. Within one split the touched vertices are grouped and ordered by key; untouched vertices form the empty-key
fragment, which stays in place and is never enumerated. An engine may sort keys by any method. A comparison sort costs at most a
factor `bits(V)` above the label elements read. A sort of the keys of one split by the lexicographic bucket method for sequences
of integers (linear in the total length of the keys plus the size of the alphabet; attributed to the book "The Design and Analysis of
Computer Algorithms", 1974) costs a constant factor ONLY if the bucket array persists across
splits: it is allocated once for each top component with one bucket for each distinct label, after the labels are ranked once by
the same method, and after each sort only the touched buckets are reset. The attributed statement charges the alphabet once for each
invocation, so without that array the alphabet would be paid at every split. Sorting is not counted in the units (8.1).

5.10 DECISION: THE REFINEMENT IS COLOUR REFINEMENT AND NOTHING STRONGER. Version `1` refines by 5.5, the colour refinement of the
incidence graph (4.1), at every call, and applies no stronger refinement before a target cell is chosen. Reasons. (1) Colour
refinement cannot split a vertex-transitive graph, or a distance-regular graph, at the fresh start: every node cell stays one cell,
so the first call branches (examples: the cube graph and the quadratic residue graph of 7.10). (2) Every refinement of fixed
strength, for every fixed `k`, the `k`-dimensional colour refinement, has families of graphs on which the search tree is
exponential (8.7); so no fixed stronger refinement removes the residue, it only moves it. (3) A stronger refinement would double
the byte-exact normative surface: every engine would have to reproduce its cells, their order, its work and the proofs of Lemma 3,
Lemma 4, property 7 and Lemmas R, G and M for the changed pair, in the same bytes. (4) The predicate `guaranteed` (7.10) with the
typed refusal `limit` (7.11) already states the residue honestly: an input that colour refinement and the search of this text
label within every engine's guarantee is named, and an input outside it is labelled by search or refused with its first failures.
A stronger refinement would be a NEW VERSION (7.10, VERSIONING), never an option of this one.

## 6. The definition

### 6.1 Calls

A CALL is made on one connected free component `K` with three arguments: the stable ordered partitions of `K` (inherited by 5.7,
or for a top component the pair that results from 5.6), the number `p` of nodes fixed by its ancestors on the path from the top
call of its top component (`p = 0` at a top call), and the CONSTANTS of its ancestors (each a fixed node with a path position
below `p`). A call returns its CERTIFICATE (6.3) and the call structure of 6.5.

THE THREE-WAY RULE. The node partition read is the one the call holds: for a nested call, the inherited partition restricted to its
component (5.7), in which a cell of the parent that spans several components may be a singleton. On a call whose node partition is:

1. DISCRETE: the call is a LEAF. Every node is fixed (6.2) and `C` is empty.
2. NOT DISCRETE, with at least one singleton node cell: FIX the singleton cells (6.2), then NEST (6.4). The call does not branch.
3. WITHOUT a singleton node cell: BRANCH. The TARGET CELL is the node cell of colour `0`. For each node `v` of the target cell, taken
   in ascending source order (2.2), there is one CHILD: individualise `v` (5.8) in a copy of the partitions (an engine may use an undo
   trail instead of a copy; the result is the same), then FIX the singleton node cells of the result (at least `{v}`), then NEST. The
   children are LISTED in that order; the certificate of the call does not depend on it, and the walk of 8.3 does. The certificate of
   the call is the smallest certificate among its children (6.3). A child whose certificate equals it ATTAINS.

A child of a branching call is never itself a branching call: it holds the singleton `{v}`, so it takes rule 1 or rule 2. A call
fixes at least one node (itself under rules 1 and 2, each child under rule 3), and each nested call is on a proper subset of the
nodes of its parent.

### 6.2 Fixing and path positions

FIX takes the singleton node cells in ascending order of colour. The node of the `i`-th of them (from `0`) takes the PATH POSITION
`p + i`, and `s` is their number. Path positions are cumulative along the ancestor path: a call receives `p`, the number of nodes
fixed by the calls above it on its own path, and its nested calls receive `p + s`. Siblings (calls on two components of one
parent) share every ancestor, so they receive the same `p` and see identical constants. A fixed node is the typed leaf `fixed(q)`
in every masked line (3.6). From the moment of fixing the node has no free occurrence, and the free occurrence numbers of every
line holding it (3.5) count free occurrences only, so they change when nodes are fixed.

### 6.3 The certificate of a call

1. `s`: the number of nodes the call fixes (under rule 3, the number the child fixes).
2. `E`: the sorted list of the ENTRIES of the lines of `K` that have no free occurrence after this fixing (under rule 3, the
   fixing of the child whose certificate is being computed). Every line of `K` holds a free node at entry; a line that still holds
   one after the fixing is emitted by the nested call on the component holding its free nodes; a line holds free nodes of one
   component only, so every line holding a blank node is emitted exactly once over the whole recursion.
3. An ENTRY of a line is the typed pair `(m, line)`: `line` is the masked line with every blank node fixed (path positions of
   nodes fixed by ancestors included), and `m` is the largest path position among its blank occurrences. Entries compare by `m`
   first, numerically, then by the encoding of `line` (3.7), bytewise.
4. `C`: the LIST of the certificates of the nested calls of NEST, in the order of 6.4; equal certificates are repeated, one
   member for each nested call.
5. The certificate is the triple `(s, E, C)`. Certificates compare by `s` first, numerically; if equal, by `E` as a list of
   entries, a proper prefix first; if equal, by `C` as a list of certificates, by this same order recursively.
6. Under rule 3 the certificate of the call is the smallest certificate of its children, each computed by items 1 to 5 after
   the child's fixing.
7. The SIZE of a certificate is `n(s, E, C) = s + n(c_1) + ... + n(c_r)` over the members `c_1 .. c_r` of `C`. LEMMA S: the size
   of the certificate of a call on `K` is `n_K`. PROVED: the call (its chosen child under rule 3) fixes `s` nodes of `K`, and the
   other nodes of `K` are the nodes of the nested components, which are disjoint; induction on `n_K`.
8. The certificate is compared only by this order. Its byte encoding (7.8) is used for the component identity and by REBUILD
   (6.8), never for a comparison.
9. THE CERTIFICATE ALONG A LEAF. A LEAF of a call is a choice of one child at every rule 3 call reached below it (a choice at the call
   itself included, when it takes rule 3). The certificate along the leaf is the triple `(s, E, C)` of items 1 to 5 formed along that
   choice: of the call itself under rules 1 and 2, of the child that the leaf takes under rule 3; with `C` the list of the certificates
   along the leaf of its nested calls. BOTH the positions that 6.5 assigns along the leaf AND the list `C` use the ONE order of 6.4: the
   nested calls are ordered by the certificates of the nested CALLS themselves (items 1 to 6, the minima), equal ones by M2, and not by
   the certificates along the leaf. For a nested call that takes rule 3 the entry of `C` is the certificate formed along the leaf's choice
   there, not that call's certificate: it is greater than that call's certificate exactly when the leaf takes a child that does not
   attain, at that call or at a rule 3 call below it. A leaf therefore needs the certificate of each nested call to be ordered; it is not
   a single path that can be ordered without exploring those calls. If `C` were sorted by the certificates along the leaf while the
   positions follow the order of 6.4, two leaves with equal certificates could give different sets of relabelled lines. The certificate
   of a call is the LEAST certificate along its leaves (property 5).

### 6.4 Nesting

After the fixing of a call (or of a child under rule 3), the free nodes are those in non-singleton node cells. They fall into free
components under the enlarged set of fixed nodes (4.2). Each is the subject of a call with: the partitions of the call (under rule 3, of
the child, individualised and refined) restricted
to its nodes and lines, re-offset (5.7); the argument `p + s`; the enlarged constants. The nested calls are ordered by certificate,
and nested calls with equal certificates by the smallest source identifier of their nodes (7.9, M2). No fresh start is ever made
below a top call.

### 6.5 Final positions

The FINAL POSITION of every node is computed by one pre-order pass over the CALL STRUCTURE of the result path (7.7), after every
level has been ordered. Each call of the structure knows, as typed integers, its offset `o`, its argument `p`, the nodes it fixed
with their path positions (under rule 3 those of its chosen child), their number `s`, and the size `n_K` of each nested call. A
call with OFFSET `o` gives the node of path position `q` fixed by it (or by its chosen child) the final position `o + (q - p)`, and
its `j`-th nested call (in the order of 6.4) the offset `o + s + t_j`, where `t_j` is the sum of the sizes `n_K` of the nested
calls before it. The offset of a top call is given by 7.3. No size or position is recovered from the contents of a certificate.
The `position` of a node is its final position. The same pass applies to the call structure below ANY child of a rule 3 call, not
only the chosen one, with `M1` and `M2` (7.9) applied inside it: a child that is not chosen has final positions of the nodes of
its call in this sense. `F1` of 7.7 and the claim in the proof of Theorem RB use it for children that are not chosen.

### 6.6 Recursion and iteration

Every recursion of this section has an iterative form with an explicit stack whose depth is at most the number of nodes of the
input (each nested call fixes at least one node). The stack is part of the working state of 7.12; no result depends on a
recursion limit, and no refusal is defined for depth.

### 6.7 Why the recursion ends and why the target cell is inside one component

- Rules 1 and 2 fix at least one node, and under rule 3 each child fixes `v`. A nested call is on a free component of the nodes
  not yet fixed, hence a proper subset of its parent's: the depth is at most the number of nodes.
- A call is made on a CONNECTED component and its target cell is a cell of that call's own node partition, so the target cell lies
  in one free component; nodes of one inherited cell that lie in different components are never in one target cell.

### 6.8 REBUILD and the completeness of certificates

REBUILD is a normative procedure that produces, from a certificate alone, a set of typed lines. `REBUILD(c, p, o, kappa)` takes a
certificate `c = (s, E, C)`, two unsigned integers `p` and `o`, and a map `kappa` from the path positions `0 .. p - 1` to `blank`
terms, and returns a set of typed lines:

1. Let `kappa'` be `kappa` extended by `p + i -> blank(u64(o + i))` for `i = 0 .. s - 1`: the node of path position `p + i` is the
   blank node whose source identifier is the eight bytes `u64(o + i)`.
2. For each entry `(m, line)` of `E`, replace every leaf `fixed(q)` of `line` by `kappa'(q)` and add the resulting typed line. A
   certificate produced by section 6 has `q < p + s` for every such leaf (6.3 item 3); REBUILD is defined on such certificates
   only, and stops on any other value.
3. For the members `c_1 .. c_r` of `C`, in order, with `t_1 = 0` and `t_(j+1) = t_j + n(c_j)` (6.3 item 7), add
   `REBUILD(c_j, p + s, o + s + t_j, kappa')`.
For a top component `K`, `rebuild(K)` is `REBUILD(cert(K), 0, 0, empty map)`, `cert(K)` the certificate of its top call. Lemma C
(7.8) makes the certificate a function of its encoding, so REBUILD can equally start from the encoding.

THEOREM RB (the certificate determines the component). PROVED. For every top component `K`, `rebuild(K)` is EQUAL to the set of
lines of `K` with every leaf `blank(b)` replaced by `blank(u64(lambda(b)))`, `lambda(b)` the local position of `b` (its final
position with the offset of `K` taken as `0`, 7.8). In particular `rebuild(K)` is isomorphic to `K`.
Proof. Final positions in this proof are taken with the offset of `K` equal to `0`. CLAIM: for every call `X` of the call structure
of `K` (6.5), with offset `o_X`, argument `p` and constants, let `kappa_X` map each path position `q < p` to
`blank(u64(final position of the constant of path position q))`; then `REBUILD(cert(X), p, o_X, kappa_X)` is the set of lines of the
component of `X` with every blank leaf replaced by `blank(u64(final position))`. Induction on the number of nodes. Let `Y` be `X`
itself under rules 1 and 2 and its chosen child under rule 3; `cert(X) = cert(Y)`. `Y` fixes `s` nodes at the path positions
`p .. p + s - 1`, and 6.5 gives the node of path position `p + i` the final position `o_X + i`; so `kappa'` maps every path position
below `p + s` to the relabelled leaf of the node that holds it. An entry of `Y` is a line of the component all of whose nodes are
fixed, masked with `fixed(q)` at each blank leaf; replacing each `fixed(q)` by `kappa'(q)` gives that line relabelled, so step 2
adds exactly the relabelled lines that `Y` places in `E`. Every other line of the component holds a free node of exactly one nested
component `K_j` (4.2) and is a line of `K_j`. The `j`-th nested call has argument `p + s`, constants mapped by `kappa'`, and offset
`o_X + s + t_j` with `t_j` the sum of the sizes of the nested calls before it (6.5), which by Lemma S is the sum of `n` over their
certificates, as step 3 computes. By induction step 3 adds exactly the relabelled lines of each `K_j`. The lines of `E` and of the
`K_j` are disjoint and together are all lines of the component. For the top call (`o = 0`, `p = 0`, empty `kappa`) the claim is the
theorem. Final positions are a bijection of the nodes of `K` onto `0 .. n_K - 1`, so the relabelling is an isomorphism. End of proof.

COROLLARY (completeness). PROVED. Two top components have equal certificates exactly when they are isomorphic. If they are
isomorphic their certificates are equal (section 9, property 1). If their certificates are equal, `rebuild` gives one set of lines,
which is `K_1` relabelled by `lambda_1` and `K_2` relabelled by `lambda_2` (Theorem RB); so `lambda_2^(-1) o lambda_1` maps the lines
of `K_1` onto the lines of `K_2`. The same argument, with one map `kappa` for both, gives two statements. (a) Two siblings (calls on two
components of one parent,
which have the same argument `p` and the same constants) have equal certificates exactly when an isomorphism that fixes every
constant maps one component onto the other. (b) The children of `v` and of `w` of one branching call have equal certificates
exactly when an automorphism of the call maps `v` to `w` (`F1` and `F2` of 7.7). The condition of (a) does not serve for (b): the
two children are on one component, the identity maps it onto itself and fixes every constant, yet not every child attains.
Counterexample: two copies of the complete graph on four nodes with one edge removed, `a0 .. a3` and `b0 .. b3`, the edges
`a2 a3` and `b2 b3` missing, joined by the edges `a2 b2` and `a3 b3`, every edge written as two lines `quad(u, <p>, v)` and
`quad(v, <p>, u)` (a cubic graph of eight nodes, the input `K4PAIR` of 13.1).
The refinement of the fresh start leaves one cell of eight; the children of `a0`, `a1`, `b0` and `b1` attain and those of `a2`,
`a3`, `b2` and `b3` do not; the order of the group is `16`.

WHAT A CERTIFICATE RECORDS. Every item that REBUILD needs is in `(s, E, C)`: the number of nodes a call fixes (`s`); the nodes a
call fixes that complete no line of its own (counted by `s`; they occur as constants in the entries of nested calls); the order of
the fixed nodes (path positions); which nested component holds a line that still has free occurrences after a fixing (the nested
certificate whose entries hold it); a node that occurs twice in a line (two leaves `fixed(q)`); the count of an annotation and every
constant (in the encoding of the entry); `empty-graph` lines with a blank name (entries like any other); the sizes of nested
components (Lemma S). Write `strip(s, E, C) = (E, [strip(c) for c in C])`, the certificate with `s` removed at EVERY level of nesting.
`strip` does not
determine the component: example 12.6 gives two non-isomorphic components with equal `strip`. Not recorded, and not needed by REBUILD:
which attaining child a branching
call chose (all attaining children have equal certificates; 7.9 fixes the choice for the canonical map), which rule a call took, and
every source identifier. A line with no blank node belongs to no component; it is in the canonical line sequence unchanged (7.6).

## 7. The whole input: order, result, record, identity, map, guarantee, outcomes, hash

7.1 TOP COMPONENTS. The incidence graph of the whole input (all nodes, all lines that hold a blank node, no fixed node) has
connected components. Each is a TOP COMPONENT. A line with no blank node belongs to no component, takes no part in the search,
and is in the canonical line sequence unchanged (7.6).

7.2 TOP CALLS. Each top component is the subject of a top call with `p = 0` and no constants. It starts FRESH and alone (5.6) and
meets no other component's nodes, lines or cells. Its nested calls inherit (5.7).

7.3 THE ORDER OF THE TOP COMPONENTS. Each top component has one COMPONENT IDENTITY, 32 bytes (7.8). The top components are
ordered ascending by the identity, compared bytewise, and top components with equal identities ascending by the smallest source
identifier of their nodes (7.9, M2). They take offsets in that order: the first has offset `0`, and each next one the offset of
the one before plus its number of nodes. No certificate of one top component is compared with that of another, and an engine
holds one 32-byte digest for each top component. Under the assumption of 7.15, equal identities occur exactly for isomorphic top
components (6.8, Corollary), which give the same set of relabelled lines whichever comes first (Theorem RB with the offsets
added), so the order among them is not observable in the canonical line sequence; the second key fixes it for the canonical map.
The nested components of every call stay ordered by certificate and then by M2; no hash enters the search.

7.4 WHY THIS IS A FUNCTION OF THE INPUT. A top call reads only its own component, through the quantities of Lemma 3, so its
certificate and the cell order inside it are functions of the isomorphism class of that component; adding or removing an
unrelated component changes no cell order and no certificate of another. The identity is a function of the certificate. The
offsets depend only on the sizes and the order.

7.5 NOT REFINED TOGETHER. Refining the top components together, or refining the whole input once and letting each component inherit the
restriction, gives the same cells as sets but in general a different order of cells inside a component (a splitter shared by node cells of
several components is queued or left out according to the size of its largest fragment, which counts nodes of the other components). It
gives different certificates and different results and is wrong; 10.5(e) prints an input.

7.6 THE CANONICAL LINE SEQUENCE AND ITS DIGEST. Let `pos(b)` be the final position (6.5) of a blank node `b`. The CANONICAL LINE
SEQUENCE is the list, in ascending bytewise order of the encoding (3.7), of the masked lines obtained from every input line, those
with no blank node included, by replacing each leaf `blank(b)` by `fixed(pos(b))`. Distinct input lines give distinct masked lines,
because `pos` is a bijection from the blank nodes onto `0 .. N - 1`, so the sequence has no repeated element. Its DIGEST is
`H(context(lines), message)` (7.15), with `message` the byte string `u64(number of lines)` followed by the encodings concatenated
in order; Lemma E makes the concatenation unambiguous. A text document with labels spelled by 7.14 is a presentation
that a format layers on top of the sequence; it writes the rows of the lines its syntax spells in the order of the canonical line
sequence, whatever the order of the input lines; a line its syntax cannot spell has no row. The
sequence and the digest are those of the WHOLE input, the `record` lines (3.2) included: the digest of the lines of the four
statement kinds alone is another value than the digest of the same lines with the records (12.8 prints both), and the positions of one
labelling are never mixed into another.

7.7 THE STATISTICS RECORD. The record is a typed value: every field has a type, and an optional field is `absent` or
`present(value)`, never a sentinel number.

DEFINITIONS. An AUTOMORPHISM of a call is a permutation `phi` of the free nodes of its component such that replacing each blank
leaf `b` of every line of the component by `phi(b)` (constants stay) maps the set of lines of the component onto itself,
annotation counts kept. `G(X)` is the group of the automorphisms of the call `X`. The RESULT PATH of a call `X` is: `X`; if `X`
takes rule 3, its CHOSEN CHILD (an attaining child, fixed by 7.9, M1) and the result path of every nested call of that child; if
`X` takes rule 1 or 2, the result path of every nested call. The PATH ORDER is the pre-order, with the chosen child immediately
after its rule 3 call and the nested calls in the order of 6.4. The result path of the input is the union of the result paths of
its top calls. A chosen child is not a call of its own in the counts below: `calls` never counts a chosen child, and
`chosen_children` counts nothing else. The NESTING DEPTH of a top call is `0`, and a nested call of a call (or of its chosen
child) at nesting depth `d` has nesting depth `d + 1`.

A FACTOR RECORD is `(kind, value, exponent)`, with `kind` the enumeration `attain` (code `0`) or `sym` (code `1`) and `value`,
`exponent` unsigned. THE ORDER OF THE GROUP is the product, over the records, of `value^exponent` for a record of kind `attain` and
of `(value!)^exponent` for a record of kind `sym`; the empty list is the order `1`. A FAILURE RECORD is
`(path_position, tied_cell, component_nodes, component_lines)`, four unsigned integers:
the argument `p` of the failing call, the size of its target cell, and the `n_K` and `m_K` of its top component. Failure records
compare lexicographically in that field order; records equal in every field are equal values, so the sorted list is one value.

DIAGNOSTIC PART (a function of the input and of its source identifiers; not structural). For each failure record of `broken_by`,
the triple `(failure record, component_source: bytes, cell_source: bytes)`: the smallest source identifier of the nodes of its top
component and the smallest source identifier of the members of its target cell, both opaque; the list is sorted ascending by the
record, then by `component_source`, then by `cell_source`.

No field of the record is ever `absent` because of how an engine searched: every engine classifies every child of every rule 3 call it
completes as attaining or not (10 and its subsections). Which calls lie on the result path is known only when the searches above them have
ended; a call an engine leaves incomplete is never on the result path. An engine may report figures of its own search (leaves visited,
children pruned, comparison units of 8.1(e), total units) beside the result; they are not part of this definition. The limited units
of the walk (8.3) are not among them: an engine that reports them reports the value of the walk.

NO FIELD COUNTS BY KIND. Every field of the record counts nodes, lines, occurrences, incidences, bytes, cells or calls, never lines of
one kind: a `record` line is one line of `lines`, its blank leaves are occurrences of `label_elements`, and its encoding with every
leaf `free`, payload and length of the list included, is a term of `line_bytes`.
NOT IN THE RECORD: the number of nodes moved by some automorphism. It is not a function of the counts above. In the lines
`quad(a, <p>, c)`, `quad(b, <p>, d)`, `quad(c, <q>, d)`, `quad(d, <q>, c)` the only automorphism other than the identity is
`(a b)(c d)`; the refinement of the fresh start (5.6) gives the cells `[a, b]` and `[c, d]`, the top call takes rule 3 on `[a, b]`, the
chosen child is
discrete, and the record holds the one attaining count `2`. All four nodes are moved, but `c` and `d` appear in no attaining count
and in no class of equal certificates.

LEMMA R (the structural record is a function of the input alone). PROVED. It uses that the target cell is the cell of colour `0` (F1); the
lemma is not claimed for another selector. F1 (EQUAL CERTIFICATES GIVE AN AUTOMORPHISM THAT MOVES `v` TO `w`). Let `v`, `w` be
nodes of the target cell of a rule 3 call `X` whose children have equal certificates. In the child of `v` the cell `{v}` has colour `0`
and keeps it (the first fragment keeps the colour, 5.3), so `v` is the first singleton in colour order and takes path position `p`; the
same holds for `w`. Each child gives a final labelling `lambda_v`, `lambda_w` of the free nodes of `X` (6.5), and
`lambda_v(v) = lambda_w(w)`. By Theorem RB (applied to `X` with its constants, 6.8 Corollary) the two sets of relabelled lines are equal.
Put `phi = lambda_w^(-1) o lambda_v`: it maps the lines of `X` onto the lines of `X`, annotation counts kept, fixes every constant, and
`phi(v) = w`. F2 (AUTOMORPHISMS GIVE EQUAL CERTIFICATES AND CARRY THE WHOLE CHILD). If `phi` is in `G(X)` then the child of `phi(v)` has
the certificate of the child of `v`, and `phi` carries the state of the child of `v` (partitions, pops, singleton cells, path positions,
entries, nested components, inherited pairs) onto the state of the child of `phi(v)`, cell by cell with equal colours and sizes. Proof.
The extension of `phi` by the identity is an automorphism of the top component that fixes every constant (a line holds free nodes of one
component only), so by property 7(d) with `K_1 = K_2` the pair of `X` is invariant under it; individualisation and refinement read only
colours, sizes, labels and masked lines, and `phi` fixes the constants, so it preserves masked lines and occurrence numbers; Lemma 3 gives
the rest. A nested component maps onto a nested component of the other child, and the two nested calls are isomorphic calls. F3 (TWO
SIBLINGS HAVE EQUAL CERTIFICATES EXACTLY WHEN AN ISOMORPHISM FIXING THE CONSTANTS MAPS ONE ONTO THE OTHER). The Corollary of 6.8. F4
(STABILISER). Let `v` be a node of the target cell and `S` the nodes in singleton cells of the refined child of `v`. The stabiliser
`G(X)_v` fixes every node of `S` (it preserves the child state, hence each singleton cell) and is exactly the group of automorphisms of
`X` that fix the constants and `S` pointwise. A line of `X` holds free nodes of at most one nested component after the fixing, and a line
with no free node holds only fixed nodes, so it is mapped to itself. PROOF OF THE LEMMA, by induction on the number of nodes of the call:
two result paths that differ only in the choice of attaining children have equal counts. Rule 1: one call. Rule 2: the two paths consist
of the call, the same fixing, and for each nested component a result path of it; by induction their counts are equal, and two nested
components that tie in the order are isomorphic (F3), so their counts are equal whichever is listed first. Rule 3: the two paths choose
attaining children `v` and `w`; by F1 there is `phi` in `G(X)` with `phi(v) = w`; by F2 `phi` carries the state of the child of `v` onto
that of the child of `w`: the REFINE reads the same label elements, the fixings remove the same numbers of vertices, incidences and
remainder components, the entries have equal encodings, and the nested calls correspond by `phi`; by induction the counts agree. The
record is defined on the result path, not on what an engine explored. End of proof.

LEMMA G (the order of the automorphism group). PROVED. For a call `X`: `|G(X)| = 1` under rule 1; under rule 2, `|G(X)|` is the
product, over the classes of equal certificate among its nested calls, of `k_c! ord(c)^(k_c)`, `k_c` the multiplicity of the class
and `ord(c)` the group order of one member; under rule 3, `|G(X)| = a(X)` times that product taken over the nested calls of the
chosen child, `a(X)` the number of attaining children. Proof. Rule 1: the pair is discrete and invariant under `G(X)` (property
7(d)), so every node is fixed. Rule 2: the singleton cells are fixed pointwise, so `G(X)` is the group of the automorphisms that fix
the constants and those nodes (F4); each permutes the nested components, mapping a component only onto one with an equal
certificate (F3); conversely, choose for each class a permutation of its members and, for each member sent to another, an
isomorphism fixing the constants; with the identity on the singleton nodes this is an automorphism, and the number of choices for a
class of `k` members of group order `ord` is `k! ord^k` (a wreath product), the classes being independent. Rule 3: the orbit of `v`
under `G(X)` lies in the set `A` of attaining children (F2) and contains it (F1), so `|A| = a(X)`; the orbit-stabiliser theorem gives
`|G(X)| = a(X) |G(X)_v|`, and `G(X)_v` is the group of rule 2 for the chosen child (F4). For the whole input the same product is taken
over the classes of equal identity among the top components, which under the assumption of 7.15 are the classes of isomorphic top
components. End of proof.
FACTORISATION LIST. Run `process(X, e)` with `e = 1` as follows. If `X` takes rule 3 and `a(X)` is at least `2`, add
`(attain, a(X), e)`. Group the nested calls of `X` (of its chosen child under rule 3) into classes of equal certificate. For a class
of multiplicity `k` at least `2`, add `(sym, k, e)` and run `process(the first member of the class in the order of 6.4, e k)`; for a
class of multiplicity `1`, run `process(its member, e)`. The members of a class are isomorphic calls (F3), so the list does not
depend on this choice (Lemma R). For a top component, start with its top call. For the whole input, group the top components into classes
of equal identity and treat each class as a class of nested calls. Merge records with equal `(kind, value)` by adding exponents and
sort ascending by `(kind code, value)`. The list is a function of the input (Lemma R); every exponent is at most the number of nodes,
so every field fits in 64 bits (the order itself need not). THE ORDER EQUALS THE NUMBER OF LABEL MAPS THAT YIELD THE RESULT: a
LABEL MAP is a bijection of the blank nodes onto `0 .. N - 1` whose replacement in the lines gives the canonical line sequence; if
`lambda_0` is one and `lambda` another, `lambda_0^(-1) o lambda` maps lines onto lines and is an automorphism of the input, and
`f -> lambda_0 o f` is a bijection from the automorphisms onto the label maps.

7.8 STABLE IDENTITY. THE CERTIFICATE ENCODING. `list(items)` is `u64(number of items)` followed by the items. The encoding of an entry
`(m, line)` is `u64(m)` followed by the encoding (3.7) of its fully fixed masked line. The encoding `cert(c)` of a certificate
`c = (s, E, C)` is `u64(s)`, followed by `list(the encodings of the entries of E)`, followed by `list(cert(c') for each c' in C)`. LEMMA
C: `cert` is injective and prefix-free. PROVED by induction on the nesting of `C`: `s` and each list count have fixed width, each entry
encoding begins with eight bytes and continues with a prefix-free line encoding (Lemma E), and the nested encodings are prefix-free by the
induction hypothesis, so a decoder knows where every part ends and recovers the certificate. No certificate is compared through this
encoding. THE COMPONENT IDENTITY of a top component `K` is the 32 bytes `H(context(component), cert(c))` (7.15), `c` the certificate of
its top call. THE STABLE IDENTITY of a blank node `b` is the typed triple `(component, local position, copy index)`: `component` the
identity of its top component `K`; `local position` the position of `b` minus the offset of `K` (6.5, 7.3), a number from `0` to
`n_K - 1`; `copy index` the number of top components that precede `K` in the order of 7.3 and have an identity equal to that of `K`. It is
defined for a blank node in every slot of every kind of line, at any depth inside a triple term, as a reifier and as a graph name: every
blank leaf is an occurrence (3.4), so every blank node lies in exactly one top component. It is obtained in the same pass as the
labelling, from the certificate of the top call, the canonical map (7.9) and the order of 7.3, with no further search. GUARANTEES.
(I1) ALONE OR EMBEDDED. PROVED. Let `K` be a top component of an input `D`, and `D'` any input that holds the lines of `K` and lines
over other blank nodes only. Then `K` is a top component of `D'`, and the component identity of `K` and the local position of each
node of `K` are the same in `D` and in `D'`. Proof. The certificate of a top call is a function of the isomorphism class of its
component (7.4), hence so is the identity. The local position is read from the call structure of `K` (6.5) with the offset removed,
and the choices that fix it (7.9) compare only source identifiers of nodes of `K`.
(I2) THE COPY INDEX. PROVED. The copy index ranks a top component among those of equal identity by smallest source identifier
(7.3). Hence adding one top component whose identity equals that of a class of `c` components leaves every component identity and
every local position unmoved, leaves the copy index of every component of another class unmoved, gives the new component the copy
index equal to the number of members of the class whose smallest source identifier is smaller than its own, and increases by one the
copy index of each member whose smallest source identifier is larger. What moves outside the identities: the offsets, hence the
positions, of the components after the new one, and with them the canonical line sequence. Example 12.5b shows it.
(I3) NO DENSE COPY INDEX IS STABLE. PROVED. No rule that is a function of the input can give the members of every class of `k` equal top
components the indices `0 .. k - 1` and keep the index of every existing member when a member is added. Proof. Take two equal components
`X` and `Y`. Labelling `{X}` gives `X` the index `0`; by stability `{X, Y}` keeps `X` at `0`, so `Y` gets `1`. Labelling `{Y}` gives `Y`
the index `0`; by stability `{Y, X}` keeps `Y` at `0`. But `{X, Y}` and `{Y, X}` are the same input, and a function of the input has one
value. End of proof. The rank of I2 is stable exactly under adding a member whose smallest source identifier exceeds those present. A rule
stable under every addition exists only if the copy field is not a dense integer, for example the member's own smallest source identifier;
this text does not use one, because a reader already has the source identifier and can pair it with the identity.
(I4) SCOPE. An identity is defined within one labelled input. Isomorphic components of different inputs have equal identities (I1
read across inputs). A reader that must tell inputs apart pairs the identity with a name of its own, outside this text; no scoped form
is defined here.
(I5) WHAT IS AND IS NOT A PROPERTY OF THE ABSTRACT GRAPH. The set of local positions occupied by each orbit of the automorphism group
of a component is a function of the abstract graph (two label maps differ by an automorphism, 7.7). Which node of an orbit receives
which of those positions, that is the identity of a twin, and every copy index, are properties of the source identifiers (7.9), not
of the abstract graph: renaming the source identifiers can exchange the identities of twins and of equal copies.
(I6) EQUAL IDENTITY MEANS ISOMORPHIC. PROVED under the assumption of 7.15. If two nodes, possibly of different inputs, have equal
`(component, local position)`, then their certificate encodings have equal digests; under the assumption they are equal, so the
certificates are equal (Lemma C), the components are isomorphic (6.8, Corollary), and the isomorphism `lambda_2^(-1) o lambda_1` of
the proof maps the one node to the other.
(I7) COLLISIONS. No textual spelling of an identity is defined; a reader that prints one encodes the typed triple by its own format's
rules. If it truncates the component identity to `t` bytes, then, if `H` is taken to behave as a random function on distinct inputs
(a model, not a theorem of this text), the probability that two of `N` non-isomorphic components share a truncated identity is at most
`N (N - 1) / 2` times `2^(-8 t)`: about `10^(-27)` for `N = 10^6` and `t = 16`, about `3 times 10^(-8)` for `t = 8`, and about
`4 times 10^(-66)` for the full 32 bytes.

7.9 THE CANONICAL MAP. The SOURCE ORDER is the bytewise order of whole source identifiers (2.2). The SMALLEST SOURCE IDENTIFIER of a
set of nodes is the least identifier of its members under that order.
(M1) At a rule 3 call of the result path the CHOSEN CHILD is the attaining child whose individualised node has the
smallest source identifier.
(M2) Nested calls with equal certificates are ordered ascending by the smallest source identifier of their nodes; so are top components
with equal identities (7.3). Components are disjoint, so this order is total.
(M3) The RESULT PATH (7.7) is defined by M1 and M2. The final positions (6.5), the local positions and the copy indices (7.8) are
computed along it. The CANONICAL MAP is the typed map from each source identifier to `(position, identity)`. The claim of the labelling
is about the canonical line sequence and holds for any map that yields it (7.7); the canonical map is a function of the input and of
its source identifiers.
(M4) INVARIANCE. If `sigma` is a bijection of blank nodes that maps the lines of one input onto the lines of another and preserves the
source order, the canonical maps agree: `map_2(sigma(v)) = map_1(v)`. The map does not depend on the order or storage of the lines, on the
engine, on pruning, on thread count or on word size. It is not invariant under a renaming that changes the source order (I5). The
labelling writes nothing to its input: a masked line is a value derived from a line, and the canonical line sequence is a new sequence.
LEMMA I (IDEMPOTENCE: THE MAP OF A RELABELLED DATASET). PROVED. Let `D` be a well-formed input, `pos` its positions (6.5), and `name` a
map from `0 .. N - 1` to byte strings that is INCREASING in the source order: `name(i)` precedes `name(j)` bytewise exactly when `i < j`.
Let `D'` be `D` with every blank node `b` renamed `name(pos(b))`. Then in `D'` the node named `name(i)` has the position `i` and the same
stable identity `(component, local position, copy index)` as the node of position `i` in `D`; and the canonical line sequence, the digest,
the certificates and the structural record of `D'` equal those of `D`, whatever the order of the lines of `D'`. In words: a dataset whose
source identifiers are ordered as the final positions of an earlier labelling of it is labelled by the identity map. THE CONDITION is that
`name` is increasing in the source order. The encoding `name(i) = u64(i)` satisfies it, and a format may store a label as the encoding of
a position and rely on the lemma; the decimal digits of `i` do not (`10` precedes `2` bytewise), and 7.14 labels are never read back.
Proof. The renaming `sigma(b) = name(pos(b))` is a bijection of the blank nodes of `D` onto those of `D'` that maps the lines of `D` onto
the lines of `D'`, so by property 1 the partitions, cells, children, certificates and attaining sets of every call of `D'` are the images
by `sigma` of those of `D`, and the identity of every top component is equal. What remains is that M1 and M2, the only rules that read
source identifiers, and the order of equal top components (7.3), choose the images of the choices made in `D`. (a) M1. At a rule 3 call of
the result path of `D` with offset `o`, the chosen child `v*` takes the path position `p` (F1), hence the position `o`; the nodes of the
call occupy the positions `o .. o + n_K - 1` (Lemma S, 6.5), and positions are a bijection, so every other node `w` of the call has a
position above `o`. In `D'` the name of `v*` is `name(o)`, and the name of `w` is `name(pos(w))`, which follows `name(o)` because `name`
is increasing; the attaining children of the call in `D'` are the images of those of `D`, so the attaining child of smallest source
identifier is `sigma(v*)`. (b) M2. Of two nested calls with equal certificates the result path of `D` lists the earlier first (6.4) and
gives it the lower block of consecutive offsets (6.5), so every position of its nodes is below every position of the other's; in `D'` the
same holds of the names, so the earlier of the two has the smaller smallest source identifier, and the order is kept. The same holds for
top components with equal identity (7.3, 7.9). By induction along the result path, from the top calls, the result path of `D'` is the
image of that of `D`, with the same offsets, so every node `sigma(b)` has the position `pos(b)`, which is `i` for the node `name(i)`; the
identities, the canonical line sequence and the digest are functions of the certificates and the positions. The order of the lines plays
no part (1.1). End of proof. A sentence for a format that stores positions: an encoding of positions meets the condition exactly when its
bytewise order (2.2) is the order of the positions: a fixed-width big-endian integer does, and so does the deterministic CBOR encoding of
an unsigned integer, bare or wrapped as a byte string, because its first byte grows with its length; the minimal big-endian bytes of the
integer with no length prefix do not (`01 00` precedes `ff`), and decimal digits do not (`10` precedes `2`).

LEMMA M. PROVED. The result path is well defined, engine-independent and computable without enumerating the group.
(i) Well defined and engine-independent: the certificate of every child is a function of the input (6.3), so whether a child attains
is a property of the call and the child; the minimum of M1 is over a finite set of identifiers compared whole, and M2 orders disjoint
components by distinct identifiers. (ii) Computable: a conforming engine skips a child only (a) on a strictly greater `s`, or a strictly
greater `E` with `s` equal (10.4), so the child does
not attain, or (b) because it lies in the orbit of an explored child under automorphisms of the call already found, or in its twin
class (10.1, 10.2), so by F2 it has that child's certificate. The attaining set is therefore the union, over the explored attaining
children, of their classes in a union-find over the automorphisms found, and its smallest source identifier is read from those
classes, with no enumeration of the group. A discard on a bound that also fires on ties, and an automorphism used outside its call
and its ancestors, are wrong (10.5). (iii) To continue inside the chosen child `v*` an engine holds it, or carries the state
of an explored attaining child `w` onto it by an automorphism `phi` with `phi(w) = v*` (F2), or explores `v*` afresh. What an
automorphism carries is the attaining SET of each call and the state of the child, NEVER THE CHOICE: at every rule 3 call below
`v*` the chosen child is taken anew by `M1` as the attaining child of smallest source identifier of that call, and its state is
obtained in the same three ways; the nested components and their smallest source identifiers (`M2`) are those of `v*`, not of `w`.
Counterexample to carrying the choice: the complete graph on `a`, `b`, `c` written in both directions (`a` smaller than `b` smaller
than `c`). The child `c`, explored with `M1` applied inside it, fixes `c`, `a`, `b` at `0`, `1`, `2`; carried onto `a` by the
transposition of `a` and `c` it gives `a`, `c`, `b` at `0`, `1`, `2`, while the canonical map has `a`, `b`, `c` at `0`, `1`, `2`.
(iv) AN ENGINE RECIPE THAT NEEDS NO TRANSPORT: if the engine visits the children in ascending source order, replaces the best child
only by a child whose certificate is strictly smaller, stops a child only on a strictly greater `s`, or a strictly greater `E` with `s`
equal (10.4), and skips a child
only as the image of an explored child under an automorphism of the call, the child it retains is `v*` and has been explored in
full. Proof. Let `m` be the minimum certificate. Every child visited before `v*` has a smaller identifier and does not attain, so its
certificate is strictly greater than `m`. `v*` is not skipped: if it were the image of an explored child `u`, then `u` would attain
(F2) with a smaller identifier. `v*` is not stopped: an `s`, or an `E` with `s` equal, that was strictly greater than that of the best so
far would make its whole certificate greater than the best so far, which is at least `m`, while it equals `m`. So `v*` is completed; the
best so far is not attaining, so the certificate of `v*` is strictly smaller and replaces it; every later attaining child ties and does
not replace it. A parallel engine may explore in any order and applies the same acceptance, a minimum over a set; the chosen child is then held,
carried as in (iii) or explored afresh. The visit of the walk of 8.3 at a rule 3 call is this recipe, with the skips and the stops that
8.3 states (a skipped child is the image of an earlier visited child under maps of the call, Lemma W (1) and (2)); what an engine
explored changes neither the child it retains nor the units, which are those of the walk. End of proof. COST OF THE MAP FOR COPIES. The canonical line sequence and its digest need
one labelling for each class of equal identity; the map needs the rule applied to each copy with that copy's own source identifiers,
because carrying the map of one copy along an isomorphism may give another valid assignment, differing by an automorphism. Inside the
guaranteed class (7.10) a copy costs one search-free run; outside it a copy costs at most what the first labelling cost. No better bound
is known for `k` copies outside the guaranteed class, and none is required.

7.10 THE GUARANTEE, THE PREDICATE `guaranteed` AND THE SEARCH-FREE RUN.
TWINS. Two distinct nodes `u`, `w` of one top component are TWINS when the transposition that exchanges them and fixes every other
node maps the set of lines of the input onto itself (a line is a typed value with its count; its image has every leaf `blank(u)`
replaced by `blank(w)` and every leaf `blank(w)` by `blank(u)`, in the terms of a `record` too, every other field unchanged). LEMMA TW.
PROVED. (a) The test is exact: the transposition is an
involution on typed lines and fixes every line that holds neither node, so it maps the finite set of lines onto itself exactly when
it maps each line holding `u` or `w` to a line of the set. (b) Twin-ness is an equivalence on distinct nodes (with each node its own
twin): if `(u w)` and `(w x)` are automorphisms then `(u x) = (w x)(u w)(w x)` is one. (c) Twin-ness does not depend on the call: a
transposition fixes every other node, so the constants of a call change nothing; every line that holds `u` or `w` is a line of every
call whose component holds both. So two nodes are twins in a call exactly when they are twins in the top component, twin classes found
at one call stay valid at every call below it, and two twins that are free in a call lie in one node cell of it (its partition is
invariant under every automorphism of the call that fixes the constants, property 7(d)).
THE PREDICATE. An input is `guaranteed` when every rule 3 call of the result path has a target cell that is ONE TWIN CLASS: any two
members are twins. By F2 the property does not depend on which attaining children the result path takes, so it is a function of the
input. It has no parameter, and a claim states no policy. An input with no rule 3 call on the result path satisfies it; the record's
`calls` then holds a zero in its third part, and no second predicate exists for that case.
THE SEARCH-FREE RUN. The labelling run of sections 5 and 6 with one rule at rule 3 calls: at a rule 3 call with target cell `T`, let
`u` be the member of `T` with the smallest source identifier, and TEST `u` against every other member `w` of `T` in ascending source
order, skipping a `w` already known to be a twin of `u`; stop at the first test that fails. If every test passes, `T` is one twin
class, every child attains (F2 with the transpositions), and the run takes the child of `u`, which is the chosen child of M1, and
continues. If a test fails, the call is a FIRST FAILURE: the run records its failure record (7.7), does not descend into it, and
continues with every other call. The twin classes found are kept for the whole top component (Lemma TW(c)) in a union-find. The run
visits the top components in ascending order of their smallest source identifier and, in each, the calls in pre-order, the nested
calls of a call in ascending order of the smallest source identifier of their nodes (the order of the walk, 8.3). The first failures,
the twin classes and the twin units do not depend on this order: a test reads and writes the twin classes of the nodes of its own
call, and two calls of the run, neither of which is an ancestor of the other, have no node in common; so the classes that a call
reads were written by itself and by its ancestors, which the run visits before it in every order.
THEOREM GR. PROVED. Every call the search-free run reaches is a call of the result path; the run records nothing exactly when the
input is `guaranteed`; and when it records nothing it has produced the certificates, the final positions, the canonical map, the
identities and the structural record of the labelling. Proof. Induction along the run. The top call is on the result path; a nested
call of a result-path call (or of its chosen child) is on it; at a rule 3 call that passes the test the run takes the child of M1, the
chosen child. A recorded call is a rule 3 call of the result path whose target cell holds two nodes that are not twins, so the input is
not `guaranteed`. If nothing is recorded, the run has visited every call of the result path and each rule 3 call passed; the attaining
count of each is `|T|`, and every value of the result is computed along the result path (6.5, 7.7, 7.8, 7.9). End of proof.
The run does not search: at each rule 3 call it explores one child. Taking the child of the smallest source identifier is
NECESSARY, not a convenience: every attaining child gives the same canonical line sequence, but different attaining children give
different canonical maps (two label maps differ by an automorphism of the input, 7.7), and a run that took any other child would
return a map that is not the one of 7.9. The first failures it records
are those of the record's `broken_by`, a function of the input (F2).
TWIN UNITS. A test of the pair `u`, `w`, whether it passes or fails, costs the START CHARGE `c(l) = 1 + ceil(b(l) / 8)` of EVERY
line of the top component that holds `u` or `w` (`b(l)` the length of the encoding of `l` with every leaf `free`, 8.1(a)). The
charge is a function of the input and of the pair alone: the outcome of the test and the order in which an engine examines the lines
play no part. WHICH pairs are tested depends on the source identifiers (the run tests the member of smallest source identifier against
the others in ascending source order), so the twin units of a whole run are a function of the input as given, source identifiers
included, and not of its isomorphism class; the next lemma bounds them for EVERY source order. LEMMA TU. PROVED. The twin units of the
search-free run on a top component are at most `4 W`, `W = sum over lines l of
d(l) c(l)`, `d(l)` the number of distinct nodes of `l`. Proof. Let `beta(v)` be the sum of `c(l)` over the lines holding `v`; `sum
over v of beta(v) = W`. Two nodes of one node cell have equal `beta`: they have equal numbers of incidences into each line cell
(stability), and the lines of one line cell have equal masked lines, hence equal encodings with every leaf `free`. A test of `u`,
`w` costs the sum of `c(l)` over the lines holding `u` or `w`, at most `beta(u) + beta(w) = 2 beta(w)`. A test that passes merges the
class of `w` into that of `u`; a node `w` is tested and passes at most once. Proof of that step: let `X` be the call at which `w` passed,
`T` its target, `u` the
smallest member of `T`, and `X'` a later call of the run, a descendant of `X`, whose target `T'` holds `w`, and `u'` any member of `T'`.
Both `w` and `u'` are free at `X'`, hence free at `X`; they lie in one node cell of `X'`, whose cells refine those of `X`, so they
lie in one node cell of `X`, which is `T`. The run descended from `X` only because every test at `X` passed, so `u'` was merged into
the class of `u` at `X`, as `w` was. So every member of `T'` is already in the class of the smallest member of `T'`, and no test is
made at `X'`; so the passing tests cost at most `2 W`. Each first failure costs one failing test, at most `2
beta(w)` for a node `w` of its component, and first failures lie on disjoint sets of nodes (two calls of the result path that are
not ancestor and descendant are on disjoint components); so the failing tests cost at most `2 W`.  End of proof. The
bound holds for a `record` as for any line: it uses only the start charge `c(l)` of a line, which is a function of the length of its
encoding with every leaf `free`, payload included, and the equality of that length among the lines of one line cell.
THE ALLOWANCE. For a top component `K` with `n` nodes, `m` lines, `V = n + m`, `O` label elements, `M` incidences,
`S = sum of c(l)` over its lines and `W` as above:
`A(K) = 2 S + O + 2 O bits(V) + M + V bits(V) + 4 W`,
and for the input `A = sum of A(K)` plus the split charge of 8.1(c). `A` is an unsigned integer computed before any search, and
its arithmetic SATURATES: every sum and product of the formulas stops at `2^64 - 1`, so a result above that is the value
`2^64 - 1`. A saturated allowance is used only as the field `allowance` of the record and in the comparison `max(L, A)` of 7.11,
where it means that the engine's own limit never trips; an input is never refused because its allowance overflowed. LEMMA A. PROVED. The
units (a) to (d) and (f) of 8.1 that
the search-free run spends on `K` are at most `A(K)`, whatever the source order. Proof. START is `S` exactly. REFINE is at most
`2 O bits(V)` (Lemma 4(b)). FIX:
on the result path every node is fixed once and every line placed in `E` once (`V` units), every incidence is removed once (`M`
units), and a vertex is counted in a non-largest remainder component at most `bits(V) - 1` times, because such a component has at most
half the nodes and lines of the component it left: at most `V + M + V (bits(V) - 1) = M + V bits(V)`. ENTRIES: every line forms one
entry, whose fully fixed encoding is `8 o(l)` bytes longer than `b(l)` (`o(l)` its occurrences), so it costs `c(l) + o(l)`, `S + O` in
all. Twin units: Lemma TU. The sum is `A(K)`. The counts of (a) to (d) are made along the result path, one child at each rule 3 call, so
they bound the units (a) to (d) of the result path of every input, `guaranteed` or not. No unit is spent comparing two top components,
since none is compared (7.3), so the
bound holds for an engine that keeps no certificate of any top component. End of proof.
THE GUARANTEE. Every conforming engine MUST label every input for which `guaranteed` holds; no limit of its own may refuse it, and only
the refusals `input`, `graph` and `width` (7.11), which concern the input and the 64-bit range, can stop it. Lemma A makes this fair:
an engine that runs the search-free run spends at most `A` units, a polynomial in the size of the input (at most a constant times
`(S + W) + (O + V) bits(V)`), known before the run. Every engine MUST perform the search-free run on every top component of the input
before it begins the
search of any top component, so that a `limit` refusal carries the first failure of every component that is not `guaranteed`
(7.11). An input that is not `guaranteed` is labelled by
search (sections 5 and 6 in full, with the shortcuts of section 10) or refused with `limit`; a refusal is never a different answer.
FACTS ABOUT THE CLASS.

- If the fresh refinement and the nesting leave every call discrete or with a singleton, that is if `calls` has a zero third part,
  the input is `guaranteed`. In particular a top component whose plain incidence graph is a forest never branches (property 6).
- `guaranteed` is NOT decided by the refinement of the fresh start (5.6) or by the statistics of the record after it. Two inputs
  with the same `blank_nodes` `6`, `lines` `9`, `label_elements` `18`, `singled_out` `0`, `unsettled` `6`, `largest_cell`
  `present(3)`, `forest` false and cells of sizes `3` and `3` after that refinement: the directed complete bipartite graph
  `quad(a_i, <p>, b_j)` for `i, j` in `0, 1, 2` is guaranteed (its target cells
  are twin classes); the lines `quad(a_i, <p>, b_i)`, `quad(b_i, <p>, a_(i+1))`, `quad(a_i, <p>, a_(i+1))` (indices modulo `3`) are not
  (no transposition of two `a` or two `b` maps the lines onto themselves; failure record `(0, 3, 6, 9)`). Likewise the hub
  `quad(h, <p>, a)`, `quad(h, <p>, b)`, `quad(h, <p>, c)` is guaranteed (group order `6`), while the same lines with the directed
  triangle `quad(a, <q>, b)`, `quad(b, <q>, c)`, `quad(c, <q>, a)` added are not (failure record `(1, 3, 4, 6)`), with the same
  refinement of the fresh start.
- Inside the class: the 2-cycle `quad(a, <p>, b)`, `quad(b, <p>, a)` (target `2`, group order `2`); the complete bipartite graph
  written in one direction (12.4; with three nodes a side, targets `3` then `2`, group order `36`); the complete graph written in both

  `quad(h, <p>, l2)`: zero
  rule 3 calls, group order `2`).
- Outside the class: the complete bipartite graph written in both directions (two nodes a side: one cell of four after the refinement of
  the fresh start, two twin
  classes, failure record `(0, 4, 4, 8)`, group order `8`); every directed cycle of three or more nodes (12.2; the 4-cycle: failure
  record `(0, 4, 4, 4)`); regular graphs such as the cube graph (the input `CUBE` of 13.1: vertices `0 .. 7`, adjacent when their binary
  forms differ in one bit)
  written in both
  directions (targets `8` and `3`, group order `48`) and
  the quadratic residue graph on `13` vertices (the input `QR13` of 13.1: `u`, `v` adjacent when `v - u` is a non-zero square modulo `13`)
  written in both directions (no two nodes are twins; failure record `(0, 13, 13, 78)`).
VERSIONING. A wider guaranteed class is a change of the version of this definition, never an option of it.

7.11 OUTCOMES AND REFUSALS. The labelling of an input has one typed OUTCOME: `labelled(result)` with the result of 1.2, or
`refused(refusal)`. A refusal carries no partial result. REFUSALS are a closed enumeration; each variant has typed fields:

| code | variant | structural fields | diagnostic fields |
|---|---|---|---|
| `0` | `input` | `reason`: `slot` `0`, `count` `1`, `repeated` `2`, `kind` `3` (3.3, W1 to W4) | none |
| `1` | `graph` | `term`: the variant of the term held by an `empty-graph` line and carried by another line (3.3, W5): `iri` `0`, `literal` `1`, `blank` `2`, `triple` `3` | for `blank`, the source identifier of that node |
| `2` | `width` | `quantity`: `nodes` `0`, `lines` `1`, `incidences` `2`, `occurrences` `3`, `length` `4` (of a byte string, a payload included, of an encoding, or the number of terms of a `record`), `count` `5`, `units` `6` (the units (a) to (d) of the result path, 8.1) | none |
| `3` | `limit` | `broken_by` of 7.7, never empty | `source` (bytes), `nodes` (unsigned), `units` (unsigned): functions of the input and of the limit `L`, defined by the walk (8.3) |

A refusal for `width` is a function of the input: its quantities are those of the reference, and a counter of an engine's own search
that overflows is not one of them. The running total `R` of the walk of 8.3 SATURATES at `2^64 - 1`, as the allowance does (7.10). `R`
can exceed `max(L, A)` only when `max(L, A)` is below `2^64 - 1`: an engine that declares `L = 2^64 - 1` refuses nothing with `limit`, and
an engine with a smaller `L` stops with `limit` at the first charge that carries `R` above `max(L, A)` and needs no counter beyond that
charge. Every sum the text forms is assigned to one quantity of the table: the sums of lengths of
encodings (`line_bytes`, `blank_bearing_bytes`) to `length`; the numbers of nodes, lines, incidences and occurrences to the quantities
of those names; the units (a) to (d) of the result path to `units`; the twin units (f) enter no quantity. The sums `S` and `W` belong to
the allowance and saturate with it (7.10); the allowance is never a quantity. The running total of the walk (8.3), the twin units
included, goes to `limit` and never to `width`, and it saturates. Once the allowance has saturated no input is refused with `limit`,
and an engine need not keep the running total. By Lemma A, whose proof counts along the result path only, the units of the result
path are at most the unsaturated allowance, so they exceed `2^64 - 1` only for an input whose labelling needs more steps than any engine
can take. When several refusals apply, the outcome is the one of smallest code, and within a variant the smallest field value that
applies (for `graph`, the smallest term code, and in the diagnostic field the smallest source identifier of that code); so every refusal
other than `limit` is a function of the input. `limit` arises only for an input that is not `guaranteed` (7.10). An engine's LIMITED UNITS are the units (a) to (d) and (f) of 8.1
that the walk of 8.3 charges; the units (e) are never limited. The outcome is `limit` exactly when the limited units of the walk exceed
`max(L, A)`, `L` the limit of the engine (8.3), and never once `A` has saturated; an allowance equal to `2^64 - 1` is saturated. The test
of step 6 of 8.3 is the only test that refuses with `limit`, and a `guaranteed` input never fails it (8.3, Lemma W (4)). Every field of
the refusal is a function of the input as given and of `L`: `broken_by` is a function of the input (7.7); `source`, `nodes` and `units`
are defined by the walk (8.3).
No caller-supplied budget exists in this definition. A `limit` refusal is NOT invariant under relabelling: the limited units depend
on the source identifiers, through the twin units (f) (7.10) and through the order and the maps of the walk (8.3), so at one `L` the
same dataset under two namings may be refused under one and labelled under the other. This is a property of the definition, which is a
function of the input as given; the walk is invariant under a bijection that preserves the source order (8.3, Lemma W (3)). No label,
digest, identity, position, structural record or canonical map of a labelled input depends on the walk or on `L`. The GUARANTEE is
invariant: the limit never trips below `A`, and `A` bounds the units (a) to (d) and (f) of the search-free run under every naming
(Lemma A), so no naming of a `guaranteed` input is ever refused.

7.12 MEMORY, ORDER AND DETERMINISM.
KEYS. Rank the distinct encodings of the CONSTANT LEAVES (`iri` and `literal` terms) of the lines once, ascending bytewise. Rank the
distinct encodings `bytes(payload)` of the PAYLOADS of the `record` lines once, ascending bytewise (the encoding begins with the
length, so the rank order is the order of the lengths first), in a rank class of their own,
never compared with the ranks of constant leaves. The KEY of a term is `(0x01, r)` or `(0x02, r)` for a constant leaf of rank `r`;
`(0x03, key of s, key of p, key of o)` for a triple term;
`(0x04)` for `free`; `(0x05, q)` for `fixed(q)`. The key of an option is `(0x20)` for `absent` and `(0x21, key)` for `present`. The key
of a line is `(kind tag, count when the kind is annotation, keys of the slots in order)`; the key of a `record` is
`(0x44, r, n, keys of the terms in order)`, `r` the rank of its payload in its own class (the
kind tag already separates a record from every other line, and the payload rank is the only element at that place), and `n` the
number of terms. A key is the concatenation of the integers
listed, the keys of the parts included in place, so it is one list of integers. Keys compare as lists of integers, element by
element, a proper prefix first.
LEMMA K (the order of encodings equals the order of keys). PROVED. For two masked lines `u`, `v`, the encoding of `u` is smaller than
that of `v` exactly when the key of `u` is smaller than the key of `v`. Proof. By Lemma E no encoding of a masked line, or of one of its
fields, is a proper prefix of another, so the first differing byte of two encodings lies inside both and inside the first pair of
corresponding fields that differ. The first byte of a term or option is its tag, the integer compared first in the key. For two
constant leaves of one variant the encodings are distinct and not prefixes of each other, so their bytewise order is the order of their
ranks. For triple terms the encodings are the tag followed by three prefix-free encodings, so the first differing part decides, as in
the key. For `fixed(q)` the encoding is the tag and a fixed-width big-endian integer, which compares as the integer. A line encoding is
the kind tag, the count (fixed width) and the slots in order. For a `record` the encoding is the tag `0x44`, `bytes(payload)`, `u64(n)`
and the `n` terms: two payload encodings are distinct and not prefixes of each other (Lemma E), so their bytewise order is the order
of their ranks in the class of payloads; the number `n` is fixed width and compares as the integer; and the terms follow in order, as
for the slots of any other kind. A record never meets a line of another kind in a comparison of fields, because the kind tags differ.
End of proof.
MEMORY. A certificate entry may be held as `(m, key of its fully fixed line)`, and entries compare as `(m, key)`; no copy of any line
text exists, because there is no line text. One rank for the constant part of a line, taken together, would NOT do: for the
lines `quad(x, <p>, y)`, `quad(x, <q>, y)`, `quad(y, <p>, x)` with `x`, `y` fixed at `0`, `1` the order is first, second, third,
but the first and third lines have the same constant part, so that rank makes them tie and cannot order them; the representation
that orders the lines is the key, with one entry for each leaf and slot. The WORKING STATE of an engine is `O(O + Z)` integers, `O` the
label elements, one integer for each blank occurrence, and `Z` the number of constant leaf occurrences of the lines that hold a blank
node, counting the payload of each `record` line as one constant, plus the distinct constant leaf encodings and the distinct payload
encodings once, and no copy of any document. The canonical line sequence is
produced by sorting the keys of the lines once the
final positions are known (a line with no blank node has a key of constants only and may be merged from any sorted source), and each
line is encoded into the hash as it is emitted, so the sequence is never held. For `k` top components an engine needs one record of
constant size for each (the identity, the size, a handle on the smallest source identifier and the means to regenerate the component);
the certificate encoding is streamed into the hash and not kept.
DETERMINISM. The canonical line sequence, its digest, the identities, the canonical map and the structural part of the record do not
depend on the thread count, the platform, the word size or the iteration order of any container. To guarantee it an engine (i) uses
exact unsigned 64-bit arithmetic and refuses on overflow (2.1), never a wrapped or floating value; (ii) never lets the order of a
container, a memory address, an insertion order or a thread's finishing time decide a comparison or a tie: every sort is by a total
order of this text, and every tie among components or children is decided by 7.9; (iii) runs children or components in parallel only
when it combines their results by the orders of 6.3, 7.3 and 7.9; (iv) shares a pruning bound between threads only if the bound stops on a
strictly greater `s`, or a strictly greater `E` with `s` equal (10.4); (v) adds the charges of the walk to its running total in the order
of the walk (8.3), whatever order it explored in. Figures an engine reports of its own search (7.7) may differ between runs; the structural part of
the record may not. The OUTCOME is in this list: for an input that is not `guaranteed` it is a function of the input and of `L`
(8.3), the same in every run, with every thread count and every schedule; a `guaranteed` input is labelled in every run.

7.13 VERIFICATION OF A CLAIM.
CHEAP: that the canonical line sequence is the input RELABELLED by the canonical map. Given the input lines, the map and the claimed
sequence, check that the map is a bijection of the blank nodes onto `0 .. N - 1`, replace every blank leaf, encode, and compare the
sorted encodings with the claim, or their digest with a claimed digest: near-linear. This shows that the claim is isomorphic to the
input, not that it is canonical.
CANONICITY WHERE THE INPUT IS GUARANTEED: re-run the search-free run (7.10) and compare; at most `A` units, polynomial. For such an
input verification of canonicity IS recomputation: nothing cheaper exists beyond the relabelling check above. In particular
where the record's `calls` has a zero third part, the re-run is refinement and nesting alone, in near-linear time; if it meets a rule
3 call the record was false. ELSEWHERE: a search within the verifier's own limit, needed only for the failing components.
NO SHORT GENERAL WITNESS. Suppose that, for every input, canonicity of a claimed sequence could be checked in polynomial time from a
polynomial-size witness. Then two non-isomorphic inputs `G` and `H` would have a polynomial witness of non-isomorphism: two claimed
sequences that differ, each with its witness (isomorphic inputs have equal canonical sequences, section 9). So graph non-isomorphism
would be in NP, which is not known. The implication is proved here; nothing normative rests on whether graph non-isomorphism is in NP.

7.14 LABEL SPELLING (the one place). A document format requires a text label for a blank node. The label of position `i` is `i` in
decimal with no leading zero and no affix, for example `0`, `7`, `10`. A label is a blank node label and is printed in the
blank-node form of the target syntax, never bare: in N-Quads `_:0`. Labels are output only and are never parsed: no implementation
parses a label to recover a position, an identity or any other meaning; the typed values are the interface. Position order differs
from the byte order of labels (`10` precedes `2`); a dataset read back from its decimal canonical labels as names is therefore not
promised the identity map of Lemma I (7.9). No textual spelling of a stable identity is defined (7.8).

7.15 THE HASH, THE IDENTIFIER AND THE SECURITY ASSUMPTION. The hash function is named in this section only.
THE IDENTIFIER of the labelling is the typed pair `(name: bytes, version: unsigned)`. The `name` of this text is the ASCII byte string
`nested-refinement-labelling` and its `version` is `1`. A name is a non-empty byte string of printable ASCII bytes without the space
(`0x21` to `0x7E`). The name is an opaque constant: nothing parses it, and no rule or reader depends on any part of it.

the name and the digits hold no space. The two contexts are the ASCII strings `nested-refinement-labelling 1 component` and

enter every identity,
and identities order the top components (7.3); so the POSITIONS of an input with two or more non-isomorphic top components, and not
only its identities and digests, depend on the name and on the version. The context is text, and the version is written in decimal
in it, because the reference interfaces of the hash
below take the context as a text string; this is the one place where the text writes a number as digits for an external requirement,

| name, `0x20`, version digits, `0x20`, `lines` | the digest of the canonical line sequence | 7.6 |

THE FUNCTION. `H(context, message)` is `BLAKE3` (the specification cited in 13.2) in its key-derivation mode, with `context` as the mode's
context string and `message` as the key material, and the function's full default output of 32 bytes. The context is passed as the ASCII
bytes given above, exactly, with no terminator and no normalisation. One function serves both shared values; a different function for
either is not this labelling, and a value computed with another function is a different value outside this text. ITS ONE COST. An
environment confined to a list of approved hash functions that does not include this one cannot produce or check these values: no
identity, no digest, and no position of an input with two or more non-isomorphic top components. THE SECURITY ASSUMPTION (the one
cryptographic assumption of this text): `H` is collision resistant at the level its designers state for it (128 bits for its 32-byte
output; this text assumes it and does not prove it). What depends on it: that equal component identities occur only for isomorphic
components (I6), hence the order of 7.3, the copy indices, `top_multiplicities` and the top-level factors of `group_order`; and that equal
digests of canonical line sequences occur only for isomorphic inputs. If it failed, two non-isomorphic top components with equal
identities would be treated as copies: their copy indices, the record's multiplicities and group order would be wrong, and the canonical
line sequence of an input holding both would depend on their source identifiers. This text does not detect that case and defines no
refusal for it. Nothing inside a top component depends on the assumption: the search, the certificates, the positions within a component
and Theorem RB use no hash.

## 8. Work

### 8.1 The unit

A WORK UNIT is one of the following. Bytes are bytes of the ENCODING (3.7); for a line `l`, `b(l)` is the length of the encoding of
`l` with every leaf `free`.
(a) START. For each line `l` of a top component, the START CHARGE `c(l) = 1 + ceil(b(l) / 8)`.
(b) REFINE. For each popped cell `W` and each incidence of a member of `W`, as many units as its label has elements (a node that
occurs `r` times in a line costs `r`).
(c) FIX. For each fixing (of a call under rules 1 and 2, and of each child under rule 3): one unit for each removed vertex (each
node fixed and each line placed in `E`); one unit for each removed incidence: a pair of a FREE node of the call and a line of the call in
which it occurs, such that the
node is fixed by this fixing or the line is placed in `E` (a constant of the call is not a vertex, and its occurrences are not
counted; a node that occurs twice in a line is one pair); and, when the remainder falls into two or more free components, as many units as the sum, over all of them, of nodes plus lines, less the largest of these sums (so the charge is a function of the sizes of the components; an engine that keeps one of several largest components in place and copies the others spends the same units, whichever it keeps). The split of the input into top components is charged in the same way, with no
removed vertex; lines with no blank node belong to no component and are not counted. A line placed in `E` is therefore charged three times
by design: as a removed vertex, through its removed
incidences, and as an entry formed.
(d) ENTRIES FORMED. For each entry `(m, line)` formed, one unit plus one unit per 8 bytes, rounded up, of the encoding of its fully
fixed masked `line`; the eight bytes of `m` are not counted. Since a `fixed` leaf has eight bytes more than a `free` leaf, the entry of
a line `l` with `o(l)` occurrences costs exactly `c(l) + o(l)`.
(e) COMPARISON. For each comparison of two entries within a comparison of two certificates: one unit for `m`; if the two values of `m` are
equal, one unit per 8 bytes, rounded up, of the bytes of ONE encoding read, which are the bytes up to and including the first byte that
differs, or the whole encoding when the two are equal (the encodings are prefix-free, so two distinct encodings differ inside both). The
comparison of the values of `s` and of list lengths is not charged. A comparison of two certificates stops at the first difference.
(f) TWIN. For each test of a pair of nodes (7.10), the start charge `c(l)` of every line of the top component that holds one of the
two nodes, whether the test passes or fails.
STRUCTURE OF THE TOTALS. The units (a) to (d) of one call are functions of the input and of the definition alone. The units of a run
are the sum over the calls the run charges. Three totals are functions of the input: the total over the REFERENCE TREE, the search in
which every child of every rule 3 call is explored; the total over the RESULT PATH (the field `units` of the record, 7.7); and the
LIMITED UNITS, the total of the walk of 8.3, which charges the calls that the stops of 10.4 and the skips of 10.1 and 10.2 leave, in
the order of that section, together with the twin units (f) of the tests that the walk makes. The units (a) to (d) of the walk are at most
those of the reference tree and at least those of the result path (8.3, Lemma W (4)). The twin units (f) are a function of the input AS
GIVEN, its source identifiers included, and of no choice of an engine, but NOT of its isomorphism class: the pair that a test examines
depends on the source order (the directed 4-cycle `a -> b -> c -> d -> a` costs `9` twin units in the search-free run and the isomorphic
`a -> c -> b -> d -> a` costs `12`); the order and the maps of the walk depend on the source order as well (8.3), so the limited units
are not invariant under relabelling and enter no quantity that is (7.7). The units (e) depend on the method used to order and to choose;
they are never limited (7.11) and stay outside the engine-independent record. Section 12 prints them under ONE reference method, named
there; its figures are a function of the input and of that method, and any engine reproduces them by running that method on the
certificates, whatever method its own search uses.
Why a long literal cannot outrun the units: the time to form or to compare an entry is at most proportional to the bytes it reads plus
a constant, and the charge is at least one unit per 8 bytes plus one.
Not counted: the sorting of keys (Lemma 4(c)), the sorting of the entries of one `E`, and bookkeeping. Discovery of components is
charged by (c), as 8.6 states.

### 8.2 The trail and lazy restriction

1. LAZY RESTRICTION IS RESULT-NEUTRAL. PROVED. The partitions restricted to a component are mathematical objects (cells, their order,
   their sizes), and any representation of them gives the same result. Colours are used only through their order, their equality and the
   sizes of cells (the next pair of the queue, the smallest colour among largest fragments, the order of the cells of one side), so an
   order-preserving renaming of colours changes no decision. An engine may delete removed vertices from their cells, keep the largest
   remainder component in place and copy out only the other components (each vertex is copied into a component of at most half the size of
   the one it left, so at most `bits(V) - 1` times along a path), and may undo a child's changes by a trail instead of copying; none
   changes a cell or its order, so none changes the result.
2. LEMMA T (the trail). PROVED. Making and undoing a child by a trail costs at most 6 times the units (b) plus twice the units (c) charged
   to that child, plus a constant. Proof. A split of a cell `X` moves only the touched vertices `T_X` (those with a non-empty key); the untouched first
   fragment stays in place; the split writes the metadata (start, size, queued flag) of at most `|T_X| + 1` fragments. Every touched
   vertex has a visited incidence, so moves plus metadata per split are at most `2 |T_X| + 1`; summed over a REFINE they are at most
   twice the units (b) plus the number of pops, and the number of pops is at most those units, because every popped cell has a member
   with an incidence; so splits cost at most 3 times the units. The individualising split costs a constant. The queue is empty at the end
   of every REFINE, so nothing is undone for it. A FIX records each removed vertex and each removed incidence once (charged in (c)); the
   copies of non-largest components are charged per vertex in (c). Undo replays the trail backwards at the same cost. The connectivity
   structure of 8.6 is never rolled back: an undo is a reinsertion, accounted in Lemma D. End of proof.
3. An engine that copies the partitions for each child spends `O(n_K + m_K)` per child outside the units; this changes no result, and its
   units are unchanged.
4. LABELS NEED NOT BE RECOMPUTED. By property 7(a) all lines of one cell share one strictly increasing renumbering of free occurrences
   after a fixing, and keys compare labels only within one cell, so an engine may keep the original occurrence numbers: equal keys stay
   equal and their order is kept.

### 8.3 The walk and the outcome of a limit

An engine declares its LIMIT `L`, an unsigned 64-bit integer; an engine that refuses nothing declares `2^64 - 1`. Whether it refuses an
input with `limit` (7.11) is decided by ONE sequential computation, THE WALK, defined in this section. The walk is defined on a
well-formed input (3.3) whose quantities fit in 64 bits (7.11): an input refused with `input`, `graph` or `width` has no walk. It is a
function of the input as given, its source identifiers included, and of `L`. It depends on no engine, no order of exploration, no
schedule, no thread count and no run.

THE SEARCH-FREE RUN is not part of the walk. It is performed first (7.10); it decides `guaranteed` and `broken_by`, spends at most `A`
(Lemma A), and none of its units enters the running total `R` below. The twin units that enter `R` are those of the tests that the walk
itself makes (step 4).

THE CHARGES. The walk keeps a RUNNING TOTAL `R`, which starts at `0` and SATURATES at `2^64 - 1`, and adds to it the LIMITED UNITS, the
units (a) to (d) and (f) of 8.1, in CHARGES. A charge is one addition, and a charge of `0` is not made. The charges are: the SPLIT of the
input into top components (c); the START of a top component (a); the REFINE of the fresh start of a top component (b); the REFINE of a
child (b), its individualisation and the refinement that follows; the FIXING of a call or of a child, ONE charge that adds its FIX-SUM,
the units (c) of its fixing without the charge for the split of the remainder together with the units (d) of its entries, and its
SPLIT, the charge for the split of its remainder into components (c), the SPLIT left out for a stopped child; the TWIN TESTS of a rule 3
call, the units (f) of all its tests, as one sum. The units (e) are never charged. A limit test follows EVERY charge (step 6), the first
included. THE TOTALS BY KIND are START, REFINE (the REFINE of the fresh starts included), FIX-SUM, SPLIT and TWIN; a
charge of a fixing counts its two parts under FIX-SUM and SPLIT, and SPLIT is one kind for the split of the input (step 1) and the split
of every call and every child.

THE STEPS.

1. One charge: the SPLIT of the input into top components (8.1(c)).
2. For each top component `K`, in ascending order of its smallest source identifier (7.9): one charge START of `K`; one charge REFINE of
   its fresh start (5.6); then step 3 on its top call. Top components of equal identity are each walked in full.
3. A CALL `X`.
   (a) `X` takes rule 1 or rule 2: one charge, the FIX-SUM of `X` plus its SPLIT; then step 3 on each nested call of `X`, one after the
   other, each in full before the next, in ascending order of the smallest source identifier of its nodes.
   (b) `X` takes rule 3: let `v_1 < .. < v_k` be the nodes of its target cell `T` in source order (2.2); `T` holds at least two nodes,
   so `X` has at least two free nodes. The BEST starts as nothing. VISIT `v_1`, `v_2`, .. in this order. To visit `v_i`:
   (i) SKIP. If `v_i` is in one class of the relation `~` of step 5 with some `v_j`, `j < i`, the child of `v_i` is SKIPPED: nothing is
   charged and nothing else is done for it. Every `v_j` with `j < i` counts here, whether its child was completed, stopped or skipped,
   because `~` is a relation on the nodes of `T`. This test is made before any charge.
   (ii) One charge REFINE of the child. Let `s` be the number of nodes in singleton node cells after it (6.3 item 1).
   (iii) FIX the child (6.2). Let `E` be its list of entries (6.3 item 2). No charge is made at this step.
   (iv) STOP ON `s`. If there is a best and `s` is strictly greater than the `s` of the best, the child is STOPPED.
   (v) STOP ON `E`. If there is a best, `s` is equal to the `s` of the best, and `E` is strictly greater than the `E` of the best, as
   lists of entries (6.3 item 5), the child is STOPPED.
   (vi) One charge for the fixing of the child: its FIX-SUM alone if it is stopped, its FIX-SUM plus its SPLIT if it survives both
   stops. Nothing more is charged or done for a stopped child.
   (vii) If this is the first child visited (it is `v_1`, which has no best and so is never stopped), step 4 on `X`. The charge TWIN TESTS
   of `X` thus follows the charge of (vi) for the child of `v_1` and precedes the first nested call of that child.
   (viii) Step 3 on each nested call of the child, as in (a). The child is COMPLETED and has its certificate (6.3).
   (ix) If there is no best, or the certificate of the child is strictly smaller than that of the best, the child becomes the best.
   When every node of `T` has been visited, the certificate of `X` is that of the best.
4. THE TWIN TESTS of a rule 3 call `X`, with `u = v_1`. `TW` is one union-find on the nodes of the top component, of the twin tests that
   passed, kept for the whole walk of the component and never undone; it starts with every node alone, and it is separate from the twin
   classes of the search-free run. For each `w` in `T` other than `u`, in ascending source order, that is not in the class of `u` in
   `TW`: TEST `(u, w)`. A test costs the start charge `c(l)` of every line of the top component that holds `u` or `w` (8.1(f)), whether
   it passes or fails; it PASSES when the transposition `(u w)` maps the set of lines of the top component onto itself (7.10), and then
   the classes of `u` and `w` are joined in `TW`. A failing test does not stop the tests. All the tests of `X` are made, then ONE charge
   TWIN TESTS of their total units, if it is not `0`.
5. THE RELATION `~`. At the visit of `v_i` of a rule 3 call `X`, `~` is the smallest equivalence relation on `T` that relates the pairs
   of (K1) and, for every KNOWN MAP `phi` of (K2) and (K3) and every node `x` of `T`, `x` to `phi(x)`. A map is a permutation of the
   free nodes of `X` that is the identity on the nodes it does not name. A map is CHECKED before use: it is used only if it carries the
   lines of `X` as `X` sees them (every leaf of a node fixed above `X` held by its position, 3.6; the state of a call of 10.1) onto
   themselves; a map that does not is dropped whole. A checked map is an automorphism of `X` that fixes its constants, so it maps `T`,
   a cell of the partition of `X`, onto itself (property 7(d)); the classes of `~` are joined within `T`. The relation at a visit comes
   from step 4 and from the children completed before `v_i`, never from a stopped or a skipped child:
   (K1) TWINS. After step 4 of `X`, every two nodes `x`, `y` of `T` that are in one class of `TW`, as `TW` stands at the visit, are
   related: `x ~ y`, by the transposition `(x y)`. Before step 4 nothing is related by (K1).
   (K2) EQUAL CHILDREN. The KEY of a completed child is its certificate. The WITNESSES of `X` are completed children, kept in the order
   of completion, which is the order of the visit; at most `M = max(floor(2^22 / n), 8)` are kept, `n` the number of free nodes of `X`,
   which is at least `2`. When a child `c_k` is completed: if a kept witness has a key equal to its key, `c_j` the first such in the order
   of completion, the map `lambda_k^(-1) o lambda_j` is known, where `lambda_m` gives each free node of `X` its final position below the
   child `c_m` with the offset taken as `0` (6.5, with M1 and M2 applied inside the child); otherwise `c_k` becomes a witness if fewer
   than `M` are kept, and if `M` are kept it is not kept and names no map to a later child. REMARK (PROVED). At a top call (`p = 0`,
   nothing fixed) two completed children have equal certificates exactly when they have equal LEAF SETS, the sets of the lines of the
   component with every blank leaf replaced by `fixed(lambda_m)` (10.1 (5)). Proof. Equal certificates give equal leaf sets (F1 of 7.7:
   by Theorem RB the two sets of relabelled lines are equal). Equal leaf sets give the automorphism `lambda_k^(-1) o lambda_j` of 10.1 (5), which sends `v_j`
   to `v_k` because both take the position `0` (F1 of 7.7), so the two children have equal certificates (F2 of 7.7). End of proof. An
   engine may therefore compare leaf sets in place of certificates at a top call: the witnesses, the maps and the classes are the same.
   (K3) CARRIED MAPS. For every completed child `c`, the maps of `CARRIED(c)`. For a completed child or a call `Y` of rule 1 or 2,
   `CARRIED(Y)` is: for each nested call `Z` of `Y`, the maps `OWN(Z)`; and, the nested calls of `Y` being taken in the order of 6.4 and
   each compared with the next in that order, for each two consecutive nested calls with equal certificates their EXCHANGE, the map that
   sends each node of the first to the node of the second with the same final position when the offset of each is taken as `0` (6.5),
   each node of the second back, and fixes every other node (10.1 (2)). `OWN(Z)` is: none for a call of rule 1; `CARRIED(Z)` for a call
   of rule 2; for a call of rule 3 (walked in full by step 3), its maps of (K2) at the end of its walk and `CARRIED(c)` for each
   completed child `c` of it. A twin test of `Z` adds nothing to `OWN(Z)`: the pair it joins is in `TW`, which (K1) reads at every rule 3
   call of the component. A carried map is lifted by the identity (10.1, LIFTING).
6. THE OUTCOME. The allowance `A` (7.10) is SATURATED when it equals `2^64 - 1`. After each charge, if `A` is not saturated and `R`
   exceeds `max(L, A)`, the walk ENDS at once with the outcome `refused(limit)`, whose fields are: `broken_by` of 7.7; `source`, the
   smallest source identifier of the top component whose step 2 holds the charge; `nodes`, its `n_K`; `units`, the value of `R` after
   the charge. The charge of step 1 never ends the walk (Lemma W (4)), so the charge that ends it lies in step 2 of a top component, and
   `source` and `nodes` are always defined. Otherwise the walk ends after the last charge with the outcome `labelled(result)`, the
   result of sections 5 to 7; `R` is then the TOTAL of the walk, the limited units of the input. This test is the only one by which an
   input is refused with `limit`, and a `guaranteed` input never fails it (Lemma W (4)).

WHAT AN ENGINE MAY DO. An engine may compute by any method, sequential or parallel, with the shortcuts of section 10 or with none, with
others of its own, provided that it reports the outcome and every field that the walk gives. It follows that: (1) it may explore
children, nested calls and top components ahead and in any order, but it adds the charges to `R` in the order of the walk and ends
at the first charge after which `R` exceeds `max(L, A)`; charges that it computed beyond that charge are not made. (2) A child that
the walk skips is charged nothing; a stopped child is charged its REFINE and its FIX-SUM (steps (ii) and (vi)), and a child that
survives the stops its REFINE and then its FIX-SUM and SPLIT in one charge, whatever work the engine did on it. (3) An engine that does
not explore a child that the walk visits has no charge to report for it unless it computes it; the walk asks no engine to do work for
the sole purpose of reporting a charge, so a method that skips a child the walk visits is a method of another walk, not of this one.
(4) An engine that stops for another cause, such as time, memory or a caller's signal, has no outcome of this text: it is not the
refusal `limit`, and it writes no result. An engine never writes a partial result and never falls back to another labelling. An engine
may report the total of a completed walk beside the result (7.7); it is then this value.

LEMMA W (the walk). PROVED.
(1) EVERY KNOWN MAP IS AN AUTOMORPHISM of `X` (7.7) that fixes every constant of `X`, so the check of step 5 never drops one. Proof. (K1):
the classes of `TW` are joined only by tests that passed, and a test is exact (Lemma TW(a)), so each join is of two twins; twin-ness is
an equivalence (Lemma TW(b)), so every two nodes of one class of `TW` are twins. Two twins `x`, `y` that are both free in `X`, as every
node of `T` is, are exchanged by the transposition `(x y)`, which maps the lines of the top component onto themselves and fixes every
other node, the constants of `X` included; every line that holds `x` or `y` is a line of `X`, so the transposition carries the lines of
`X`, as `X` sees them, onto themselves (Lemma TW(c), 10.2). (K2): the children `c_j` and `c_k` have equal certificates, so by F1 of 7.7 (the
Corollary of 6.8, (b)) the map is an automorphism of `X` that sends `v_j` to `v_k`. (K3): an exchange is an automorphism of the call
that holds the two nested calls (10.1 (2)); a map that is an automorphism of a nested call, extended by the identity, is an automorphism
of every call above it (LIFTING, 10.1); by induction on the nesting each carried map is an automorphism of `X`. End of proof.
(2) THE WALK RETAINS THE CHOSEN CHILD. At every rule 3 call `X` the best at the end is the chosen child `v*` of M1; `v*` is neither
skipped nor stopped; no attaining child is stopped, and an attaining child other than `v*` is skipped or completed; when `X` has at most `2,048` free nodes, the attaining children are the members of the class of `v*` in `~` after
the last visit. So the certificate, the call structure, the final positions and the canonical map that the walk gives are those of
sections 5 to 7. Proof. Induction on the nodes of the call; below `X` the walk gives certificates and chosen children as stated. Let `m`
be the smallest certificate among the children of `X` and `v*` the attaining child of smallest source identifier. A child that attains
is never stopped (10.4). `v*` is not skipped: otherwise `v* ~ v_j` with `j` smaller, so a composite `psi` of known maps, transpositions
of (K1) and their inverses, an automorphism of `X` by (1), sends `v_j` to `v*`, and by F2 of 7.7 the child of `v_j` has certificate `m`
and attains with a smaller identifier, which is false. So `v*` is completed. Every child visited before `v*` does not attain, so any
that is completed has a certificate greater than `m`; `v*` therefore becomes the best, and no later child, whose certificate is at
least `m`, replaces it. A skipped child attains exactly when the child it is linked to does (F2), and a stopped one does not attain.
When `n` is at most `2,048`, `M` is at least `floor(2^22 / n)`, which is at least `n`, and `n` is at least the number of children, so
every completed child whose key equals no kept witness is kept: the cap of (K2) never refuses a witness, every completed attaining child
has the certificate `m`, and (K2) joins it to the first completed attaining child; hence the attaining children form one class, that
of `v*`, and no other child is in it (F2). End of proof. Above `2,048` free nodes the class of `v*` may hold fewer than the attaining
children; the record (7.7) is defined by sections 5 to 7 and does not depend on it.
(3) A FUNCTION OF THE INPUT. Every step reads the source order, sizes, certificates, the final positions below a completed child
(functions of the input as given, by M1 and M2), the witnesses, which are kept in the order of the visit, the outcomes of the twin
tests, which are exact, and `TW`, which is built in the order of the walk; the units of a charge are functions of the call (8.1). So
the walk, `R` after each charge and every field are functions of the input as given and of `L`. If `sigma` is a bijection that maps the
lines of one input onto those of another and preserves the source order, it maps the walk onto the walk, charge by charge: sizes,
certificates and units are those of the images (section 9, property 1), and the orders and the final positions are carried by M4.
(4) THE TOTAL. (a) The units (a) to (d) of the walk are at most those of the reference tree: every call the walk charges is a call of the
reference tree, charged at most its units there (a stopped child only its REFINE and its FIX-SUM), and at most once. (b) They are at
least those of the result path, which is walked in full, by (2). (c) The charge of step 1 is at most `A`, which contains the split of the
input, so it never ends the walk. (d) If the input is `guaranteed`, the total is at most `A`: at every rule 3 call that the walk
reaches, `T` is one twin class, so every test of step 4 passes and by (K1) every `v_j` with `j` above `1` is in the class of `v_1`; only
`v_1` is visited, it is completed (there is no best), and it is the chosen child. By induction the calls reached are those of the result
path. Its tests are made before the nested calls of its child, so a call is tested before every call below it, as in the search-free
run, and two calls of the walk that are neither ancestor nor descendant have no node in common: the pairs tested, their order and their
units are those of the search-free run, at most `4 W` (Lemma TU). The other charges are the START, the fresh REFINE and the units (b) to
(d) of the result path: the units that Lemma A bounds by `A`. End of proof.

COMPARISON WITH OTHER CHARGES. Take the same input and the same `L`; an engine that charges its own units counts the units (8.1) of the
calls it explores and of its twin tests. (a) The units (a) to (d) of an engine that explores every child of every rule 3 call are at least
those of the walk (Lemma W (4)); its twin tests are its own, and no inclusion of the twin units is claimed. (b) An engine that charges a
subset of the calls of the walk, each at most as much, with the same twin tests, counts at most the total of the walk: it refuses nothing
that the walk labels, and may label inputs that the walk refuses. (c) An engine whose calls are neither a subset nor a superset of the walk's
has no inclusion, and the refusals of the two differ in both directions, on different inputs. In particular an input whose total of the
walk does not exceed `A` is labelled at every `L`. The result of a labelled input does not depend on which engine labelled it (section 7).

EXAMPLE. The directed 4-cycle `a -> b -> c -> d -> a` has the allowance `232` and the limited units `154`; the isomorphic
`a -> c -> b -> d -> a` has the allowance `232` and the limited units `202`. Neither exceeds `A`, so neither is refused at any `L`; but the totals
differ, because the maps of (K2) depend on the source order. `COMPL(3, 5)` (13.1), on `n0` to `n7`, has the allowance `2,608` and the limited
units `3,506`, and under the relabelling of seed `5` the limited units `4,539`: at `L = 3,506` the first is labelled and the second
is refused with `limit`.

### 8.4 What the units bound

The units count normative steps and are NOT a clock; they serve the refusal rule and the allowance, and no claim is made that totals
are comparable across engines except the three of 8.1 (the reference tree, the result path and the walk). The time of an engine that follows 8.2 is bounded by them as follows: refinement,
by Lemma 4; sorting, at most a factor `bits(V)` for a comparison sort or a constant for a bucket sort over a persistent array (Lemma
4(c)); the trail, a factor 6 (Lemma T); discovery of components, `O(log^2 V)` amortised per removed incidence over any prefix of the
run (8.6); entries and comparisons, a constant per unit (8.1).

### 8.5 Cost over components

Property 8 of section 9: the units of a call after its fixing are at most the units of the fixing plus `1 + bits(r)` times the sum of
the units of its `r` nested calls, counting the comparisons of a merge sort of the nested certificates, each comparison charged as
8.1(e) charges it. The bound concerns an engine that orders by such a sort; the units (e) of another method differ.

### 8.6 Discovery of components

Components are mathematical objects (4.2), so any method of finding them is result-neutral; the method affects time only. Discovery is
charged by the units of the fixing: one unit for each incidence of a removed vertex (8.1(c)). The bound used is the article
"Poly-logarithmic deterministic fully-dynamic algorithms for connectivity, minimum spanning tree, 2-edge, and biconnectivity", Journal of
the ACM 48(4), 2001, pages 723 to 760 (Lemma D rests on it). For a run with no undo (deletions only), its Theorem 6 (a deletions-only
minimum spanning forest algorithm, stated for the spanning forest, which gives connectivity), initialised on `n` vertices and `m` edges,
serves any sequence of deletions in `O(m log^2 n)` in total, the initialisation included. For a run with undo by reinsertion, its Theorem
3 (fully dynamic connectivity) gives `O(log^2 n)` amortised time per insertion or deletion, on the assumption of its section 1 that the
graph starts with no edges; and its section 2.1 gives the identity of the tree that contains a vertex, and the size of that tree, as
queries of time `O(log n)` (a size is a query of that cost, not a constant-time read). That the vertices of a tree can be listed in time
linear in its size is NOT stated by the article: it is this text's own inference from the structure that section 2.1 describes, a balanced
binary tree over the Euler tour of the tree, which a walk of that tree lists. That the bound is an amortised total over any prefix of the
run follows from the article's proof (each insertion is charged in advance for the work that its later deletions cause), not from a
sentence of it, so a refusal at a limit does not break it. An engine that builds the incidence graph of a top component by one insertion
for each incidence, deletes the incidences of the removed vertices at each fixing, identifies the components of the remainder by one root
query for each former neighbour of a removed vertex, and undoes a child by reinserting its deleted incidences, spends at most
`O((I_built + I_removed + I_reinserted) log^2 V)` on connectivity over any prefix of its run, plus the enumeration of the non-largest
components, which (c) charges. This bounds discovery within a polylogarithmic factor of the units. A CONSTANT FACTOR IS NOT KNOWN for this
use: the lower bound of the article "Logarithmic lower bounds in the cell-probe model", SIAM Journal on Computing 35(4), 2006, concerns
fully dynamic connectivity with arbitrary queries, its authors state that it does not cover deletions alone, and this use has
stack-ordered undo and structured queries, so it is no evidence either way. An engine that searches the whole remainder at each fixing may
spend time above its units by a factor of the depth of the nest; this changes no result and its units are unchanged. This is the one place
where the units bound the work within a polylogarithmic factor and not within a constant. LEMMA D. PROVED, from Theorem 3 and section 2.1
of the article cited above. It concerns time only: no result, no unit and no refusal depends on it; only the time bounds of 8.4 and the
remark on undo in Lemma T do. The discovery and the undo of any search cost
`O((I_built + I_removed + I_reinserted) log^2 V)` over any prefix, plus the enumeration of the non-largest components (charged in (c)),
where `I_built` counts the incidences inserted once at the start, `I_removed` the incidences removed by fixings, and each removed
incidence is reinserted at most once, by the undo of its fixing. Proof. A fixing deletes the incidences of every removed vertex; the
components of the remainder are the trees of the structure that contain the former neighbours of the removed vertices (the component of
the call was connected, so every component of the remainder touches one of them); each is identified by a root query, its size is one
query of section 2.1, and the non-largest are enumerated (by this text's own inference above) and copied (charged); undoing a child
reinserts the deleted incidences. The `I_built` term is covered by the units, since the start refinement visits every incidence at least
once. End of proof.

### 8.7 The size of the search

PROVED: (T1) a component whose plain incidence graph is a tree never branches, nor does anything nested in it (property 6); its work is
Lemma 4 plus its fixing charges. (T2) If the fresh refinement of a top component is discrete there is one leaf (rule 1). A remark on which
nothing normative rests: for a uniformly random graph the refinement is discrete with probability tending to one, a statement of the kind
that Theorem 1.2 of the article "Random graph isomorphism", SIAM Journal on Computing 9(3), 1980, pages 628 to 635, proves for a
different algorithm (sorting by degree and coding adjacency to the vertices of highest degree); this text gives no argument that carries
it over; the statement is attributed also to the article "Canonical labelling of graphs in linear average time" (1979). No number from
either article is used, and nothing in this text depends on either. (T3) If at every rule 3 call of the result path the target cell is one
twin class, the search-free run explores one child per call, and the work is at most `A` (Lemma A). (T4) The reference tree of a call on a
component of `n` nodes has at most `2 n! - 1` calls, and each call has at most `n` children. Proof. Let `f(n)` be the largest number of
calls of the reference tree of a call on `n` nodes; `f(1) = 1`. A call that takes rule 1 has one call. A call that takes rule 2 has itself
and the calls of its nested calls, whose components are disjoint and hold at most `n - 1` nodes in all. A call that takes rule 3 has
itself and, for each of at most `n` children, the calls of that child's nested calls, again disjoint and on at most `n - 1` nodes in all.
Suppose `f(m) <= 2 m! - 1` for `m < n`. Since a sum of factorials of positive integers with sum at most `n - 1` is at most `(n - 1)!`, the
nested calls of one child or of a rule 2 call hold at most `2 (n - 1)! - 1` calls, so `f(n) <= 1 + n (2 (n - 1)! - 1) <= 2 n! - 1` for
`n >= 2`. End of proof. Each child costs at most `2 O bits(V)` units of REFINE (Lemma 4(a)), at most `2 V + M` units of FIX and at most
`S + O` units of ENTRIES, so the work of the reference tree is at most `S` plus `n (2 n! - 1)` times `2 O bits(V) + 2 V + M + S + O`:
exponential in `n`, and the only general bound. No polynomial bound is known or claimed in general, and none can hold for a computation by
individualisation and refinement as follows. The article "An exponential lower bound for individualization-refinement algorithms for graph
isomorphism" (STOC 2018, arXiv 1705.03283; its Theorem 3.2, Proposition 3.1 and section 8) shows that for every fixed `k` there are
families of rigid graphs on which the search tree of every individualisation-refinement procedure whose cell selector, refinement operator
and node invariant are no stronger than `k`-dimensional colour refinement, as defined in the note "The k-Dimensional Weisfeiler-Leman
Algorithm" (Immerman and Sengupta, arXiv 1907.09582, 2019; the `k`-dimensional Weisfeiler-Leman algorithm is called `k`-dimensional colour
refinement in this text), has exponentially many nodes (the first article calls such procedures `k`-realizable), even when the whole
automorphism group is given and with recursion on components. Written as lines (each undirected edge as two lines, each colour class by a
line with a colour constant), each such graph is an input on which an engine that follows this definition searches an exponential tree;
this encoding is this text's own. The same article notes that these graphs have bounded colour-class size, for which isomorphism in
polynomial time is known by group-theoretic methods (reported there). The defence is the engine's limit and the refusal `limit` (8.3), and
the guarantee of 7.10 for the class it covers.

## 9. Properties

Each property is proved by the argument given with it. In
this section a call on `K` holds a stable pair `pi` in its own STRUCTURE `A` (the constants of its ancestors; the nodes fixed by this
call, or by this child, are still vertices in singleton cells), and a component `K'` left after the fixing has its own structure `B`
(the nodes just fixed are constants `fixed(q)`; the free occurrences are numbered anew; the vertices are the nodes of `K'` and the lines
holding one of them).

1. INVARIANCE UNDER RENAMING AND LINE ORDER. PROVED. The partitions, the target cell, the fixing order, the free components and the
   entries read only encodings of masked lines, labels, colours, sizes and path positions (Lemma 3 and sections 5 to 7); none reads a
   source identifier or the order of the input. By induction over the nested calls, an isomorphism `sigma` between two inputs maps the
   partitions of each call onto those of the corresponding call and the children onto children, and gives equal certificates. The
   smallest certificate and the ascending order of certificates are equal as values, so the certificates and the identities are
   equal. The canonical line sequences are equal when the top components of the input form at most one class of equal identity; with
   two or more non-isomorphic top components they are equal under the assumption of 7.15, as property 2 states, because the order of
   7.3 between two such components is decided by their identities.
2. EQUAL RESULT EXACTLY WHEN ISOMORPHIC. PROVED, the "if" direction under the assumption of 7.15 when the input has two or more
   non-isomorphic top components. "If": property 1 gives equal certificates and identities; within a class of equal identity the order
   of 7.3 is not observable (Theorem RB); between classes the order is by identity, a function of the certificate. "Only if": the
   canonical line sequence is the input with its nodes renamed by a bijection (the final positions), so two equal canonical line
   sequences give a bijection of nodes that maps lines onto lines with their counts and constants; lines with no blank node are in the
   sequence unchanged. If `H` collided on the certificate encodings of two non-isomorphic top components of one input, the order of
   those two would be decided by their source identifiers, and the "if" direction could fail for that input (7.15).
3. EQUAL CERTIFICATES GIVE EQUAL RELABELLED LINES. PROVED. Two calls with the same argument `p`, the same constants and equal
   certificates, given the same offset, give the same set of lines with every blank leaf replaced by `fixed(final position)`: by the
   claim in the proof of Theorem RB, that set is a function of the certificate, `p`, the offset and the final positions of the
   constants. So which of two attaining children is chosen, and which of two nested calls with equal certificates comes first, is not
   observable in the canonical line sequence. No rule uses this property to compute a size or a position (6.5).
4. TERMINATION. PROVED. A pair enters the queue only at the start (at most one for each vertex) and when a cell is split (at most one
   for each new cell, and there are at most as many new cells as vertices), so at most `2V` pairs are inserted in one REFINE and at most
   `2V` are popped; each pop splits cells or leaves the partitions unchanged. The recursion of 6.7 has depth at most the number of nodes
   and each call has at most `n_K` children, so the search is finite.
5. THE NESTED MINIMUM IS THE GLOBAL MINIMUM. PROVED. A leaf of the whole search is a choice of one child at every rule 3 call reached,
   and its certificate is the certificate along the leaf (6.3 item 9). The value `s` of a call does not depend on the choices made in
   its nested calls, nor does `E`. Let the nested calls of a child, in the order of 6.4, have the certificates `c_1 <= .. <= c_r` (the
   minima), and let a leaf have the certificates `x_1, .., x_r` along it in those places; then `x_i >= c_i` for every `i`, because
   `c_i` is the least certificate along the leaves of the `i`-th call (by induction on the nodes), so the list `(x_i)` is not smaller
   than `(c_i)` in the order of lists, the first difference being a greater entry of `x`. The choices in different nested calls are
   independent, and taking in each of them a leaf with the least certificate gives `(c_i)` exactly. So the least certificate along the
   leaves of a child is `(s, E, (c_i))`, and that of a call is the least over its children: the definition of 6.3.
6. NO BRANCHING ON FORESTS. PROVED, directly from the stability of the inherited pair (property 7(a), (b)). A component whose plain
   incidence graph `T` is a tree (a vertex for each free node, a vertex for each line, one edge for each incidence carrying its label; the
   fixed nodes are constants, not vertices) has a singleton node cell in its pair, so rule 3 never applies to it or to the components
   nested in it. LEMMA F. Let `T` be a finite forest whose vertices carry colours (node or line; for a line the encoding of its masked
   line) and whose edges carry labels, and let `pi` be a partition of its vertices that refines the colours and is stable (for all cells
   `X`, `W`, all `x` in `X` have the same multiset of labels of edges into `W`). Then for any `x`, `y` in one cell there is an
   automorphism of `T` that preserves colours, labels and `pi` and maps `x` to `y`. So the cells of `pi` are exactly the orbits of the
   automorphisms of `(T, pi)`. Proof. Build `phi` by a simultaneous breadth-first search from `x` and `y`. (1) `phi(x) = y`. (2) By
   stability `x` and `y` have equal multisets of pairs (label, cell of the neighbour); pair their neighbours by a bijection preserving
   both entries. (3) For a paired `x'` and `y'` reached through edges `e`, `e'` of equal label, the full multisets of `x'` and `y'` are
   equal and the entries of the parents, (label of `e`, cell of `x`) and (label of `e'`, cell of `y`), are equal, so the remaining
   neighbours of `x'` and `y'` have equal multisets; pair them and continue. A forest has no cycle, so each search meets every vertex of
   its tree once and every edge of a tree is a search edge. Hence `phi` is a bijection from the tree of `x` onto the tree of `y`
   preserving adjacency, labels and cells. If the two trees are one, `phi` is an automorphism (extended by the identity outside it);
   otherwise exchange the two trees by `phi` and its inverse. An automorphism preserving `pi` maps a vertex into its own cell, so each
   orbit lies in a cell, and by the construction each cell lies in one orbit. End of proof. THE CENTRE. The incidence graph of a connected
   free component is a tree `T`. Its centre, one vertex or two adjacent vertices, is fixed as a set by every automorphism of `T`:
   automorphisms preserve distances, hence eccentricities, and the centre is the set of vertices of least eccentricity, which in a tree is
   one vertex or two adjacent ones. Apply this to the automorphisms of `(T, pi)` with `pi` the pair of the call. (a) A node centre is
   fixed, so its orbit, hence its cell (Lemma F), is a singleton. (b) A line centre holds at least one free node; the labels on its edges
   are pairwise disjoint sets of occurrence numbers, hence distinct; an automorphism that fixes the line fixes each neighbour, so each
   neighbouring node has a singleton cell. (c) Two centres are a node and a line; `pi` refines the colours node and line, so an
   automorphism does not exchange them, and the node is fixed. In every case some node cell is a singleton. NESTED COMPONENTS. A free
   component left after fixing is the subgraph of `T` induced by its free nodes and their lines, with the fixed nodes deleted: a connected
   forest, hence a tree; the labels at a line stay pairwise disjoint after the renumbering; its inherited pair is stable and refines the
   colours (property 7(a), (b)). Lemma F and the centre argument apply again, by induction on the size. A top component starts fresh: the
   pair after REFINE is stable (Lemma 1) and refines the colours, so the argument applies to it too.
7. THE INHERITED PAIR. PROVED (the order of the cells differs from a fresh start; here only the cells as sets are compared). Let `K'` be
   a free component left by a call (or by a child of a rule 3 call) on `K`.
   (a) STABLE IN `B`. Fixing renumbers the free occurrences of every line holding a newly fixed node. Two lines `l_1`, `l_2` of one cell
   of `pi` have equal keys into every singleton cell `{f}`, so each newly fixed `f` occurs in both at the same occurrence numbers of
   `A`; their masked lines in `A` are equal, hence their numbers of occurrences. So the map from occurrence numbers of `A` to
   occurrence numbers of `B` is one function for all lines of one cell: it depends only on which numbers of `A` were fixed, and it is
   strictly increasing on the numbers that remain, so the labels of the lines of one cell are renumbered by one strictly increasing map
   and keys keep equality and order. A node `u` of `K'`: all its lines lie in `K'`, and its key in `B` into a cell `W` restricted to `K'`
   is the image of its key in `A` under those renumberings, so cell-mates stay equal. A line `l` of `K'`: its free nodes lie in `K'`, it
   has no incidence into another component, and the newly fixed nodes are no longer vertices; its key in `B` into a node cell
   restricted to `K'` is its renumbered key in `A`. So `pi` restricted to `K'` is stable in `B`.
   (b) EQUAL MASKED LINES. Two lines of one cell have equal keys into the singleton cell of each newly fixed node, so they hold the same
   node at the same occurrence numbers of `A`; with equal masked lines in `A`, their masked lines in `B` are equal.
   (c) EQUAL AS A SET TO A FRESH COARSEST START. Write `R(x)` for the coarsest stable refinement of `x` (it exists and is unique by Lemma
   2) and `start_X` for the pair with one node cell and the line cells keyed by the encoding of the masked line in the structure `X`.
   HYPOTHESIS `(H)`: the pair of every call, as a set partition and in its own structure, is `R(start)`; and the pair of a rule 3
   child, after its REFINE, is `R(start meet {v})` in the structure of its call (meet is the common refinement). Base: a top call, by
   Lemma 2. Rule 3 child: REFINE from `pi meet {v}` (seeded by the fragments, valid by Lemma 1) ends at `R(pi meet {v}) = R(R(start)
   meet {v})`, and the identity `R(R(s) meet t) = R(s meet t)` holds: `R(s meet t)` is stable and refines `s`, so it refines `R(s)` by
   Lemma 2; it also refines `t`; so it is a stable refinement of `R(s) meet t` and refines `R(R(s) meet t)`; conversely `R(R(s) meet t)`
   is stable and refines `s meet t`, so it refines `R(s meet t)`. Step to `K'`: let `Q` be the coarsest stable partition of `K'` in `B`
   from `start_B`, `pi'` the pair of the call after its fixing (`R(start_A meet S)`, `S` the singletons of the nodes individualised),
   and `rho` the partition that is `Q` on `K'` and `pi'` elsewhere (the cells of `pi'` minus the members of `K'`). `rho` is stable in
   `A`: keys of `K'` lines into a singleton cell `{f}` agree because equal masked lines in `B` place the same fixed nodes at the same
   places; keys among vertices of `K'` are the keys in `B` carried back by the common renumbering of (a), which is one function for the
   lines of one cell of `Q` because their masked lines in `B` are equal; vertices outside `K'` have no incidence into `K'` except through
   a fixed node, a singleton, so their keys are those of `pi'`; fully fixed lines see only singletons. `rho` refines `start_A meet S`
   (equal masked lines in `B` imply equal masked lines in `A`, since `fixed(q)` reads back as `free` for a node fixed at this step), so
   `rho` refines `pi'` by Lemma 2, hence `Q` refines `pi'` restricted to `K'`. Conversely `pi'` restricted to `K'` is stable in `B` by
   (a) and refines `start_B` by (b), so it refines `Q`. So they are equal as sets, which is `(H)` for the nested call in
   its structure `B`.
   (d) SIBLINGS AND AUTOMORPHISMS. Let `f` map `K_1` onto `K_2`, two components of one parent, preserving constants, labels and masked
   lines in `B`. Then `sigma`, equal to `f` on `K_1`, to `f` inverse on `K_2` and to the identity elsewhere, is an automorphism of the
   whole top component (the labels of `A` and `B` correspond through a renumbering that is a function of the pattern of fixed
   occurrences, which `f` preserves). It fixes every node fixed so far, individualised nodes included, and keeps each ancestor component
   setwise, because `K_1` and `K_2` lie inside every ancestor component. By induction along the whole path from the top call, every
   partition on the path is invariant under `sigma`: the pair of a top call is a function of its component (Lemma 3), and each later
   pair is a function of the earlier pair, the structure and the individualised nodes, all fixed by `sigma`. So every cell of a parent
   meets `K_1` and `K_2` in sets of equal size, the cells meeting `K_1` are exactly those meeting `K_2`, and after restriction and
   re-offsetting the corresponding cells have equal colours: equal inherited pairs, hence equal certificates. The same argument with
   `K_1 = K_2` shows that the partitions of a call are invariant under every automorphism of the call.
8. COST ADDS OVER COMPONENTS. PROVED. Let a call, or a child, end its fixing with nested calls on the components `K_1 .. K_r`. Each
   nested call reads only its own component: REFINE visits incidences of members of its own cells, a line holds free nodes of one
   component only, and the inherited pair and the constants are its own; so the units of each nested call are those of its own call
   tree. Ordering the `r` certificates by merge sort takes at most `bits(r)` levels. Charge each comparison of a merge to the certificate
that it outputs. A comparison of two certificates stops at the first difference and reads no more entries than the certificate
   output holds; a comparison of two entries costs (8.1(e)) one unit for `m` plus, when the values of `m` are equal, one unit per 8
   bytes of ONE encoding read, which is at most the units (d) charged when the entry output was formed (one unit plus one per 8 bytes
   of its whole encoding). So each certificate output is charged, at each level, at most the units (d) of its own entries, and these
   are among the units of its call tree, because every entry is charged when formed. Hence the ordering costs at most `bits(r)` times
   the sum of the units of `K_1 .. K_r`, and `units(parent after its fixing) <= units(fixing) + (1 + bits(r)) (units(K_1) + ... +
   units(K_r))`: a sum, never a product, up to the factor of the ordering.

## 10. Shortcuts

Any method that yields the result defined by sections 5 to 7 is permitted; none is normative. The units of an engine's limit are those
of the walk of 8.3, which uses the stops of 10.4 and the skips of 10.1 and 10.2 as 8.3 states, whatever method the engine uses. This section sorts methods into three
CLASSES. PROVED: the shortcuts that the text proves equal to the reference, each with its proof and an input on which it fires.
WRONG: shortcuts shown wrong, each with a counterexample under ONE stated naming and ONE visiting order. NOT PROVED HERE: every other method (10.6): the text
makes no claim about it. Every shortcut is scoped to ONE call on one free component, with the state of that call. An engine
classifies every child of every rule 3 call it completes as attaining or not, because no field of the record may be absent (7.7); a
call it leaves incomplete is never on the result path. The inputs named here are given line by line in 13.1.

10.1 THE CHECKED AUTOMORPHISM: ONE RULE. PROVED. THE STATE OF A CALL is its component, the nodes fixed above it with their path
positions (its constants), its lines as the call sees them (every fixed leaf held by its position, 3.6; annotation counts and payloads
kept) and its partitions. A map is an AUTOMORPHISM OF A CALL when it is a permutation of the FREE nodes of the call that carries the
set of the call's lines, as the call sees them, onto itself, with every leaf already fixed above the call held fixed; it then also
preserves the call's cells, because the partition is a function of that state (Lemma 3, property 7(d)). THE CHECK is against the call's
OWN state, never against "the lines of the component" or "of the top call": a map that moves a node fixed on the way down is an
automorphism of the component and NOT of the call. At a top call nothing is fixed, so its state is the lines of its component. A map
valid at a call is valid at a deeper call only if it is checked there (a map that moves a node fixed in between fails the check),
except by LIFTING, below.
THE RULE. A map that has been checked to be an automorphism of a call may be used AT THAT CALL, however it was found. What it
licenses is this and no more: a child `phi(v)` of the call may be skipped as the image of an EXPLORED child `v` of the same call, and
their classes are joined for the attaining count. Proof. If `phi` is an automorphism of the call and `v` is in the target cell, the
child of `phi(v)` has the certificate of the child of `v` (F2 of 7.7). So `phi(v)` attains exactly when `v` does, and among the
children of one call only one child of each orbit of the group generated by the checked maps of the call need be explored; the number
of attaining children is the size of the UNION of the orbits, within the target cell, of the explored attaining children (Lemma M); an
orbit is counted once, however many explored children it holds. It does NOT license carrying a choice or a certificate across a map:
Lemma M (iii) states why, and 10.5(c) is the counterexample. The image of a STOPPED child may be skipped as well and is classified not
attaining (its certificate is that of the stopped child, which is greater than the best so far, by F2); that is not observable in the
result. A map checked at one call and used at another must be checked there.
LIFTING. PROVED, and the one case in which a map is used at a call without a check there: a checked automorphism of a nested call fixes
that call's constants, which include the node individualised in the child and every node fixed there; extended by the identity on the
rest of the parent's component it is an automorphism of the parent call, because a line holds free nodes of one nested component only,
and so of every ancestor.
HOW CANDIDATE MAPS ARE FOUND, with what each costs; every candidate is checked before use, in one pass over the lines of the call.
(1) TWO CHILDREN OF ONE CALL WITH EQUAL CERTIFICATES, whether or not either of them attains: the map that sends each node of the call to
the node of the other child with the same final position (6.5, below any child). It costs the two children, which are explored in any
case. By F1 of 7.7, which uses no minimality, for two children `v`, `w` with equal certificates the map `lambda_w^(-1) o lambda_v` is an
automorphism of the call with `phi(v) = w`: the Corollary of 6.8, (b).
(2) THE EXCHANGE OF TWO TIED NESTED CALLS (equal certificates) of an explored child, or of a rule 2 call. The map sends each node of the
first call to the node of the second that has the same final position when the offset of each of the two nested calls is taken as `0`
(6.5), and each node of the second back to the first, and fixes every other node; it is `lambda_2^(-1) o lambda_1`, the isomorphism `f` of
the Corollary of 6.8, (a), which fixes every constant, extended as in property 7(d): it carries the lines of the call onto themselves
because a line of the call holds free nodes of one nested component only. For `k` tied calls the exchanges of consecutive calls in the
order of 6.4 are `k - 1` maps and generate every permutation of the class. It costs the two nested calls, which are explored in any case.
(3) A LIFTED AUTOMORPHISM of a nested call, as above; it costs nothing.
(4) TWINS (10.2); the cost is the twin units of 7.10.
(5) TWO COMPLETE LEAVES OF A TOP CALL `X` (the restriction to a top call is part of the statement). A LEAF of `X` (6.3 item 9) has its
final positions `lambda` of the nodes of the
component, computed by 6.5 along it.
Write `L(lambda)` for the set of the lines of the component with every blank leaf replaced by `fixed(lambda(leaf))`. Proof. Let two
leaves with positions `lambda_1`, `lambda_2` have `L(lambda_1) = L(lambda_2)`. Then `phi = lambda_2^(-1) o lambda_1` is a permutation of
the nodes, and a line `l` of the component is carried to the line `phi(l)`, because `lambda_2` maps `phi(l)` to the same line of
`L(lambda_2)` that `lambda_1` maps `l` to; so `phi` carries the set of lines onto itself, annotation counts and payloads kept, and nothing
is fixed at a top call: `phi` is an automorphism of `X`. The route costs two complete leaves, that is, two whole paths of the
search. Used at a call that is not a top call, a map built from leaves must be checked there (10.5(b)).
10.2 TWINS (route (4)). PROVED. Two nodes of the target cell that are twins (7.10) are exchanged by a transposition that is an
automorphism of the call, so the rule of 10.1 applies to it. A transposition fixes every other node, so whether it is an automorphism does
not depend on which nodes are constants: twins in one call are twins in every call that holds both (Lemma TW(c)). One child stands for a
class of twins.

10.3 LAZY RESTRICTION. PROVED in 8.2 item 1: any representation of the restricted partitions that has the same cells in the same
order gives the same result.

10.4 THE STRICT STOP. PROVED. An engine may stop a child as soon as `s` is known and is strictly GREATER than the `s` of the best
certificate so far; or, `s` being equal, as soon as `E` is known (after the fixing of the child, before any nested call) and is strictly
GREATER than the `E` of the best so far, as lists of entries in the order of 6.3. A child whose `s` is smaller, or whose `s` is equal and
`E` smaller or equal so far, is continued; when completed it replaces the best if its certificate is strictly smaller. A stopped child is
classified as not attaining. Proof. The order of 6.3 is lexicographic on `(s, E, C)`, so `s`, and then `E` with `s` equal, are prefixes of
the order: if the `s` of a child is strictly greater than that of the best so far, or its `s` is equal and its `E` strictly greater, its
certificate is strictly greater than the best so far, which is at least the minimum, so the child does not attain. A child that attains is
therefore never stopped. INPUTS ON WHICH THEY FIRE (children of a rule 3 call visited in ascending source order, no other shortcut,
counted at the rule 3 calls of the result path): the stop on `s` fires `3` times on `FIFTEEN` (the children of `a1` and `a2` have `s = 2`,
those of `c0`, `c1`, `c2` have `s = 3`); the stop on `E` fires `6` times on `CUB8`, whose children have equal `s` and different `E`. In the walk of 8.3, whose skips are on,
the stop on `s` fires `1` time on `FIFTEEN` and the stop on `E` `2` times on `CUB8`, counted at every rule 3 call that the walk visits. WHY
THERE IS NO STOP ON A NESTED CALL. The nested part `C` of a certificate is complete only when every nested call of the child has returned,
that is, when the whole certificate of the child is known; a stop at that point saves no work. A stop on a part of a nested call, the `E`
of a nested call alone, is wrong, because the first member of `C` is compared by its `s` before its `E`, so a nested call with a smaller
`s` and a larger `E` makes a smaller certificate. Example (`COMPL(4, 6)`, `70` lines, one cell of ten after the refinement of the fresh
start): the children of `n00` and of `n04` both have `s = 2` and equal `E`; the child of `n00` nests one call whose certificate has
`s = 2` and whose first entry has `m = 3`, the child of `n04` one whose certificate has `s = 4` and whose first entry has `m = 2`; the
child of `n00` is the smaller while its nested `E` is the larger. An engine that stops a child on the `E` of its nested calls returns,
when it visits a node of the 6-cycle first, a certificate that is not the minimum.

10.5 WRONG. Each shortcut below fails with every proved shortcut on. Each counterexample is printed under ONE naming, the relabelling of
13.1 by the stated seed, and ONE visiting order of the children (ascending or descending in source order).
(a) THE STOP ON A TIE. A child stopped when `s` is equal and `E` is greater OR EQUAL is wrong, because an `E` that is equal is not a tie
of the certificates; a tie is known only when the whole certificate is known, that is, when the child is completed; a stopped child yields
no map. Counterexample, seed `1`, ascending order, the attaining counts at rule 3 calls of the result path: on `COMPL(3, 5)` `1` in place
of `5` and `1` in place of `2`; on `COMPL(4, 6)` `2` in place of `4`, `1` in place of `6` and `1` in place of `2`; on `K4PAIR` `2` in
place of `4`; on `Z4Z4` `1` in place of each of `16`, `3`, `2` and `2`. On the 2-cycle the stop on a tie fails only when the skip by twins
is off (the second child is stopped, so the map that would join it to the first is never found, and the record holds the attaining count
`1` and the group order `1` in place of `2` and `2`).
(b) A MAP USED AT A CALL WHERE IT WAS NEITHER CHECKED NOR LIFTED: every map found anywhere in the top component used at every call.
Counterexample on `Z4Z4`, seed `3`, ascending order: the attaining count at a rule 3 call is `4` in place of `16`.(c) CARRYING A CHOICE OR A CERTIFICATE ACROSS A MAP: Lemma M (iii), on the complete graph on `a`, `b`, `c` in both directions: the state
of the child `c`, carried onto `a` by the transposition of `a` and `c`, gives `a 0`, `c 1`, `b 2`, and the canonical map has `a 0`,
`b 1`, `c 2`.
(d) A NODE INVARIANT COMPARED BEFORE THE CERTIFICATE is another labelling: it changes which leaf is smallest and breaks property 5.
Counterexample (no visiting order enters): the invariant `s`, larger first, on `FIFTEEN`: the definition gives the positions `a1 0`, `a2 1`,
`c0 2`, `c2 3`, `c1 4`, the invariant gives `c0 0`, `c2 1`, `c1 2`, `a1 3`, `a2 4`, and the canonical line sequences differ.
(e) A PARTITION SHARED BY TWO COMPONENTS, or a refinement of the whole input before the components start (7.5): the cells are equal as
sets and the order of the cells differs. Counterexample (no visiting order enters): `SHARED`. The second component refined alone has the
node cells `[y1] [y2] [y3] [y0]`, refined with the first it has `[y1] [y3] [y2] [y0]`; every cell is a singleton, so the positions, and
the canonical line sequence of that component, differ.
(f) FIXING A WHOLE CLASS OF TWINS AT CONSECUTIVE POSITIONS. Counterexample, `K33` (7.10), whose target cell of three nodes is one twin
class: the child of the definition fixes ONE node (`s = 1`) and the nested call fixes the others, while fixing the class at once would
give `s = 3`, so the certificate, and with it the component identity of 7.8, would differ from the definition's (the canonical line
sequence is the same).

10.6 NOT PROVED HERE. Every method not listed in 10.1 to 10.4, whether or not an engine uses it, for example a quotient of parallel
lines (lines that hold the same nodes in the same places, merged into one vertex of a weight): the text makes no claim about it. An
engine that uses such a method carries the burden of showing that its results equal the reference's.

## 11. Out of scope

This text does not define, and a conforming engine does not perform:

- READING A SYNTAX: how lines are obtained from a text or binary document; the labelling takes typed lines (1.1).
- LOWERING: writing one kind of line as another (for example a reifier binding as a `quad`), and which statements a format supplies as
  `reifier-binding` and `annotation` lines (3.2).
- DE-DUPLICATION BETWEEN KINDS: removing a line because another line of another kind states the same thing.
- TEXT PRESENTATIONS: the syntax of a document, which lines it spells, and the label of a blank node beyond the one spelling of 7.14;
  a text document writes the rows of the lines it spells in the order of the canonical line sequence (7.6).
- A DATASET MULTISET DIGEST: a value that commits to the multiset of lines and component identities and can be updated line by line.
  It would be a different value, with its own construction and security bound, defined outside this text.
- NAMING OF DATASETS: telling apart equal identities of different datasets; a reader pairs an identity with a name of its own (7.8, I4).
- THE LIMIT OF WORK of an engine and its data structures (8.3).
- A REFINEMENT STRONGER THAN COLOUR REFINEMENT: decided against for version `1` (5.10); it would be a new version.
- Lines of a kind other than the five of 3.2, terms of a kind other than the four of 3.1, and any lowering into them.
- THE MEANING OF A PAYLOAD: what a `record` line stands for, how a payload is built and read, and whether two payloads are the same
  record; the labelling treats a payload as one constant (3.2).
USES (not normative). The typed values of section 7 serve four uses, each built from them and nothing else: a text document for output,
whose row order and label syntax are the format's (7.14); the digest alone (7.6), for receipts and pins; the ordered typed lines of the
canonical line sequence, delivered as they are produced, for comparison and per-statement witnesses; and the stable identity and
canonical map (7.8, 7.9), for stable names of blank nodes and for attributing results back to the source. A labelling may be recomputed
each time a small dataset is read, so its cost on small inputs is a cost of reading: that is why the guaranteed class (7.10) and the
linear working state (7.12) are requirements.

## 12. Worked examples

All values are computed under the identifier
`(nested-refinement-labelling, 1)` (7.15). The `twin units` of a block are those of the source identifiers printed in it (8.1).

NOTATION. For a word `c`, `<c>` is the term `iri(c)` whose value is the ASCII bytes of `c`; in particular `<p>` is the one byte `p`
(`0x70`), likewise `<q>` (`0x71`), `<r>` (`0x72`), `<s>` (`0x73`), `<t>` (`0x74`), `<h>` (`0x68`) and `<o>` (`0x6f`). `"v"^^<t>` is the
literal with the lexical form `v`, the datatype `t` and no language or direction. `<<(s p o)>>` is the term `triple(s, p, o)`. A
line written with three terms is a `quad` with the graph `absent`; `quad(s, p, o, g)` has the graph `present(g)`. `reifier-binding(r,
t)`, `annotation(k, r, p, o)` and `empty-graph(g)` are lines of those kinds (3.2) with the graph `absent`, `k` the count.
`record("m", [t_1, .., t_n])` is the line of the kind `record` whose payload is the ASCII bytes between the quotes (`record("", [])`
has the empty payload and the empty list) and whose list holds the terms `t_1` to `t_n` in that order. Every other
single letter, or letter with digits, is a blank node, and the letters are its source identifier as ASCII bytes. `Lk` names the
`k`-th input line. Within a block:

- Hexadecimal values are the encodings of 3.7 and 7.8, with no separator. A value longer than 96 hexadecimal digits continues on the
  following lines, 96 digits to a line; ONE rule of indentation holds everywhere: a continuation line starts in the column of the
  first digit of its value. The line breaks are not part of the value.
- `start line cells` lists the encoding of one line of each line cell after 5.6 (every leaf `free`), in colour order.
- A refinement step is written `start` (after 5.6), `indiv(v)` (after steps 1 and 2 of 5.8) or `pop(side,colour)` (after the
  pair was processed by 5.5), followed by `N=` the node cells and `L=` the line cells in colour order, each in brackets, and `Q=` the
  queue in ascending order (`-` when empty). The members inside a cell are written in source order, which carries no meaning (5.1).
- A call is written with its rule, its argument `p`, its `s`, and the nodes it fixes with their path positions. Its units are its own:
  REFINE counts the refinement after the individualisation of a child (the refinement of the fresh start is in the record), FIX and
  ENTRIES are 8.1(c) and (d). Each entry is written `m` and the encoding of its fully fixed masked line. A nested call is preceded by
  its certificate. Under rule 3 every child is written with its refinement; a child whose certificate equals that of an earlier
  child is not expanded again; `one twin class` states whether the target cell is one class of twins (7.10).
- `allowance` gives `S`, `O`, `M`, `V`, `W` and `A(K)` of 7.10; `twin units` are those of the search-free run (8.1(f), 7.10) for the
  source identifiers printed in the block.
- A stable identity (7.8) is written `(t, local position, copy index)`, `t` the index of its top component in the block, whose
  component identity is printed with it. `positions and stable identities` lists each node with its position.
- ORDER OF EVERY LIST OF A BLOCK. The top components are listed in the order of 7.3; the nodes of a component, the members of a cell
  and the target cell in ascending source order (2.2); the children of a rule 3 call in ascending source order of the node individualised;
  the nested calls of a call in the order of 6.4; the nodes that a call fixes in ascending path position; the entries of a call in
  ascending order of 6.3 item 3; `positions and stable identities` in ascending `position`; the canonical line sequence in ascending
  bytewise order of the encoding (7.6).
- `units over the reference tree` are the units (a) to (d) over the tree in which every child of every rule 3 call is explored (8.1).
  The comparison units (8.1(e)) are ILLUSTRATIVE and stay outside the engine-independent record. They are counted under ONE
  reference method, on the reference tree: the children of a rule 3 call are visited in ascending source order, and each child
  after the first is compared with the best so far, one comparison of two certificates for each child (a child replaces the best only
  if its certificate is strictly smaller); the nested calls of a call are listed in the order of their smallest source identifiers and
  ordered by insertion sort, each element moving from the back of the sorted prefix towards the front while the element before it is
  greater, one certificate comparison for each step including the one that stops; the comparison of two certificates is charged as
  8.1(e) says. No other comparison is charged: the classification of the children as attaining or not, and the checks of
  equality, are not. The figures are a function of the input and of this method, and an engine reproduces them by running this method
  on the certificates, whatever method its own search uses; they are in no result, and are never limited (7.11).
- The record of a top component, and that of the whole input, are the structural part of 7.7; a factor record is written with the
  name of its kind (`attain` or `sym`), then its `value` and `exponent`.

## 13. Named inputs and sources

### 13.1 Named inputs

NAMED INPUTS. Where an input is described as a graph, its lines are these: for every edge `{u, v}` the two lines `quad(u, <p>, v)` and
`quad(v, <p>, u)`, for every arc `(u, v)` the one line `quad(u, <p>, v)`; the blank nodes are named as stated. The GIVEN ORDER of the
lines of such an input, which `tau` of RELABELLING permutes, is the order of its description. Edges and arcs that the description lists
stand in the order written, an edge `{u, v}` giving its line `quad(u, <p>, v)` first. Edges and arcs that a condition selects are taken in
ascending lexicographic order of the pair of node numbers `(i, j)`, the nodes being numbered from `0` in the order in which the
description states them; an edge is taken with `i` below `j`, the node `u` of the smaller number, and gives `quad(u, <p>, v)` first. Lines
written for several indices are in the order of nested loops with the first index outermost. A node written `v_i` has
the source identifier `v` followed by the decimal digits of `i` (`v0`, `v10`), a node written `t_i_j` the byte `t`, the digits of `i`,
the byte `_` and the digits of `j`; the nodes `x_i`, `y_i`, `a_i`, `b_i`, `n_i` are written the same way, and a node written with a
plain name (`a`, `h`, `l1`) has that name. `CUBE`: nodes `v0` to `v7`, the edge `{v_i, v_j}` when `i` and `j` differ in exactly one
binary digit (`12` edges, `24` lines). `QR13`: nodes `r00` to `r12`, the edge `{r_i, r_j}` when `(j - i) mod 13` is a non-zero square
modulo `13` (the squares are `1`, `3`, `4`, `9`, `10`, `12`; `39` edges, `78` lines); `QR13S` is the same on the nodes `s00` to `s12`.
`Z4Z4`: nodes `vij` for `i`, `j` in `0 .. 3`, the edge between `vij` and `vkl` when `((k - i) mod 4, (l - j) mod 4)` is one of `(1, 0)`,
`(3, 0)`, `(0, 1)`, `(0, 3)`, `(1, 1)`, `(3, 3)` (`48` edges, `96` lines). `K4PAIR`: nodes `a0` to `a3` and `b0` to `b3`, the edges
`{a_i, a_j}` and `{b_i, b_j}` for `i < j` except `{a2, a3}` and `{b2, b3}`, and the edges `{a2, b2}` and `{a3, b3}` (`12` edges, `24`
lines). `COMPL(k_1, k_2)`: on `k_1 + k_2` nodes `n0`, `n1`, .. (`n00`, `n01`, .. when there are ten or more), the first cycle on the
first `k_1` nodes in order, the second on the next `k_2`, and the line `quad(x, <p>, y)` for every ordered pair of distinct nodes
`(x, y)` that is not an edge of a cycle (`COMPL(4, 6)`: `70` lines; `COMPL(3, 5)`: `40` lines). `FIFTEEN`: the nodes `a1`, `a2`, `c0`,
`c1`, `c2` and the lines `quad(c0, <p>, c1)`, `quad(c1, <p>, c2)`, `quad(c2, <p>, c0)`, and `quad(x, <p>, y)` and `quad(y, <p>, x)` for
`x` in `a1`, `a2` and `y` in `c0`, `c1`, `c2`. `CUB8`: nodes `w0` to `w7`, the edges `{w0, w1}`, `{w0, w4}`, `{w0, w5}`, `{w1, w6}`,
`{w1, w7}`, `{w2, w3}`, `{w2, w4}`, `{w2, w6}`, `{w3, w5}`, `{w3, w6}`, `{w4, w7}`, `{w5, w7}` (`24` lines). `SHARED`: the lines
`quad(x0, <p>, x1)`, `quad(x0, <p>, x2)`, `quad(y0, <p>, y3)`, `quad(y1, <p>, y0)`, `quad(y1, <p>, y2)`, `quad(y2, <p>, y0)`,
`quad(y3, <p>, y0)`.

RELABELLING. The generator `MIX64` has a state, an unsigned 64-bit integer; `next` adds `0x9E3779B97F4A7C15` to the state (modulo
`2^64`), sets `z` to the state, sets `z` to `(z xor (z >> 30))` times `0xBF58476D1CE4E5B9`, sets `z` to `(z xor (z >> 27))` times
`0x94D049BB133111EB`, and returns `z xor (z >> 31)`, all products modulo `2^64`. `S(seed)` is the stream from the state `seed`. `DRAW(m)`,
for `1 <= m <= 2^32`, is the high 64 bits of the 128-bit product of `next()` and `m`. `PERM(n)` starts from `[0, 1, ..., n - 1]` and,
for `i = n - 1` down to `1`, draws `j = DRAW(i + 1)` and swaps the entries `i` and `j`. THE RELABELLING of an input of `N` blank nodes
and `m` lines by the seed `s`: the nodes are numbered `0 .. N - 1` in ascending order of their source identifiers; `pi = PERM(N)`
from `S(s)`; the node numbered `k` receives the source identifier `u64(pi[k])`; then `tau = PERM(m)` from the continuing stream; the
line at place `i` of the relabelled input is line `tau[i]` of the input in its given order. The seeds are `1` to `20`.
