# Canonical labelling of blank nodes: version 1

This text defines version `1` of a canonical labelling of the blank nodes of a set of typed lines of five kinds. Its identifier
is the pair of the name `nested-refinement-labelling` and the version `1` (7.15).

Conventions. `never` states a fact of the definition and is no key word; the duties of an engine are `L1-1` and the sentences with
a key word. A term is defined where it first appears in capitals; other capitals, the key words apart, are emphasis. Identifiers
and formulas are written in backticks. `u64(n)` is the eight-byte big-endian form of an unsigned integer
`n`. `bits(v)` is the number of binary digits of an unsigned integer `v` (`bits(0) = 0`, `bits(7) = 3`, `bits(8) = 4`).
`ceil(x / 8)` is integer division by eight rounded up. A section number such as 5.5 names a part of this text; a term can be used
before
the section that defines it, and its number is then given at its first use. The notation of the
examples (`<p>`, `<<(s p o)>>`, `quad(s, p, o, g)` and the like) is defined at the head of section 12.

## 1. Scope and result

1. The input is a finite SEQUENCE of typed lines (section 3) over a set of blank nodes, in which by (W3) no line occurs twice; so
   the lines form a SET, and the order of the sequence is never read. A blank node of the input is named by its
   SOURCE IDENTIFIER, an opaque byte string: two blank leaves denote one node exactly when their source identifiers are equal
   as byte strings. The definition never reads the form of a source identifier; it only compares two whole identifiers
   bytewise (7.9). The labelling never sees text, and no rule of this text parses a string.
2. The RESULT of the labelling is three typed values.
   (a) The CANONICAL MAP: for every blank node of the input, its `position` (an unsigned integer, 6.5) and its `identity`
   (the typed triple `(component: 32 bytes, local position: unsigned, copy index: unsigned)`, 7.8). It is a typed map from
   source identifiers to these values (7.9). A listing of the map that is written or reported as a sequence has its entries in
   ascending `position`; the positions of the nodes are distinct (6.5), so this order is total.
   (b) The CANONICAL LINE SEQUENCE: every input line, those with no blank node included, with each blank leaf replaced by
   `fixed(position)`, in ascending bytewise order of the line encoding (3.7), together with its DIGEST (7.6).
   (c) The STATISTICS RECORD (7.7), a typed record, which states among other values whether the input is `guaranteed` (7.10). The
   CLAIM of
   the labelling is (b). It is defined by the least certificate along the leaves of each top call (6.3 item 9, 7.2), under the
   refinement of section 5 and the target cell of 6.1. It is never defined by pruning, by traversal order or by the order of the
   input. The
   map (a) is a second normative object (7.9); the claim about (b) holds for any map that yields (b).
3. Two inputs have equal canonical line sequences exactly when they are isomorphic: some bijection of their blank nodes maps
   the lines of one onto the lines of the other, every constant and every annotation count being kept (section 9, property
   2). This rests on the completeness of certificates (6.8) and, for inputs with two or more non-isomorphic top components (7.1),
   on the assumption of 7.15.
4. The definition has no limit on work. An engine declares a limit `L` of limited units (8.3); whether it refuses an input with
   `limit` is a function of the input as given and of `L`, subject to the guarantee of 7.10. A claim of canonicity is checkable by
   a reader (7.13).
5. ROUTE INDEPENDENCE. The canonical line sequence, its digest, the positions, the identities and the STRUCTURAL RECORD (the
   structural part of the record, 7.7) depend only on the SET of typed lines, not on the route by which they were produced: two
   routes that yield the same set
   of typed lines yield the same result. Of these, the canonical line sequence, its digest and the structural record depend
   moreover only
   on the isomorphism class of that set (1.3); the canonical map (7.9), and with it which node of an orbit (7.7) receives which
   identity
   and every copy index, depends also on the source identifiers.
6. RECORDS AND POSITIONS. Labels and positions are those of the WHOLE input. The same lines of the four kinds of RDF statements,
   with
   and without `record` lines (3.2), get different positions and different canonical line sequences, and the digest of the lines
   alone is another value than the digest of the lines and the records.  For an input with no `record` line no rule of this text
   that concerns records applies (the payload
   ranks
   of 7.12 are a class of their own and are empty), so its results are those of the four kinds of RDF statements alone.
7. No label is ever read back. The label spelling of 7.14 is an output for document formats that require text; no rule of this
   text parses a label to recover a position, an identity or any other meaning.

REQUIREMENT LANGUAGE. The key words "MUST", "MUST NOT", "REQUIRED", "SHALL", "SHALL NOT", "SHOULD", "SHOULD NOT",
"RECOMMENDED", "NOT RECOMMENDED", "MAY", and "OPTIONAL" in this document are to be interpreted as described in BCP 14
[RFC2119] [RFC8174] when, and only when, they appear in all capitals, as shown here. This document uses the key words MUST, MUST
NOT and MAY, and no other. **L1-1.** An engine MUST give, for every input, the outcome and the result that sections 3 to 8 define.

## 2. Values and their order

1. Every value compared is an unsigned 64-bit integer, a byte string, a finite list of values, or a masked line with its encoding
   (3.7). All integers in this text are unsigned 64-bit integers. When a quantity of 7.11 would need a larger value the outcome is
   the typed refusal `width` (7.11), and an engine never wraps or saturates such a quantity; the two exceptions are the allowance
   (7.10) and the running
   total of the walk (8.3),
   which saturate.
2. Integers compare numerically. Byte strings compare bytewise, each byte an unsigned integer from `0` to `255`, a proper prefix
   before its extensions: this is the order of source identifiers, which are compared whole as byte strings (7.9). A byte string
   inside a typed value is compared through the encoding of the value (3.7), hence by its length first (3.7(c)). Lists compare
   element
   by element, a proper prefix before its extensions; in particular the empty list is smaller than every other list. Two
   values of different kinds are never compared: every list position that is compared holds one kind. Masked lines compare by
   their encodings (3.7), bytewise; every other typed value compares by the order stated where it is defined.
3. No floating-point number, no locale and no container iteration order is used. No hash decides anything inside the search
   of sections 5 and 6. The hash `H` (7.15) is used only for the two SHARED VALUES, the digest (7.6) and the component identity
   (7.8), and, through the component
   identity, for the order of the top components (7.3). No sort of this text depends on its stability. Every sort is by a total
   order of its elements, and elements that compare equal
   are equal values: the labels of a key (5.2), the entries of one `E` (6.3), the failure records and the factor records (7.7),
   the
   encodings of lines (7.6) and the ranks (7.12). Where different elements can tie, the text orders them: nested calls and top
   components with equal certificates or identities by 7.9 (M2), and the children of a rule 3 call by source identifier (6.1).
   Vertices put in one fragment by a split (5.3) form one cell, which has no inner order (5.1).

## 3. Typed lines, masking and encoding

3.1 TERMS. A TERM is one of four variants: `iri(bytes)`; `literal(lexical: bytes, datatype: bytes, language: absent |
present(bytes), direction: absent | present(ltr | rtl))`; `blank(b)`, with `b` the source identifier of a blank node of the
input; `triple(s, p, o)`, three terms, nesting allowed (written `<<(s p o)>>` in this text). Absence is always the explicit
variant
`absent`, never an empty string
or a sentinel. A source identifier is any byte string, the empty one included. Every literal carries its datatype; its
language and direction are options, and the labelling gives no meaning to their combination: every combination is a distinct
value, encoded as given (3.7). Language tags are compared as bytes; a format that compares them without regard to letter case
writes them in one case before lines reach the labelling. A term of another kind (for example a triple term with no content) has
no variant in this version; an input that holds one, a direction that is neither `ltr` nor `rtl`, or a `free` or `fixed` leaf
presented as input, is refused as `input` with the reason `slot` (W1, 7.11).

3.2 LINES. A LINE is a tagged variant of a closed list of five KINDS. The tag of a kind (3.7) is its one integer code; no
other number identifies a kind.

| tag | kind | slots, in order |
|---|---|---|
| `0x40` | `quad` | `subject`, `predicate`, `object`, `graph: absent \| present(term)` |
| `0x41` | `reifier-binding` | `reifier`, `triple`, `graph: absent \| present(term)` |
| `0x42` | `annotation` | `count: unsigned`, `reifier`, `predicate`, `object`, `graph: absent \| present(term)` |
| `0x43` | `empty-graph` | `graph` (a term) |
| `0x44` | `record` | `payload: bytes`, `terms: list(term)` |

SLOTS ADMIT VARIANTS. A `predicate` slot admits only `iri`. The `triple` slot of a `reifier-binding` admits only `triple`. Every
other
slot, every term of the list of a `record`, and every position inside a `triple` term, admits every variant of 3.1; any further
restriction a data model places on terms is applied before lines reach the labelling. Apart from (W1), no rule of the labelling
depends on either restriction. WHAT THE KINDS DENOTE. A dataset is a default
graph and a
set of named graphs, each named graph a pair of a name and a graph, and a graph can be empty. A `quad` line is one statement; its
graph
slot is `absent` for the default graph and holds the name otherwise. A `reifier-binding` line states that its reifier stands for
its
triple term, and an `annotation` line states one property of a reifier; each is a statement of the graph its graph slot names. A
format
that carries reifiers and annotations as rows of their own supplies these kinds, and one that does not supplies none. A `record`
line
denotes nothing (below). A line CARRIES a name when its kind has a graph slot (`quad`, `reifier-binding`, `annotation`) and that
slot is
`present` and holds the name; a name held anywhere else in a line (as a subject, an object, a reifier, or inside a triple term) is
not
carried. An `empty-graph` line denotes that the pair (its name, the empty graph) is in the dataset. Every named graph is therefore
denoted
exactly once: a non-empty one by the lines that carry its name, an empty one by its `empty-graph` line. An `empty-graph` line is
supplied
for each empty named graph and for nothing else. Two consequences: (i) a name
carried ONLY
by `reifier-binding` or `annotation` lines, with no `quad` line, names a NON-EMPTY graph (a reifier binding or an annotation in a
graph is
a statement in that graph), so it has no `empty-graph` line, and supplying one for it is ill formed (3.3, W5); (ii) a name that
occurs in
lines only as a subject, an object, a reifier or inside a triple term, and is carried by no line, is not thereby a named graph: if
the
dataset has a named graph of that name and the graph is empty, its `empty-graph` line is supplied (12.7 shows a blank node that is
the
name of an empty graph and also a subject). Nothing is inferred from the absence of a line. With these rules the set of lines that
denotes
a dataset is a function of the abstract dataset, once a format has fixed which statements it supplies as `reifier-binding` and
`annotation` lines. Two formats share the results of this labelling for a dataset only if they supply the same lines for it: a
format that
writes a reifier binding as a `quad` line and one that writes it as a `reifier-binding` line obtain different results, and that
choice is
the format's (section 11). THE RECORD KIND. A `record` line holds a byte string, its PAYLOAD, and a finite LIST of terms in a
fixed order.
The payload can be empty and the list can be empty. The payload is ONE CONSTANT: the labelling compares it whole, charges it by
its length
like any constant, never reads it, gives it no meaning, and reserves no value and no prefix of it. A record DENOTES NOTHING in
RDF: it is
no statement of a graph, it has no graph slot and carries no name, it takes no part in (W5) or in the rule for `empty-graph`
lines, and a
name that occurs only inside records is not thereby a named graph. It takes FULL part in everything else: its blank leaves are
occurrences, in list order and at any depth inside its terms, as the slots of any other kind are (3.4); it is a line of its top
component,
a vertex of the incidence graph, a line of every refinement, of every twin test (7.10), of every certificate, of the canonical
line sequence, of its digest (7.6) and of the component identity (7.8). A blank node that occurs only in records is a node like
any other (12.12). What a payload means is the meaning a
format
gives it (section 11); a line is supplied by a format that has such lines, and a format that has none supplies none. For an input
with no
`record` line no rule that concerns records applies, and its results are those of the four kinds of RDF statements alone (section
12,
blocks 12.1 to 12.7). THE COUNT. Only the `annotation` kind has a `count`, an unsigned integer at least `1`: the multiplicity with
which a
format carries that annotation (a format whose annotations form a set always supplies `1`; a caller whose annotations form a
multiset
supplies ONE `annotation` line whose count is the number of identical rows, and `k` identical lines are ill formed (W3)). The
labelling
treats the count as a field of the line: it takes part in the encoding and therefore in every order, and has no further meaning
here.
A multiplicity on another kind would be a
new
kind or a new version, never a field of an existing kind. A blank node can occur in any slot, at any depth inside a `triple` term,
as a
reifier and as a graph name; every such occurrence takes part in the labelling in the same way (3.4). The blank name of an empty
graph is
an occurrence like any other.

3.3 WELL-FORMED INPUT. An input is WELL FORMED when all of the following hold:
(W1) SLOT: every slot, every term of the list of a `record`, and every position inside a term, holds a variant of 3.1 that the
place
admits (3.2), and the payload of a `record` is a byte string. A refusal for an input value that a typed interface cannot represent
(a direction that is neither `ltr` nor `rtl`, a `free` or `fixed` leaf supplied as input) binds only an engine whose input type
can
carry that value;
(W2) COUNT: every `annotation` count is at least `1`;
(W3) REPEATED: no two lines of one kind agree in every field (two identical lines included); the annotation count is the ONLY
field that
this condition ignores. The kind is part of the line: `quad(a, <p>, c)` and `annotation(1, a, <p>, c)` are two lines of two kinds
and an
input that holds both is well formed. A `record` is compared WHOLE, its payload and its entire list of terms, the order of the
list
included: `record("m", [a, b])` and `record("m", [b, a])` are two records. The interface receives a SEQUENCE of lines, and this
condition
makes it a set; an engine that collected the lines into a set before checking would have repaired the input;
(W4) KIND: every line is of one of the five kinds (an interface that receives a kind as a number receives one of the five tags).
(W3)
compares the lines of
the five known kinds only: a line of no known kind is refused for its kind and takes part in no comparison of (W3). When an input
violates (W4) and another condition, the outcome is
decided by 7.11 like any other: the input [a line of no known kind, `quad(b, <p>, c)`, `quad(b, <p>, c)`] is refused as
`repeated`;
(W5) GRAPH: no line carries (3.2) a term that an `empty-graph` line holds. Two terms are equal exactly when they are the same
typed value: equal variants, equal constant fields, and at every depth equal source identifiers at the blank leaves. No encoding
is used (a term that holds a blank leaf has none, 3.7). Examples, each a well-formed input that is labelled:
`empty-graph(<<(a <p> b)>>)` with `quad(a, <p>, b, <<(b <p> a)>>)`, and `empty-graph(<<(a <p> <o>)>>)` with
`quad(x, <p>, y, <<(b <p> <o>)>>)`: in each pair the two triple terms are different typed values, although they are equal
when every blank leaf is read as `free`;
(W6) WIDTH: every byte string length (a payload included), every number of terms of a `record` and every count is at most `2^64 -
1`,
and every quantity of 7.11 fits in 64 bits (the allowance saturates instead, 7.10). A count above `2^64 - 1` is the refusal
`width`
with the quantity `count`, not the refusal `input`.
An input that is not well formed is REFUSED, with the typed refusal `input` for (W1) to (W4), `graph` for (W5) and `width` for
(W6) (7.11). These conditions are checked on the input before any labelling, except the quantity `units` of (W6), which is known
when the result path is; the labelling detects every one of them itself. An engine never repairs an input: it never sums
counts, removes a line, drops a duplicate or changes a term.
THE EMPTY INPUT, with no line, is well formed and is labelled: it has no top component, its canonical line sequence is empty, its
digest is `H(context(lines), u64(0))` (7.6, 7.15), the 32 bytes
`bf4b40a613c1cc97ababeccefc1c1c810dde52818d8928603f2180376f06fb5e`,
and its record (7.7) holds the number `0` in every field of numbers, the empty list in every list and `guaranteed` true.

3.4 OCCURRENCES. The OCCURRENCES of a line are its `blank` leaves in a depth-first, left-to-right walk of its slots in the order
of the
table of 3.2 (the `count` and the payload are no slot; the terms of a `record` are its slots, in list order), descending into
`triple`
terms in the order `s`, `p`, `o`. A node that occurs `r` times in a line has `r` occurrences. Every operation that changes a line
replaces
leaves of the typed value, never bytes.

3.5 FIXED AND FREE. In a call (section 6) some nodes are FIXED: each has a PATH POSITION `q`, an unsigned integer (6.2). The
other nodes of the call are FREE. The FREE OCCURRENCES of a line are the occurrences of free nodes, numbered `0, 1, ...` in
walk order; fixed occurrences carry no number.

3.6 MASKED LINE. The MASKED LINE of a line in a call is the same typed value with each `blank` leaf replaced by one of two
variants: `free` for a free node, `fixed(q)` for a fixed node of path position `q`. Every other field is unchanged, the
annotation count and the payload of a record included. A masked line with no `free` leaf is FULLY FIXED. A line with no blank node
is its
own masked line.

3.7 ENCODING. Every masked line has ONE normative byte encoding, built from three primitives: `u64(n)`; `bytes(b)`, which is
`u64(length of b)` followed by the bytes of `b`; and the one-byte TAG of a variant. The tags are fixed by this table; no other
tag value is used.

| tag | variant | fields after the tag, in order |
|---|---|---|
| `0x01` | `iri` | `bytes(value)` |
| `0x02` | `literal` | `bytes(lexical)`, `bytes(datatype)`, option of `bytes(language)`, option of a direction |
| `0x03` | `triple` | three terms |
| `0x04` | `free` | none |
| `0x05` | `fixed` | `u64(q)` |
| `0x20` | `absent` | none |
| `0x21` | `present` | one value of the type the option holds |
| `0x30` | `ltr` | none |
| `0x31` | `rtl` | none |
| `0x40` | `quad` | `subject`, `predicate`, `object`, option of a term (the graph) |
| `0x41` | `reifier-binding` | `reifier`, `triple` term, option of a term (the graph) |
| `0x42` | `annotation` | `u64(count)`, `reifier`, `predicate`, `object`, option of a term (the graph) |
| `0x43` | `empty-graph` | one term |
| `0x44` | `record` | `bytes(payload)`, `u64(n)`, then `n` terms, `n` the length of the list |

An option is `absent` (`0x20`, no field) or `present` (`0x21`, then one value). The direction option holds `absent`, or
`present` followed by `ltr` or `rtl`. Example: the masked line `quad(free, iri(p), free)` with the graph `absent` is the
fourteen bytes `40 04 01 0000000000000001 70 04 20`. The masked line `record("m", [free])`, payload the byte `m` (`0x6d`) and a
list of
one term, is the nineteen bytes `44 0000000000000001 6d 0000000000000001 04`.
LEMMA E (the encoding is injective and prefix-free).
CONSEQUENCES. (a) Two distinct masked lines never have one encoding as a prefix of the other, so their bytewise order is decided
at the first differing byte, inside both. (b) The order of two masked lines is NORMATIVELY the bytewise order of their encodings,
wherever the definition orders masked lines: the start of a top component (5.6), the entries of a certificate (6.3) and the
canonical line sequence (7.6). (c) Every integer has fixed width and big-endian form, so integers inside an encoding compare
numerically (path position `10` follows `9`); a byte string is preceded by its length, so a shorter byte string precedes a
longer one whatever their contents. (d) The count of an annotation precedes its terms, so annotation lines order first by
count. (e) The payload of a record precedes its list, and its length precedes its bytes and the length of the list its terms, so
records order by payload (a shorter payload first, then bytewise), then by the number of terms, then by the terms in order.

3.8 NO SPELLING CARRIES MEANING. Every comparison is on typed values or on their encodings; a source identifier is compared
whole and never inspected; a hash context is hashed whole and never inspected; the label spelling of 7.14 is output only.
Nothing a rule produces is parsed by another rule. The table lists every rule of sections 5 to 7 that consumes a value the
definition produced.

| rule | what it reads | typed or opaque |
|---|---|---|
| start of a top component (5.6) | encodings of masked lines with every leaf `free` | typed, compared bytewise |
| `KEY` and `SPLIT` (5.2, 5.3) | labels: lists of free occurrence numbers | typed integers |
| the queue (5.4, 5.5) | side numbers, colours, cell sizes | typed integers |
| FIX (6.2) | colours of singleton node cells | typed integers |
| entries (6.3) | `m` and the encoding of the fully fixed masked line | typed integer and typed encoding |
| order of certificates (6.3) | `s`, then the entries of `E`, then `C` | typed |
| final positions (6.5) | the call structure: offsets, arguments `p`, path positions, `s`, sizes `n_K` | typed integers; nothing is recovered from a certificate's contents |
| REBUILD (6.8) | a certificate | typed |
| order of top components (7.3) | the component identity (32 bytes, compared whole), then the smallest source identifier | opaque bytes compared whole |
| the canonical map (7.9) | source identifiers | opaque bytes compared whole |
| component identity (7.8) | the certificate encoding, hashed whole | typed encoding, opaque digest |
| digest (7.6) | the number of lines and the line encodings, hashed whole | typed encoding, opaque digest |
| twin test (7.10) | typed lines, compared as values | typed |
| record (7.7) | integers of the steps above | typed integers |
| label spelling (7.14) | a position | typed integer; the label is output only |

## 4. The incidence graph

1. The INCIDENCE GRAPH of a set of free nodes and the lines holding at least one of them has the nodes and the lines as
   vertices. A node `u` and a line `l` are INCIDENT when `u` occurs in `l`. The LABEL of the incidence is the ascending list of
   the free occurrence numbers of `u` in `l` (one element for each free occurrence of `u` in `l`).
2. Two free nodes are CONNECTED when one line holds both; a fixed node connects nothing. Given a set `F` of fixed nodes, a FREE
   COMPONENT is a connected component of the incidence graph of the nodes outside `F` and of the lines that hold at least one
   of them. A line holds free nodes of at most one free component.
3. The NODES of a component are its free nodes; its LINES are the lines holding at least one of them. `n_K` is the number of
   nodes of a component `K`, `m_K` its number of lines, and `M_K` its number of INCIDENCES (a node that occurs twice in a line
   is one incidence). `O_K`, the number of LABEL ELEMENTS, is the number of free occurrences in its lines. Colours and cells are
   relative to one component.

## 5. Refinement

5.1 ORDERED PARTITIONS. A call holds two ordered partitions: of the nodes of its component (SIDE `0`) and of its lines (SIDE
`1`). A CELL is a part of one of them. The COLOUR of a cell is the number of vertices of its side in the cells before it. A cell
of colour `c` and size `s` therefore occupies the positions `c .. c + s - 1` of its side, and splitting one cell never changes
the colour of another. A partition is DISCRETE when every cell has one vertex. The vertices inside one cell have no order.

5.2 KEY. For a vertex `x` and a set `W` of vertices of the other side, `KEY(x, W)` is the list, sorted ascending, of the labels
(4.1) of the incidences between `x` and members of `W`, one item for each incidence. It is the empty list when there is none.
Items are lists of integers and compare as in section 2.

5.3 SPLIT. `SPLIT(X, W)`, for a cell `X` of colour `c` and a set `W` of the other side:

1. Group the vertices of `X` by equal `KEY(x, W)`.
2. If there is one group, `X` is unchanged.
3. Otherwise the groups are the FRAGMENTS of `X`, placed in ascending order of key (the group with the empty key, if any,
   therefore comes first).
4. The fragments replace `X` and take the colours `c`, `c + s_1`, `c + s_1 + s_2`, ... in that order, `s_i` being their sizes.
   The first fragment keeps the colour `c`.

5.4 QUEUE. The QUEUE is a set of pairs `(side, colour)`, each naming a cell of the current partition. Its NEXT pair is its
smallest, compared by side first and then by colour. The queue is keyed by colour, never by cell identity: a pair `(s, c)` names
whichever cell of side `s` has colour `c` at the moment it is read. THE QUEUE RULE, applied when a cell `X` of side `s` and colour
`c` has been split into fragments `F_1 .. F_k` (`k` at least 2):

1. If `(s, c)` is in the queue, the pairs of all fragments are added (`(s, c)` already names `F_1`).
2. Otherwise the pairs of all fragments are added except the pair of the LARGEST fragment. Among fragments of maximal size the
   one of smallest colour is the largest.

5.5 REFINE. While the queue is not empty:

1. Remove the next pair `(s, c)` from the queue, and let `W` be the cell of side `s` and colour `c`.
2. List the cells of the other side, `1 - s`, that have at least two vertices and hold a vertex incident to a member of `W`. Order
   the list by ascending colour.
3. Visit the listed cells in that order. For each listed cell `X`, apply `SPLIT(X, W)` and, if `X` split, the queue rule to its
   fragments. A split changes the colour of no other cell (5.1), so each listed colour still names its listed cell when that cell
   is visited; `W` lies on the other side and is not changed during the visits.
When the queue is empty the pair of partitions is STABLE: for every cell `W` of either side, all vertices of each cell `X` of the
other side have one key with respect to `W`. Remark: visiting every cell of the other side in step 2 gives the same result. Within
one pop the listed cells can also be visited in ANY order with the same
result.

5.6 FRESH START OF A TOP COMPONENT. A top component (7.1) starts alone, from nothing inherited. Its node partition has one cell,
of colour `0`. Its lines are placed in cells in ascending order of the ENCODING (3.7) of their masked lines with every leaf
`free`, equal encodings forming one cell. The count of an annotation is part of that encoding (3.7(d)); no other key is used.
The queue holds the pair of every cell of both sides. Then REFINE. Nothing is refined over the whole input (7.5).

5.7 INHERITED START OF A NESTED CALL. A call on a free component that is not a top component does not start from one cell. It
takes the ordered partitions of its parent restricted to its own nodes and lines, with the order of the cells kept and the
colours re-offset by the convention of 5.1 (the first cell of each side has colour `0`). The queue is empty: the inherited pair
is stable (section 9, property 7). Only the ORDER of the cells matters: an engine can keep the parent's colours, gaps included,
or any order-preserving renaming of them (8.2).

5.8 INDIVIDUALISATION. To individualise the node `v` of a node cell `X` of colour `c` and size at least 2, in a stable pair of
partitions with an empty queue:

1. `X` is split into the fragments `{v}` (colour `c`) and `X` without `v` (colour `c + 1`).
2. The queue rule (5.4) is applied as for a cell that is not in the queue: of two fragments of equal size the one of smaller
   colour is the largest and is left out. Hence with `|X| = 2` the fragment `{v}` is left out and the pair `(0, c + 1)` is
   queued; with `|X|` greater than 2 the largest is `X` without `v` and the pair `(0, c)` is queued.
3. REFINE. During this REFINE `v` is a vertex of a singleton cell and its occurrences are free occurrences; it is fixed by the
   FIX that follows (6.1).
The result is a function of the input alone.

5.9 LEMMAS (all with respect to one call).

LEMMA 1 (the result is stable).

LEMMA 2 (coarsest stable). At every moment of REFINE, every stable pair `rho` of partitions that refines the start also
refines the current pair. With Lemma 1 the end of REFINE is
the COARSEST stable refinement of the start. In it two nodes share a cell only if they have equal multisets of (line cell, own
label), and lines in one line cell have equal masked lines and equal node cells at each free occurrence number.

LEMMA 3 (the order is a function of the input alone). Each decision reads only side numbers, colours, cell sizes, labels
and encodings of masked lines: the next pair, which cells are split, the order of the fragments, which fragment is left out.
None reads a source identifier or a storage order.

LEMMA 4 (work of refinement). Let REFINE run on a component either from the fresh start (every cell queued) or from a
stable pair with the fragments of one individualisation queued. `deg(x)` is the number of incidences of a vertex `x`. The label
elements read by REFINE are the sum, over incidences, of the length of the label times the number of popped cells that contain
the line of the incidence plus the number of popped cells that contain its node.
(a) One REFINE. A vertex `x` lies in at most `bits(t)` popped cells, `t` the size of its cell at its first pop. Hence the label
elements read are at most `2 O bits(V)`, with
`O` the label elements and `V = n + m` the vertices of the component.
(b) Along the result path. Along a top call's fresh start followed by any sequence of individualisations, fixings and inherited
starts, the label elements read by all REFINEs total at most `2 O bits(V)`, with `O` and `V` those of the top component. The
same holds for the whole result path (7.7).
(c) The sorting of keys is not counted in the units (8.1).

5.10 THE REFINEMENT IS COLOUR REFINEMENT AND NOTHING STRONGER. Version `1` refines by 5.5, the colour refinement of the
incidence graph (4.1), at every call, and applies no stronger refinement before a target cell is chosen.
A stronger refinement would be a NEW VERSION (7.10, VERSIONING), never an option of this one.

## 6. The definition

### 6.1 Calls

A CALL is made on one connected free component `K` with three arguments: the stable ordered partitions of `K` (inherited by 5.7,
or for a top component the pair that results from 5.6), the number `p` of nodes fixed by its ancestors on the path from the top
call of its top component (`p = 0` at a top call), and the CONSTANTS of its ancestors (each a fixed node with a path position
below `p`). A call returns its CERTIFICATE (6.3) and the call structure of 6.5.

THE THREE-WAY RULE. The node partition read is the one the call holds: for a nested call, the inherited partition restricted to
its
component (5.7), in which a cell of the parent that spans several components can be a singleton. On a call whose node partition
is:

1. DISCRETE: every node is fixed (6.2) and `C` is empty.
2. NOT DISCRETE, with at least one singleton node cell: FIX the singleton cells (6.2), then NEST (6.4). The call does not branch.
3. WITHOUT a singleton node cell: BRANCH. The TARGET CELL is the first node cell (colour `0`, 5.1). For each node `v` of the
   target cell,
   taken
   in ascending source order (2.2), there is one CHILD: individualise `v` (5.8) in a copy of the partitions, then FIX the
   singleton node cells of the result (at least `{v}`), then NEST.
   The
   children are LISTED in that order; the certificate of the call does not depend on it, and the walk of 8.3 does. The certificate
   of
   the call is the smallest certificate among its children (6.3). A child whose certificate equals it ATTAINS.

A child is not a call: it holds the singleton `{v}`, and it TAKES rule 1 when its refined node partition is discrete and rule 2
otherwise. A call
fixes at least one node (itself under rules 1 and 2, each child under rule 3), and each nested call is on a proper subset of the
nodes of its parent.

### 6.2 Fixing and path positions

FIX takes the singleton node cells in ascending order of colour. The node of the `i`-th of them (from `0`) takes the PATH POSITION
`p + i`, and `s` is their number. Path positions are cumulative along the ancestor path: a call receives `p`, the number of nodes
fixed by the calls above it on its own path, and its nested calls receive `p + s`. Siblings (calls on two components of one
parent) share every ancestor, so they receive the same `p` and see identical constants. A fixed node is the typed leaf `fixed(q)`
in every masked line (3.6). From the moment of fixing the node has no free occurrence, and the free occurrence numbers of every
line holding it (3.5) count free occurrences only, so they change when nodes are fixed.

### 6.3 The certificate of a call

1. `s`: the number of nodes the call fixes (under rule 3, the number the child fixes).
2. `E`: the sorted list of the ENTRIES of the lines of `K` that have no free occurrence after this fixing (under rule 3, the
   fixing of the child whose certificate is being computed). Every line of `K` holds a free node at entry; a line that still holds
   one after the fixing is emitted by the nested call on the component holding its free nodes; a line holds free nodes of one
   component only, so every line holding a blank node is emitted exactly once over the whole recursion.
3. An ENTRY of a line is the typed pair `(m, line)`: `line` is the masked line with every blank node fixed (path positions of
   nodes fixed by ancestors included), and `m` is the largest path position among its blank occurrences. Entries compare by `m`
   first, numerically, then by the encoding of `line` (3.7), bytewise.
4. `C`: the LIST of the certificates of the nested calls of NEST, in the order of 6.4; equal certificates are repeated, one
   member for each nested call.
5. The certificate is the triple `(s, E, C)`. Certificates compare by `s` first, numerically; if equal, by `E` as a list of
   entries, a proper prefix first; if equal, by `C` as a list of certificates, by this same order recursively.
6. Under rule 3 the certificate of the call is the smallest certificate of its children, each computed by items 1 to 5 after
   the child's fixing.
7. The SIZE of a certificate is `n(s, E, C) = s + n(c_1) + ... + n(c_r)` over the members `c_1 .. c_r` of `C`. LEMMA S: the size
   of the certificate of a call on `K` is `n_K`.
8. The certificate is compared only by this order. Its byte encoding (7.8) is used for the component identity, never for a
   comparison.
9. THE CERTIFICATE ALONG A LEAF. A LEAF of a call is a choice of one child at every rule 3 call reached below it (a choice at the
   call
   itself included, when it takes rule 3). The certificate along the leaf is the triple `(s, E, C)` of items 1 to 5 formed along
   that
   choice: of the call itself under rules 1 and 2, of the child that the leaf takes under rule 3; with `C` the list of the
   certificates
   along the leaf of its nested calls. BOTH the positions that 6.5 assigns along the leaf AND the list `C` use the ONE order of
   6.4: the
   nested calls are ordered by the certificates of the nested CALLS themselves (items 1 to 6, the minima), equal ones by M2, and
   not by
   the certificates along the leaf. For a nested call that takes rule 3 the entry of `C` is the certificate formed along the
   leaf's choice
   there, not that call's certificate: it is greater than that call's certificate exactly when the leaf takes a child that does
   not
   attain, at that call or at a rule 3 call below it. A leaf therefore needs the certificate of each nested call to be ordered; it
   is not
   a single path that can be ordered without exploring those calls. The
   certificate
   of a call is the LEAST certificate along its leaves (property 5).

### 6.4 Nesting

After the fixing of a call (or of a child under rule 3), the free nodes are those in non-singleton node cells. They fall into free
components under the enlarged set of fixed nodes (4.2). Each is the subject of a call with: the partitions of the call (under rule
3, of
the child, individualised and refined) restricted
to its nodes and lines, re-offset (5.7); the argument `p + s`; the enlarged constants. The nested calls are ordered by
certificate,
and nested calls with equal certificates by the smallest source identifier of their nodes (7.9, M2). No fresh start is ever made
below a top call.

### 6.5 Final positions

The FINAL POSITION of every node is computed by one pre-order pass over the CALL STRUCTURE of the result path (7.7), after every
level has been ordered. Each call of the structure knows, as typed integers, its offset `o`, its argument `p`, the nodes it fixed
with their path positions (under rule 3 those of its chosen child), their number `s`, and the size `n_K` of each nested call. A
call with OFFSET `o` gives the node of path position `q` fixed by it (or by its chosen child) the final position `o + (q - p)`,
and
its `j`-th nested call (in the order of 6.4) the offset `o + s + t_j`, where `t_j` is the sum of the sizes `n_K` of the nested
calls before it. The offset of a top call is given by 7.3. No size or position is recovered from the contents of a certificate.
The `position` of a node is its final position. The same pass applies to the call structure below ANY child of a rule 3 call, not
only the chosen one, with `M1` and `M2` (7.9) applied inside it: a child that is not chosen has final positions of the nodes of
its call in this sense.

### 6.6 Recursion and iteration

Every recursion of this section has an iterative form with an explicit stack whose depth is at most the number of nodes of the
input (each nested call fixes at least one node). No result depends on a recursion limit, and no refusal is defined for depth.

### 6.7 Termination and the target cell

- The depth of the recursion is at most the number of nodes.
- The target cell of a call lies in one free component; nodes of one inherited cell that lie in different components are never in
  one target cell.

### 6.8 REBUILD and the completeness of certificates

REBUILD is a function with which Theorem RB is stated, and which no engine has to compute; it produces, from a certificate
alone, a set of typed lines. `REBUILD(c, p, o, kappa)` takes a
certificate `c = (s, E, C)`, two unsigned integers `p` and `o`, and a map `kappa` from the path positions `0 .. p - 1` to `blank`
terms, and returns a set of typed lines:

1. Let `kappa'` be `kappa` extended by `p + i -> blank(u64(o + i))` for `i = 0 .. s - 1`: the node of path position `p + i` is the
   blank node whose source identifier is the eight bytes `u64(o + i)`.
2. For each entry `(m, line)` of `E`, replace every leaf `fixed(q)` of `line` by `kappa'(q)` and add the resulting typed line. A
   certificate produced by section 6 has `q < p + s` for every such leaf (6.3 item 3); REBUILD is defined on such certificates
   only.
3. For the members `c_1 .. c_r` of `C`, in order, with `t_1 = 0` and `t_(j+1) = t_j + n(c_j)` (6.3 item 7), add
   `REBUILD(c_j, p + s, o + s + t_j, kappa')`.
For a top component `K`, `rebuild(K)` is `REBUILD(cert(K), 0, 0, empty map)`, `cert(K)` the certificate of its top call.

THEOREM RB (the certificate determines the component). For every top component `K`, `rebuild(K)` is EQUAL to the set of
lines of `K` with every leaf `blank(b)` replaced by `blank(u64(lambda(b)))`, `lambda(b)` the local position of `b` (its final
position with the offset of `K` taken as `0`, 7.8). In particular `rebuild(K)` is isomorphic to `K`.

COROLLARY (completeness). Two top components have equal certificates exactly when they are isomorphic. Two further statements
hold, with one map `kappa` for both. (a) Two siblings (calls
on two
components of one parent,
which have the same argument `p` and the same constants) have equal certificates exactly when an isomorphism that fixes every
constant maps one component onto the other. (b) The children of `v` and of `w` of one branching call have equal certificates
exactly when an automorphism of the call maps `v` to `w` (`F1` and `F2` of 7.7). The condition of (a) does not serve for (b): not
every child attains.
Counterexample: two copies of the complete graph on four nodes with one edge removed, `a0 .. a3` and `b0 .. b3`, the edges
`a2 a3` and `b2 b3` missing, joined by the edges `a2 b2` and `a3 b3`, every edge written as two lines `quad(u, <p>, v)` and
`quad(v, <p>, u)` (a cubic graph of eight nodes, the input `K4PAIR` of 13.1).
The refinement of the fresh start leaves one cell of eight; the children of `a0`, `a1`, `b0` and `b1` attain and those of `a2`,
`a3`, `b2` and `b3` do not; the order of the group is `16`.

Write `strip(s, E, C) = (E, [strip(c) for c in C])`, the certificate with `s` removed at EVERY level of
nesting.
`strip` does not
determine the component: example 12.6 gives two non-isomorphic components with equal `strip`.

## 7. The whole input: order, result, record, identity, map, guarantee, outcomes, hash

7.1 TOP COMPONENTS. The incidence graph of the whole input (all nodes, all lines that hold a blank node, no fixed node) has
connected components. Each is a TOP COMPONENT. A line with no blank node belongs to no component, takes no part in the search,
and is in the canonical line sequence unchanged (7.6).

7.2 TOP CALLS. Each top component is the subject of a top call with `p = 0` and no constants. It starts FRESH and alone (5.6) and
meets no other component's nodes, lines or cells. Its nested calls inherit (5.7).

7.3 THE ORDER OF THE TOP COMPONENTS. Each top component has one COMPONENT IDENTITY, 32 bytes (7.8). The top components are
ordered ascending by the identity, compared bytewise, and top components with equal identities ascending by the smallest source
identifier of their nodes (7.9, M2). They take offsets in that order: the first has offset `0`, and each next one the offset of
the one before plus its number of nodes. No certificate of one top component is compared with that of another. Under the
assumption of 7.15, equal identities occur exactly for isomorphic top
components (6.8, Corollary), which give the same set of relabelled lines whichever comes first (Theorem RB with the offsets
added), so the order among them is not observable in the canonical line sequence; the second key fixes it for the canonical map.
The nested components of every call stay ordered by certificate and then by M2; no hash enters the search.

7.4 FUNCTION OF THE INPUT. The certificate of a top call and the cell order inside it are functions of the isomorphism class of
its component; the identity is a function of the certificate; the offsets depend only on the sizes and the order.

7.5 NOT REFINED TOGETHER. Refining the top components together, or refining the whole input once and letting each component
inherit the
restriction, gives the same cells as sets but in general a different order of cells inside a component. It
gives different certificates and different results and is wrong; 10.5(e) prints an input.

7.6 THE CANONICAL LINE SEQUENCE AND ITS DIGEST. Let `pos(b)` be the final position (6.5) of a blank node `b`. The CANONICAL LINE
SEQUENCE is the list, in ascending bytewise order of the encoding (3.7), of the masked lines obtained from every input line, those
with no blank node included, by replacing each leaf `blank(b)` by `fixed(pos(b))`. Distinct input lines give distinct masked
lines,
because `pos` is a bijection from the blank nodes onto `0 .. N - 1`, so the sequence has no repeated element. Its DIGEST is
`H(context(lines), message)` (7.15), with `message` the byte string `u64(number of lines)` followed by the encodings concatenated
in order; Lemma E makes the concatenation unambiguous. A text document with labels spelled by 7.14 is a presentation
that a format layers on top of the sequence: its rows are those of the lines its syntax spells, in the order of the canonical line
sequence whatever the order of the input lines; a line its syntax cannot spell has no row. The
sequence and the digest are those of the WHOLE input, the `record` lines (3.2) included: the digest of the lines of the four
statement kinds alone is another value than the digest of the same lines with the records (12.8 prints both).

7.7 THE STATISTICS RECORD. The record is a typed value: every field has a type, and an `option` field is `absent` or
`present(value)`, never a sentinel number.

DEFINITIONS. An AUTOMORPHISM of a call is a permutation `phi` of the free nodes of its component such that replacing each blank
leaf `b` of every line of the component by `phi(b)` (constants stay) maps the set of lines of the component onto itself,
annotation counts kept. `G(X)` is the group of the automorphisms of the call `X`. An ORBIT of a group of automorphisms is the set
of the images of one node under its members. The RESULT PATH of a call `X` is: `X`; if `X`
takes rule 3, its CHOSEN CHILD (an attaining child, fixed by 7.9, M1) and the result path of every nested call of that child; if
`X` takes rule 1 or 2, the result path of every nested call. The PATH ORDER is the pre-order, with the chosen child immediately
after its rule 3 call and the nested calls in the order of 6.4. The result path of the input is the union of the result paths of
its top calls. A chosen child is not a call of its own in the counts below: `calls` never counts a chosen child, and
`chosen_children` counts nothing else. The NESTING DEPTH of a top call is `0`, and a nested call of a call (or of its chosen
child) at nesting depth `d` has nesting depth `d + 1`.

units

LEMMA R (the structural record is a function of the input alone).  F1 (EQUAL CERTIFICATES GIVE AN AUTOMORPHISM THAT MOVES `v` TO
`w`). Let `v`, `w` be
nodes of the target cell of a rule 3 call `X` whose children have equal certificates. Then `G(X)` holds a `phi` with `phi(v) = w`.
F2 (AUTOMORPHISMS GIVE EQUAL CERTIFICATES AND CARRY THE WHOLE CHILD). If `phi` is in `G(X)` then the child of
`phi(v)` has
the certificate of the child of `v`, and `phi` carries the state of the child of `v` (partitions, pops, singleton cells, path
positions,
entries, nested components, inherited pairs) onto the state of the child of `phi(v)`, cell by cell with equal colours and sizes.

LEMMA G (the order of the automorphism group). For a call `X`: `|G(X)| = 1` under rule 1; under rule 2, `|G(X)|` is the
product, over the classes of equal certificate among its nested calls, of `k_c! ord(c)^(k_c)`, `k_c` the multiplicity of the class
and `ord(c)` the group order of one member; under rule 3, `|G(X)| = a(X)` times that product taken over the nested calls of the
chosen child, `a(X)` the number of attaining children.
FACTORISATION LIST. Run `process(X, e)` with `e = 1` as follows. If `X` takes rule 3 and `a(X)` is at least `2`, add
`(attain, a(X), e)`. Group the nested calls of `X` (of its chosen child under rule 3) into classes of equal certificate. For a
class
of multiplicity `k` at least `2`, add `(sym, k, e)` and run `process(the first member of the class in the order of 6.4, e k)`; for
a
class of multiplicity `1`, run `process(its member, e)`. The members of a class are isomorphic calls (6.8, Corollary (a)), so the
list does not
depend on this choice (Lemma R). For a top component, start with its top call. For the whole input, group the top components into
classes
of equal identity and treat each class as a class of nested calls. Merge records with equal `(kind, value)` by adding exponents
and
sort ascending by `(kind code, value)`. The list is a function of the input (Lemma R); every exponent is at most the number of
nodes,
so every field fits in 64 bits (the order itself need not). THE ORDER EQUALS THE NUMBER OF LABEL MAPS THAT YIELD THE RESULT: a
LABEL MAP is a bijection of the blank nodes onto `0 .. N - 1` whose replacement in the lines gives the canonical line sequence.

7.8 STABLE IDENTITY. THE CERTIFICATE ENCODING. `list(items)` is `u64(number of items)` followed by the items. The encoding of an
entry
`(m, line)` is `u64(m)` followed by the encoding (3.7) of its fully fixed masked line. The encoding `cert(c)` of a certificate

7.9 THE CANONICAL MAP. The SOURCE ORDER is the bytewise order of whole source identifiers (2.2). The SMALLEST SOURCE IDENTIFIER of

(M1) At a rule 3 call of the result path the CHOSEN CHILD is the attaining child whose individualised node has the

(M2) Nested calls with equal certificates are ordered ascending by the smallest source identifier of their nodes; so are top
components
with equal identities (7.3). Components are disjoint, so this order is total.
(M3) The RESULT PATH (7.7) is defined by M1 and M2. The final positions (6.5), the local positions and the copy indices (7.8) are
computed along it. The CANONICAL MAP is the typed map from each source identifier to `(position, identity)`. The claim of the
labelling
is about the canonical line sequence and holds for any map that yields it (7.7); the canonical map is a function of the input and
of
its source identifiers.
(M4) INVARIANCE. If `sigma` is a bijection of blank nodes that maps the lines of one input onto the lines of another and preserves
the
source order, the canonical maps agree: `map_2(sigma(v)) = map_1(v)`. The map does not depend on the order or storage of the
lines, on the
engine, on pruning, on thread count or on word size. It is not invariant under a renaming that changes the source order (I5). The
labelling writes nothing to its input: a masked line is a value derived from a line, and the canonical line sequence is a new
sequence.
LEMMA I (IDEMPOTENCE: THE MAP OF A RELABELLED DATASET). Let `D` be a well-formed input, `pos` its positions (6.5), and
`name` a
map from `0 .. N - 1` to byte strings that is INCREASING in the source order: `name(i)` precedes `name(j)` bytewise exactly when

the same
stable identity `(component, local position, copy index)` as the node of position `i` in `D`; and the canonical line sequence, the
digest,
the certificates and the structural record of `D'` equal those of `D`, whatever the order of the lines of `D'`. THE CONDITION
is that
`name` is increasing in the source order. The encoding `name(i) = u64(i)` satisfies it, and a format can store a label as the
encoding of
a position and rely on the lemma; the decimal digits of `i` do not (`10` precedes `2` bytewise), and 7.14 labels are never read
back.
An encoding of positions meets the condition exactly when its bytewise order (2.2) is the order of the positions: a fixed-width
big-endian integer does, and so does the deterministic CBOR
encoding of
an unsigned integer, bare or wrapped as a byte string, because its first byte grows with its length; the minimal big-endian bytes
of the
integer with no length prefix do not (`01 00` precedes `ff`).

LEMMA M. The result path is well defined, engine-independent and computable without enumerating the group.
(i) Well defined and engine-independent. (ii) Computable: a conforming engine skips a child only (a) on a strictly greater `s`, or
a
strictly
greater `E` with `s` equal (10.4), so the child does
not attain, or (b) because it lies in the orbit of an explored child under automorphisms of the call already found, or in its twin
class (10.1, 10.2), so by F2 it has that child's certificate. A discard on a bound that also fires on ties, and an automorphism
used outside its call
and its ancestors, are wrong (10.5). (iii) To continue inside the chosen child `v*` an engine holds it, or carries the state
of an explored attaining child `w` onto it by an automorphism `phi` with `phi(w) = v*` (F2), or explores `v*` afresh. What an
automorphism carries is the attaining SET of each call and the state of the child, NEVER THE CHOICE: at every rule 3 call below
`v*` the chosen child is taken anew by `M1` as the attaining child of smallest source identifier of that call, and its state is
obtained in the same three ways; the nested components and their smallest source identifiers (`M2`) are those of `v*`, not of `w`.
Counterexample to carrying the choice: the complete graph on `a`, `b`, `c` written in both directions (`a` smaller than `b`
smaller
than `c`). The child `c`, explored with `M1` applied inside it, fixes `c`, `a`, `b` at `0`, `1`, `2`; carried onto `a` by the
transposition of `a` and `c` it gives `a`, `c`, `b` at `0`, `1`, `2`, while the canonical map has `a`, `b`, `c` at `0`, `1`, `2`.

7.10 THE GUARANTEE, THE PREDICATE `guaranteed` AND THE SEARCH-FREE RUN.
TWINS. Two distinct nodes `u`, `w` of one top component are TWINS when the transposition that exchanges them and fixes every other
node maps the set of lines of the input onto itself (a line is a typed value with its count; its image has every leaf `blank(u)`
replaced by `blank(w)` and every leaf `blank(w)` by `blank(u)`, in the terms of a `record` too, every other field unchanged).
LEMMA TW.
(a) The test is exact: it maps the finite set of lines onto itself exactly
when
it maps each line holding `u` or `w` to a line of the set. (b) Twin-ness is an equivalence on distinct nodes (with each node its
own
twin). (c) Twin-ness does not depend on the call. So two nodes are twins in a call exactly when they are twins in the top
component, twin classes
found
at one call stay valid at every call below it, and two twins that are free in a call lie in one node cell of it (its partition is
invariant under every automorphism of the call that fixes the constants, property 7(d)).
THE PREDICATE. An input is `guaranteed` when every rule 3 call of the result path has a target cell that is ONE TWIN CLASS: any
two
members are twins. By F2 the property does not depend on which attaining children the result path takes, so it is a function of
the
input. It has no parameter. An input with no rule 3 call on the result path satisfies it; the
record's
`calls` then holds a zero in its third part, and no second predicate exists for that case.
THE SEARCH-FREE RUN. The labelling run of sections 5 and 6 with one rule at rule 3 calls: at a rule 3 call with target cell `T`,
let
`u` be the member of `T` with the smallest source identifier, and TEST `u` against every other member `w` of `T` in ascending
source
order, skipping a `w` already known to be a twin of `u`; stop at the first test that fails. If every test passes, `T` is one twin
class, every child attains (F2 with the transpositions), and the run takes the child of `u`, which is the chosen child of M1, and
continues. If a test fails, the call is a FIRST FAILURE: the run records its failure record (7.7), does not descend into it, and
continues with every other call. The twin classes found are kept for the whole top component (Lemma TW(c)) in a union-find. The
run
visits the top components in ascending order of their smallest source identifier and, in each, the calls in pre-order, the nested
calls of a call in ascending order of the smallest source identifier of their nodes (the order of the walk, 8.3). The first
failures,
the twin classes and the twin units do not depend on this order.
THEOREM GR. Every call the search-free run reaches is a call of the result path; the run records nothing exactly when the
input is `guaranteed`; and when it records nothing it has produced the certificates, the final positions, the canonical map, the
identities and the structural record of the labelling.
The run does not search: at each rule 3 call it explores one child. The first failures it records
are those of the record's `broken_by`, a function of the input (F2).
TWIN UNITS. A test of the pair `u`, `w`, whether it passes or fails, costs the START CHARGE `c(l) = 1 + ceil(b(l) / 8)` of EVERY
line of the top component that holds `u` or `w` (`b(l)` the length of the encoding of `l` with every leaf `free`, 8.1(a)). The
charge is a function of the input and of the pair alone: the outcome of the test and the order in which an engine examines the
lines
play no part. WHICH pairs are tested depends on the source identifiers (the run tests the member of smallest source identifier
against
the others in ascending source order), so the twin units of a whole run are a function of the input as given, source identifiers
included, and not of its isomorphism class; the next lemma bounds them for EVERY source order. LEMMA TU. The twin units of
the
search-free run on a top component are at most `4 W`, `W = sum over lines l of
d(l) c(l)`, `d(l)` the number of distinct nodes of `l`. The
bound holds for a `record` as for any line.
THE ALLOWANCE. For a top component `K` with `n` nodes, `m` lines, `V = n + m`, `O` label elements, `M` incidences,
`S = sum of c(l)` over its lines and `W` as above:
`A(K) = 2 S + O + 2 O bits(V) + M + V bits(V) + 4 W`,
and for the input `A = sum of A(K)` plus the split charge of 8.1(c). `A` is an unsigned integer computed before any search, and
its arithmetic SATURATES: every sum and product of the formulas stops at `2^64 - 1`, so a result above that is the value
`2^64 - 1`. A saturated allowance is used only as the field `allowance` of the record and in the comparison `max(L, A)` of 7.11,
where it means that the engine's own limit never trips; an input is never refused because its allowance overflowed. LEMMA A.
The
units (a) to (d) and (f) of 8.1 that
the search-free run spends on `K` are at most `A(K)`, whatever the source order.
THE GUARANTEE. Every conforming engine MUST label every input for which `guaranteed` holds; no limit of its own refuses it, only
the refusals `input`, `graph` and `width` (7.11), which concern the input and the 64-bit range, can stop it, and an engine that
ends with no outcome (8.3 (4)) has not labelled it and has not refused it. An engine that
refuses with `limit` MUST report in `broken_by` the first
failure of every top component that is not `guaranteed` (7.11). An input that is not `guaranteed` is labelled by
search (sections 5 and 6 in full, with the shortcuts of section 10) or refused with `limit`; a refusal is never a different
answer.
FACTS ABOUT THE CLASS.

- If the fresh refinement and the nesting leave every call discrete or with a singleton, that is if `calls` has a zero third part,

VERSIONING. A wider guaranteed class is a change of the version of this definition, never an option of it.

7.11 OUTCOMES AND REFUSALS. The labelling of an input has one typed OUTCOME: `labelled(result)` with the result of 1.2, or
`refused(refusal)`. A refusal carries no partial result. REFUSALS are a closed enumeration; each variant has typed fields:

| code | variant | structural fields | diagnostic fields |
|---|---|---|---|
| `0` | `input` | `reason`: `slot` `0`, `count` `1`, `repeated` `2`, `kind` `3` (3.3, W1 to W4) | none |
| `1` | `graph` | `term`: the variant of the term held by an `empty-graph` line and carried by another line (3.3, W5): `iri` `0`, `literal` `1`, `blank` `2`, `triple` `3` | for `blank`, the source identifier of that node |
| `2` | `width` | `quantity`: `nodes` `0`, `lines` `1`, `incidences` `2`, `occurrences` `3`, `length` `4` (of a byte string, a payload included, of an encoding, or the number of terms of a `record`), `count` `5`, `units` `6` (a part of a field `units` of 7.7) | none |
| `3` | `limit` | `broken_by` of 7.7, never empty | `source` (bytes), `nodes` (unsigned), `units` (unsigned): functions of the input and of the limit `L`, defined by the walk (8.3) |

A refusal for `width` is a function of the input: its quantities are values that this text defines (7.7), and a counter of an
engine's own
search
that overflows is not one of them. The running total `R` of the walk of 8.3 SATURATES at `2^64 - 1`, as the allowance does (7.10).
`R`
can exceed `max(L, A)` only when `max(L, A)` is below `2^64 - 1`: an engine that declares `L = 2^64 - 1` refuses nothing with
`limit`, and
an engine with a smaller `L` stops with `limit` at the first charge that carries `R` above `max(L, A)` and needs no counter beyond
that
charge. Every sum the text forms is assigned to one quantity of the table: the sums of lengths of
encodings (`line_bytes`, `blank_bearing_bytes`) to `length`; the numbers of nodes, lines, incidences and occurrences to the
quantities
of those names; the units (a) to (d) of the result path to `units`; the twin units (f) enter no quantity. The sums `S` and `W`
belong to
the allowance and saturate with it (7.10); the allowance is never a quantity. The running total of the walk (8.3), the twin units
included, goes to `limit` and never to `width`, and it saturates. Once the allowance has saturated no input is refused with
`limit`,
and an engine need not keep the running total. By Lemma A, the units of the result path are at most the unsaturated allowance.
When several refusals apply, the outcome is the one of smallest code, and within a variant the smallest field value that
applies (for `graph`, the smallest term code, and in the diagnostic field the smallest source identifier of that code); so every
refusal
other than `limit` is a function of the input. `limit` arises only for an input that is not `guaranteed` (7.10). An engine's
LIMITED UNITS are the units (a) to (d) and (f) of 8.1
that the walk of 8.3 charges; the units (e) are never limited. The outcome is `limit` exactly when the limited units of the walk
exceed
`max(L, A)`, `L` the limit of the engine (8.3), and never once `A` has saturated; an allowance equal to `2^64 - 1` is saturated.
The test
of step 6 of 8.3 is the only test that refuses with `limit`, and a `guaranteed` input never fails it (8.3, Lemma W (4)). Every
field of
the refusal is a function of the input as given and of `L`: `broken_by` is a function of the input (7.7); `source`, `nodes` and
`units`
are defined by the walk (8.3).
No caller-supplied budget exists in this definition. A `limit` refusal is NOT invariant under relabelling: the limited units
depend
on the source identifiers, through the twin units (f) (7.10) and through the order and the maps of the walk (8.3), so at one `L`
the
same dataset under two namings can be refused under one and labelled under the other. This is a property of the definition, which
is a
function of the input as given; the walk is invariant under a bijection that preserves the source order (8.3, Lemma W (3)). No
label,
digest, identity, position, structural record or canonical map of a labelled input depends on the walk or on `L`. The GUARANTEE is
invariant: the limit never trips below `A`, and `A` bounds the units (a) to (d) and (f) of the search-free run under every naming
(Lemma A), so no naming of a `guaranteed` input is ever refused.

7.12 ORDER AND DETERMINISM.
KEYS. Rank the distinct encodings of the CONSTANT LEAVES (`iri` and `literal` terms) of the lines once, ascending bytewise. Rank
the
distinct encodings `bytes(payload)` of the PAYLOADS of the `record` lines once, ascending bytewise (the encoding begins with the
length, so the rank order is the order of the lengths first), in a rank class of their own,
never compared with the ranks of constant leaves. The KEY of a term is `(0x01, r)` or `(0x02, r)` for a constant leaf of rank `r`;
`(0x03, key of s, key of p, key of o)` for a triple term;
`(0x04)` for `free`; `(0x05, q)` for `fixed(q)`. The key of an option is `(0x20)` for `absent` and `(0x21, key)` for `present`.
The key
of a line is `(kind tag, count when the kind is annotation, keys of the slots in order)`; the key of a `record` is
`(0x44, r, n, keys of the terms in order)`, `r` the rank of its payload in its own class (the
kind tag already separates a record from every other line, and the payload rank is the only element at that place), and `n` the
number of terms. A key is the concatenation of the integers
listed, the keys of the parts included in place, so it is one list of integers. Keys compare as lists of integers, element by
element, a proper prefix first.
LEMMA K (the order of encodings equals the order of keys). For two masked lines `u`, `v`, the encoding of `u` is smaller
than
that of `v` exactly when the key of `u` is smaller than the key of `v`.

DETERMINISM. The canonical line sequence, its digest, the identities, the canonical map and the structural part of the record do
not
depend on the thread count, the platform, the word size or the iteration order of any container.  Figures an engine reports of its
own search (7.7) can differ between runs; the structural part of the record cannot. The OUTCOME is in this list: for an input that
is not `guaranteed` it is a function of the input and of `L`
(8.3), the same in every run, with every thread count and every schedule; a `guaranteed` input is labelled in every run.

7.13 VERIFICATION OF A CLAIM.
CHEAP: that the canonical line sequence is the input RELABELLED by the canonical map. Given the input lines, the map and the
claimed
sequence, check that the map is a bijection of the blank nodes onto `0 .. N - 1`, replace every blank leaf, encode, and compare
the
sorted encodings with the claim, or their digest with a claimed digest: near-linear. This shows that the claim is isomorphic to
the
input, not that it is canonical.
CANONICITY WHERE THE INPUT IS GUARANTEED: re-run the search-free run (7.10) and compare; at most `A` units, polynomial. For such
an
input verification of canonicity IS recomputation. In particular
where the record's `calls` has a zero third part, the re-run is refinement and nesting alone, in near-linear time; if it meets a
rule
3 call the record was false. ELSEWHERE: a search within the verifier's own limit, needed only for the failing components.

7.14 LABEL SPELLING (the one place). A document format requires a text label for a blank node. The label of position `i` is `i` in
decimal with no leading zero and no affix, for example `0`, `7`, `10`. In a text document a label stands in the blank-node form of
the syntax, never bare: in N-Quads `_:0`. Labels are output only: no rule of this text parses a label to recover a position, an
identity or any other meaning; the typed values are the interface. Position order differs
from the byte order of labels (`10` precedes `2`); a dataset read back from its decimal canonical labels as names is therefore not
promised the identity map of Lemma I (7.9). No textual spelling of a stable identity is defined (7.8).

7.15 THE HASH, THE IDENTIFIER AND THE SECURITY ASSUMPTION. The hash function is named in this section only.
THE IDENTIFIER of the labelling is the typed pair `(name: bytes, version: unsigned)`. The `name` of this text is the ASCII byte
string
`nested-refinement-labelling` and its `version` is `1`. A name is a non-empty byte string of printable ASCII bytes without the
space
(`0x21` to `0x7E`). The name is an opaque constant: nothing parses it, and no rule or reader depends on any part of it.
THE CONTEXT of a shared value is the byte string formed by the name, the byte `0x20`, the version written in decimal ASCII digits
with no
leading zero, the byte `0x20`, and the value's own name: `component` for the component identity (7.8) and `lines` for the digest
of the
canonical line sequence (7.6). There is no terminator: the hash takes the context as a whole byte string. The rule is injective.
The two contexts, `context(component)` and `context(lines)`, are the ASCII strings `nested-refinement-labelling 1 component` and
`nested-refinement-labelling 1 lines`, the bytes `6e65737465642d726566696e656d656e742d6c6162656c6c696e67 203120
636f6d706f6e656e74` and
`6e65737465642d726566696e656d656e742d6c6162656c6c696e67 203120 6c696e6573` (spaces in the hex for reading only). The name and the
version
enter every identity,
and identities order the top components (7.3); so the POSITIONS of an input with two or more non-isomorphic top components, and
not
only its identities and digests, depend on the name and on the version. Nothing reads the digits back. These two contexts are the
only reserved strings of this text.

| context | separates | used in |
|---|---|---|
| name, `0x20`, version digits, `0x20`, `component` | the component identity | 7.8 |
| name, `0x20`, version digits, `0x20`, `lines` | the digest of the canonical line sequence | 7.6 |

THE FUNCTION. `H(context, message)` is `BLAKE3` (the specification cited in 13.2) in its key-derivation mode, with `context` as

context string and `message` as the key material, and the function's full default output of 32 bytes. The context is passed as the
ASCII
bytes given above, exactly, with no terminator and no normalisation. One function serves both shared values; a different function
for
either is not this labelling, and a value computed with another function is a different value outside this text. THE SECURITY
ASSUMPTION (the one
cryptographic assumption of this text): `H` is collision resistant at the level its designers state for it (128 bits for its
32-byte
output; this text assumes it and does not prove it). What depends on it: that equal component identities occur only for isomorphic
components (I6), hence the order of 7.3, the copy indices, `top_multiplicities` and the top-level factors of `group_order`; and
that equal
digests of canonical line sequences occur only for isomorphic inputs. If it failed, two non-isomorphic top components with equal
identities would be treated as copies: their copy indices, the record's multiplicities and group order would be wrong, and the
canonical
line sequence of an input holding both would depend on their source identifiers. This text does not detect that case and defines
no
refusal for it. Nothing inside a top component depends on the assumption: the search, the certificates, the positions within a
component
and Theorem RB use no hash.

## 8. Work

### 8.1 The unit

A WORK UNIT is one of the following. Bytes are bytes of the ENCODING (3.7); for a line `l`, `b(l)` is the length of the encoding
of
`l` with every leaf `free`.
(a) START. For each line `l` of a top component, the START CHARGE `c(l) = 1 + ceil(b(l) / 8)`.
(b) REFINE. For each popped cell `W` and each incidence of a member of `W`, as many units as its label has elements (a node that
occurs `r` times in a line costs `r`).
(c) FIX. For each fixing (of a call under rules 1 and 2, and of each child under rule 3): one unit for each removed vertex (each
node fixed and each line placed in `E`); one unit for each removed incidence: a pair of a FREE node of the call and a line of the
call in
which it occurs, such that the
node is fixed by this fixing or the line is placed in `E` (a constant of the call is not a vertex, and its occurrences are not
counted; a node that occurs twice in a line is one pair); and, when the remainder falls into two or more free components, as many
units as the sum, over all of them, of nodes plus lines, less the largest of these sums. The split of the input into top
components is charged in the same way, with no
removed vertex; lines with no blank node belong to no component and are not counted. A line placed in `E` is therefore charged
three times
by design: as a removed vertex, through its removed
incidences, and as an entry formed.
(d) ENTRIES FORMED. For each entry `(m, line)` formed, one unit plus one unit per 8 bytes, rounded up, of the encoding of its
fully
fixed masked `line`; the eight bytes of `m` are not counted. Since a `fixed` leaf has eight bytes more than a `free` leaf, the
entry of
a line `l` with `o(l)` occurrences costs exactly `c(l) + o(l)`.
(e) COMPARISON. For each comparison of two entries within a comparison of two certificates: one unit for `m`; if the two values of
`m` are
equal, one unit per 8 bytes, rounded up, of the bytes of ONE encoding read, which are the bytes up to and including the first byte
that
differs, or the whole encoding when the two are equal (the encodings are prefix-free, so two distinct encodings differ inside
both). The
comparison of the values of `s` and of list lengths is not charged. A comparison of two certificates stops at the first
difference.
(f) TWIN. For each test of a pair of nodes (7.10), the start charge `c(l)` of every line of the top component that holds one of
the
two nodes, whether the test passes or fails.
STRUCTURE OF THE TOTALS. The units (a) to (d) of one call are functions of the input and of the definition alone. The units of a
run
are the sum over the calls the run charges. Three totals are functions of the input: the total over the REFERENCE TREE, the search
in
which every child of every rule 3 call is explored; the total over the RESULT PATH (the field `units` of the record, 7.7); and the
LIMITED UNITS, the total of the walk of 8.3, which charges the calls that the stops of 10.4 and the skips of 10.1 and 10.2 leave,
in the order of 8.3, together with the twin units (f) of the tests that the walk makes. The units (a) to (d) of the walk are
at most
those of the reference tree and at least those of the result path (8.3, Lemma W (4)). The twin units (f) are a function of the
input AS
GIVEN, its source identifiers included, and of no choice of an engine, but NOT of its isomorphism class: the pair that a test
examines
depends on the source order (the directed 4-cycle `a -> b -> c -> d -> a` costs `9` twin units in the search-free run and the
isomorphic
`a -> c -> b -> d -> a` costs `12`); the order and the maps of the walk depend on the source order as well (8.3), so the limited
units
are not invariant under relabelling and enter no quantity that is (7.7). The units (e) depend on the method used to order and to
choose;
they are never limited (7.11) and stay outside the engine-independent record. Section 12 prints them under ONE reference method,
named
there; its figures are a function of the input and of that method, and no engine has to report them.
Not counted: the sorting of keys (Lemma 4(c)), the sorting of the entries of one `E`, and bookkeeping. Discovery of components is
charged by (c), as 8.4 states.

### 8.2 Lazy restriction

1. LAZY RESTRICTION IS RESULT-NEUTRAL. Any representation of the partitions restricted to a component that has the same cells in
   the same order gives the same result, and an order-preserving renaming of colours changes no decision.
2. LABELS NEED NOT BE RECOMPUTED. An engine can keep the original occurrence numbers of the free occurrences after a fixing: equal
   keys stay equal and their order is kept (section 9, property 7(a)).

### 8.3 The walk and the outcome of a limit

The LIMIT `L` of an engine is an unsigned 64-bit integer; that of an engine that refuses nothing is `2^64 - 1`. Whether it
refuses an
input with `limit` (7.11) is decided by ONE sequential computation, THE WALK, defined in this section. The walk is defined on a
well-formed input (3.3) whose quantities fit in 64 bits (7.11): an input refused with `input`, `graph` or `width` has no walk. It
is a
function of the input as given, its source identifiers included, and of `L`. It depends on no engine, no order of exploration, no
schedule, no thread count and no run.

THE SEARCH-FREE RUN is not part of the walk. It is performed first (7.10); it decides `guaranteed` and `broken_by`, spends at most
`A`
(Lemma A), and none of its units enters the running total `R` below. The twin units that enter `R` are those of the tests that the
walk
itself makes (step 4).

THE CHARGES. The walk keeps a RUNNING TOTAL `R`, which starts at `0` and SATURATES at `2^64 - 1`, and adds to it the LIMITED
UNITS, the
units (a) to (d) and (f) of 8.1, in CHARGES. A charge is one addition, and a charge of `0` is not made. The charges are: the SPLIT
of the
input into top components (c); the START of a top component (a); the REFINE of the fresh start of a top component (b); the REFINE
of a
child (b), its individualisation and the refinement that follows; the FIXING of a call or of a child, ONE charge that adds its
FIX-SUM,
the units (c) of its fixing without the charge for the split of the remainder together with the units (d) of its entries, and its
SPLIT, the charge for the split of its remainder into components (c), the SPLIT left out for a stopped child; the TWIN TESTS of a
rule 3
call, the units (f) of all its tests, as one sum. The units (e) are never charged. A limit test follows EVERY charge (step 6), the
first
included.

THE STEPS.

1. One charge: the SPLIT of the input into top components (8.1(c)).
2. For each top component `K`, in ascending order of its smallest source identifier (7.9): one charge START of `K`; one charge
   REFINE of
   its fresh start (5.6); then step 3 on its top call. Top components of equal identity are each walked in full.
3. A CALL `X`.
   (a) `X` takes rule 1 or rule 2: one charge, the FIX-SUM of `X` plus its SPLIT; then step 3 on each nested call of `X`, one
   after the
   other, each in full before the next, in ascending order of the smallest source identifier of its nodes.
   (b) `X` takes rule 3: let `v_1 < .. < v_k` be the nodes of its target cell `T` in source order (2.2); `T` holds at least two
   nodes,
   so `X` has at least two free nodes. The BEST starts as nothing. VISIT `v_1`, `v_2`, .. in this order. To visit `v_i`:
   (i) SKIP. If `v_i` is in one class of the relation `~` of step 5 with some `v_j`, `j < i`, the child of `v_i` is SKIPPED:
   nothing is
   charged and nothing else is done for it. Every `v_j` with `j < i` counts here, whether its child was completed, stopped or
   skipped,
   because `~` is a relation on the nodes of `T`. This test is made before any charge.
   (ii) One charge REFINE of the child. Let `s` be the number of nodes in singleton node cells after it (6.3 item 1).
   (iii) FIX the child (6.2). Let `E` be its list of entries (6.3 item 2). No charge is made at this step.
   (iv) STOP ON `s`. If there is a best and `s` is strictly greater than the `s` of the best, the child is STOPPED.
   (v) STOP ON `E`. If there is a best, `s` is equal to the `s` of the best, and `E` is strictly greater than the `E` of the best,
   as
   lists of entries (6.3 item 5), the child is STOPPED.
   (vi) One charge for the fixing of the child: its FIX-SUM alone if it is stopped, its FIX-SUM plus its SPLIT if it survives both
   stops. Nothing more is charged or done for a stopped child.
   (vii) If this is the first child visited (it is `v_1`, which has no best and so is never stopped), step 4 on `X`. The charge
   TWIN TESTS
   of `X` thus follows the charge of (vi) for the child of `v_1` and precedes the first nested call of that child.
   (viii) Step 3 on each nested call of the child, as in (a). The child is COMPLETED and has its certificate (6.3).
   (ix) If there is no best, or the certificate of the child is strictly smaller than that of the best, the child becomes the
   best.
   When every node of `T` has been visited, the certificate of `X` is that of the best.
4. THE TWIN TESTS of a rule 3 call `X`, with `u = v_1`. `TW` is one union-find on the nodes of the top component, of the twin
   tests that
   passed, kept for the whole walk of the component and never undone; it starts with every node alone, and it is separate from the
   twin
   classes of the search-free run. For each `w` in `T` other than `u`, in ascending source order, that is not in the class of `u`
   in `TW` when its turn comes: TEST `(u, w)`. A test costs the start charge `c(l)` of every line of the top component that holds
   `u` or `w` (8.1(f)),
   whether
   it passes or fails; it PASSES when the transposition `(u w)` maps the set of lines of the top component onto itself (7.10), and
   then
   the classes of `u` and `w` are joined in `TW`. A failing test does not stop the tests. All the tests of `X` are made, then ONE
   charge
   TWIN TESTS of their total units, if it is not `0`.
5. THE RELATION `~`. At the visit of `v_i` of a rule 3 call `X`, `~` is the smallest equivalence relation on `T` that relates the
   pairs
   of (K1) and, for every KNOWN MAP `phi` of (K2) and (K3) and every node `x` of `T`, `x` to `phi(x)`. A map is a permutation of
   the
   free nodes of `X` that is the identity on the nodes it does not name. A map is CHECKED before use: it is used only if it
   carries the
   lines of `X` as `X` sees them (every leaf of a node fixed above `X` held by its position, 3.6; the state of a call of 10.1)
   onto
   themselves; a map that does not is dropped whole. A checked map is an automorphism of `X` that fixes its constants, so it maps
   `T`,
   a cell of the partition of `X`, onto itself (property 7(d)); the classes of `~` are joined within `T`. The relation at a visit
   comes
   from step 4 and from the children completed before `v_i`, never from a stopped or a skipped child:
   (K1) TWINS. After step 4 of `X`, every two nodes `x`, `y` of `T` that are in one class of `TW`, as `TW` stands at the visit,
   are
   related: `x ~ y`, by the transposition `(x y)`. Before step 4 nothing is related by (K1).
   (K2) EQUAL CHILDREN. The KEY of a completed child is its certificate. The WITNESSES of `X` are completed children, kept in the
   order
   of completion, which is the order of the visit; at most `M = max(floor(2^22 / n), 8)` are kept, `n` the number of free nodes of
   `X`,
   which is at least `2`. When a child `c_k` is completed: if a kept witness has a key equal to its key, `c_j` the first such in
   the order
   of completion, the map `lambda_k^(-1) o lambda_j` is known, where `lambda_m` gives each free node of `X` its final position
   below the
   child `c_m` with the offset taken as `0` (6.5, with M1 and M2 applied inside the child); otherwise `c_k` becomes a witness if
   fewer
   than `M` are kept, and if `M` are kept it is not kept and names no map to a later child.
   (K3) CARRIED MAPS. For every completed child `c`, the maps of `CARRIED(c)`. For a completed child or a call `Y` of rule 1 or 2,
   `CARRIED(Y)` is: for each nested call `Z` of `Y`, the maps `OWN(Z)`; and, the nested calls of `Y` being taken in the order of
   6.4 and
   each compared with the next in that order, for each two consecutive nested calls with equal certificates their EXCHANGE, the
   map that
   sends each node of the first to the node of the second with the same final position when the offset of each is taken as `0`
   (6.5),
   each node of the second back, and fixes every other node (10.1 (2)). `OWN(Z)` is: none for a call of rule 1; `CARRIED(Z)` for a
   call
   of rule 2; for a call of rule 3 (walked in full by step 3), its maps of (K2) at the end of its walk and `CARRIED(c)` for each
   completed child `c` of it. A twin test of `Z` adds nothing to `OWN(Z)`: the pair it joins is in `TW`, which (K1) reads at every
   rule 3
   call of the component. A carried map is lifted by the identity (10.1, LIFTING).
6. THE OUTCOME. The allowance `A` (7.10) is SATURATED when it equals `2^64 - 1`. After each charge, if `A` is not saturated and
   `R`
   exceeds `max(L, A)`, the walk ENDS at once with the outcome `refused(limit)`, whose fields are: `broken_by` of 7.7; `source`,
   the
   smallest source identifier of the top component whose step 2 holds the charge; `nodes`, its `n_K`; `units`, the value of `R`
   after
   the charge. The charge of step 1 never ends the walk (Lemma W (4)), so the charge that ends it lies in step 2 of a top
   component, and
   `source` and `nodes` are always defined. Otherwise, and when no charge is made, the walk ends after its last step with the
   outcome `labelled(result)`, the
   result of sections 5 to 7; `R` is then the TOTAL of the walk, the limited units of the input. This test is the only one by
   which an
   input is refused with `limit`, and a `guaranteed` input never fails it (Lemma W (4)).

THE FREEDOM OF AN ENGINE. An engine MAY compute by any method, sequential or parallel, with the shortcuts of section 10 or with
none,
with
others of its own, and it MUST report the outcome and every field that the walk gives. It follows that: (1) it can explore
children, nested calls and top components ahead and in any order, but it adds the charges to `R` in the order of the walk and ends
at the first charge after which `R` exceeds `max(L, A)`; charges that it computed beyond that charge are not made. (2) A child
that
the walk skips is charged nothing; a stopped child is charged its REFINE and its FIX-SUM (steps (ii) and (vi)), and a child that
survives the stops its REFINE and then its FIX-SUM and SPLIT in one charge, whatever work the engine did on it. (3) An engine
whose `L` is below `2^64 - 1` computes every charge of the walk up to the charge that ends it, the REFINE and the FIX-SUM of a
child that it would not otherwise explore included.
(4) An engine that stops for another cause, such as time, memory or a caller's signal, has no outcome of this text: it is not the
refusal `limit`, and it writes no result. An engine MUST NOT write a partial result or fall back to another labelling. An engine
MAY report the total of a completed walk beside the result (7.7), and MUST then report this value.

LEMMA W (the walk).
(1) EVERY KNOWN MAP IS AN AUTOMORPHISM of `X` (7.7) that fixes every constant of `X`, so the check of step 5 never drops one.
(2) THE WALK RETAINS THE CHOSEN CHILD. At every rule 3 call `X` the best at the end is the chosen child `v*` of M1; `v*` is
neither
skipped nor stopped; no attaining child is stopped, and an attaining child other than `v*` is skipped or completed; when `X` has
at most `2,048` free nodes, the attaining children are the members of the class of `v*` in `~` after
the last visit. So the certificate, the call structure, the final positions and the canonical map that the walk gives are those of
sections 5 to 7. Above `2,048` free nodes the class of `v*` can hold fewer than the
attaining
children; the record (7.7) is defined by sections 5 to 7 and does not depend on it.
(3) A FUNCTION OF THE INPUT. The walk, `R` after each charge and every field are functions of the input as given and of `L`. If
`sigma` is a bijection that
maps the
lines of one input onto those of another and preserves the source order, it maps the walk onto the walk, charge by charge.
(4) THE TOTAL. (a) The units (a) to (d) of the walk are at most those of the reference tree. (b) They are
at
least those of the result path, which is walked in full, by (2). (c) The charge of step 1 is at most `A`, which contains the split
of the
input, so it never ends the walk. (d) If the input is `guaranteed`, the total is at most `A`.

An input whose total of the walk does not exceed `A` is labelled at every `L`. The result of a labelled input does not depend on
which engine labelled it
(section 7).

EXAMPLE. The directed 4-cycle `a -> b -> c -> d -> a` has the allowance `232` and the limited units `154`; the isomorphic
`a -> c -> b -> d -> a` has the allowance `232` and the limited units `202`. Neither exceeds `A`, so neither is refused at any
`L`; but the totals
differ, because the maps of (K2) depend on the source order. `COMPL(3, 5)` (13.1), on `n0` to `n7`, has the allowance `2,608` and
the limited
units `3,506`, and under the relabelling of seed `5` the limited units `4,539`: at `L = 3,506` the first is labelled and the
second
is refused with `limit`.

### 8.4 Discovery of components

Components are mathematical objects (4.2), so any method of finding them is result-neutral; the method affects time only.
Discovery is
charged by the units of the fixing: one unit for each incidence of a removed vertex (8.1(c)).

### 8.5 The size of the search

(T1) A component whose plain incidence graph is a tree never branches, nor does anything nested in it (property 6); its
work is
Lemma 4 plus its fixing charges. (T2) If the fresh refinement of a top component is discrete there is one leaf (rule 1). (T3) If
at every rule 3 call of the result path the target
cell is one
twin class, the search-free run explores one child per call, and the work is at most `A` (Lemma A). (T4) The reference tree of a
call on a
component of `n` nodes has at most `2 n! - 1` calls, and each call has at most `n` children.

## 9. Properties

In
this section a call on `K` holds a stable pair `pi` in its own STRUCTURE `A` (the constants of its ancestors; the nodes fixed by
this
call, or by this child, are still vertices in singleton cells), and a component `K'` left after the fixing has its own structure
`B`
(the nodes just fixed are constants `fixed(q)`; the free occurrences are numbered anew; the vertices are the nodes of `K'` and the
lines
holding one of them).

1. INVARIANCE UNDER RENAMING AND LINE ORDER. The partitions, the target cell, the fixing order, the free components and the
   entries read no source identifier and not the order of the input. An isomorphism `sigma` between two inputs maps the partitions
   of each call onto those of the corresponding call and the children onto children, and gives equal certificates and equal
   identities. The canonical line sequences are equal when the top components of the input form at most one class of equal
   identity; with two or more non-isomorphic top components they are equal under the assumption of 7.15 (property 2).
2. EQUAL RESULT EXACTLY WHEN ISOMORPHIC. Two inputs have equal canonical line sequences exactly when they are isomorphic; the "if"
   direction holds under the assumption of 7.15 when the input has two or more non-isomorphic top components.
3. EQUAL CERTIFICATES GIVE EQUAL RELABELLED LINES. Two calls with the same argument `p`, the same constants and equal
   certificates, given the same offset, give the same set of lines with every blank leaf replaced by `fixed(final position)`. So
   which of two attaining children is chosen, and which of two nested calls with equal certificates comes first, is
   not
   observable in the canonical line sequence. No rule uses this property to compute a size or a position (6.5).
4. TERMINATION. At most `2V` pairs are inserted in one REFINE and at most `2V` are popped. The recursion of 6.7 has depth at most
   the number of
   nodes
   and each call has at most `n_K` children, so the search is finite.
5. THE NESTED MINIMUM IS THE GLOBAL MINIMUM. A leaf of the whole search is a choice of one child at every rule 3 call
   reached,
   and its certificate is the certificate along the leaf (6.3 item 9). The least certificate along the leaves of a child is `(s,
   E, (c_i))`, where `c_1 <= .. <= c_r` are the certificates of its nested calls in the order of 6.4, and that of a call is the
   least over its children: the definition of 6.3.
6. NO BRANCHING ON FORESTS. A component whose
   plain
   incidence graph `T` is a tree (a vertex for each free node, a vertex for each line, one edge for each incidence carrying its
   label; the
   fixed nodes are constants, not vertices) has a singleton node cell in its pair, so rule 3 never applies to it or to the
   components
   nested in it. LEMMA F. Let `T` be a finite forest whose vertices carry colours (node or line; for a line the encoding of its
   masked
   line) and whose edges carry labels, and let `pi` be a partition of its vertices that refines the colours and is stable (for all
   cells
   `X`, `W`, all `x` in `X` have the same multiset of labels of edges into `W`). Then for any `x`, `y` in one cell there is an
   automorphism of `T` that preserves colours, labels and `pi` and maps `x` to `y`. So the cells of `pi` are exactly the orbits of
   the
   automorphisms of `(T, pi)`.
7. THE INHERITED PAIR. The order of the cells differs from a fresh start; here only the cells as sets are compared. Let
   `K'` be
   a free component left by a call (or by a child of a rule 3 call) on `K`.
   (a) STABLE IN `B`. `pi` restricted to `K'` is stable in `B`. Fixing renumbers the free occurrences of every line holding a
   newly fixed node; the labels of the lines of one cell are renumbered by one strictly increasing map, and keys keep equality and
   order.
   (b) EQUAL MASKED LINES. Two lines of `K'` in one cell of `pi` have equal masked lines in `B`.
   (c) EQUAL AS A SET TO A FRESH COARSEST START. Write `R(x)` for the coarsest stable refinement of `x` (it exists and is unique
   by Lemma
   2) and `start_X` for the pair with one node cell and the line cells keyed by the encoding of the masked line in the structure
      `X`.
The pair of every call, as a set partition and in its own structure, is `R(start)`; and the pair of a rule 3 child, after its
REFINE, is `R(start meet {v})` in the structure of its call (meet is the common refinement).
   (d) SIBLINGS AND AUTOMORPHISMS. Let `f` map `K_1` onto `K_2`, two components of one parent, preserving constants, labels and
   masked
   lines in `B`. Then `sigma`, equal to `f` on `K_1`, to `f` inverse on `K_2` and to the identity elsewhere, is an automorphism of
   the whole top component that fixes every node fixed so far, individualised nodes included, and `K_1` and `K_2` have equal
   inherited pairs after restriction and re-offsetting, hence equal certificates. The partitions of a call are invariant under
   every automorphism of the call.
8. COST ADDS OVER COMPONENTS. Let a call, or a child, end its fixing with nested calls on the components `K_1 .. K_r`.
Each nested call reads only its own component, so the units of each nested call are those of its own call tree. When the `r`
certificates are ordered by a merge sort, each comparison charged as 8.1(e) charges it, `units(parent after its fixing) <=
units(fixing) + (1 + bits(r)) (units(K_1) + ... + units(K_r))`: a sum, never a product, up to the factor of the ordering. The
bound concerns an engine that orders by such a sort; the units (e) of another method differ.

## 10. Shortcuts

Any method that yields the result defined by sections 5 to 7 is permitted; none is normative. The units of an engine's limit are
those
of the walk of 8.3, which uses the stops of 10.4 and the skips of 10.1 and 10.2 as 8.3 states, whatever method the engine uses.
10.1 to 10.4 give the shortcuts whose result is that of sections 5 to 7, each with the condition under which it holds; 10.5 gives
methods whose result is not. Every shortcut is scoped to ONE call on one free component, with the state of that call.  The inputs
named here are given line by line in 13.1.

10.1 THE CHECKED AUTOMORPHISM: ONE RULE. THE STATE OF A CALL is its component, the nodes fixed above it with their path
positions (its constants), its lines as the call sees them (every fixed leaf held by its position, 3.6; annotation counts and
payloads
kept) and its partitions. A map is an AUTOMORPHISM OF A CALL when it is a permutation of the FREE nodes of the call that carries
the
set of the call's lines, as the call sees them, onto itself, with every leaf already fixed above the call held fixed; it then also
preserves the call's cells, because the partition is a function of that state (Lemma 3, property 7(d)). THE CHECK is against the
call's
OWN state, never against "the lines of the component" or "of the top call": a map that moves a node fixed on the way down is an
automorphism of the component and NOT of the call. At a top call nothing is fixed, so its state is the lines of its component. A
map valid at a call is valid at another call only if it is checked there (a map that moves a node fixed in between fails the
check); the one exception is LIFTING, below, which carries a checked map of a nested call to its ancestors.
THE RULE. A map that has been checked to be an automorphism of a call can be used AT THAT CALL, however it was found. What it
licenses is this and no more: a child `phi(v)` of the call can be skipped as the image of an EXPLORED child `v` of the same call,
and
their classes are joined for the attaining count. `phi(v)` attains exactly when `v` does (F2 of 7.7): among the
children of one call only one child of each orbit of the group generated by the checked maps of the call need be explored; the
number
of attaining children is the size of the UNION of the orbits, within the target cell, of the explored attaining children (Lemma
M); an
orbit is counted once, however many explored children it holds. It does NOT license carrying a choice or a certificate across a
map:
Lemma M (iii) states why, and 10.5(c) is the counterexample. The image of a STOPPED child can be skipped as well and is classified
not
attaining (its certificate is that of the stopped child, which is greater than the best so far, by F2); that is not observable in
the
result.
LIFTING. The one case in which a map is used at a call without a check there: a checked automorphism of a nested call
fixes
that call's constants, which include the node individualised in the child and every node fixed there; extended by the identity on
the
rest of the parent's component it is an automorphism of the parent call, because a line holds free nodes of one nested component
only,
and so of every ancestor.
HOW CANDIDATE MAPS ARE FOUND. Every candidate is checked before use.
(1) TWO CHILDREN OF ONE CALL WITH EQUAL CERTIFICATES, whether or not either of them attains: the map that sends each node of the
call to
the node of the other child with the same final position (6.5, below any child). By F1 of 7.7, which uses no minimality, for two
children `v`, `w` with equal certificates the map `lambda_w^(-1) o lambda_v`
is an
automorphism of the call with `phi(v) = w`: the Corollary of 6.8, (b).
(2) THE EXCHANGE OF TWO TIED NESTED CALLS (equal certificates) of an explored child, or of a rule 2 call. The map sends each node
of the
first call to the node of the second that has the same final position when the offset of each of the two nested calls is taken as
`0`
(6.5), and each node of the second back to the first, and fixes every other node; it is `lambda_2^(-1) o lambda_1`, the
isomorphism `f` of
the Corollary of 6.8, (a), which fixes every constant, extended as in property 7(d): it carries the lines of the call onto
themselves
because a line of the call holds free nodes of one nested component only. For `k` tied calls the exchanges of consecutive calls in
the
order of 6.4 are `k - 1` maps and generate every permutation of the class.
(3) A LIFTED AUTOMORPHISM of a nested call, as above.
(4) TWINS (10.2); the cost is the twin units of 7.10.
(5) TWO COMPLETE LEAVES OF A TOP CALL `X` (the restriction to a top call is part of the statement). A LEAF of `X` (6.3 item 9) has
its
final positions `lambda` of the nodes of the
component, computed by 6.5 along it.
Write `L(lambda)` for the set of the lines of the component with every blank leaf replaced by `fixed(lambda(leaf))`. If two leaves
with positions `lambda_1`, `lambda_2` have `L(lambda_1) = L(lambda_2)`, then `phi = lambda_2^(-1) o lambda_1` is an automorphism
of `X`. Used at a call that is not a top call, a map built from leaves has to be checked there (10.5(b)).
10.2 TWINS (route (4)). Two nodes of the target cell that are twins (7.10) are exchanged by a transposition that is an
automorphism of the call, so the rule of 10.1 applies to it. A transposition fixes every other node, so whether it is an
automorphism does
not depend on which nodes are constants: twins in one call are twins in every call that holds both (Lemma TW(c)). One child stands
for a
class of twins.

10.3 LAZY RESTRICTION (8.2 item 1). Any representation of the restricted partitions that has the same cells in the same
order gives the same result.

10.4 THE STRICT STOP. An engine can stop a child as soon as `s` is known and is strictly GREATER than the `s` of the best
certificate so far; or, `s` being equal, as soon as `E` is known (after the fixing of the child, before any nested call) and is
strictly
GREATER than the `E` of the best so far, as lists of entries in the order of 6.3. A child whose `s` is smaller, or whose `s` is
equal and
`E` smaller or equal so far, is continued; when completed it replaces the best if its certificate is strictly smaller. A stopped
child is
classified as not attaining. A child that attains is never stopped. NO STOP ON A NESTED CALL. A stop on a part of a nested call,
the `E` of a nested call alone, does not give the result of this text: the first member of `C` is compared by its `s` before its
`E`.

10.5 WRONG. Each shortcut below, used with every shortcut of 10.1 to 10.4, does not give the result of this text.
(a) THE STOP ON A TIE. A child stopped when `s` is equal and `E` is greater OR EQUAL is wrong, because an `E` that is equal is not
a tie
of the certificates; a tie is known only when the whole certificate is known, that is, when the child is completed; a stopped
child yields
no map.
(b) A MAP USED AT A CALL WHERE IT WAS NEITHER CHECKED NOR LIFTED: every map found anywhere in the top component used at every
call.
(c) CARRYING A
CHOICE OR A CERTIFICATE ACROSS A MAP: Lemma M (iii), on the complete graph on `a`, `b`, `c` in both directions: the state
of the child `c`, carried onto `a` by the transposition of `a` and `c`, gives `a 0`, `c 1`, `b 2`, and the canonical map has `a
0`,
`b 1`, `c 2`.
(d) A NODE INVARIANT COMPARED BEFORE THE CERTIFICATE is another labelling: it changes which leaf is smallest and breaks property
5.
Counterexample (no visiting order enters): the invariant `s`, larger first, on `FIFTEEN`: the definition gives the positions `a1
0`, `a2 1`,
`c0 2`, `c2 3`, `c1 4`, the invariant gives `c0 0`, `c2 1`, `c1 2`, `a1 3`, `a2 4`, and the canonical line sequences differ.
(e) A PARTITION SHARED BY TWO COMPONENTS, or a refinement of the whole input before the components start (7.5): the cells are
equal as
sets and the order of the cells differs. Counterexample (no visiting order enters): `SHARED`. The second component refined alone
has the
node cells `[y1] [y2] [y3] [y0]`, refined with the first it has `[y1] [y3] [y2] [y0]`; every cell is a singleton, so the
positions, and
the canonical line sequence of that component, differ.
(f) FIXING A WHOLE CLASS OF TWINS AT CONSECUTIVE POSITIONS. Counterexample, the directed complete bipartite graph `quad(a_i, <p>,
b_j)` for `i, j` in `0, 1, 2` (7.10), whose target cell of three nodes is one
twin
class: the child of the definition fixes ONE node (`s = 1`) and the nested call fixes the others, while fixing the class at once
would
give `s = 3`, so the certificate, and with it the component identity of 7.8, would differ from the definition's (the canonical
line
sequence is the same).

## 11. Out of scope

This text does not define:

- READING A SYNTAX: how lines are obtained from a text or binary document; the labelling takes typed lines (1.1).
- LOWERING: writing one kind of line as another (for example a reifier binding as a `quad`), and which statements a format
  supplies as
  `reifier-binding` and `annotation` lines (3.2).
- DE-DUPLICATION BETWEEN KINDS: removing a line because another line of another kind states the same thing.
- TEXT PRESENTATIONS: the syntax of a document, which lines it spells, and the label of a blank node beyond the one spelling of
  7.14.

- A DATASET MULTISET DIGEST: a value that commits to the multiset of lines and component identities and can be updated line by
  line.
  It would be a different value, with its own construction and security bound, defined outside this text.
- NAMING OF DATASETS: telling apart equal identities of different datasets; a reader pairs an identity with a name of its own
  (7.8, I4).
- THE VALUE of the limit `L` of an engine (8.3) and its data structures: each engine chooses them.
- A REFINEMENT STRONGER THAN COLOUR REFINEMENT (5.10): it would be a new version.
- Lines of a kind other than the five of 3.2, terms of a kind other than the four of 3.1, and any lowering into them.
- THE MEANING OF A PAYLOAD: what a `record` line stands for, how a payload is built and read, and whether two payloads are the
  same
  record; the labelling treats a payload as one constant (3.2).

## 12. Worked examples

All values are computed under the identifier
`(nested-refinement-labelling, 1)` (7.15). The `twin units` of a block are those of the source identifiers printed in it (8.1).

NOTATION. For a word `c`, `<c>` is the term `iri(c)` whose value is the ASCII bytes of `c`; in particular `<p>` is the one byte
`p`
(`0x70`), likewise `<q>` (`0x71`), `<r>` (`0x72`), `<s>` (`0x73`), `<t>` (`0x74`), `<h>` (`0x68`) and `<o>` (`0x6f`). `"v"^^<t>`
is the
literal with the lexical form `v`, the datatype `t` and no language or direction. `<<(s p o)>>` is the term `triple(s, p, o)`. A
line written with three terms is a `quad` with the graph `absent`; `quad(s, p, o, g)` has the graph `present(g)`.
`reifier-binding(r,
t)`, `annotation(k, r, p, o)` is a line of that kind (3.2) with the graph `absent`, `k` the count, and `empty-graph(g)` is the
line of that kind whose term is `g`.
`record("m", [t_1, .., t_n])` is the line of the kind `record` whose payload is the ASCII bytes between the quotes (`record("",
[])`
has the empty payload and the empty list) and whose list holds the terms `t_1` to `t_n` in that order. Every other word of
lower-case letters and digits is a blank node, and the letters are its source identifier as ASCII bytes. `Lk` names the
`k`-th input line. Within a block:

- Hexadecimal values are the encodings of 3.7 and 7.8 and the 32 bytes of an identity (7.8) or a digest (7.6), with no separator.
  A value longer than 96 hexadecimal digits continues on
  the
  following lines, 96 digits to a line; ONE rule of indentation holds everywhere: a continuation line starts in the column of the
  first digit of its value. The line breaks are not part of the value.
- `start line cells` lists the encoding of one line of each line cell after 5.6 (every leaf `free`), in colour order.
- A refinement step is written `start` (after 5.6), `indiv(v)` (after steps 1 and 2 of 5.8) or `pop(side,colour)` (after the
  pair was processed by 5.5), followed by `N=` the node cells and `L=` the line cells in colour order, each in brackets, and `Q=`
  the
  queue in ascending order (`-` when empty). The members of a node cell are written in source order and those of a line cell in
  ascending `k`, which carries no meaning
  (5.1).
- A call is written with its rule, its argument `p`, its `s`, and the nodes it fixes with their path positions. Its units are its
  own:
  REFINE counts the refinement after the individualisation of a child (the refinement of the fresh start is in the record), FIX
  and
  ENTRIES are 8.1(c) and (d). Each entry is written `m` and the encoding of its fully fixed masked line. A nested call is preceded
  by
  its certificate. Under rule 3 every child is written with its refinement; a child whose certificate equals that of an earlier
  child is not expanded again; `one twin class` states whether the target cell is one class of twins (7.10).
- `allowance` gives `S`, `O`, `M`, `V`, `W` and `A(K)` of 7.10; `twin units` are those of the search-free run (8.1(f), 7.10) for
  the
  source identifiers printed in the block.
- A stable identity (7.8) is written `(t, local position, copy index)`, `t` the index of its top component in the block, whose
  component identity is printed with it. `positions and stable identities` lists each node with its position.
- ORDER OF EVERY LIST OF A BLOCK. The top components are listed in the order of 7.3; the nodes of a component, the members of a
  node cell and the target cell in ascending source order (2.2); the children of a rule 3 call in ascending source order of the
  node
  individualised;
  the nested calls of a call in the order of 6.4; the nodes that a call fixes in ascending path position; the entries of a call in
  ascending order of 6.3 item 3; `positions and stable identities` in ascending `position`; the canonical line sequence in
  ascending
  bytewise order of the encoding (7.6).
- `units over the reference tree` are the units (a) to (d) over the tree in which every child of every rule 3 call is explored
  (8.1), summed over the top components, FIX including the charge for the split of the input (8.1(c)).
  The comparison units (8.1(e)) are ILLUSTRATIVE and stay outside the engine-independent record. They are counted under ONE
  reference method, on the reference tree: the children of a rule 3 call are visited in ascending source order, and each child
  after the first is compared with the best so far, one comparison of two certificates for each child (a child replaces the best
  only
  if its certificate is strictly smaller); the nested calls of a call are listed in the order of their smallest source identifiers
  and
  ordered by insertion sort, each element moving from the back of the sorted prefix towards the front while the element before it
  is
  greater, one certificate comparison for each step including the one that stops; the comparison of two certificates is charged as
  8.1(e) says. No other comparison is charged: the classification of the children as attaining or not, and the checks of
  equality, are not. The figures are a function of the input and of this method, and no engine has to report them; they
  are in no result, and are never limited (7.11).
- The record of a top component, and that of the whole input, are the structural part of 7.7, and `diagnostic` is its diagnostic
  part, each triple written `(failure record, component_source, cell_source)`; a factor record is written with the
  name of its kind (`attain` or `sym`), then its `value` and `exponent`.

## 13. Named inputs and sources

### 13.1 Named inputs

NAMED INPUTS. Where an input is described as a graph, its lines are these: for every edge `{u, v}` the two lines `quad(u, <p>, v)`
and
`quad(v, <p>, u)`, for every arc `(u, v)` the one line `quad(u, <p>, v)`; the blank nodes are named as stated. A node written
`v_i` has
the source identifier `v` followed by the decimal digits of `i` (`v0`, `v10`), in two digits where the input lists its nodes so
(`r_3` is `r03`); a node of any other letter is written the same way, and a node written with
a
plain name (`a`, `h`, `l1`) has that name. `CUBE`: nodes `v0` to `v7`, the edge `{v_i, v_j}` when `i` and `j` differ in exactly
one
binary digit (`12` edges, `24` lines). `QR13`: nodes `r00` to `r12`, the edge `{r_i, r_j}` when `(j - i) mod 13` is a non-zero
square
modulo `13` (the squares are `1`, `3`, `4`, `9`, `10`, `12`; `39` edges, `78` lines).
`K4PAIR`: nodes `a0` to `a3` and `b0` to `b3`, the
edges
`{a_i, a_j}` and `{b_i, b_j}` for `i < j` except `{a2, a3}` and `{b2, b3}`, and the edges `{a2, b2}` and `{a3, b3}` (`12` edges,
`24`
lines). `COMPL(k_1, k_2)`: on `k_1 + k_2` nodes `n0`, `n1`, .. (`n00`, `n01`, .. when there are ten or more), the line `quad(x,
<p>, y)` for every ordered pair of distinct nodes `(x, y)` such that `{x, y}` is no edge of the cycle on the first `k_1` nodes in
order or of the cycle on the next `k_2`, and no other line (`COMPL(3, 5)`: `40` lines). `FIFTEEN`: the nodes `a1`, `a2`,
`c0`,
`c1`, `c2` and the lines `quad(c0, <p>, c1)`, `quad(c1, <p>, c2)`, `quad(c2, <p>, c0)`, and `quad(x, <p>, y)` and `quad(y, <p>,
x)` for
`x` in `a1`, `a2` and `y` in `c0`, `c1`, `c2`. `SHARED`: the lines
`quad(x0, <p>, x1)`, `quad(x0, <p>, x2)`, `quad(y0, <p>, y3)`, `quad(y1, <p>, y0)`, `quad(y1, <p>, y2)`, `quad(y2, <p>, y0)`,
`quad(y3, <p>, y0)`.

RELABELLING. The generator `MIX64` has a state, an unsigned 64-bit integer; `next` adds `0x9E3779B97F4A7C15` to the state (modulo
`2^64`), sets `z` to the state, sets `z` to `(z xor (z >> 30))` times `0xBF58476D1CE4E5B9`, sets `z` to `(z xor (z >> 27))` times
`0x94D049BB133111EB`, and returns `z xor (z >> 31)`, all products modulo `2^64`. `S(seed)` is the stream from the state `seed`.
`DRAW(m)`,
for `1 <= m <= 2^32`, is the high 64 bits of the 128-bit product of `next()` and `m`. `PERM(n)` starts from `[0, 1, ..., n - 1]`
and,
for `i = n - 1` down to `1`, draws `j = DRAW(i + 1)` and swaps the entries `i` and `j`. THE RELABELLING of an input of `N` blank
nodes by the seed `s`: the nodes are numbered `0 .. N - 1` in ascending order of their source identifiers; `pi = PERM(N)`
from `S(s)`; the node numbered `k` receives the source identifier `u64(pi[k])`.
