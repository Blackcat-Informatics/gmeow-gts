# Part P6: Signatures

## 0. Scope, dependencies and notation

This part defines the signature record that stands in the envelope's `signatures` list (section 1); the signed subject and the
message that every algorithm signs (section 2); public keys, key identifiers and the typed inputs of a verifier (section 3); the
two signature algorithms of registry version `1`, their verification policies and the construction of the composite (section 4);
what a writer does when it signs (section 5); the verification procedure with its typed outcomes (section 6); coverage and
authentication (section 7); what a signature binds and what it authenticates, with the assumption each claim rests on (section 8);
co-signing (section 9); and the one subject (section 10).

It depends on:

- part `P2` (Registry) for every enumeration, every finding code and every reason. This part uses `P2`'s labels and assigns no
  code. Numeric codes shown in this part are those of `P2`, shown as bytes of examples or as `P2` states them.
- part `P1` (Model and wire), by section title: "The typed model and its one serialization" for the notation and `E(x)`; "The
  frame envelope" for the envelope and its `signatures` field; "The predecessor and transparent frames" for the predecessor,
  transparent frames and the EFFECTIVE IDENTIFIER;
  "Identifiers, hashing and every preimage" for `H_ctx`, the context table and `h0`; "The checks of every frame" for the link
  states `linked`, `healed` and `broken`; "A value against registry versions" for a value the reader does not know; and, where
  cited, "Scope, dependencies and notation", "What a decoder rejects", "Growing a file", "The header", "The resync item", "The one
  construction and the hash context table", "What the identifier commits to" and "The payload check".
- part `P5` (Durability), by section title: "The `commit` frame and the committed prefix" for the `commit` frame, its two checks
  and the valid commit; "The range" for the root a commit carries; and, where cited, "Leaves, nodes and root", "What the root
  binds", "What a commit does not show", "Consistency", "What a proof proves, and what it does not", "The `parity` frame" and
  "Checking a parity frame".
- part `P3` (RDF content), "Graph states", for the state that a commit names.
- part `P7` (Canonical form and reading) embeds the records defined here in the typed result of reading. The values of those
  records are defined by this part.

Notation.

- `E(x)` is the deterministic encoding of `x` (`P1`). `H_ctx(c, m)` is the hash of `P1` with the context `c`; this part uses the
  contexts `signature_subject` and `key_id`. `digest` is 32 bytes in registry version `1`.
- A byte string is written in hexadecimal, two digits per byte, with a space between bytes. `<K>`, `<H0>`, `<F>` and similar
  names in angle brackets stand for bytes of the length that the example states, which it does not write out; an example never
  states a digest it cannot show by
  hand. `a || b` is concatenation. `[x:y]` of a byte string is the slice from byte `x` up to but not including byte `y`, the
  first byte being `0`.
- A frame is TRANSPARENT exactly when its envelope field `transparent` is `true`; every other frame is ORDINARY. A frame with no
  effective identifier is an item of its segment with a position; it is not an ordinary frame for any count: it has no ordinal, is
  not a leaf of the range, is not counted by a commit or an index, is not a predecessor and is never the head. The HEAD of a
  segment is the effective identifier of its last ordinary frame that has one, or the identifier `h0` of its opener when there is
  none. These
  sentences are defined by `P1`, "The predecessor and transparent frames", and are used here as they stand. The EFFECTIVE
  IDENTIFIER of a frame is the term that `P1` defines in the same section, including the frame that has none. The ORDINARY
  POSITION of an ordinary frame is the number of ordinary frames of its segment that stand before
  it, so the first ordinary frame has ordinary position `0`. A POSITION is the zero-based index of an item among all the items of
  its segment, the opener being `0` (`P2`, "The fields of a finding that are present").
- A frame has a LINK THAT HOLDS when its link state is `linked` or `healed`.
- A requirement has an
  identifier `P6-n`.
- `r` is the registry version the reader implements and `h` the `registry_version` of the segment's opener (`P2`, "A value
  against the registry versions").

## 1. The signature record

```text
signature = [
  algorithm: enum(signature_algorithm),   ; element 0: ed25519 or mldsa65_ed25519 in registry version 1
  key_id:    digest,                      ; element 1: the identifier of the public key (3.2)
  value:     bytes,                       ; element 2: exactly the length the algorithm fixes (4.1, 4.2)
]
```

A signature stands in the `signatures` list of the envelope of a frame (`P1`), a list of zero or more signature records in
ascending bytewise order of `E(signature)` (`P1`, "Scope, dependencies and notation"), no record twice. The list is outside the
identifier of the frame:
adding a signature or removing one changes no identifier, no range root and no message of any other signature, and is a storage
rewrite (`P1`) that writes the list again in that order. Two writers of one frame and one set of signatures write one list,
whatever the order in which the signers signed.

Grammar violations. A frame whose `signatures` field is outside this grammar is `malformed_value` and opaque with `malformed`
(`P1`); the one finding carries the reason of the first violation in the order of the bytes (`P1`, "What a decoder rejects"), and
the table lists the violations; it is not an order of precedence. The order of the list is judged for each record when that record
has been read: a violation inside the record stands earlier in the bytes and decides first. A grammar violation inside a record is
therefore judged before the order of the list: for the record `k`, a violation inside it comes before its order violation, a
violation of an earlier record comes before both, and the comparison of `E(signature)` with the record before it is made only on a
record that has been read and found well formed.

| violation | reason |
|---|---|
| `signatures` is not an array | `wrong_type` |
| an element of the list is not an array | `wrong_type` |
| an element does not have exactly three fields | `wrong_length` |
| `algorithm` is not a `uint` | `wrong_type` |
| `algorithm` is `0` | `unregistered_value` |
| `algorithm` is a code that `P2`, "A value against the registry versions", judges `malformed_value` | by that rule |
| `key_id` is not a byte string | `wrong_type` |
| `key_id` is a byte string of another length than 32 | `wrong_length` |
| `value` is not a byte string | `wrong_type` |
| any head of the record is in a longer form than the shortest | `not_deterministic` |
| a record whose encoding is not above the encoding of the record before it in bytewise order (smaller, or equal to it) | `unordered_or_repeated` |

An `algorithm` that is not a code of the reader's version, in a segment whose `h` is above `r`, is not a grammar violation: it is
handled by step 1 of the procedure of section 6. The length of `value` is not a grammar rule: a value of the wrong length is the
reason `value_length` of step 4 of that procedure, so that damage to a signature is a signature outcome and the frame still folds.

**P6-1.** A writer MUST write every signature as the record of section 1, with the fields in that order and no other field.

**P6-2.** A reader MUST judge the `signatures` field of every frame that passes the envelope test by the table of section 1 and
MUST report a violation as `malformed_value` with the reason of the first violation in the order of the bytes of the field, as
section 1 states.

**P6-3.** A writer MAY write a signature on any ordinary frame and MUST NOT write one on a transparent frame; a reader MUST report
a non-empty `signatures` list on a `parity` frame as `parity_damaged` (`P5`, "The `parity` frame" and "Checking a parity frame").
A reader MUST NOT verify a signature of a transparent frame: it yields no signature result and the frame is never authenticated.

**P6-4.** A reader MUST give the outcome of each signature of a list from that signature, the inputs and the segment alone: the
outcome of one signature MUST NOT depend on another signature of the list, on the order of the list or on a signature of another
frame, and the result lists the signatures in the order of the list.

**P6-5.** A writer MUST NOT write two signatures with equal `algorithm` and `key_id` in the list of one frame.

**P6-52.** A writer MUST write the `signatures` list of every frame in ascending bytewise order of `E(signature)`, with no
record twice, and a writer that adds or removes a signature MUST write the list again in that order. A writer writes each
signature once: a writer given a signature equal to one already in the list MUST NOT add it. A reader MUST report a list
that is not in that order, or that holds a record twice, as `malformed_value` with the reason `unordered_or_repeated`, once for
the field (P1-9).

## 2. The signed subject

```text
subject = [
  algorithm:         enum(signature_algorithm),   ; the algorithm of the signature
  key_id:            digest,                      ; the key identifier of the signature
  header_identifier: digest,                      ; h0 of the opener of the segment that holds the frame
  frame_identifier:  digest,                      ; the EFFECTIVE identifier of the frame
]
message = H_ctx(signature_subject, E(subject))    ; 32 bytes: the one message that every algorithm signs
```

With 32-byte digests `E(subject)` is the byte `84`, the head of the `algorithm` code, and then three times `58 20` followed by the
32 bytes: `1 + 1 + 3 * 34 = 104` bytes for a code below `24` (the head of a `uint` below `24` is one byte). The context
`signature_subject` is a row of the context table of `P1` (the spelling `gts2 signature subject`) used by no other preimage, so
that the message is the output of a function that no other part of the format evaluates.

**P6-6.** A signer and a verifier MUST form the subject from the `algorithm` and the `key_id` of the signature record, the
identifier
`h0` of the segment that holds the frame (the `h0` of its opener, a header or a resync item, computed from the opener as read:
`P1`, "Identifiers, hashing and every preimage"), and the
effective identifier of the frame that carries the signature; the identifier of a frame whose link state is `healed` is the
identifier computed for it, so a signature made before the damage stays valid after it.

**P6-7.** A signer and a verifier MUST form the subject as exactly the record above, encoded by `E`; it has no other form, no
`option` field and no field whose
presence changes the meaning of another.

**P6-8.** A signer and a verifier MUST compute the `message` as `H_ctx(signature_subject, E(subject))` and MUST give a signature
algorithm of this part no other message: `ed25519` signs the 32 bytes of the `message` (4.1); `mldsa65_ed25519` takes the 32 bytes
of the
`message` as the message `M` of its construction (4.2).

**P6-9.** A writer MAY write a signature on an ordinary frame of any type; no rule of the format makes the subject differ by the
type of
the frame, and a profile that requires signatures on every `commit` states a requirement on the result, never a second subject.

## 3. Public keys, key identifiers and the typed inputs of a verifier

### 3.1 Public keys

The `public_key` of an algorithm is a byte string of the form its standard fixes, and no other form is admitted.

| `signature_algorithm` | `public_key` | length |
|---|---|---|
| `ed25519` | the encoding `ENC(A)` of the point `A` of RFC 8032 section 3.1 and section 5.1.2 | 32 bytes |
| `mldsa65_ed25519` | the ML-DSA-65 key (FIPS 204 section 7.2), then the `Ed25519` key of the row above | 1952 + 32 = 1984 bytes |

**P6-10.** A signer MUST use a `public_key` of exactly the length of its row of 3.1; the first 1952 bytes of a composite key are
the ML-DSA
component and the last 32 bytes the `Ed25519` component, and no other byte, prefix or length field stands in a key.

### 3.2 The key identifier

```text
key_preimage = [ algorithm: enum(signature_algorithm), public_key: bytes ]
key_id       = H_ctx(key_id, E(key_preimage))     ; 32 bytes; the context key_id is a row of the context table of P1
```

For `ed25519` `E(key_preimage)` is `82 01 58 20` and the 32 bytes of the key (36 bytes). For `mldsa65_ed25519` it is `82 02 59
07 c0`
and the 1984 bytes (1989 bytes), because `1984` is `0x07c0`. The identifier binds the algorithm and the whole key: two keys of
different algorithms or of different bytes have different identifiers, except with the probability of a collision of the hash.
An identifier is a digest and is never parsed: no reader splits it, and no part of it carries a meaning.

**P6-11.** A signer MUST compute the `key_id` of a signature as `H_ctx(key_id, E([algorithm, public_key]))` of its algorithm and
its public key, and a
reader MUST NOT derive any other fact from the bytes of a `key_id`.

### 3.3 The typed inputs of a verifier

```text
inputs   = [ trusted_keys: list(digest), held_keys: list(held_key) ]
held_key = [ key_id: digest, algorithm: enum(signature_algorithm), public_key: bytes ]
```

`held_keys` are the keys the verifier can check a signature with. `trusted_keys` are the key identifiers whose signatures the
verifier is prepared to rely on. Both are inputs of the caller, never of a file: no header, frame or field of a file names a key
as trusted, and a file cannot widen the verifier's trust. A key the verifier holds and does not trust verifies a signature to
`valid` and authenticates nothing. A key the verifier trusts and does not hold verifies nothing. How a key reaches a verifier,
how long it stays trusted and whether it is revoked are outside the file: a verifier that distrusts a key removes it from
`trusted_keys`, and that is the whole of expiry and revocation for this format; no check reads a clock, a certificate or a
network. Both lists are SETS, and each is held WITHOUT REPEATS in ASCENDING BYTEWISE ORDER of the encoding of its elements:
`E(key_id)`
for `trusted_keys` and `E(held_key)` for `held_keys`. A verifier that is given a list in another order, or with an element
twice (two elements of equal encoding), forms the list of this form from it before it reads any signature: it sorts the list
and keeps one element of each equal encoding. Two held keys with one `key_id` and different bytes are two elements.

A held key is CONSISTENT with a signature when its `algorithm` equals the `algorithm` of the signature and
`H_ctx(key_id, E([algorithm, public_key]))` of the held key equals the `key_id` of the signature.

**P6-12.** A verifier MUST take the held key for a signature as the held keys whose `key_id` equals the `key_id` of the signature,
among which it uses the first CONSISTENT one (3.3) in the order of the list `held_keys`, which is ascending bytewise order of
`E(held_key)`.

**P6-13.** A verifier MUST treat `trusted_keys` and `held_keys` as sets: it MUST form each in ascending bytewise order of the
encoding of its elements and without repeats before it reads, and the result MUST NOT depend on the order in which the caller
gave the elements or on the repetition of an element.

**P6-14.** A reader MUST give in its result the inputs in the form of 3.3 (each list ascending and without repeats), so that a
difference between two readers is explained by them and two callers who give the same sets get the same result (`P7`, "The
records").

**P6-15.** A signer MUST generate a key for one `signature_algorithm`. A signer MUST generate the `Ed25519` component and the
ML-DSA component of a `mldsa65_ed25519` key fresh for it and MUST NOT use either as, or take either from, a key of `ed25519` or of
any other
composite or standalone use (RFC 10073 sections 3.1 and 9.3).

## 4. The two algorithms and their verification policies

Registry version `1` holds exactly two signature algorithms (`P2`). A reader of registry version `1` implements both in
full, and the result of verifying one signature is the same for every reader (`P2`, "The mandatory algorithm set"). Each
algorithm has ONE verification policy, written here as an exact procedure whose result does not depend on any library (P6-21).

### 4.1 `ed25519`

THE ALGORITHM. `Ed25519` of RFC 8032 section 5.1 in its pure form: no pre-hash, no context (`dom2` is empty), the hash `SHA-512`
(FIPS 180-4). The message is the 32-byte `message` of section 2, or the 127 bytes of `M'` for the half of a composite (4.2). The
`value` is 64 bytes `ENC(R) || ENC(S)` (RFC 8032 section 5.1.6
step 6): `R` is `value[0:32]` and the integer `S` is the little-endian value of `value[32:64]`. The public key is 32 bytes
(3.1). The constants are those of RFC 8032 section 5.1, Table 1: `p = 2^255 - 19`; the group order
`L = 2^252 + 27742317777372353535851937790883648493`; the base point `B`; the cofactor `8`.

THE CURVE, THE ENCODING OF A POINT AND ITS DECODING, STATED HERE. The curve is `-x^2 + y^2 = 1 + d * x^2 * y^2` over the integers
modulo `p`, with `d = -121665 / 121666 mod p = 37095705934669439343138083508754565189542113879843219016388785533085940283555`.
The base point is `B = (Bx, By)` with `By = 4 / 5 mod p =
46316835694926478169428394003475163141307993866256225615783033603165251855960`
and `Bx = 15112221349535400772501151409588531511454012693041857206046113283949847762202`, the root of even parity. The identity is
`(0, 1)`. The sum of two points is the sum of the group of the curve, given by the formula of RFC 8032 section 5.1.4; any formula
that computes that sum gives the same point. The ENCODING `ENC(P)` of a point `P = (x, y)` is the 32 bytes of the little-endian
integer `y + 2^255 * (x mod 2)`. The DECODING of 32 bytes `s` is: let `y` be the little-endian integer of the low 255 bits of `s`
and `b` the top bit of `s`; fail when `y` is not below `p`; let `u = y^2 - 1` and `v = d * y^2 + 1`, both modulo `p`; fail when
`u / v` has no square root modulo `p`; let `x` be a square root of `u / v`; fail when `x` is `0` and `b` is `1`; when `x mod 2`
differs from `b` replace `x` by `p - x`; the point is `(x, y)`. The decoding is a function of the 32 bytes: a non-zero residue
has two roots that differ in parity, and `0` has one. RFC 8032 is the one document that judges any question these sentences leave
(sections 5.1 to 5.1.4).

THE EIGHT ENCODINGS OF SMALL ORDER. A point has small order when `8` times the point is the identity. The curve of `Ed25519` has
`8 * L` points (Table 1 of RFC 8032: cofactor `2^3`, order `L`, a prime), so exactly eight points have small order. Each has one
canonical encoding (RFC 8032 section 5.1.3: `y` below `p`, and the sign bit clear when `x` is `0`). The eight are, with the
order of the point, and in the form `y` as 32 little-endian bytes with the sign bit of `x` in the top bit of the last byte:

1. the identity `(0, 1)`, order `1`:
   `01 00 00 00 00 00 00 00 00 00 00 00 00 00 00 00 00 00 00 00 00 00 00 00 00 00 00 00 00 00 00 00`
2. `(0, p - 1)`, order `2`:
   `ec ff ff ff ff ff ff ff ff ff ff ff ff ff ff ff ff ff ff ff ff ff ff ff ff ff ff ff ff ff ff 7f`
3. `y = 0`, sign bit clear, order `4`:
   `00 00 00 00 00 00 00 00 00 00 00 00 00 00 00 00 00 00 00 00 00 00 00 00 00 00 00 00 00 00 00 00`
4. `y = 0`, sign bit set, order `4`:
   `00 00 00 00 00 00 00 00 00 00 00 00 00 00 00 00 00 00 00 00 00 00 00 00 00 00 00 00 00 00 00 80`
5. order `8`, sign bit clear:
   `26 e8 95 8f c2 b2 27 b0 45 c3 f4 89 f2 ef 98 f0 d5 df ac 05 d3 c6 33 39 b1 38 02 88 6d 53 fc 05`
6. the same `y`, sign bit set, order `8`:
   `26 e8 95 8f c2 b2 27 b0 45 c3 f4 89 f2 ef 98 f0 d5 df ac 05 d3 c6 33 39 b1 38 02 88 6d 53 fc 85`
7. order `8`, sign bit clear:
   `c7 17 6a 70 3d 4d d8 4f ba 3c 0b 76 0d 10 67 0f 2a 20 53 fa 2c 39 cc c6 4e c7 fd 77 92 ac 03 7a`
8. the same `y`, sign bit set, order `8`:
   `c7 17 6a 70 3d 4d d8 4f ba 3c 0b 76 0d 10 67 0f 2a 20 53 fa 2c 39 cc c6 4e c7 fd 77 92 ac 03 fa`

  A non-canonical alias of a small-order point (`y` equal to `p` or
`p + 1`, which are `0` and `1` again) is not a point encoding at all (RFC 8032 section 5.1.3 step 1) and is judged by check 4.

THE POLICY. `Ed25519-Strict(public_key, message, value)` is the first of these checks that fails, in this order, and `valid` when
none fails. The lengths (`value` of 64 bytes, `public_key` of 32 bytes) were checked before it (section 6, step 4).

1. SCALAR. When the integer `S` is not below `L`: `non_canonical_scalar`.
2. KEY. When `public_key` is byte for byte one of the eight encodings above: `small_order_key`.
3. NONCE. When `value[0:32]` is byte for byte one of the eight encodings above: `small_order_nonce`.
4. EQUATION. Decode `A` from `public_key` by the DECODING stated above (`y` below `p`; a square root of `u / v` exists;
   `x = 0` with the sign bit set fails); when decoding fails, `equation_failed`. Let `k` be the little-endian integer of
   `SHA-512(value[0:32] || public_key || message)` reduced modulo `L`, and let `R'` be the point `[S]B - [k]A`. The signature
   holds exactly when the 32-byte encoding `ENC(R')` equals `value[0:32]`; otherwise `equation_failed`.

The equation of check 4 is `[S]B = R + [k]A` over the decoded points, with `R` the point that `value[0:32]` encodes. It is the one
defining equation of this format. Because the encoding of a point is unique, `ENC(R')` equals `value[0:32]` exactly when
`value[0:32]` is the canonical encoding of a point `R` equal to `R'`, so an `R` that is not a canonical point encoding never
satisfies it and no separate decoding of `R` exists. `k` is reduced modulo `L` before the multiplication, so that the result
for a key that has a component of small order (a mixed-order key) is one value for every implementation.

**P6-16.** A signer MUST write an `ed25519` signature value as the 64 bytes `ENC(R) || ENC(S)` of RFC 8032 section 5.1.6 over the
32-byte
`message`, with no pre-hash and no context.

**P6-17.** A verifier MUST report the result of `Ed25519-Strict` as the first failing check of the order SCALAR, KEY, NONCE,
EQUATION, over the whole input: a value whose `S` is not below `L` is `non_canonical_scalar` whatever its key and its nonce are,
and a key of small order is `small_order_key` whatever its nonce is.

**P6-18.** A verifier MUST report `non_canonical_scalar` exactly when the integer of `value[32:64]` is at least `L`.

**P6-19.** A verifier MUST report `small_order_key` exactly when `public_key` equals one of the eight encodings of 4.1, and
`small_order_nonce` exactly when `value[0:32]` equals one of them, comparing all 32 bytes including the sign bit.

**P6-20.** A verifier MUST accept a signature at check 4 exactly when `A` decodes by RFC 8032 section 5.1.3 and
`ENC([S]B - [k]A)` equals `value[0:32]`, with `k` the hash value reduced modulo `L`, and MUST report every other
input at that check as `equation_failed`.

**P6-21.** A verifier that computes with a library MUST give, for every input, the result of P6-17 to P6-20.

**P6-22.** A verifier MAY check a key once and keep the result for later signatures of that key, and MUST still report, for each
signature, the first failing check of P6-17 over the whole input (a bad scalar is `non_canonical_scalar` even when the key is
also of small order).

THE STRICT POLICY ACCEPTS NO MORE THAN RFC 8032. Every signature that `Ed25519-Strict` accepts is accepted by the
verification of RFC 8032 section 5.1.7. A forgery under this policy is a forgery under RFC 8032, and the policy rejects every
signature that RFC 8032 rejects and some
that it accepts.

MIXED-ORDER KEYS AND MALLEABILITY. A key that has a component of small order but is not itself of small
order (a mixed-order key) is not detected and not refused, so a signature under such a key that satisfies the equation is
`valid`. The value is not malleable: for a given `R`, `S` is the one integer below
`L` and `ENC(R)` is unique, so a valid value has no second encoding (RFC 8032 section 8.4).

THE SIGNING SIDE. An honest signer (RFC 8032 section 5.1.6) produces `S` below `L`, an `R` that is in the prime-order
subgroup, and a key in that subgroup, so the policy rejects no honest signature but one case: a nonce `r` reduced
to `0` gives the identity as `R`, which check 3 rejects. A signer
that meets it writes no signature (P6-30).

### 4.2 `mldsa65_ed25519`: the composite

THE SOURCE OF THE CONSTRUCTION. RFC 10073 (Composite Module-Lattice-Based Digital Signature Algorithm (ML-DSA) for Use in X.509
Public Key Infrastructure, November 2026, Standards Track), the algorithm `id-MLDSA65-Ed25519-SHA512` of its section 6, built by
its sections 2.2, 3.2 and 3.3 and serialized by its section 4. RFC 10073 is the source and the authority of the construction; this
part states every byte of it for this format. The
parts of the RFC that concern X.509 and PKIX (its sections 5, 7 and 8, object identifiers, the encoding of keys and signatures in
certificates) are not used: keys and signature values are the raw forms of its section 4, which its section 5 permits.

The parameters the RFC fixes for this algorithm (its section 6):

| parameter | value |
|---|---|
| ML-DSA variant | ML-DSA-65 of FIPS 204 |
| traditional algorithm | `Ed25519` of RFC 8032 |
| `Prefix` | the 32 ASCII bytes `CompositeAlgorithmSignatures2025` |
| `Label` | the 30 ASCII bytes `COMPSIG-MLDSA65-Ed25519-SHA512` |
| `PH` (pre-hash) | `SHA-512`, 64 bytes of output |
| `ctx` | the application context; this format uses the empty string, `len(ctx) = 0` |

`Prefix` in hexadecimal is `43 6f 6d 70 6f 73 69 74 65 41 6c 67 6f 72 69 74 68 6d 53 69 67 6e 61 74 75 72 65 73 32 30 32 35`
(32 bytes, equal to the 64 hexadecimal digits of RFC 10073 section 2.2). `Label` in hexadecimal is
`43 4f 4d 50 53 49 47 2d 4d 4c 44 53 41 36 35 2d 45 64 32 35 35 31 39 2d 53 48 41 35 31 32` (30 bytes).

THE MESSAGE REPRESENTATIVE. With `M` the 32-byte `message` of section 2:

```text
M' = Prefix || Label || len(ctx) || ctx || PH(M)
   = Prefix (32 bytes) || Label (30 bytes) || 00 || SHA-512(M) (64 bytes)       ; 127 bytes
```

THE TWO HALVES (RFC 10073 section 3.2 step 4 and section 3.3 step 4).

- The ML-DSA half is `ML-DSA.Sign(sk, M', ctx = Label)` and `ML-DSA.Verify(pk, M', signature, ctx = Label)` of FIPS
  204 section 5.2 (Algorithm 2) and section 5.3 (Algorithm 3): the pure form, not `HashML-DSA`, with the 30 bytes of
  `Label` as the FIPS 204 context. FIPS 204 forms its own internal message from the context and `M'` (its Algorithm
  2, line 10), which is outside this part.
- The `Ed25519` half is the pure `Ed25519` signature over the whole of `M'` with no context (the `value` of 4.1 over the 127
  bytes of `M'`). Its verification is the policy of 4.1.

ENCODINGS (RFC 10073 section 4 and Table 1; Appendix A, Table 6, for `id-MLDSA65-Ed25519-SHA512`).

| item | form | length |
|---|---|---|
| public key | the ML-DSA-65 key (FIPS 204 section 7.2), then the `Ed25519` key (RFC 8032 section 3.1) | 1984 bytes |
| `value` | the ML-DSA-65 signature (FIPS 204 section 7.2, 3309 bytes), then the `Ed25519` one (64 bytes) | 3373 bytes |
| private key (held by a writer, never in a file) | the 32-byte ML-DSA seed, then the 32-byte `Ed25519` seed | 64 bytes |

THE POLICY. `Composite-Strict(public_key, message, value)` is, in this order:

1. SPLIT. `mldsaPK = public_key[0:1952]`, `ed25519PK = public_key[1952:1984]`, `mldsaSig = value[0:3309]`,
   `ed25519Sig = value[3309:3373]`. The lengths of `public_key` and `value` were checked before it (section 6, step 4).
2. FORM `M'` from `message` as above.
3. CLASSICAL. Run `Ed25519-Strict(ed25519PK, M', ed25519Sig)` of 4.1. When it reports any reason, the composite is `invalid` with
   `composite_classical_failed`; the reasons of 4.1 are not reported beside it.
4. POST-QUANTUM. Run `ML-DSA.Verify(mldsaPK, M', mldsaSig, ctx = Label)`. When it returns false, the composite is `invalid` with
   `composite_post_quantum_failed`.
5. The composite is `valid`.

THE DECODING OF THE ML-DSA HALF. `mldsaPK` and `mldsaSig` are opaque bytes to the typed model, cut at the fixed offsets of step 1.
Their decoding is the decoding of FIPS 204 section 7.2, run inside `ML-DSA.Verify`, and FIPS 204 of 13 August 2024 is the one
document that judges it; a library is not asked. A key of 1952 bytes decodes in every case. A signature of 3309 bytes is the 48
bytes `c~`, the 3200 bytes of `z` and the 61 bytes `y` of the hint (55 index bytes, then 6 count bytes, one count for each of
the 6 polynomials); the decoding of the hint (`HintBitUnpack`) refuses exactly these four things, and each refusal makes
`ML-DSA.Verify` return false, so the composite is `composite_post_quantum_failed`: a count above 55; a count below the index
reached by the counts before it; indices of one polynomial that do not strictly increase; a non-zero byte among the index bytes
that no count reaches.

**P6-23.** A writer and a verifier MUST form `M'` exactly as above: the 32 bytes of `Prefix`, the 30 bytes of `Label`, the single
byte `00`, and the 64 bytes of `SHA-512(message)`, 127 bytes in all, with the `message` of section 2 as `M`.

**P6-24.** A signer and a verifier MUST compute the ML-DSA half by `ML-DSA.Sign` and `ML-DSA.Verify` of FIPS 204 with `M'` as the
message and the 30 bytes of `Label` as the context, and the `Ed25519` half as the pure `Ed25519` signature and verification over
`M'` with no context.

**P6-25.** A signer MUST use a composite public key of 1984 bytes and MUST write a composite `value` of 3373 bytes, in the layouts
above; any other length of
either is `value_length` (section 6, step 4).

**P6-26.** A verifier MUST report `valid` for a composite only when both halves pass, MUST run the classical half by the policy of
4.1 over `M'`, and MUST report the first failure of the order CLASSICAL then POST-QUANTUM: a composite whose both halves fail is
`composite_classical_failed`. A verifier MUST NOT report `valid` after skipping a half.

**P6-27.** A composite is one algorithm in one signature record: a writer MUST NOT write it as two signature records, one per
half, and a verifier MUST NOT report a half of a composite signature as a signature of any algorithm.

## 5. Signing

A signature is made by a writer that holds the secret key. This section states what a writer does so that every signature of
the format is reproducible and is checked by the policy of section 4 before it is written.

DETERMINISTIC SIGNING ONLY. A signature is a function of the secret key and the `message`: for `ed25519`, RFC 8032 section
5.1.6 derives the nonce from the second half of the SHA-512 hash of the private key and the message; for the composite,
the `Ed25519` half is that signature over `M'` and the ML-DSA half is the deterministic variant of FIPS 204, in which the
32-byte value `rnd` of its Algorithm 2 (line
5) is the string of 32 zero bytes (FIPS 204 sections 3.4 and 5.2). A verifier cannot tell a deterministic signature from a hedged
one: FIPS 204 section 3.4 states that the same verification algorithm verifies both. The rule is therefore a rule of the writer;
no outcome of reading depends on it, and
section 8 states what it gives and what it does not defend.

**P6-28.** A writer MUST sign deterministically: `ed25519` by RFC 8032 section 5.1.6, and the composite with the `Ed25519` half
likewise and the ML-DSA half by the deterministic variant of FIPS 204 with `rnd` equal to 32 zero bytes.

**P6-29.** A writer MUST verify each signature it has made, by the procedure of section 6 with its own public key as the held
key, before it writes the signature, and MUST write it only when the status is `valid`.

**P6-30.** A writer whose signing or self-verification fails MUST write no signature for that frame and MUST report the failure
to its caller; it MUST NOT write a signature that fails the policy of section 4.

## 6. Verification: the procedure and its typed outcomes

### 6.1 The records

```text
inputs           = [ trusted_keys: list(digest), held_keys: list(held_key) ]        ; 3.3
signature_result = [ status:         enum(signature_status),                         ; valid, invalid or unverified
                     reason:         option(enum(reason)),                           ; absent for valid
                     algorithm:      uint,                                           ; the code of the signature as read
                     key_id:         digest,                                         ; the key_id of the signature, always kept
                     coverage_first: uint,                                           ; section 7
                     coverage_end:   uint,                                           ; section 7
                     authenticated:  bool ]                                          ; section 7
```

`signature_result` is the record of one signature, held in the list `signatures` of the `frame_result` of the frame that carries
it (`P7`, "The records"), in the order of the signatures of the frame. The `algorithm` is the code the record holds; for the
status `unverified` with the reason `unknown_signature_algorithm` it is a code outside the reader's enumeration, kept as read
(`P2`, "Enumerations of the typed result").

### 6.2 The procedure

Precondition. A frame with no effective identifier has no subject: it carries no `signature_result`, is not counted, and is
never covered or authenticated. The segment's opener is readable and its `hash_algorithm` is one the reader implements, so that
`h0` and the effective identifiers can be computed. When it is not, no signature of the segment is verified, the segment has no
`signature_result`, and its `authenticity` is `unauthenticated` (7); the segment already carries the finding of its opener (`P1`,
"The header" and "The resync item"; `P2`, "The finding table").

`Verify(signature, frame)` for one signature of one ordinary frame, the held keys and the segment. The result is the FIRST step
that gives an outcome; a step gives its outcome, no later check is made, and step 7 fills the record.

1. ALGORITHM. When the `algorithm` of the signature is not a code of the reader's registry version (the case of a segment whose
   `h` is above `r`, `P2`, "A value against the registry versions"): status `unverified`, reason
   `unknown_signature_algorithm`. The `key_id` is kept.
2. KEY HELD. When no held key has the `key_id` of the signature: status `unverified`, reason `key_not_held`.
3. KEY CONSISTENT. When no held key with that `key_id` is consistent with the signature (3.3): status `invalid`, reason
   `key_mismatch`. Otherwise the first consistent held key in the order of the list `held_keys` is the key of the following steps.
4. LENGTHS. When the `public_key` of the key is not the length of its algorithm (32 bytes for `ed25519`, 1984 for
   `mldsa65_ed25519`), or the `value` is not the length of its algorithm (64 bytes, 3373 bytes): status `invalid`, reason
   `value_length`. The key is judged first and then the value; one reason covers both.
5. MESSAGE. Form the subject and the `message` of section 2.
6. ALGORITHM CHECKS. For `ed25519`, `Ed25519-Strict` of 4.1 over the `message`; for `mldsa65_ed25519`, `Composite-Strict` of 4.2.
   A failing check gives status `invalid` and its reason; no failing check gives status `valid` and no reason.
7. RESULT. Fill the record of 6.1 with the outcome; the fields `coverage_first`, `coverage_end` and `authenticated` are
   those of section 7.

Findings. A signature with the status `invalid` has one finding, `signature_invalid`, and one with the status `unverified` has
one finding, `signature_unverified`; each carries the reason of its result, the subject `[3, signature_algorithm]` with the code
of the signature's algorithm, and the `position` of the frame that carries the signature, and no `offset`, `length` or `count`
(`P2`, "The finding table"). A `valid` signature has no finding. A signature has no other finding: in particular an unknown
algorithm is not also `unknown_enumeration_value`.

**P6-31.** A verifier MUST run the steps of 6.2 in order and MUST report the outcome of the first step that gives one, over the
whole input, for every signature of every ordinary frame that passes the envelope test, has an effective identifier, stands in a
segment whose opener outcome is `readable`, whose `signatures` field is in grammar and whose `signatures` element is at most the
reader's `stored_bytes_per_frame` (`P1`, "The payload check"), whatever the fold state of the frame (an
opaque or unknown-typed frame carries signature results too); no
step of 6.2 is left to a library's parser (the decoding inside a primitive is judged as P6-53 states), and a later check MUST NOT
replace the reason of an earlier one.

**P6-53.** A verifier MUST decode an `ed25519` key by the DECODING of 4.1, and MUST decode the ML-DSA key and
signature of a composite by FIPS 204 section 7.2; it MUST NOT apply another decoding to either, and the one document that judges
a question left open by 4.1 or 4.2 is RFC 8032 for the `Ed25519` half, FIPS 204 for the ML-DSA half and RFC 10073 for their
composition.

**P6-32.** A verifier of registry version `r`, for a signature whose `algorithm` is not a code of version `r` in a segment whose
`h` is above `r`, MUST report status `unverified`, reason `unknown_signature_algorithm`, keep the `key_id`, and report exactly
one finding, `signature_unverified`, with that reason and the subject `[3, signature_algorithm]`; it MUST NOT check the key, the
length or the value of that signature, and MUST NOT report `unknown_enumeration_value` for it. In a segment whose `h` is at most
`r` the same code is `malformed_value` by section 1 and no signature result exists for the frame.

**P6-33.** A verifier MUST report `key_not_held` exactly when no held key has the `key_id` of the signature, and `key_mismatch`
exactly when some held key has it and none is consistent with the signature's algorithm and `key_id`; `key_not_held` has one
meaning, a signature whose key the verifier lacks.

**P6-34.** A verifier MUST report `value_length` for a public key of another length than its algorithm's and for a value of
another length than its algorithm's, judging the key first, and MUST do so before any check of section 4; a value one byte short
or one byte long is `value_length` and never a failure of a half of a composite.

**P6-35.** A verifier MUST give a `signature_result` the `reason` `absent` for the status `valid`, one of the eight invalid
reasons of `P2`
for `invalid`, and `unknown_signature_algorithm` or `key_not_held` for `unverified` (`P2`, "The `reason` enumeration"), and MUST
keep the `key_id` of the signature in every case.

**P6-36.** A verifier MUST report exactly the findings of 6.2: one per signature that is not `valid`, with the fields stated, and
none for a `valid` one.

**P6-37.** A verifier MUST NOT read a clock, a certificate, a revocation list or a network, and MUST NOT consult `trusted_keys` to
decide a status: a signature is `valid` under a key the verifier holds whether or not it trusts the key. Trust enters only the
field `authenticated` (section 7).

**P6-38.** A reader MUST NOT consult a signature, a held key or a trusted key in a step of healing or recovery (`P1`, "The checks
of every frame"): the result of those steps is a function of the bytes, of the reader's registry version and of its capacity
alone, and the effective
identifier a signature is checked
against is an output of them, never an input.

**P6-39.** A verifier MUST decide a status by the steps of 6.2 alone and MUST NOT change it because of the link, the coverage or
the authentication of the frame, which are separate typed facts (section 7).

**P6-40.** For one registry version, one set of inputs and the same bytes, every verifier MUST give the same `signature_result`
for every signature (`P2`, "A value against the registry versions").

## 7. Coverage and authentication

A signature outcome (section 6) says whether `value` verifies. Two further facts are separate typed facts and are never folded
into the status: what the signature COVERS, and whether it AUTHENTICATES.

### 7.1 Coverage

The UNBROKEN RUN of an ordinary frame `f` of ordinary position `o` is the unbroken run of `P1` ("What the identifier commits
to", Claim 2): the maximal run of consecutive ordinary frames that ends at `f` and in which every frame has a link that holds and
none but the first is detached, that is the frames of ordinary positions `s` to `o`, with `s` the least position such that the
frames `s` to `o` all have a link
that holds and none of the frames `s + 1` to `o` is detached (`P1`, "The predecessor and transparent frames"). The unbroken run is
empty when the link of `f` does not hold. Transparent frames are not part of the sequence
of ordinary frames and neither end nor break an unbroken run.

The COVERAGE of a signature on `f` is the half-open interval of ordinary positions `[coverage_first, coverage_end)`: for the
status `valid` and a link of `f` that holds, `coverage_first = s` and `coverage_end = o + 1`; in every other case the coverage is
EMPTY and `coverage_first = coverage_end = o`. A frame before a broken link is not covered by a signature after it, and a frame
before a detached frame is covered by no signature on the detached frame or on a frame after it. The broken frame is not covered;
the detached frame and a frame after either are covered.

### 7.2 Authentication

A signature AUTHENTICATES when all of these hold, and its `authenticated` is then `true`:

1. its status is `valid`;
2. its `key_id` is a member of `trusted_keys`;
3. the link of the frame that carries it holds;
4. when the frame is a `commit`, the commit is VALID in the sense of `P5` ("The `commit` frame and the committed prefix"): its
   link holds, its `count` equals its ordinary position and the root recomputed over `h0` and the effective identifiers of the
   frames before it equals its `root`. A frame of the type `commit` whose content is outside the grammar of the type is not a
   valid commit, and a signature on it does not authenticate; and
5. neither the algorithm of the signature nor the hash algorithm of its segment is retired in the registry version of the reader
   (`P2`, section 9a). Retirement changes this condition and no status, reason or coverage.

The AUTHENTICATED FRAMES of a segment are the ordinary frames `g` of ordinary position `p` for which some authenticating signature
on a frame `f` of ordinary position `o` at least `p` exists and either

- `f` is not a `commit` and `p` lies in the unbroken run of `f` (the coverage of the signature); or
- `f` is a `commit` and the link of `g` holds.

The second case extends the first and is the whole value of the root check: a valid commit's root holds the effective identifier
of every earlier ordinary frame as a leaf, so each such identifier, and `h0`, is the signer's even across a frame whose link is
broken, and an earlier frame whose own link holds is bound by its own identifier (section 8, claim 3). A frame whose link is
broken is never authenticated, and a frame after the last authenticating signature's frame is never authenticated.

The result fields. `authenticated_frames` is the number of authenticated counted frames. `authenticity` is a function of the
COUNTED frames, the ordinary frames other than `index` frames, none of which is a frame without an effective identifier (an index
is an accelerator whose every field a reader checks against the file, and a canonical file ends with one after its commit):
`authenticated` when there is at least one counted frame and every one is an authenticated frame, `partly_authenticated` when some
but not all are, and `unauthenticated` otherwise (including a segment with no counted frame and a segment with no signature). A
canonical file whose last commit carries an authenticating signature is therefore `authenticated`. `transparent_frames` lists
the positions of the transparent frames, none of which is an ordinary frame, covered or authenticated. The three states and the
count are typed values; no word is printed and no phrase is matched.

AUTHENTICATION IS ABOUT IDENTIFIERS. An authenticated frame has an identifier that is the signer's, and the identifier fixes the
frame's type, flags, content, decoded length and payload digest. The payload bytes are the signer's only when the payload check
of the frame holds (`P1`, "The payload check"), and the frame is folded only when its fold state says so. A consumer that wants
signed content combines two facts of the result, `authenticity` `authenticated` (or the authenticated frames it needs) and
`integrity` `intact`, and this part does not combine them for it.

**P6-41.** A verifier MUST compute the coverage of each signature as in 7.1, the unbroken run being over ordinary frames whose
links hold and of which none but the first is detached,
and MUST report an empty coverage with `coverage_first` and `coverage_end` both equal to the ordinary position of the frame for
every signature that is not `valid` and for every signature on a frame whose link does not hold.

**P6-42.** A verifier MUST set `authenticated` to `true` exactly when the five conditions of 7.2 hold, and to `false` otherwise.

**P6-43.** A verifier MUST report as authenticated exactly the frames of 7.2 and MUST NOT report a transparent frame, a frame
whose link does not hold, a frame after the last authenticating signature's frame, or a frame because of a proof (`P5`, "What a
proof proves, and what it does not") as authenticated.

**P6-44.** A verifier MUST set `authenticated_frames` to the number of authenticated counted frames and `authenticity` by the rule
of 7.2, over the ordinary frames other than `index` frames.

**P6-45.** A verifier MUST decide `authenticated` and `authenticity` by 7.2 alone and MUST NOT widen either for a profile, a
lineage of segments, a header field or an input other than `trusted_keys`; a profile MAY require more of the result (`P7`, "The
profile mechanism").

**P6-46.** A verifier MUST report coverage and authentication as typed fields (`coverage_first`, `coverage_end`, `authenticated`,
`authenticated_frames`, `authenticity`) and MUST NOT report either as a word or a phrase, and MUST NOT report a signature as
`authenticated` on the strength of its status alone.

## 8. What a signature binds and authenticates

ASSUMPTIONS. Each claim below states which of these it rests on. They are assumptions, not theorems of this format.

- `A1`. `H_ctx` is collision resistant. `A2`. Two different contexts give unrelated functions (`P1`, "The one construction and
  the hash context table").
- `A3`. `Ed25519` is existentially unforgeable under chosen message attack (EUF-CMA) when verified as RFC 8032 section 5.1.7
  verifies. `A4`. ML-DSA-65 is EUF-CMA (FIPS 204). `A5`. `SHA-512` is collision resistant (RFC 10073 section 9.2.1: the composite
  is EUF-CMA secure if one component is and the pre-hash is collision resistant).
- `A6`. The secret key of a key in `trusted_keys` is held only by the party the verifier means by that key, and the verifier's
  `trusted_keys` is right. The format does not make this true; the verifier's inputs do.

CLAIM 1: WHAT THE SUBJECT BINDS. A signature of algorithm `a` and key `K` on frame `f` that verifies `valid` shows, under `A1`
to `A5`, that the holder of `K` signed the subject `[a, K, h0, e]` and no other subject, where `e` is the effective identifier
of `f`. The four fields bind:

- the ALGORITHM `a`, by the field itself;
- the KEY, by `K`, which is `H_ctx(key_id, E([a, public_key]))` (3.2), so under `A1` it names one key and one algorithm;
- the SEGMENT, by `h0`, which is the hash of the opener record, so any change to the opener is another segment; a signed commit
  of a segment opened by a resync item covers the item through leaf `0` of its range (`P5`, "Leaves, nodes and root");
- the FRAME, by `e`, which, when the link of `f` holds, is `H_ctx(frame, E(identity))` and covers the frame's type, flags,
  content, decoded length, payload digest and its predecessor's effective identifier. Through the predecessor, `e` covers the
  fields and the effective identifiers of the frames of the UNBROKEN RUN that ends at `f` (7.1), and the identifier
  that the nearest broken frame before that run stores, or the `anchor` that a detached first frame of the run has as its
  predecessor, and nothing before it; only when the run reaches the first frame and that frame is not detached, or the `anchor` of
  a detached first frame of the run is `h0`, does `e`
  cover `h0` (`P1`, "What the identifier commits to", Claim 2).

CLAIM 2: NO SUBSTITUTION OF ALGORITHM OR KEY. No signature made for `(a, K)` is accepted as a signature of another algorithm or
key over the same frame, except by a break of `A1` to `A5`, and the claim is made for keys that are honestly generated and
held as `A6` says, under the unforgeability games of `A3` and `A4`. The claim does not cover a public key chosen by an adversary
after it has seen a signature: the unforgeability games of
`A3` and `A4` concern a key generated honestly, and nothing is claimed about a key chosen by an adversary so that an
existing value verifies over a message that contains that key. Two keys with
equal bytes under different algorithms have different identifiers (3.2); the `Ed25519` half of a composite signs the 127
bytes of `M'` and a standalone `ed25519` signature signs 32 bytes, so no standalone signature is a half of a composite or
conversely. The key identifier also decides the algorithm that the verifier uses: step 3 of 6.2 takes only a held key whose
algorithm and bytes hash to the identifier.

CLAIM 3: WHAT A VALID SIGNATURE ON A `commit` AUTHENTICATES. Let `c` be a `commit` frame whose signature authenticates
(7.2). Then, under `A1` to `A6`:

- the opener, by `h0`, is the signer's opener; and the effective identifier of every ordinary frame before `c`
  is the signer's, because the root of `c` has each as a leaf (`P5`, "What the root binds") and the identifier
  of `c` covers its `count` and `root`;
- every earlier ordinary frame whose link holds has the type, flags, content, decoded length and payload digest
  that the signer saw, because its effective identifier is a leaf of the signed root and equals the hash of
  exactly those fields and its predecessor;
- the commit's own claim, that exactly `count` ordinary frames precede it and that their range root is `root`.

It does NOT authenticate:

- a transparent frame (parity): not a leaf and not covered, so inserting or removing one changes nothing signed (the result lists
  the transparent frames);
- the storage form, the stored bytes, the stored check and the `signatures` list of any frame: a storage rewrite changes them
  without changing an identifier; and the `offsets` item and the locator;
- the payload bytes of a frame, except through its `payload_digest`: they are the signer's only when the payload check holds;
- any frame after `c`, and any frame whose link is broken;
- hiding: a `suppress` frame, clear or opened from a sealed frame, is content and is authenticated only like any content, so a
  `suppress` frame outside every authenticating signature can hide authenticated lines; the visibility state of the result carries
  the count of folded `suppress` frames that are not authenticated (`P7`, "The typed result of reading"), and a consumer that
  shows the visible dataset reads it; a `suppress` frame opened from a sealed frame is authenticated exactly when the sealed
  frame that carries it is an authenticated frame;
- a graph state: it is named by the effective identifier of its commit (`P3`, "Graph states"), and a signature on that commit
  authenticates what the commit covers in its own segment (above). It authenticates nothing hidden or added by frames outside
  the state, and it gives no reader a state: a commit names a state only to a reader for which every ordinary frame before it in
  its run is linked or healed and folded or not content (`P3`), and for another reader the state is unavailable whatever the
  signature; two readers that each find the state find equal states (`P3`, claim ST3);
- that `c` is the latest state: a rollback to an earlier valid commit, or a suffix lost from the end, is not
  detected by the file (`P5`, "What a commit does not show"), and a party that recorded a later root detects it
  by a consistency proof against that root;
- that the signer signed only one history: a signer can sign two commits at one position, which a consistency proof between their
  roots shows as `consistency_diverged` (`P5`, "Consistency");
- the truth or the meaning of the content, the signing time, or the identity behind a key (`A6`); the format has no timestamp.

CLAIM 4: A SIGNATURE ON A FRAME THAT IS NOT A COMMIT. It authenticates the frame and, through the unbroken run of the frame (7.1),
the frames of its coverage. It does not carry the root check, so a signed `index` frame, for instance, authenticates the frames
before it that its chain covers and says nothing of the commits' roots.

CLAIM 5: STRIPPING AND ADDING. The signatures are outside every identifier and every root, so removing a signature leaves a file
that is indistinguishable from one never signed by that key, and adding one needs no change to any identifier. A defence against
stripping is a requirement on the result, that the key of a named signer appears among the authenticating signatures of the frames
needed (a profile rule, section 9); no check of the core can supply it.

DETERMINISTIC SIGNING. It gives: a signature that is a function of the key and the subject, so that the same signer, the
same frame and the same file give the same bytes, which lets a storage rewrite re-sign without
creating a second signature, and removes the signing-time random source and with it a weak or repeated random input as a way to
leak a key (for `Ed25519` this is inherent in RFC 8032; for ML-DSA it is the deterministic variant of FIPS 204). It does not
defend: fault attacks and side channels against the signer, which FIPS 204 section 3.4 states are harder to mitigate without
randomness: a writer verifies each signature before it writes it (section 5), so a signature that a fault makes invalid is not
written, and this format states no other countermeasure; equivocation by the signer; and
anything on the verifier's side, because verification is the same for either form.

CLAIM 6: THE COMPOSITE. Under `A3`, `A4` and `A5`, a forgery of a composite signature on a subject the holder did not sign needs a
forgery of both halves or a collision of `SHA-512` (RFC 10073 section 9.2.1, which states the one-half-suffices property for
EUF-CMA). RFC 10073 section 9.2.2 states that the composite is not strongly unforgeable (SUF-CMA) against a quantum adversary, and
that two valid signatures of one key over one message can exchange halves. This format needs only EUF-CMA: no identifier, root or
preimage contains a signature, nothing depends on the uniqueness of a signature value, and a signature assembled from the halves
of two honest signatures of one subject is a valid signature of that subject by the holder. The classical half under the policy
of 4.1 accepts no more than RFC 8032, so `A3` is the assumption of RFC 8032 itself.

## 9. Co-signing and pinning

A frame can carry several signatures, each by its own key. Each is verified and reported by itself (P6-4): the result lists every
signature with its `status`, `reason`, `algorithm`, `key_id`, coverage and `authenticated`, and no signature outranks another.

There are two ways for a second signer to sign the same state.

- ADDING A SIGNATURE. The second signer signs the same frame: its subject is `[a, K2, h0, e]` with the same `h0` and `e`, and
  the record is inserted into the frame's `signatures` list at its place in ascending bytewise order of `E(signature)`. This is a
  storage rewrite (`P1`, "The frame envelope"): it changes
  no identifier, no root and no other signature.
- APPENDING A COMMIT. A writer that only appends writes another `commit` frame with the `count` and `root` of its own position,
  that is, over `h0` and every frame before it including the first commit, and its signer signs that frame. The second signer's
  signature then authenticates, by the chain, the first commit's identifier as well. A co-signer appends its commit only at the
  end of a file that ENDS AT AN ITEM BOUNDARY (`P1`, "Growing a file"), decided by its own walk of the bytes to their end: no
  capacity
  of a read bounds that walk, neither a total of the read nor `items_per_read`, since the co-signer writes to the file and gives
  no result of reading it. When its walk ends in an incomplete item or in bytes that cannot be delimited, it writes nothing.

A migration from one algorithm to another is the same act: the signer adds a signature of the new algorithm under a new key
beside the old, and each is reported separately. A verifier that requires two signers, or a particular algorithm, reads the
results and checks the keys and algorithms of the authenticating signatures; whether a given set of signers suffices is a rule
of the caller or of a profile, which reports a failure as `profile_violation` with the subject `[4, profile, present(check)]`
(`P7`, "The profile mechanism"), and never a rule of the file. The file holds no pin: no header field, frame or list names
the algorithms or keys a signature can use.

**P6-47.** A writer that adds a signature to an existing frame MUST sign the effective identifier of that frame and the `h0` of
its segment, MUST change no other field of the frame (`P1`, "The frame envelope"), MUST NOT remove or alter the signatures
already present, MUST NOT add a signature equal to one already present, and MUST write the list again in the order of P6-52.

**P6-48.** A writer that appends a commit for a second signer MUST write it as a commit of its own position under `P5`: `count`
the number of ordinary frames before it, and `root` over `h0` and those frames.

**P6-54.** A co-signer MUST append a commit only at the end of a file that ends at an item boundary, judged by its walk of
the bytes to their end and not by any capacity of a read, and MUST write no byte when its walk ends in an incomplete item or in
bytes that cannot be delimited.

**P6-49.** A verifier MUST report each signature of a frame by itself, MUST NOT merge or rank signatures by algorithm or
key (the results stand in the order of the list), and MUST NOT make a decision about a signer set; the decision is the caller's.

**P6-50.** A profile that pins an algorithm or a key, or requires a signature on every `commit` or from a named key, MUST state
its requirement over the fields of the `signature_result` and report a breach as `profile_violation` with the subject `[4,
profile, present(check)]`, the check being a value of its own
enumeration and the `reason` `absent` (`P2`, `P7`); no signature of the core file names such a requirement.

## 10. The one subject

**P6-51.** A reader of registry version `1` MUST NOT verify a signature over any subject other than that of
section 2. A reader MUST handle a value of `signature_algorithm` that its version does not hold only by section 1 and step 1 of
6.2.

## 11. Worked examples

Numeric codes appear in these examples only as the bytes of enumeration values: the code of `ed25519` (`1`) and of
`mldsa65_ed25519` (`2`) in the registry of version `1`, and the code `3` as a value that no registry of version `1` holds. `P2` is
the authority for every code. A name in angle brackets is 32 bytes that the example does not state, unless the example gives it
another length; `<V>` is 64 bytes.

### 11.1 The subject and its bytes

A signature of `ed25519` with the key identifier `<K>` on the frame whose effective identifier is `<F>`, in the segment whose
header identifier is `<H0>`. The subject is the typed value

```text
subject = [ ed25519, <K>, <H0>, <F> ]
```

and `E(subject)` is, element by element:

```text
84                       array of 4 elements
01                       algorithm: the code of ed25519, below 24, one byte
58 20 <K: 32 bytes>      key_id: byte string of 32 bytes
58 20 <H0: 32 bytes>     header_identifier
58 20 <F: 32 bytes>      frame_identifier
```

Its length is `1 + 1 + 34 + 34 + 34 = 104` bytes (the head `58 20` is two bytes and the digest 32): a 32-byte `bytes` item is
the head byte `0x58` (major type `2`, length in one following byte),
the byte `0x20`, and 32 bytes, so 34; three of them are `102`; the array head and the code add `2`. The message is
`H_ctx(signature_subject, E(subject))`: 32 bytes of the key-derivation mode of `BLAKE3` with the context `gts2 signature subject`.
For a signature of `mldsa65_ed25519` the subject is the same record with the
code `2`, the bytes `84 02 58 20 ...`, also 104 bytes.

### 11.2 The key identifier and its bytes

For an `ed25519` key of the 32 bytes `<PK>`: `key_preimage = [1, <PK>]`, `E(key_preimage) = 82 01 58 20 <PK>`, 36 bytes, and
`key_id = H_ctx(key_id, E(key_preimage))`. For a composite key of 1984 bytes `<PK2>`: `E(key_preimage) = 82 02 59 07 c0
<PK2>`, with
`59 07 c0` the head of a byte string of 1984 bytes (`0x07c0` is `7 * 256 + 192`), `1 + 1 + 3 + 1984 = 1989` bytes.

### 11.3 The message representative of the composite

For a `message` `<M>` of 32 bytes, `M'` is 127 bytes: bytes `0` to `31` the `Prefix`, bytes `32` to `61` the `Label`, byte `62`
the value `00`, bytes `63` to `126` the 64 bytes of `SHA-512(<M>)`. The first 63 bytes, which do not depend on `<M>`, are, with
the byte count of the three runs `32 + 30 + 1`:

```text
43 6f 6d 70 6f 73 69 74 65 41 6c 67 6f 72 69 74 68 6d 53 69 67 6e 61 74 75 72 65 73 32 30 32 35     Prefix
43 4f 4d 50 53 49 47 2d 4d 4c 44 53 41 36 35 2d 45 64 32 35 35 31 39 2d 53 48 41 35 31 32           Label
00                                                                                                   len(ctx)
```

The 64 bytes `SHA-512(<M>)` are not stated. The sizes: the
`value` is `3309 + 64 = 3373` bytes and the key `1952 + 32 = 1984` bytes; the ML-DSA half verifies `M'` with

### 11.4 The procedure on a failing case of each reason

One segment: the header at position `0` (`h0 = <H0>`); `F1` a `blob` frame at position `1` (ordinary position `0`, effective
identifier `<I1>`); `F2` a `commit` frame at position `2` (ordinary position `1`, effective identifier `<I2>`, content `[1, <R2>]`
with `<R2>` the root over `h0` and `<I1>`), whose link holds and whose commit is valid. `F2` carries one signature
`[1, <KA>, <V>]`, made by the key `A` with `key_id` `<KA>` and public key `<PKA>`, over the subject `[1, <KA>, <H0>, <I2>]`. The
inputs are `trusted_keys = [<KA>]` and `held_keys = [[<KA>, 1, <PKA>]]` unless a case changes them. A signature result is written
`[status, reason, algorithm, key_id, coverage_first, coverage_end, authenticated]`. The unchanged case gives
`[valid, absent, 1, <KA>, 0, 2, true]`, no finding, `authenticated_frames` `2` and `authenticity` `authenticated`.

Cases (the first column is the case; the result is `[status, reason, algorithm, key_id, coverage_first, coverage_end,
authenticated]`; every failing signature has one finding, `signature_invalid` or `signature_unverified`, with the subject
`[3, signature_algorithm]` and the position `2`).

1. CHANGE: the segment's `h` is `2`, the reader's `r` is `1`, the signature's `algorithm` is `3`. DECIDES: step 1. RESULT:
   `[unverified, unknown_signature_algorithm, 3, <KA>, 1, 1, false]`. FINDINGS: `signature_unverified` with the code `3` as the
   subject's value, and `unsupported_registry_version` once for the segment.
2. CHANGE: `held_keys` is empty. DECIDES: step 2. RESULT: `[unverified, key_not_held, 1, <KA>, 1, 1, false]`.
3. CHANGE: `held_keys = [[<KA>, 1, <PKB>]]` with `<PKB>` another key. DECIDES: step 3. RESULT:
   `[invalid, key_mismatch, 1, <KA>, 1, 1, false]`.
4. CHANGE: `<V>` has 63 bytes. DECIDES: step 4. RESULT: `[invalid, value_length, 1, <KA>, 1, 1, false]`.
5. CHANGE: `value[32:64]` is the integer `S + L`, which is at least `L`; the held key is consistent, so steps 2 to 4 pass.
   DECIDES: step 6, check 1. RESULT: `[invalid, non_canonical_scalar, 1, <KA>, 1, 1, false]`.
6. CHANGE: the held key is the encoding `01` followed by 31 bytes `00`, with its own consistent identifier `<KS>` as the
   signature's `key_id`; `S` is below `L`. DECIDES: step 6, check 2. RESULT: `[invalid, small_order_key, 1, <KS>, 1, 1, false]`.
7. CHANGE: `value[0:32]` is `ec`, 30 bytes `ff`, then `7f`; the key is `A`; `S` is below `L`. DECIDES: step 6, check 3. RESULT:
   `[invalid, small_order_nonce, 1, <KA>, 1, 1, false]`.
8. CHANGE: `value[32:64]` is `S + 1`, still below `L`. DECIDES: step 6, check 4. RESULT:
   `[invalid, equation_failed, 1, <KA>, 1, 1, false]`.
9. CHANGE: as case 8 but the key is the alias `y = p`, which does not decode, with its own consistent identifier as the
   signature's `key_id`. DECIDES: step 6, check 4. RESULT: `equation_failed`,
   not `small_order_key`.
10. CHANGE: as case 6 and also `value[32:64]` is `S + L`. DECIDES: step 6, check 1. RESULT: `non_canonical_scalar`: the scalar is
    checked before the key.
11. CHANGE: the signature is a composite (`2`, `<KC>`, a value of 3373 bytes); one bit of the last 64 bytes is changed and the
    first
    3309 bytes are intact. DECIDES: step 6, `Composite-Strict` 3. RESULT: `[invalid, composite_classical_failed, 2, <KC>, 1,
    1, false]`.
12. CHANGE: as case 11 but one bit of the first 3309 bytes is changed and the last 64 are intact. DECIDES: step 6,
    `Composite-Strict` 4. RESULT: `[invalid, composite_post_quantum_failed, 2, <KC>, 1, 1, false]`.
13. CHANGE: as case 11 with both parts changed. DECIDES: step 6, `Composite-Strict` 3. RESULT: `composite_classical_failed`: the
    classical half is checked first.
14. CHANGE: the composite value has 3372 bytes. DECIDES: step 4. RESULT: `value_length`, not a failure of a half.

In every failing case the one signature is not authenticating, `authenticated_frames` is `0` and `authenticity` is
`unauthenticated`. The effects on the other facts are none: the link of `F2`, the commit and the fold are independent of the
signature (P6-39). In case 5 the held key is consistent, so steps 2 to 4 pass and the scalar is the first failure.

### 11.5 Coverage and authentication of a short segment with two commits and two signers

The segment: the header (position `0`); `F1` `blob` (position `1`, ordinary position `0`); `F2` `commit` `C1` (position `2`,
ordinary position `1`, `count` `1`); `F3` `blob` (position `3`, ordinary position `2`); `F4` `commit` `C2` (position `4`,
ordinary position `3`, `count` `3`); `F5` `index` (position `5`, ordinary position `4`), as a canonical file ends. Every link
holds and both commits are valid. The signers: `A` signs `F2` (signature `SA1`) and `F4` (signature `SA2`); `B` co-signs `F4`
(signature `SB`). The list of `F4` holds `SA2` and `SB` in ascending bytewise order of their encodings, and in this example
the encoding of `SA2` is the smaller, so `SA2` comes first. The inputs are
`trusted_keys = [<KA>]` and `held_keys` with both keys.

| signature | frame | status | coverage | authenticated |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `SA1` | `F2` | `valid` | `[0, 2)` | `true`: valid, trusted, link holds, `C1` valid |
| `SA2` | `F4` | `valid` | `[0, 4)` | `true` |
| `SB` | `F4` | `valid` | `[0, 4)` | `false`: `<KB>` is not in `trusted_keys` |

The authenticated frames are the ordinary positions `0` to `3`. The counted frames are `F1` to `F4` (the `index` `F5`
is not counted), so `authenticated_frames` is `4` of `4` and `authenticity` is `authenticated`. If `trusted_keys` holds
both keys, `SB` is `true` and the counts are the same. If a `blob` frame `F4b` stands between `C2` and the index, it is
the uncommitted tail (`uncommitted_tail`, `count` `1`, by `P5`), covered by no signature: the counted frames are five,
`authenticated_frames` is `4` and `authenticity` is `partly_authenticated`; a signature of `A` on `F4b` (not a `commit`) would
cover `[0, 5)` and make it `authenticated`.

A broken frame in the middle. The content of `F3` is altered after the writing and its `identifier` field is not (its link is
`broken`, finding `chain_break`). The link of `F4` holds, because `F4` is linked against the stored identifier of `F3`, and
the signature `SA2` is `valid`, because its subject holds the stored identifier of `F4`. The unbroken run of `F4` is `F4` alone
(the
frame before it is broken), so the coverage of `SA2` is `[3, 4)`. `C2` is valid (its root is over the stored identifiers the
signer saw), so `SA2` authenticates, and the authenticated frames are those of ordinary position at most `3` whose link holds:
`F1`, `F2` and `F4`. `F3` is neither covered nor authenticated. `authenticated_frames` is `3` of `4` and `authenticity` is
`partly_authenticated`. `SA1` is unchanged, `[valid, absent, 1, <KA>, 0, 2, true]`.
